The independent proof reviewed is in bipartite.md. The bipartite proof is valid. The triangle-free nonbipartite case at \(\kappa=3\) is impossible for the exact residual, as shown below.

## Bipartite case

- Connectivity supplies \(\delta\ge k\), and every neighborhood is independent, so the induced-star bound supplies \(\Delta\le k+1\). In particular each side has at least \(k\) vertices.
- For same-side \(x,y\) sharing \(z\), the displayed double star is induced. No private vertex equals \(z\); private vertices have no edges to each other or to \(z\), and each is adjacent to exactly its designated endpoint. The tree inequality gives \(|N(x)\triangle N(y)|\le k-1\), hence \(|N(x)\cap N(y)|\ge(d(x)+d(y)-k+1)/2>d(y)/2\).
- Transitivity follows by intersecting the two strict majorities in \(N(y)\). Equality cases for the relation are automatic. Connectedness gives an even-length walk between any same-side pair and grouping consecutive edges applies transitivity, so all such pairs share a neighbor.
- The two incidence bounds are correct. If \(d(x_0)=k\) then \((|X|-1)(k+1)\le2k^2\); if every member of \(X\) has degree \(k+1\) then \((|X|-1)(k+3)\le2k(k+1)\). Each common-neighbor incidence charges a vertex of \(N(x_0)\) at most \(k\) times, accounting for its edge to \(x_0\). Both inequalities imply \(|X|\le2k-1\) for \(k\ge3\).
- Removing a side gives the stated component count even if the other side has no induced edges, since each remaining vertex is a singleton. Balancing adds at most one vertex and no degree drops. The balanced part size satisfies \(3\le r\le2k-1\). In the reverse closure step, a restored cycle using the added edge yields the spanning path \(x_1,y_1,\ldots,x_r,y_r\) with \(x_1=u,y_r=v\). The two index sets of original edges \(uy_i\) and \(x_iv\) overlap by the degree sum. Neither endpoint index can belong to the overlap because \(uv\) is missing. Cutting at \(x_iy_i\) and adding the two original edges gives a genuine cycle using both pieces, without repeating a vertex. The starting complete bipartite graph has a Hamilton cycle because \(r\ge3\). Deleting the balancing vertex if present, or a single cycle edge otherwise, produces the required path.

The no-induced-five-cycle extension is sound. The only potential new edges among the leaves are between the two private sets, and each such edge completes an induced five-cycle since triangle-freeness excludes its chords involving the shared neighbor. The resulting equivalence classes have no internal edges and a walk from either endpoint of a fixed edge covers the two classes. The diameter-four argument also checks: the two endpoint leaf sets are disjoint by shortestness; the at-most-\(d(v)\) losses in aggregate use disjointness of \(N(x)\) and \(N(y)\). Edges within a leaf set would be triangles, while edges across leaf sets or to other path vertices would shorten the chosen geodesic or create a triangle.

## Triangle-free and nonbipartite at \(k=3\)

A shortest odd cycle is induced because a chord creates a smaller odd cycle on one side. Triangle-freeness and the induced-tree bound force length five. If a vertex has distance at least two from the cycle, choose an exact distance-two vertex \(x\) and its distance-one neighbor \(y\). When \(y\) has two cycle neighbors, delete one; when \(y\) has one, delete any other cycle vertex. The four remaining cycle vertices form a path and \(y,x\) attach as a two-edge pendant path, a contradiction.

The \(S_i\) and \(T_i\) classification checks. Two vertices in the same \(S_i\) share a cycle neighbor, so are nonadjacent. Deleting the other cycle neighbor gives the forbidden induced tree. For two nonconsecutive roles, the representatives likewise share the unique common cycle neighbor, and deleting it attaches them as leaves at the two internal vertices of the four-vertex path. Thus at most two \(S\)-roles occur and they are consecutive. For the single-neighbor types, reflection reduces the possible inter-type adjacency to \(T_0T_1\) or \(T_0T_2\). The two displayed induced trees have no chords because the outside vertices have only their specified cycle neighbors. Adjacent vertices cannot both be in \(T_0\) by triangle-freeness.

If no \(T_i\) occurs, even two consecutive \(S\)-roles collectively see only four cycle vertices, leaving one of degree two. Once \(T_0\) is nonempty, minimum degree three forces both allowed consecutive roles \(S_2,S_3\), with the two outside neighbors \(u,v\) of every member of \(T_0\). For each \(T_i\) the only feasible required consecutive pair is \(S_{i+2},S_{i+3}\), so all other \(T_i\) are empty. The representatives \(u,v\) are nonadjacent because they share a \(T_0\) neighbor.

The path \(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4,v,w_1,u\) is valid, with the final edge to \(w_2\) also valid if allowed. The latter possibility is actually excluded: \(\{c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,w_1,w_2\}\) induces a six-vertex tree, with both \(w\)'s leaves at \(c_0\). For the only surviving graph, the cycle contributes at most two independent vertices, and the only outside two-element independent choice is \(\{u,v\}\). That choice rules out \(c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4\), so the entire graph has \(\alpha\le3\). This conflicts with \(\alpha\ge5\) for the exact residual. Thus the \(k=3\) triangle-free branch can use the bipartite proof alone, with the nonbipartite branch eliminated by contradiction.
