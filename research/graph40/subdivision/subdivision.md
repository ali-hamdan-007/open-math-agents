# Graph 40 for subcubic subdivisions

Consider the exact residual WrittenOnTheWallII.GraphConjecture40.conjecture40.R1.H1. Its desired inequality, with \(n=|V|\), is \(p+n+1\le 2f\), where \(p\) is the minimum vertex-disjoint path-cover cardinality and \(f\) is the maximum number of vertices in an induced forest.

**Branch.** Let \(G\) be a connected bipartite graph on at least four vertices, with parts \(X,Y\). Suppose every member of \(Y\) has degree two and every member of \(X\) has degree two or three. Then \(p+n+1\le 2f\). This includes subdivisions of connected loopless multigraphs of minimum degree two and maximum degree three, with a distinct subdivision vertex for every edge. Neither vertex-deletion connectedness nor the strict scattering inequality is needed in this branch.

**Path cover.** For \(U\subseteq X\), count incidences between \(U\) and \(Y\) to get \(2|U|\le2|N(U)|\). Hall supplies a matching saturating \(X\). In particular \(|X|\le|Y|\). Its edges together with unmatched singletons give \(|Y|\) paths. If a member of \(Y\) is unmatched, connectedness gives it a neighbor in \(X\). Add it to the end of that neighbor's matching edge. If no member is unmatched, the matching is perfect and contains at least two edges. Connectedness gives an edge joining different matched pairs, which concatenates those edges into a four-vertex path. Either way, \(p\le|Y|-1\).

**Induced forest.** A vertex \(y\in Y\) determines a distinct-edge occurrence in a multigraph \(M\) on \(X\), with endpoints the two neighbors of \(y\). Parallel occurrences are allowed. The degree in \(M\), counting multiplicities, equals the degree in \(G\). Choose a two-coloring of \(X\) minimizing the number of monochromatic edge occurrences. If a vertex had at least two monochromatic incident occurrences, at most one other incident occurrence would have the opposite color. Switching the vertex would decrease the objective. Thus each color induces a multigraph of maximum degree at most one, hence a matching and isolated vertices. With \(T\) a larger color class, the induced graph on \(Y\cup T\) is a forest. It has the matching subdivided by the corresponding members of \(Y\), plus isolated members of \(Y\) and leaves adjacent to \(T\) arising from the remaining occurrences. Therefore \(2f\ge2|Y|+|X|\), which is at least \(p+n+1\).

The same argument separates into two independent sufficient conditions. The path-cover bound applies to every connected bipartite graph on at least four vertices with a matching saturating \(X\). The induced-forest bound applies whenever \(X\) can be partitioned into two classes such that each \(x\) has at most one neighbor in \(Y\) that also sees a different vertex of the same class as \(x\). In particular, the degree-two requirement on \(Y\) may be weakened in the forest proof if this coloring condition can still be established.

This is a paper proof of one unbounded branch, rather than a proof of the parent residual. The local Lean file addresses the acyclicity implication used in the general forest condition.

## Extensions beyond maximum degree three

The same full-branch conclusion holds if the projected multigraph \(M\), counting multiplicities, is 3-degenerate. Process the vertices in a degeneracy ordering with at most three incident edge occurrences to earlier vertices. Assign each new vertex a color for which at most one of those occurrences is monochromatic. In the resulting color classes any hypothetical cycle has a latest vertex with two earlier incident cycle edges, a contradiction. Parallel edges are handled by counting them twice when they share two endpoints. The matching and path-cover arguments are unchanged, because they only use the lower bound two on left degree and the right degree exactly two.

For example, this proves the bound for subdivisions of connected simple triangle-free planar graphs of minimum degree at least two. Every subgraph of a simple triangle-free planar graph has a vertex of degree at most three by the planar edge bound, so the base graph is 3-degenerate. It also proves the bound for subdivisions of connected loopless bipartite multigraphs of minimum degree at least two, with no upper degree bound: use the base bipartition as the two induced forests.
