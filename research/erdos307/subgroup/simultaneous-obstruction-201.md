# An obstruction to simultaneous lifting modulo 3 and 67

There are finite sets of distinct odd primes, of arbitrarily large reciprocal mass, that admit a common quadratic partition modulo 201 and admit the two swap equations separately modulo 3 and modulo 67. No partition of any of these supports satisfies both swap equations modulo 201. The construction can occupy 44 of the 132 reduced residue classes modulo 201.

For a finite support \(S\), write \(N=\prod_{p\in S}p\) and \(C=N\sum_{p\in S}p^{-1}\). For a split with side products \(A,B\) and reciprocal sums \(U,V\), the swap equations are \(AU=B\) and \(BV=A\). When these equations hold in a modulus coprime to \(N\), they imply \(A^2+B^2=C\).

## A common quadratic split and incompatible swap equations

Take \(n=1784+4422j\) distinct primes in residue class 136 modulo 201, with \(j\ge0\) large enough for any prescribed reciprocal mass. The class is reduced. The sum of reciprocals of primes in a reduced class diverges. For example, the Dirichlet-series form of Dirichlet's theorem makes \(\sum_{p\equiv136\pmod{201}}p^{-s}\) unbounded as \(s\) decreases to 1, and this series is bounded above by \(\sum_{p\equiv136\pmod{201}}1/p\) for every \(s>1\). We can therefore take the first \(n\) primes of the class for sufficiently large \(j\). [MIT 18.785, Lecture 18, §18.4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-785-number-theory-i-fall-2021/mit18_785f21_lec18.pdf#page=7)

Each support prime is 1 modulo 3 and 2 modulo 67. Since \(n\equiv2\pmod{66}\) and \(n\equiv42\pmod{67}\), the product and cofactor sum are

\[
N\equiv4\pmod{201},\qquad C\equiv17\pmod{201}.
\]

Indeed modulo 67 they are \(2^n=4\) and \(n2^{n-1}=17\), while modulo 3 they are 1 and 2. A side of size 33 has squared product 1 and its complement has squared product 16 modulo 201. This is a common quadratic split.

Any simultaneous swap solution has, modulo 67, squared product \(x=A^2\) satisfying

\[
x^2-17x+16=(x-1)(x-16)=0.
\]

If the first side has size \(k\), then \(x=4^k\). The order of 4 modulo 67 is 33, so the roots force \(k\equiv0\) or \(2\pmod{33}\). Modulo 3 the swap equation \(AU=B\) requires \(U=1\), or \(k\equiv1\pmod3\). These conditions are incompatible for every side size.

A side of size 7 satisfies the swap equations modulo 3. A side of size 1683 satisfies them modulo 67: its squared product is 1 because 33 divides 1683, and its reciprocal sum is \(1683/2\equiv4=N/A^2\pmod{67}\). Its complement has reciprocal sum \(C/N-4=1/4\pmod{67}\). Each local choice preserves the starting squared product and odd parity in its own prime modulus. Three placements on each side of the common size-33 split can also be preserved in each local choice. Both proposed side sizes and their complements have size at least three.

## Occupying 44 residue classes

Let \(\chi:\mathbb F_{67}^{\times 2}\to\mathbb F_3\) be the homomorphism defined by \(\chi(4^j)=j\pmod3\). Use the 44 reduced residue classes \(r\pmod{201}\) satisfying

\[
r^{-1}\bmod3=\chi(r^2\bmod67).
\]

The square group has 33 elements, of which 22 give a nonzero value under \(\chi\). Each of those 22 has two roots modulo 67, and each root determines a unique nonzero residue modulo 3 through the displayed relation. The original class 136 is one of the 44.

Pad the original support with a positive multiple of 4422 distinct primes in each of the other classes. Each padding group has product 1 and reciprocal sum 0 modulo 201, since \(4422=66\cdot67\) is divisible by 201 and by the order of every unit modulo 201. The values \(N,C\) and the common quadratic split persist, and the two separate local witnesses persist with all padding primes in their complements. For any subset of the padded support, regardless of how many classes it uses, additivity of \(\chi\) now gives \(U\bmod3=\chi(A^2\bmod67)\). The only possible squared products in a simultaneous swap split are still 1 and 16 modulo 67, which give \(U\equiv0\) and \(2\pmod3\), respectively. The required value is 1. Each occupied class can independently have arbitrarily large reciprocal mass by choosing sufficiently many padding primes in it.

## The subgroup obstruction

For an odd modulus \(M\), put \(G=(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^{\times 2}\times\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) and write \(g_r=(r^2,1)\) for a unit residue. If \(R\) is the set of occupied residue classes, let \(H\) be the subgroup of \(G\times(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)\) generated by \((g_r,r^{-1})\) for \(r\in R\). Write \(P\) for its projection to \(G\), and let \(K=\{u:(0,u)\in H\}\) be the subgroup of available vertical translations. For each \(g\in P\), the fiber in \(H\) is a coset of \(K\). If each occupied class has at least the order of its atom minus one primes, every element of \(H\) is attained by a subset. This holds because use of each generator with any coefficient modulo its order can be realized by a choice of copies from its class.

The projection of \(K\) to \(\mathbb F_p\) is zero for a prime \(p\mid M\) exactly when there is a homomorphism \(\psi:P\to\mathbb F_p\) with \(\psi(g_r)=r^{-1}\bmod p\) for all \(r\in R\). In one direction, the second coordinate modulo \(p\) then factors through \(P\). Conversely, such a factorization forces the second coordinate to vanish modulo \(p\) on the kernel. A proper subgroup of the cyclic additive group modulo \(M\) has zero projection at some prime divisor of \(M\), so these homomorphisms detect every proper vertical kernel. They describe the subgroup obstruction. Finite supports with fewer copies can have further restrictions on their attainable subset sums.

For odd squarefree \(M\), a subgroup obstruction is possible for some nonempty collection of classes exactly when distinct prime divisors \(p,q\mid M\) satisfy \(p\mid q-1\). Necessity follows because \(\psi\) must be nonzero and \(|G|=2\prod_{q\mid M}(q-1)/2\). For sufficiency, take a primitive root \(a\) modulo \(q\) and a CRT class equal to \(a\) modulo \(q\) and 1 at all other prime factors. The square-group homomorphism through \(\mathbb F_q^{\times 2}\) that sends \(a^2\) to 1 modulo \(p\) satisfies the required reciprocal relation in this class.

The same construction produces an actual simultaneous swap obstruction for every such pair. Choose \(n\equiv2\pmod{q-1}\), \(n\equiv(1+a^4)/a\pmod q\), and \(n\equiv2\) at all remaining prime factors of \(M\). These constraints are compatible. Take the first \(n\) primes in the stated CRT class with \(n\) sufficiently large. A side of size \(q-1\) gives the common quadratic split. At \(q\), a simultaneous swap split must have squared product 1 or \(a^4\), with corresponding counts 0 or 2 modulo \((q-1)/2\), and hence modulo \(p\). At \(p\) the required count is 1. Separate local splits exist at \(q\) by choosing the size congruent to 0 modulo \(q-1\) and to \(a^3\) modulo \(q\), and at the remaining prime factors by choosing the size even and congruent to 1 modulo the corresponding prime.

At \(M=201\), the square-and-parity group is cyclic of order 66. A nonzero homomorphism from any of its subgroups to \(\mathbb F_3\) is, up to multiplication by 2, the restriction of \(\chi\) on the squared-product coordinate. A subgroup obstruction therefore allows at most 44 occupied classes, and the padded example reaches this bound.
