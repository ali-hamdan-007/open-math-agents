# Additive saturation at an omitted odd prime

Let ℓ be an odd prime outside a finite support S of distinct primes. Put
N=∏p and C=∑N/p. For a subset T, write A=∏_{p∈T}p and
U=∑_{p∈T}1/p in F_ℓ, and write B,V for the complement's quantities.
For r∈F_ℓ^× define d_r=lcm(2,ord(r²)), and let
D=lcm(2,(ℓ−1)/2)=max_r d_r. Set

    K_ℓ=1+D(ℓ−2)+2∑_{r∈F_ℓ^×}(d_r−1).

If |S|≥K_ℓ, then for every x attained as A² by a subset of a
prescribed cardinality parity, all U∈F_ℓ occur with that same x
and parity. The complement's parity is consequently preserved.
The statement also holds for subsets whose two side sizes are at
least b, provided |S|≥K_ℓ+2b and x has a witness with both side sizes
at least b. In particular, take b=3 for odd sides of size at least
three. The simpler sufficient threshold is
1+D(ℓ−2)+2(ℓ−1)(D−1)+2b, where D≤ℓ−1.

To prove this, start with a witness and reserve b primes from each
side, requiring all these primes to remain on their original sides.
Write m_r for the number of unreserved primes of residue r. If the
witness selected t_r of these, let a_r be the least nonnegative
residue of t_r modulo d_r. We may select independently any count
a_r+j d_r for 0≤j≤q_r=floor((m_r−a_r)/d_r). This preserves the
squared product and parity, since d_r is even and (r²)^d_r=1.
The reciprocal sums available from class r form a translate of the
sum of q_r copies of {0,d_r/r}. The step d_r/r is nonzero, because
d_r divides ℓ−1. A nonempty proper subset X of F_ℓ strictly grows
on replacing it by X∪(X+d_r/r): equality would force invariance
under the additive group generated by d_r/r, hence X=F_ℓ.
Thus at least ℓ−1 such steps give the whole field. If there were
at most ℓ−2 steps, the bound

    m_r ≤ a_r+d_r q_r+d_r−1 ≤ d_r q_r+2(d_r−1)

would give ∑m_r≤D(ℓ−2)+2∑(d_r−1)=K_ℓ−1, a contradiction.
Reserved primes only translate the reciprocal sums and multiply the
squared products by constants.

Consequently the existence of a genuine partition of the prescribed
parity and size range passing A²+B²=C implies the existence of one
passing both AU=B and BV=A. Indeed, set x=A² and use saturation to
choose U=N/x. Then V=C/N−U=x/N, since C=x+N²/x, so both swap equations
hold. The converse follows directly by adding them after multiplying
by B and A respectively. Neither attainment in a residue-generated
subgroup nor ambient square checks by themselves supply the required
starting partition. Exact sizes are outside the theorem, and the
existing mod-5 example with first side size 33 shows that large mass
cannot give the exact-size analogue.

The reciprocal mass criterion is explicit: it suffices to exceed
the reciprocal sum of the first K_ℓ+2b−1 eligible primes (omit ℓ and
also omit 2 for odd supports). An elementary alternative is mass
greater than log(K_ℓ+2b) for arbitrary supports, or greater than
½ log(2(K_ℓ+2b)−1) for odd supports. In fact the j-th eligible prime
is at least j+1, or 2j+1 for odd supports, respectively, and summing
these reciprocal bounds is bounded by the corresponding integrals
from 0 to the largest allowed cardinality.

At ℓ=5 all d_r=2, giving K_5=15, or 21 with both side sizes at
least three. This replaces the existing note's >40 global bound
by summing independent contributions across residue classes. Its
mass-2 assertion still follows from the first-40 check. More
generally the first-prime mass cutoff is below 2 for each odd
prime ℓ≤29 on odd supports, even with K_ℓ+6, as certified by the
integer upper bounds in /tmp/check_prime1916.py.

## Residue-specific mass cutoff

One can improve the mass bound using primes in their actual residue
classes. List the eligible primes of each class r in increasing order.
Set c_r=2(d_r−1). If a witness has fewer than ℓ−1 additive steps
after reserving b primes on each side, put s_r for the number reserved
in residue class r. The total count in that class is at most
c_r+d_r q_r+s_r. Its reciprocal mass is at most the sum of the first
c_r terms of its prime list, the next q_r blocks of d_r terms, and
s_r additional terms. Here a term is the reciprocal of the listed
prime. The additional terms are bounded by the first s_r terms
after the c_r baseline, since their indices are shifted up by d_r q_r.

Thus a witness with fewer than ℓ−1 steps can have reciprocal mass
no greater than the sum of the following three numbers:

- The reciprocal mass of the first c_r primes from every residue.
- The ℓ−2 largest block sums across all residue classes, using each
  class's first ℓ−2 blocks of d_r primes after its baseline.
- The 2b largest individual reciprocal terms across all classes
  among the first 2b terms after each baseline.

A mass strictly larger than this computable bound guarantees the
same saturation. When b=3 and the support is odd, the bound is
below 2 for every odd prime ℓ≤41. The verifier uses rounded-up
integer reciprocals for both mass bounds.
