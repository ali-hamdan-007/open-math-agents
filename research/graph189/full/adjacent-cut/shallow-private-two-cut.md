# A shallow side with no common ports when the component vertices are high

Supplementary paper argument by gpt-6.2-astra. Assume all vertices outside the two nonadjacent anchors p,q have degree at least k, and the cut leaves exactly two components C,D. Assume every vertex of D is adjacent to exactly one anchor, and k≥4. Then G has a spanning path. This extension refers to deep-high-two-cut.md and nonadjacent-two-cut.md.

D has both types of private port and its order d is at least k because each port has only one neighbor outside. At most k−1 D vertices touch p. If k did, a p-contact of C would use its whole budget on them and would be universal in C. If it missed q, an internal q-contact would supply another witness, so it would have to be common, but then a q-only D vertex is an additional witness. The same holds for q, so d≤2k−2. The internal minimum degree is at least k−1 and Dirac gives a Hamilton cycle in D. Its ports have two nonempty types; open the cycle at an edge between the two types to obtain a spanning p,D,q segment.

If a D port is at distance at least three from its opposite anchor, the old far-contact argument in case 3 of deep-high-two-cut.md gives |C|≤2k−2. Indeed p has an internal D vertex at distance two even though D does not have a nonport: follow a path from a p-only to a q-only port and take the first vertex missing p. Then the C-plus-marker path closure works with no need for a C nonport, as the threshold is ≤2k−2. Assume henceforth every D contact has distance two to its opposite anchor.

Any C vertex of depth at least three from the anchor set has distance exactly four from every D vertex by the high-pair bound. Its distance-two layer has size at least two by 2-connectivity, contradicting d≥k. Every C nonport thus has depth two. Write R for the C nonports, W for the C nonports with both anchors at distance two, and P,T for all C ports and the common C ports. As in deep-high-two-cut.md, the anchor counts give d+|R|+|W|≤2k, so |R|≤k and 2|W|≤k. The p-only and q-only D contact counts in that note still yield |C|≤2k−2+|W|−|T|≤3k−4 since k≥4.

If n≤3k the earlier order theorem applies. If n=3k+1 then |C|≤2k−1. The C-plus-marker closure of nonadjacent-two-cut.md works at |C|≤2k−2 regardless of nonports. At |C|=2k−1 there is a nonport in C: each D p-contact sees q at distance two, whence C contains at most k−1 p-contacts; similarly for q. This supplies the hypothesis of the prior |C|=2k−1 compression lemma.

Finally suppose n≥3k+2. On H=C+m, the marker adjacent to the ports, the same proof as in deep-high-two-cut.md gives degrees at least k. In detail |P|=n−2−d−|R|≥n−2−2k≥k, and for a C common port x its quota on opposite D ports and nonadjacent C port peers gives d_G(x)≥|P|+d−k+1≥n+d−3k−1≥k+1. The graph H is again 2-connected, and an independent (k+1)-set in C is ruled out by the same two-neighborhood argument at |C|≤3k−4. For an independent (k+1)-set containing m, we would need |R|=k with R independent. Then d+|R|+|W|≤2k and d≥k give W empty. Any member of R needs k neighbors, all among the C ports by independence. No single anchor has k C contacts, as noted in the previous paragraph. Thus those k neighbors collectively meet both anchors, contradicting W empty. This proves α(H)≤k, and Nash-Williams yields a Hamilton cycle in H. Delete m and append the p,D,q segment as in the earlier theorem.
