# A nonadjacent two-cut must have exactly one side of a particular form

Paper proof by gpt-6.2-astra. Let p,q be nonadjacent vertices separating a qualifying 2-connected graph G into exactly two components. Suppose every component vertex has degree at least k and both components contain a vertex adjacent to neither p nor q. A port is a vertex adjacent to at least one of p,q. Call a side balanced in this statement if it has no common port, every port has distance exactly two from the anchor it misses, and every nonport has distance two from both anchors.

If G has no spanning path, precisely one of the two components is balanced.

For two nonports on opposite sides the distance is exactly four since both are high. Hence all nonports see at least one anchor at distance two. Each side has at most k−2 nonports by the opposite-nonport lemma of adjacent-separator.md. If both components are balanced, the positive-even budgets of p and q, added together, count every port once and every nonport twice. Thus n≤2k+2, a traceable order by Easwar–Johari. It remains to exclude two unbalanced sides.

For a component D failing one of the three conditions, the opposite side C has order at most 2k−2, as follows.

1. If D contains a common port, it sees all C ports at distance two. There are thus at most k such ports and at most k−2 nonports.
2. Suppose D contains a nonport w having distance two only to p. Every C vertex missing p has distance at least four from w: entry through p costs 2+2 and entry through q costs at least 3+1. High vertex distances are at most four, so all these vertices use even slots at w, and p itself uses another. Hence at most k−1 vertices in C miss p. There are also at most k−1 p-contacts in C. Otherwise a p-contact in D uses its entire even budget on C, so it is universal in D. It must be a common port: if it misses q, the q-contact in D puts q at positive even distance two. Thus w would see q at distance two through this internally universal common port, a contradiction.
3. Suppose D has a private p-contact w with distance at least three to q. Every C vertex x at distance at least three from p has distance four from w: entry through p costs at least 1+3, entry through q at least 3+1, and high pairs are at distance at most four. Each C vertex at distance one from p also has positive even distance from w. Since w is not universal in D (otherwise an internal q-contact would give distance two to q), a shortest internal path to a missed vertex produces an internal distance-two witness. Thus at most k−1 C vertices have p-distance other than two. Also at most k−1 C vertices have p-distance two, as the path inside D from a p-contact to a nonport yields a vertex of D at distance two from p. This gives the desired C bound. In particular C has at most k−1 p-contacts.

If both sides fail a condition, both have order at most 2k−2. On one side C use the two-anchor closure of adjacent-separator.md to get a spanning path of C,p,q rooted at either specified anchor. For the other side D use one anchor and a leaf as in that manuscript. If |D|≤2k−3, the ordinary closure completes D. At |D|=2k−2 and when the opposite C fails in case 2 or 3, omit p identified by that case: at least k−1 D vertices miss p and become universal in D, giving a spanning path from any retained-anchor contact by alternation. If both sides only fail in case 1, each contains a common port and the earlier theorem applies. Otherwise select as one-anchor side the component opposite one that fails in case 2 or 3. Gluing the paths gives the result.

For the remaining balanced side D, put a,b,r for its number of p-ports, q-ports and nonports respectively. Its order d is at least k+1 since a nonport has k neighbors within D. Both positive-even budgets give b+r≤k and a+r≤k, whence d−k≤min(a,b). Every private port in D has at least k−1 internal neighbors and every nonport at least k. These elementary bounds constrain the remaining case, whose general traceability is open here.

## The larger side must contain at least 2k vertices

In the residual case the balanced side D is Hamilton-connected. In fact each anchor has a distance-two witness in C, by following a path inside C from an anchor contact to a nonport until the first vertex missing the anchor. Consequently the inequalities at the two anchors strengthen to b+r≤k−1 and a+r≤k−1. Hence d+r≤2k−2 and d≤2k−3. Each D vertex has degree at least k−1 internally, so its missing degree sums are at least 2k−2≥d+1. Adding a marker between any two prescribed endpoints and applying ordinary cycle closure proves Hamilton-connectedness. In particular, distinct original p,q contacts in D furnish a spanning p,D,q segment.

More generally, suppose some two-cut has two components C,D, with |D|≥2, D admitting a spanning p,D,q segment, every C vertex of degree at least k, and C having a nonport. If c=|C|≤2k−1, then G is traceable. No assumption about adjacency of p and q is needed.

Adjoin a marker m to C. Join m to all common C ports and to one arbitrary p-only and q-only port of each type actually present. Every two distinct marker neighbors are assignable to distinct anchors (there is at most one private neighbor of either given type). Replacing m in any spanning path by the spanning p,D,q segment therefore gives a spanning path of G, also when m is an endpoint. Every C vertex has at least k−1 neighbors in the augmented graph: a common port lost two but gained the marker, a private port lost just one, and a nonport keeps every neighbor. If c≤2k−2, then ordinary path closure completes C. The same holds for c=2k−1, by the following argument.

In the terminal closure using only edges inside C, each vertex of current degree at least k is universal inside C. Thus a nonuniversal C vertex has final degree precisely k−1 and no added edge ever touched it. Write S for all such nonuniversal vertices. Every nonport has at least k neighbors, so take any nonport u: it is adjacent in the original graph to every member of S since it is universal after closure. Every missing C peer of a vertex x in S likewise lies in S, hence is at original distance two from x via u. There are at least c−1−(k−1)=k−1 such missing peers. If x is common it also sees two distinct D ports via p,q, exceeding k. (D has distinct p,q contacts from its spanning segment.) If x is private, there is an original opposite-anchor contact y outside S. Indeed every C common port is outside S by the preceding argument; when no common port exists a marked private contact of the opposite type has augmented degree at least k and is universal. The edge xy is original since x never gained an edge. The opposite anchor is therefore at distance two from x, as is a same-anchor D port. These two additional witnesses exceed the budget. No x in S can remain, so C is complete in the closure. This proves traceability after reversing closure and expanding m.

In particular the remaining unbalanced side C has c≥2k, while d≥k+1. Thus any nontraceable graph with this sort of all-high deep two-cut has n=c+d+2≥3k+3. This closes the full such two-cut family through order 3k+2.

## Traceability of every deep two-cut with high component vertices

**Theorem.** Under the original hypotheses of this note, G has a Hamilton path. This includes the nonadjacent two-cut with exactly one balanced side, at every order.

Retain the balanced side D with its spanning p,D,q segment. Assume n≥3k+3 since the previous section handles smaller orders. Let P be the set of all ports of C, R its nonports, W the subset of R at distance two from both anchors, and T the common ports. Divide R minus W into R_p and R_q according to its unique nearest anchor.

The order of C satisfies |C|≤2k−2+|W|−|T|≤3k−4. To see this, select a p-only port v in D. It sees all C p-contacts at distance two and all of R_q at distance four: the p-entry route costs at least 1+3, the q-entry route costs 2+2, and hence the shortest has length four. The vertex q is at distance two from v by balance. Therefore the number of C p-contacts plus |R_q| is at most k−1. The analogous inequality for the q-contacts and R_p holds. Their sum is |C|+|T|−|W|≤2k−2, as claimed, since |W|≤|R|≤k−2.

Form H from C by adjoining a vertex m adjacent to every port of C. This graph is 2-connected. Deleting m leaves the connected graph C. After deletion of any C vertex, original connecting paths in G project to walks in H using m for each excursion outside C; all boundary vertices of C are ports. The anchors and D are connected in G and therefore may be identified for this purpose.

The minimum degree of H is at least k. The positive-even budgets of p and q together count each D port once, each D nonport twice, and each C nonport at least once. Hence d+r+|R|≤2k. It follows that |P|=n−2−d−|R|≥n−2−2k+r≥k+1+r. This is the degree of m. A private C port and a C nonport retain their ambient degrees. A common C port x sees every D port and all its missing C-port peers at distance two. Thus its ambient degree satisfies
[
  d_G(x)ge 2+(|P|-1)-(k-(d-r)) ge n+d-3k-1.
]
Since n≥3k+3 and d≥k+1, this is at least k+3. Losing one net neighbor in H leaves sufficient degree.

The independence number of H is at most k. An independent set containing m has at most 1+|R|≤k−1 members. If I consists of k+1 C vertices, every pair of its members has a common neighbor in C: each has at least k−2 internal neighbors, and two disjoint such neighborhoods together with I would require |C|≥3k−3. Thus every vertex of I already sees all k peers at distance two in G. A port of I would also see a D port sharing one of its anchors at distance two. A nonport would see one of the anchors at distance two. Both contradict the cap. Therefore there is no such independent I.

Finally |H|≤3k−3, δ(H)≥k and α(H)≤k. Nash-Williams' Hamiltonicity theorem for 2-connected graphs with δ(H)≥max{(|H|+2)/3,α(H)} gives a Hamilton cycle in H. Deleting m gives a spanning path of C starting at an original port. To its start attach the spanning p,D,q segment in the orientation matching that port. This is a spanning path of G. The precise Nash-Williams condition is recalled in [the LMS account of his results](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/72EADE1B2998420316021000FAC541A4/S0024609303002315a.pdf/crispin_st_j_a_nashwilliams_19322001.pdf).
