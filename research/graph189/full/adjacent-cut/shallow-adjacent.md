# Adjacent separating pairs with a shallow component

Paper proof by gpt-6.2-astra. Keep the notation in adjacent-separator.md. Here pq is an edge, there is a common port in each side, and all high vertices of D are ports. We prove traceability for k≥6. Combined with that manuscript and the prior result for cuts producing three or more components, this settles arbitrary adjacent two-vertex cuts for k≥6. We invoke the previous three-k order theorem whenever n≤3k and the previous degree-two theorem whenever the minimum degree equals two. We may therefore assume n>3k and minimum degree at least three.

If D contains no high vertex, it is a singleton with degree two, which was excluded. Write d=|D| and b for its port count. A common C port sees all D ports at distance two, so b≤k. Similarly, a=|P_C|≤k. The high vertex in D gives d≥k−1 and at most one D vertex is a nonport, so k−2≤b≤k and d≤k+1.

If b=k−2, necessarily d=k−1, with its unique low vertex a nonport. Every high D vertex is universal in D and sees both anchors; hence D is a clique. A C vertex at distance three or more from the anchor set would put the low D vertex at distance five and lead to the same complete-incidence contradiction proved in adjacent-separator.md (the computation only needs a nonempty far set). Thus all C nonports have distance two to the anchor set. The low D vertex sees both anchors and all these nonports at even distances two and four. Hence C has at most k−2 nonports, and n≤2+(k−1)+k+(k−2)<3k. Thus b≥k−1.

Every common C port is adjacent to all but at most one other C vertex, and is universal in C when b=k. For the first assertion, in the torso induced on C,p,q, its internal distance-two layer has size at most one (both anchors are its neighbors and b vertices outside the torso cost slots). The torso is 2-connected: after one deletion any remaining path in G projects to a walk by replacing excursions through D by the edge pq or deleting return excursions. A layer of size one therefore cannot have any vertices beyond it. For b=k the distance-two layer is empty.

Let t be the number of C nonports. A common C port has at least t−1 of these as neighbors, so each anchor sees at least t−1 nonports at distance two. Thus t≤k+1. If t=k+1, no private port may exist in either component, since it would be an additional distance-two witness for the opposite anchor through pq. All C ports are then common, and the two anchors have equal distances to each C vertex. Some C vertex must have depth at least three since otherwise t>k already exhausts the anchors' caps. A vertex at depth exactly three is an odd tie, which was proved impossible for high vertices, and impossible for a low vertex when the opposite side has no high nonport (adjacent-separator.md). Hence t≤k, and |C|=a+t≤2k. Every p-private port is at distance two from q, and vice versa. In particular the number j of private ports in C satisfies j≤2(k−t+1). For b=k this improves to j≤2(k−t).

**A path through D between the two anchors.** If D contains no low vertex, its nonadjacent internal degree sums are at least 2(k−2)≥d+1, since d≤k+1 and k≥6. Thus D is Hamilton-connected, by cycle closure with a prescribed-endpoint marker. Select distinct usable contacts for p and q; D has at least two distinct ports since b≥k−1.

If D contains the low vertex z, select distinct usable contacts u,v as follows. If z is a nonport it has at least three internal neighbors, so choose any distinct usable high contacts. If z is a private port use z as one contact and choose a port of the other anchor as the other; z has at least two high internal neighbors. If z is common, use it as one contact and choose another high port different from at least one internal neighbor of z. Such a choice exists since z has an internal neighbor and b≥k−1≥5, and z can use either anchor. On D add a marker adjacent only to the two chosen endpoints. All high D vertices can be completed to a clique by ordinary cycle closure, since their original internal degrees are at least k−2 and 2(k−2)≥d+1. For a port z at an endpoint, traverse from z through an internal high neighbor different from the other endpoint, then cover the clique ending at that endpoint. For a nonport z insert it between two of its internal neighbors in a clique path between the chosen endpoints. At least three neighbors ensure we can choose a pair other than the two prescribed endpoints together, and any such pair can occur consecutively. Closing through the marker and reversing closure gives a spanning D path whose ends attach to distinct anchors.

**Replacing the opposite side by a marker.** Adjoin to C a marker m adjacent only to its common ports. Any spanning path of this graph lifts to G by replacing m with p, the spanning path through D, and q in a suitable orientation. Its neighbors, whether one or two, are common ports. Ordinary path closure in the augmented graph has threshold |C|. Every common C port has augmented degree at least |C|−1, so can join every C vertex by closure, since C is connected and has more than one vertex. Common ports are high: their ambient degree is at least |C| (they miss at most one in C and see both anchors) and |C|≥2k−2 follows from n>3k and d≤k+1. Every high nonport retains degree at least k, and every high private port has degree at least k−1. We now construct a spanning path of the closure.

* If |C|≤2k−2, complete all high vertices in C. Start at m and a common port. If there is a low z in C, choose its high internal neighbor different from the starting common port and end through that neighbor at z. This is possible since z cannot be common, and degree at least three gives it at least two internal neighbors even if it is a private port. If there is no low vertex, traverse the high clique.
* If |C|=2k−1, every high nonport can join all high C vertices. As a,t≤k and a+t=2k−1 we have t≥k−1 and j≤4. There are at least k−2≥4 high nonports, and at least two common ports: when t=k−1 one has a=k and a−j≥k−4≥2; when t=k one has a=k−1 and j≤2. Start with m and one common port and reserve a second common port for the end. Interleave the high private ports with distinct high nonports, which now join every high vertex; append all remaining high nonports and unreserved common ports. Finish with the reserved common port, and append the low vertex of C if present. A low private port, if present, is excluded from the interleaved high ports and appears only as this final vertex.
* If |C|=2k, then a=t=k and j≤2. The high nonports form a clique by closure. Make one block of the high nonports, singletons of the j high private ports, and an additional singleton of the low C vertex if present. There are at most four blocks. At least k−2≥4 common ports are universal in C after closure and see m. Starting at m, interleave these blocks with distinct common ports and append any unused common ports. This spans the closure.

Reversing the traceability-preserving closures and expanding m completes the proof.

## The remaining four small degrees

For k≤2, connected graphs of minimum degree one have a cut vertex unless they have only two vertices, and minimum degree two is covered by the earlier degree-two theorem. For k=3,4,5 we still assume minimum degree at least three, both components contain a high vertex, and n>3k. The counting paragraphs up through |C|≤2k apply without using k≥6. The earlier diameter-five obstruction gives minimum degree at least seven whenever the diameter is five; hence these graphs have diameter at most four. Every C vertex is within two of the anchor set: if one had depth at least three, every D vertex would have to be exactly four away and its own second sphere would supply at least two further witnesses. This would leave at most k−2 D vertices, contradicting d≥k−1.

In particular when t=k the two private types cannot both occur in the whole graph. If each type occurred, each anchor would see a private port of the other type at distance two and would have to miss at least one of the k C nonports at distance two. But a fixed common port in C is adjacent to all but at most one C vertex. The missed nonport would therefore have to be the same for both anchors, contradicting the preceding depth-two property.

It now suffices to check two finite families per parameter profile. The accompanying check_small_separators.py runs on Python 3.10 or later and has no dependencies. Its output columns are k, surviving profiles, C instances, and D instances. An instance is counted once per compatible parameter profile, so repeated graphs can contribute more than once.

A profile chooses a,b,t with 1≤a≤k, k−1≤b≤k and 0≤t≤k, and one of zero or one nonports in D. It allocates ports on both sides among common, p-only and q-only, keeping at least one common each. The total order must exceed 3k. The count of either private type over both components is at most k−t+1 (at most k−t when b=k), and the two types cannot both occur at t=k. The low vertex, if present outside the anchors, is chosen once per possible role by symmetry. A D nonport must be that low vertex, and with minimum degree three no low vertex exists at k=3. A C common port has degree at least |C|≥2k−2 and cannot be low. A high D vertex at d=k−1 must be common for its ambient degree to reach k.

For each profile enumerate all D graphs, and all graphs resulting from the specified path-closure steps on C with a marker adjacent to exactly the common ports. In C make every common port universal within C. A pair of high vertices can be joined if the sum of their lower degree bounds k for a nonport and k−1 for a port reaches |C|, the marker graph's path-closure threshold. In D force a common port to be universal if there are k opposite ports. Enumerate every subset of all remaining possible edges independently. A vertex of role r must have at least its original ambient lower bound (three if low, k otherwise) minus its number of anchor neighbors within its component. Discarding a graph that violates this remains valid after closure, which only adds edges. For a vertex v the following vertices give distinct positive even-distance witnesses in the original graph: opposite ports sharing an anchor with v, the other anchor if v is private, and any same-side port sharing an anchor with v but missing an edge to v in the graph being checked. A post-closure nonedge was also an original nonedge. Discard totals above k. These necessary filters need not characterize realizability.

Where both sides admit at least one graph, the script considers every surviving instance. Using exact path search with states (fixed start, last vertex, visited set), it verifies that every D instance has a spanning path with ends assignable to distinct anchors and every C instance with its marker has a spanning path. The same reversal of path closure and expansion of the marker therefore prove traceability. No profile with surviving graphs on both sides failed either test.

Consequently an adjacent two-vertex separator is impossible in a counterexample for every k. The small-degree argument is computer-assisted and depends on the earlier diameter-five and small-order results. The k≥6 proof does not use the finite enumeration.
