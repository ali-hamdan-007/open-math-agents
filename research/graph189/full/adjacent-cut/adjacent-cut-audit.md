# Independent audit of the adjacent two-vertex cut theorem

Under the full positive-even-distance hypothesis with k the second-smallest degree, any graph with an adjacent separating pair is traceable. The conclusion uses the existing reductions for non-2-connected graphs, the order n≤3k, minimum degree two, and a two-cut producing at least three components. The new work addresses exactly two components. The nonadjacent two-component result remains conditional on a common port and a high nonport on each side.

## Two-component reductions and reconstruction

A port touches a separator vertex; a common port touches both. Independently checked the cap/odd-tie bounds for the common-port-free side, including the complete-incidence contradiction for a high odd tie. The high odd-tie exclusion and the exclusion of a low odd tie opposite a side with zero high nonports use adjacency of the anchors but do not need a common-port-free side. This also justifies reusing them to prove t≤k for a shallow component.

If both sides have high nonports and common ports, the maximal C case has k ports, k−2 high nonports U, and the exceptional low nonport z. The opposite D is Hamilton-connected by the degree-sum threshold |D|+1. In the 2k-vertex marker graph, each U has degree at least k and each high port at least k−1, so every claimed addition inside C meets the path-closure threshold 2k−1. The distinct pairs e and f form a linear forest, and all k−1 gaps of an ordering of the k ports are filled by the one original edge e, the single marker at f, and the k−3 remaining U vertices. The initial z,U pair supplies the remaining U. Reversing closure need preserve only traceability. In any path of the original auxiliary graph, the at most two neighbors of its marker are assignable to different anchors because at most one is private. With prescribed distinct D contacts, the Hamilton-connectedness of D supplies a replacement through both anchors. A marker endpoint needs only one assignment and leaves the opposite anchor as path endpoint. The earlier leaf-closure constructions similarly need only unprescribed traceability in the augmented graph, since a leaf forces the relevant endpoint and first edge.

In the shallow case, deleting one torso vertex still leaves the C,p,q torso connected: replace each excursion across D along a surviving G path by pq if it joins distinct anchors, or erase it otherwise. Thus a common C port with at most one internal distance-two witness has at most one missing C neighbor. In C plus its marker its degree is at least |C|−1, and all C vertices have positive degree, so path closure really makes every such port universal inside C. A high nonport has auxiliary degree at least k, a high port at least k−1. For k≥6, the opposite D has order at most k+1, so the high-high cycle-closure sums there meet |D|+1 after adjoining a two-contact marker. When z belongs to D its specified position in the completed high clique path is feasible: if a nonport it has ≥3 internal neighbors so some pair differs from the two prescribed endpoints together; if a port used as an endpoint it has an internal neighbor other than the selected opposite endpoint. The three large-C cases have sufficient common ports and high nonports for their respective clique and block constructions. Closure on C is reversed before expansion of the marker, whose neighbors are all original common ports. No edges incident to the marker are added. These observations check both endpoint and internal replacement.

## Finite check at k=3,4,5

I audited both small.py and the final check_small_separators.py against the parameter reduction. Roles are determined exactly by the subset of the two anchors adjacent to each vertex. All profiles with 1≤a≤k, k−1≤b≤k, t≤k and at most one D nonport occur before necessary pruning. The low vertex can be chosen at a fixed index of each role by simultaneous relabeling; the no-low-in-components case includes both possible low anchors. A D nonport is forced to be low. A common C port cannot be low because its ambient degree is at least |C|≥2k−2. At k=3 there is no low vertex after the minimum-degree-two reduction. The mixed-private-types exclusion at t=k is valid because private ports of both types would oblige both anchors to miss a C nonport. A common C port misses at most one C nonport, so the two anchors must miss the same one, violating the proven depth-two bound.

A high vertex in a D component with d=k−1 sees both anchors. With k opposite ports, a common D port already spends its even budget on them and thus sees every D vertex by an edge. All other compulsory edges on C are permitted path-closure additions. The program enumerates every subset of noncompulsory edges. Its component-degree filter remains necessary after closure. The witness filter uses distinct original vertices: same-side nonneighbors sharing an anchor, opposite-side vertices sharing an anchor, and the other anchor for a private vertex. A nonedge of the closure was a nonedge originally. Filtering only those witnesses is therefore safe. Profiles with no candidates on one side cannot come from a graph. Since both desired path predicates depend only on the one side and its role vector, checking all surviving graphs on each side separately covers the Cartesian product of graph pairs. A C marker only meets common ports, and the endpoint condition on D assigns the endpoints bijectively to the two anchors. Their paths combine as in the large-k proof.

In addition to running the delivered code, check-small-paths.py in this directory replaces its recursive Hamilton path tester with a forward subset dynamic program. The DP computes all reachable endpoints for each vertex subset and fixed starting vertex. It reproduced the same (profiles, C instances, D instances) totals: (8,22,11), (81,535,519), (82,1507,4040).

## Independent short check of the required small-k diameter bound

Only the original diameter bound and its high-endpoint refinement are needed here, together with the elementary first two deductions of the earlier diameter-five analysis. For completeness, if diameter five occurs, let A,B,C,D,F be the layers from its exceptional endpoint, with respective orders a,b,c,d,f. All vertices outside the endpoint have degree at least k and a<k. The cap at the endpoint gives b+d≤k. Degree counting gives a+b≥k and d+f−1≥k. Every A–F pair has distance four by the layer difference and the high-endpoint result, so every A vertex sees F and at least one midpoint C at even distance. Put h=k−f≥1 and j=d+f−1−k≥0. In particular a+f≥k+1.

A–B incidence cannot be complete. Otherwise all C would be within two of all A, so c≤h. A C vertex with more than j neighbors in D would reach all F in two steps (each far vertex misses at most j D vertices), exceeding k with its a A witnesses. Consequently every C vertex would have degree at most b+c−1+j≤b+d−2<k. The missing A–B edge implies a+b≥k+1 and d≤a−1. If h=1, each A vertex is saturated by F and its one C witness. This forces A to be a clique (any missing pair sees a two-edge path through the endpoint), and then each missed B vertex would be at distance two through that clique. This would force complete A–B incidence, already excluded. Hence h≥2, and d≥h+1 implies a≥d+1≥4.

For k≤5, a<k leaves only a=4,k=5,d=3,h=2. Hence j=0, and every far vertex sees all D vertices by edges. At least two C vertices have a neighbor in D, since a sole such vertex would be a cut vertex separating D and F from the endpoint. A far vertex now sees those two at distance two and all four A at distance four, contradicting k=5. This proves diameter at most four in the required small cases without invoking the stronger earlier minimum-degree-seven theorem.
