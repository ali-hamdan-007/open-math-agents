# Traceability of all orders at most three times the second-smallest degree

**Theorem.** Let G satisfy the even-distance condition in Written on the Wall II, Graph Conjecture 189. Write k for the second-smallest degree. Then G has a spanning path whenever n≤3k.

## Reductions and the 1-tough case

Easwar–Johari's Theorem B permits us to assume G is 2-connected and n>2k+3. Hence k≥4. If every vertex has degree at least k, the established n≤δ+2k theorem already applies. Otherwise let z be the unique vertex of degree less than k and put X=V(G) minus z. Every vertex of X has degree at least k in G. If X contains an independent set I of size k+1, then any independent pair in I at distance at least three would have two disjoint neighborhoods of size at least k each, disjoint also from I. This would require n≥3k+1. Thus all pairs in I have distance two, and the saturated-independent-set theorem gives traceability. We may assume α(X)≤k, whence α(G)≤k+1.

Suppose G is 1-tough. Jianping Li's Theorem 8 states that if the minimum sum σ₃(X) of the ambient degrees of three independent vertices in X is at least n, some cycle contains at least min{|X|,|X|+σ₃(X)/3−α(X)} members of X. Here σ₃(X)≥3k≥n and α(X)≤k. Therefore some cycle covers all X. If X has no independent triple, an X-covering cycle follows from the prescribed-set Chvátal–Erdős theorem of Broersma, Li, Li, Tian and Veldman: α(X)≤2≤κ(X), where κ(X) is the minimum size of a separator of two vertices of X. If X is a clique, it itself has a spanning cycle. The only vertex that the covering cycle could omit is z. In that event, open the cycle at a neighbor of z and prepend z. This proves traceability.

## Failing cuts

Suppose G is not 1-tough. Choose a vertex set S with s=|S| and t=c(G−S)≥s+1. By 2-connectivity and independence of component representatives, 2≤s≤k. Every component containing a high vertex (an element of X) has size at least k−s+1. A component of this minimum size consisting entirely of high vertices has all its vertices adjacent to every vertex of S. From such a vertex, every vertex of another component that meets S is at distance two. Moreover every nonsingleton component has at least two distinct vertices adjacent to S, since a unique attachment vertex would separate the rest of that component from G.

**Suppose every component contains a high vertex.** Then t≤α(X)≤k, hence s≤k−1. The order bound is n≥s+(s+1)(k−s+1).

- If 3≤s≤k−2, the right side is at least 4k−5 by concavity. This exceeds 3k for k≥6. Only k=5,s=3 can remain (k=4 has no such s), with n=15 and four components of size three. Choose an all-high component. Its vertex reaches at least two attachment vertices in each of the three other components at distance two, exceeding the cap five.
- If s=2, there are three components, each of size at least k−1, with at most one excess vertex in total. There is an all-high component of size k−1 because at most one component contains z. A vertex in it sees both members of S. Every high vertex in the other two components has at least one S-neighbor, since its component has size at most k. Thus at least 2(k−1)−1>k other vertices are at distance two.
- If s=k−1, there are exactly k components all of size at least two, with at most one excess vertex. There is an all-high pair of vertices, each adjacent to all S. It sees at least two attachment vertices in every other component, so at least 2(k−1)>k vertices at distance two.

**Suppose some component contains no high vertex.** That component is the singleton z; in particular all neighbors of z lie in S. There are at least s high components, all of order at least k−s+1. Thus n≥1+s+s(k−s+1).

- For 4≤s≤k−2 this lower bound is at least 4k−7, exceeding 3k for k≥8. For k=6,s=4 the four required high components have size at least three with at most one excess. For k=7,s=4 or 5 all required high components have their minimum size. In each case an ordinary high component of minimum size reaches all vertices of every other high component at distance two because their sizes are at most k, as well as z. These witnesses exceed k in each case. For k≤5 the interval is empty.
- For s=3, the three required high components each have size at least k−2 and the total excess is at most two. There is at least one minimum-size high component (an additional component, if possible, only consumes the excess), and no high component is larger than k. Any vertex in the minimum-size component sees all S, hence all high vertices in the other components and z at distance two. This gives at least 2(k−2)+1>k.
- For s=k−1, the k−1 required high components each have size at least two, with at most two excess vertices. There is an all-high pair since k≥4. From it there are at least two distance-two witnesses in each of the k−2 other high components, plus z. Again 2(k−2)+1>k.
- For s=k, there are exactly k high components because α(X)≤k. The total number of high vertices outside S is at most 2k−1, so one such component is a singleton x. Then x sees all S. Each of the other k components contributes at least one distance-two witness, and a nonsingleton would contribute two. Consequently every component is a singleton and n=2k+1, contrary to setup.

It remains to handle s=2 with a singleton z. Then z sees both members p,q of S by 2-connectivity. If there are three or more high components, n≥3+3(k−1)=3k. Equality forces three components all of size k−1, each entirely joined to S, yielding more than k distance-two witnesses in the other components. Thus there are precisely two high components A and B, with orders a≤b, a,b≥k−1, and a+b+3≤3k. Each has a neighbor of p and of q, since deleting just p or just q cannot disconnect G.

For a high component C of order c≤2k−3 fix one of p,q, say q. In the induced graph on C and q, each C vertex has degree at least k−1, because only p could be a missing ambient neighbor. Introduce a marker r adjacent only to q. The augmented order is c+2. For any distinct nonadjacent vertices in C their degree sum is at least 2k−2≥c+1, so the ordinary path-closure theorem permits completing C to a clique without changing traceability. The resulting graph has a spanning path starting r,q and entering C through a neighbor of q. Consequently the unclosed graph has a spanning path; the marker is a leaf and forces the orientation starting r,q. This gives a spanning path through C whose initial vertex is adjacent to q in the original graph. The same applies with p.

Only the larger component could have b>2k−3. This forces b=2k−2, a=k−1 and n=3k. Every vertex of A sees both p and q. Counting z and all B vertices touching p or q in the distance-two set of such a vertex shows that at most k−1 vertices of B touch S. Thus at least k−1 B vertices miss both p and q and have degree at least k inside B. In the graph on B,q and the marker r, these vertices can join every B vertex by path closure, whose threshold is now 2k−1: all other B vertices have degree at least k−1. After these additions, if there are at least k such vertices, each remaining B vertex has degree at least k and B becomes a clique. Otherwise there are exactly k−1. Pick a q-neighbor y in B, necessarily outside this group. Its degree is now at least k through q and the k−1 group members, so it can join every other B vertex. All remaining B vertices have degree at least k through y and the group, and the closure again contains a complete B. Thus the marker argument supplies the required spanning B path in this case as well.

Take a spanning A path ending at an original p-neighbor and a spanning B path beginning at an original q-neighbor. Connecting them through p,z,q produces a spanning path in G. This exhausts all failing cuts, including small k.

- R. Easwar and S. Johari, Theorem B for graphs that are not 2-connected or have n≤2k+3. Their Lemma 3.1 is the ordinary path-closure rule used with the marker. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11719
- Jianping Li, “Cycles Containing Many Vertices of Subsets in 1-Tough Graphs with Large Degree Sums,” Ars Combinatoria 48 (1998), 195–212, Theorem 8, pp.196–197. The degrees in σ₃(X) are ambient degrees. Theorem 9 directly states the cyclability condition σ₃(X)≥max{n,3α(X)−2}. https://combinatorialpress.com/article/ars/Volume%20048/volume-48-paper-18.pdf
- H. J. Broersma, H. Li, J. Li, F. Tian and H. J. Veldman, “Cycles through subsets with large degree sums,” Discrete Mathematics 171 (1997), 43–54, doi:10.1016/S0012-365X(96)00071-4. Its subset connectivity criterion covers the case without independent triples. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012365X96000714
