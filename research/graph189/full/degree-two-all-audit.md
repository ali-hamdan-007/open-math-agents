# Audit of Graph 189 at minimum degree two

The all-diameter paper proof is valid after a correction in the reused circumference step, detailed below. The rooted order inequalities have a separate kernel audit. Nothing here assumes that the paper proof of traceability has been formalized.

## Upstream diameter lemma and order convention

The exact upstream lemma is `OMA.R_WrittenOnTheWallII_GraphConjecture189_conjecture189_R1.diameter_le_five`, at line 514 of the local exact R1 source, introduced by [OMA PR #2971](https://github.com/GuillaumeBAUD-coder/open-math-agents/pull/2971). Its hypotheses are connectedness, nontrivial finite vertices, decidable adjacency, and \(\forall x,\ \mathrm{distEven}(G,x)\le 1+\mathrm{secondSmallestDegree}(G)\). If diameter were at least six, an initial seven-vertex geodesic segment \(v_0,\ldots,v_6\) would have distance-five endpoint pairs \(v_0,v_5\) and \(v_1,v_6\). The R1 lemma `five_endpoints` at line 504 says each pair has an endpoint of degree below the second-smallest degree. These pairs are disjoint, contradicting `degree_pair`, which allows only one such vertex. The all-diameters note originally shortened the identifier to `diam_le_five`.

The paper convention excludes the reference vertex and the Lean count includes it. Thus the paper cap \(k\) on *other* even-distance vertices corresponds to the Lean cap \(1+k\). The cases of order \(n\le 2k+2\), order \(n=2k+3\) after assuming 2-connectivity, and non-2-connected graphs are covered by [Easwar–Johari, Theorem B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11719).

## Root eccentricity

- At \(k=2\), a distance-four pair would give a distance-two separating layer of size at least two and a nonempty distance-four layer at one endpoint. Both layers count against the cap, a contradiction. A longer distance also supplies a distance-four pair by taking the first four edges of a geodesic. The checked theorem `Graph189LowDegreeTwo.traceable_min_two_sigma_two` has the exact extra size condition \(n>5\) and diameter at most three. The smaller orders are covered by Theorem B.
- At eccentricity at most three, the rooted Lean theorems `order_le_three_sigma` and `order_le_three_sigma_sub_one` at lines 1132 and 1125 of Graph189DegreeTwoRooted.lean use \(\forall x,\ \mathrm{dist}(z,x)\le3\), rather than a global diameter assumption. Their axiom audit reports only the standard three axioms. The orders at \(k=3,4\) are \(9,11\), respectively.
- At eccentricity four, the visibility count is exact. If both anchors miss a vertex of \(B\), their degree lower bound forces each to have exactly one miss, with \(|B|=k-1\), \(|D|=1\), and the anchor edge present. The misses differ because every member of \(B\) has an anchor neighbor. Every remote in \(C\) has a \(B\)-neighbor, and lying at distance two from only one anchor forces its \(B\)-neighborhood to be exactly the corresponding private vertex.
- For \(k\ge4\), the two anchor-visible sets each have at most \(k-1\) elements. If the union has at least \(2k-3\), the overlap is at most one. Empty overlap forces a common vertex to use all other \(B\)-vertices as neighbors, creating at least \(2k-3\) even-distance remotes plus \(z\). A nonempty overlap consists of a single \(t\), with \(k-2\) exclusive remotes per side. Each common vertex sees at least \(k-3\) of its \(k-2\) possible other \(B\)-neighbors, so sees at least one private vertex, and seeing both exceeds the cap since \(2k-3>k\). If it sees \(p\) and misses \(q\), it sees \(t\) to reach degree \(k\). Its cap is saturated by \(z,q,P\), forcing no \(t\)-to-\(Q\) edges. Every member of \(Q\) then sees \(P\) by its degree bound. But this saturates \(p\)'s cap using \(Q,z\), and the missing anchor. The far vertex \(f\) has at least \(k\) neighbors in \(C\), with only \(k-1\) members outside \(P\), so gives one additional distance-two vertex at \(p\). These strict inequalities hold at \(k=4\) as written and give \(n\le11\).
- For \(k=3\), the only order above nine in this layout is ten, with exactly two exclusive remotes per side and no overlap. Since \(f\) has at least three remote neighbors, it meets both sides. Thus \(z\), the missing anchor, and \(f\) exhaust each private vertex's distance-two allowance. A private-to-private edge would bring a remote from the other side to distance two, and any cross-class remote edge would do the same. Each remote then has exactly three possible neighbors and sees all three. Every consecutive edge of \(b,z,a,p,P_1,P_2,f,Q_1,Q_2,q\) is present, and the entries exhaust all ten vertices.
- At eccentricity five, all distances from an anchor to the fifth layer lie between four and five by the upstream diameter cap. The distance-five endpoint lemma excludes five because both endpoints are high. A shortest four-edge path from anchor to fifth layer must increase the layer at every step, so a midpoint gives a separate distance-two contribution. Consequently \(|F|\le k-1\). Comparing to \(|F|\ge k-|D|+1\) and \(|D|\le2\) forces \(|D|=2\), \(|F|=k-1\), and \(|B|=k-2\). Each anchor must then see the other anchor and every member of \(B\). It sees all of \(C\) at distance two, so \(|C|\le1\), contrary to 2-connectivity because \(C\) separates \(z\) from \(F\).

## Repair to the \(k\ge5,\ n\le3k-1\) traceability argument

Let \(J=G-z\) and \(N=|V(J)|\). The reduction to \(\alpha(J)\le k\) by the saturated independent-set theorem checks. If \(J\) is 2-connected, the proof that it is 1-tough checks, including the tight component count for a cut of size \(k-1\). An ordinary two-vertex component there necessarily sees all of the cut, giving the two extra distance-two witnesses relative to the single low anchor.

The degree argument gives \(\sigma_3(J)\ge3k-2\), \(\delta(J)\ge k-1\), and \(N\le3k-2\). [Bauer–Veldman–Morgana–Schmeichel, Theorem 9](https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/files/6736746/Bauer90long.pdf) requires its lower-bound parameter \(s\) to satisfy \(s\le N\). Directly setting \(s=3k-2\) is only licensed at equality. Split as follows.

- If \(N\le3k-3\), apply Theorem 13 of the same paper: a 1-tough graph with \(\delta\ge\max(N/3,\alpha-1)\) is Hamiltonian. Here both bounds follow from \(\delta(J)\ge k-1\) and \(\alpha(J)\le k\).
- If \(N=3k-2\), apply Theorem 9 with \(s=N\) when an independent triple exists. Its lower bound \(\min\{N,N+N/3-\alpha(J)\}\) is greater than \(N-1\). If no independent triple exists, use Chvátal–Erdős with \(\kappa(J)\ge2\ge\alpha(J)\).

If \(J\) has a cut vertex, its two components have sizes between \(k\) and \(2k-3\). In each marker augmentation all original incident edges at component vertices persist because their only possible external neighbors in \(G\) are \(z\) and the cut vertex. The added marker raises the order to at most \(2k\), so Bondy–Chvátal closure makes each component a clique. Distinct attachments to the two boundary vertices exist by 2-connectivity of \(G\). Deleting the degree-two marker from the original augmented Hamilton cycle gives the required spanning path through the component. The two resulting paths combine into a spanning cycle of \(G\).

With the circumference split, every order and eccentricity is covered.
