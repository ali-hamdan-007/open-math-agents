# The smaller of the two exterior sizes forces a clique with four common neighbors

Assume the bounded-order degree-three residual, k≥15 and n≤3k+2, and let H be the Hamilton-connected exterior of a longest path Q through the low vertex z. Suppose M=|H|=k−3. Every vertex of H has at least k−(M−1)=4 neighbors on Q, so there are at least 4M attachment incidences. If some attachment position has only one neighbor in H, the single-contact packing bound of [the attachment proof](gap-packing.md) gives at most n−3≤3k−1 incidences. This is strictly less than 4k−12 when k≥15. Therefore every attachment position has at least two H-neighbors.

Equation (5) of the same proof now bounds the number of attachment positions by 2+floor((n−M−4)/(M+1))≤2+floor((2k+1)/(k−2))=4. Thus there are exactly four distinct outside attachment vertices. Every H vertex sees all four. To reach degree k it must also see all M−1=k−4 other H vertices, so H is a clique of vertices with identical closed neighborhoods in G. This is a necessary structural condition, and does not yet span the graph. The next question for M=k−3 is therefore the precise four-attachment gluing problem; it no longer requires handling arbitrary Hamilton-connected components.
