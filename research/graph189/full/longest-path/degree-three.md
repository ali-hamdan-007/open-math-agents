# The omitted component in the minimum-degree-three order residual

The companion [independent audit](degree-three-audit.md) also resolves the last order-six boundary here. Together the two arguments establish Hamilton-connectedness of the omitted component in every case.

Suppose that \(G\) is 2-connected, has minimum degree \(\delta=3\) and second-smallest degree \(k\), has order \(n\leq3k+2\), and every vertex has at most \(k\) vertices at distance exactly two. Assume \(G\) is nontraceable. The near-spanning theorem of `attachments.md` implies that \(k\geq6\), since the only possible omitted vertex is the unique low vertex \(z\) and \(2d(z)\leq k\). In particular every longest path omits just \(z\). Let \(Q\) be longest among paths through \(z\), write \(M=n-|V(Q)|\), and set \(h=\lfloor k/2\rfloor\). The internal degree theorem of `gap-packing.md` gives every omitted component minimum degree at least \(h\).

**Conclusions.** There is just one omitted component \(H\) of \(Q\). It is 2-connected and Hamiltonian. Its order satisfies \(\lfloor k/2\rfloor+1\leq M\leq k-1\), with the better lower bound \(k-3\) when \(k\geq15\). It is Hamilton-connected except possibly when \(k\) is odd and \(M=k-1\). In that possible case, every vertex of internal degree exactly \(h\) and every one of its internal neighbors is adjacent to both path neighbors of \(z\) on \(Q\).

## Rotate from a shortest tail

Choose a longest unrooted path \(P=(v_1,\ldots,v_{n-1})\) whose shorter endpoint tail has minimum length over all longest paths. The tails are the initial and final segments outside the interval between the first and last neighbors of \(z\). Orient \(P\) so its left tail is the shorter. Write \(i_1<i_2<i_3\) for the indices of the three neighbors of \(z\), and put \(l=i_1-1\) and \(r=n-1-i_3\) for the two positive tail lengths. Each inter-attachment gap is at least \(M+1\), since replacing the intervening vertices by \(z\) would otherwise produce a path through \(z\) omitting fewer than \(M\) vertices.

If \(l>M\), the tail and gap lengths yield \(n-2\geq 2l+2(M+1)\geq4M+4\), so \(M<k\). Suppose then that \(l\leq M\). Any edge \(v_1v_t\) gives the rotated longest path \((v_{t-1},\ldots,v_1,v_t,\ldots,v_{n-1})\). For a pivot \(t\) with \(i_j<t\leq i_{j+1}\), the first attachment on the rotated path is the vertex originally at position \(i_j\), now at position \(t-i_j\). Its tail length is \(t-i_j-1\). Thus the first \(l\) pivots in this interval are forbidden by minimality of the chosen tail. The final \(M-l\) pivots are also forbidden: the original first attachment moves to position \(t-i_1\) and becomes consecutive among attachments with the original attachment at \(i_{j+1}\). Their gap \(i_{j+1}+i_1-t\) would be at most \(M\). The two ranges of forbidden pivots are disjoint within each gap because each gap has length at least \(M+1\).

In addition, the first \(l\) positions after the final attachment \(i_3\) are forbidden as pivots by the same minimal-tail argument, and exist because \(r\geq l\). In all, at least \(2M+l\) of the \(n-2\) positions besides \(v_1\) are forbidden as neighbors of \(v_1\). Since \(v_1\) is high and has no neighbor outside \(P\), we have \(k+2M+l\leq n-2\leq3k\). As \(l\geq1\), this gives \(M\leq k-1\).

## Connectivity and Hamiltonicity of the omitted component

Each omitted component has order at least \(h+1\) by its internal degree. Two components would exceed \(k-1\), so there is a unique component \(H\). If \(w\) were a cut vertex of \(H\), each component of \(H-w\) would have at least \(h\) vertices, since every original \(H\)-degree is at least \(h\) and at most one neighbor can lie outside such a component. This would imply \(M\geq2h+1\geq k\), a contradiction. Its order is at least three, so \(H\) is 2-connected. Also \(h\geq M/2\), so Dirac's theorem gives Hamiltonicity.

When \(k\) is even or \(M\leq k-2\), one has \(2h\geq M+1\). The two-endpoint marker and cycle-closure argument of `gap-packing.md` gives Hamilton-connectedness. In particular, if \(k\geq15\) and \(M\leq k-2\), the two-side incidence count gives \(M(k-M)\leq n-4\leq3k-2\). The product decreases for \(M>k/2\), and at \(M=k-4\) it exceeds \(3k-2\), so \(M\geq k-3\). Larger values of \(M\) already satisfy the same lower bound.

Finally let \(k=2h+1\), \(M=k-1\), and suppose an omitted vertex \(x\) has internal degree exactly \(h\). If no such vertex exists, \(H\) is Hamilton-connected by the same marker argument. For a neighbor \(y\) of \(x\) in \(H\), use the attachment notation from `gap-packing.md`. We have \(a_x\geq h+1>k/2\), and the proof there gives \(q_x=h\geq a_x-1\), so \(a_x=h+1\). In its counting chain
\[
 k\geq a_x+r_x+a_y-|A_x\cap A_y|+q_y-q_x
 \geq a_x+a_y-1+q_y-q_x\geq a_x+k-1-q_x=k
\]
every inequality is an equality. In particular \(a_y+q_y=k\) and \(|A_x\cap A_y|=r_x+1\). The latter can occur only if both positions immediately around \(z\) on \(Q\) are in the intersection: each singleton run contributes at most one, each other nonsingleton run contributes zero, and only the run across \(z\) can contribute two. Thus \(x\) and all its \(H\)-neighbors attach to both path neighbors of \(z\), and those \(H\)-neighbors have degree exactly \(k\) in \(G\).

## All three neighbors of the low vertex lie on the rooted path

The coordinator observed that every neighbor of \(z\) already lies on \(Q\). If \(z\) is an endpoint, an omitted neighbor could be prepended. Otherwise the two path neighbors of \(z\) are distinct and the only possible omitted neighbor \(c\) is the third neighbor. Let \(u\) be either of the path neighbors of \(z\). If \(u\) had a neighbor in \(H\), a path inside the connected \(H\) from \(c\) to that neighbor, possibly a one-vertex path, could replace the edge \(zu\) on \(Q\) and lengthen it. Thus \(u\) has no neighbor in \(H\). In particular it misses \(c\) and each of the at least \(h\) neighbors of \(c\) in \(H\). These neighbors of \(c\) all lie at distance two from \(z\), since \(c\) was the sole possible omitted neighbor of \(z\). On the other hand every neighbor of \(u\) belongs to \(\{z\}\), the other two neighbors of \(z\), or \(D_2(z)\). The missed vertices give \(d(u)\leq2+k-h<k\), a contradiction.

Let \(T=D_2(z)\cap V(H)\). For each \(u\in N(z)\), its disjoint contributions to degree come from at most three vertices of \(\{z\}\cup(N(z)\setminus\{u\})\), at most \(k-|T|\) vertices of \(D_2(z)\setminus V(H)\), and its own neighbors in \(T\). Thus \(|T\setminus N_H(u)|\leq3\). In particular, when \(|T|\geq5\), all three neighbors of \(z\) have at least two neighbors in \(H\). If \(H\) is Hamilton-connected, any two of them on the same side of \(z\) on \(Q\) must be separated by at least \(M+1\) positions, using the same spanning splice as in the incidence bound.

These are necessary conditions on the degree-three residual. They leave open the step of absorbing the omitted component into a single path through \(z\).

## Sharper structure when \(M=k-1\)

Suppose \(M=k-1\) and \(H\) is Hamilton-connected. If \(|T|\geq5\), the three vertices of \(N(z)\) have at least two neighbors in \(H\). Consider any fourth attachment position of \(Q\), if one exists, besides these three. Among these four positions, any consecutive pair on either side of \(z\) has at least one of the three original contacts, so the pair admits a spanning splice through \(H\) and must be at least \(M+1\) positions apart. The four positions require at least \(2(M+1)+2=2k+2\) usable internal positions even when they are divided between the two sides. But \(Q\) has at most \(n-M-2\leq2k+1\) internal positions. Therefore the only \(H\)-attachments are the three vertices of \(N(z)\). Every vertex of \(H\) has at least two neighbors outside \(H\) by its ambient degree, so \(T=V(H)\). It follows that all edges from \(\{z\}\cup N(z)\cup V(H)\) to its complement pass through \(D_2(z)\setminus V(H)\), of size at most one. The complement is nonempty: the two unrooted attachment gaps of size at least \(M+1=k\) and the two positive tails yield \(n\geq2k+4\). This violates 2-connectivity. Consequently \(|T|\leq4\) in the Hamilton-connected case.

Suppose next that \(k=2h+1\geq9\), \(M=2h\), and \(H\) is not Hamilton-connected. Let \(S\) be the set of internal degree-\(h\) vertices. If \(|S|\leq h-1\), the endpoint-marker closure proves Hamilton-connectedness after all: each of the at least \(h+1\) higher-degree vertices has degree at least \(h+1\), so can be made adjacent to every original vertex by closure at augmented order \(2h+1\). Every degree-\(h\) vertex originally missed at least one of those higher-degree vertices, and now gains enough degree for closure to complete the original graph. Thus \(|S|\geq h\). Every vertex outside \(S\) has a neighbor in \(S\), since it has degree at least \(h+1\) and has at most \(h-1\) potential neighbors outside \(S\). The equality case of the internal-degree argument then gives \(T=V(H)\), since all of \(S\) and its internal neighbors attach to the two immediate neighbors of \(z\) on \(Q\).

Each of the three neighbors of \(z\) now has at least \(2h-3\) contacts in \(H\). For any specified \(x\in V(H)\), there are spanning paths from \(x\) to at least \(h\) distinct other vertices of \(H\). Open a Hamilton cycle at \(x\) to get a first spanning path. Holding \(x\) fixed, rotate at the opposite endpoint through each of its at least \(h\) internal neighbors. These pivots give distinct endpoints. Hence for any prescribed \(x\), at least one spanning-path endpoint is a contact of each neighbor of \(z\), since \(h+(2h-4)>2h-1\) for \(h\geq4\). It follows that any fourth attachment position can splice a spanning \(H\)-path to any of the three original contacts, by fixing an arbitrary \(H\)-neighbor at the fourth position. The three original contacts can likewise be paired among themselves. The same gap packing excludes a fourth attachment, and the same one-vertex separator contradicts 2-connectivity. Thus \(H\) is Hamilton-connected when \(k\geq9\) and \(M=k-1\), even at odd \(k\). This leaves \(k=7\) as the only possibly non-Hamilton-connected boundary.

In the Hamilton-connected case, group attachment positions on either side of \(z\) into individual positions with multiple contacts and maximal consecutive runs (in the ordered list of attachment positions) of positions with the same sole neighbor in \(H\). Consecutive groups admit a spanning splice and so are separated by gaps of at least \(M+1\). Thus four groups would again require at least \(2(M+1)+2\) internal positions, which are unavailable. There are at most three groups. At least two groups must be positions with multiple contacts: with at most one such group, each of the \(M\geq5\) vertices would need a group consisting of positions whose sole neighbor is that vertex. If there are three multi-contact groups, those three positions are all attachments, and every vertex of \(H\) has at least \(M-2\) neighbors in \(H\), giving a clique with at most a matching removed. If there are exactly two multi-contact groups, at most one vertex of \(H\) can have any other attachment. All other vertices attach to the two common positions and must be universal in \(H\) by their high degree. This makes \(H\) a clique. If there is a singleton-neighbor group, deleting that sole neighbor along with the two common positions separates the other vertices of the clique from the rest of the graph.
