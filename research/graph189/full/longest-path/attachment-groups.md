# Outer tails of a longest path through a degree-three vertex

Let G be finite, simple and 2-connected, of order n≤3k+2, minimum degree three and second-smallest degree k. Suppose each vertex has at most k vertices at distance exactly two. Then G has a Hamiltonian path.

We use the previously established degree-three exterior reduction (see `degree-three.md`, `degree-three-audit.md`, `gap-packing.md`, and `attachments.md`). Its precise input to the proof is this: in a nontraceable G as above, k≥6, there is a unique degree-three vertex z, and for a longest path Q containing z every neighbor of z lies on Q. The induced graph H outside Q is Hamilton-connected, of order M satisfying floor(k/2)+1≤M≤k−1. The six-vertex odd boundary k=7, M=6 uses the last Hamilton-connectedness argument of `degree-three-audit.md`. All vertices other than z have degree at least k in G.

## Outer tails and attachment groups

Order Q from left to right. Its attachment positions are its vertices with a neighbor in H. They exclude z. On each side of z, list the attachment positions in path order. A position with at least two neighbors in H is a group of its own. Partition the remaining positions into maximal runs of consecutive entries in that list with the same sole neighbor in H, breaking the runs also at every multiple-neighbor entry. Each such run is one group. Let g be the total number of groups on the two sides.

If a side contains s>0 groups, consider the tail strictly beyond its outermost attachment, towards its endpoint of Q. The tail has at least M vertices. Otherwise replace it with a spanning path of H whose last vertex sees the outermost attachment, keeping the attachment and all the rest of Q. This increases the order and retains z. Between the last attachment of one group and the first of the next are at least M interior vertices. Indeed the two attachment positions have distinct representatives among their H-neighbors by the definition of a group. A spanning path of H joining the representatives replaces the intervening vertices while retaining z. Each side therefore supplies one tail and s−1 intervening gaps, all disjoint and each with at least M vertices, in addition to at least s attachment vertices. Including z gives

    |Q|≥1+g(M+1),    g≤floor((3k+1−M)/(M+1)).                    (1)

The argument also applies if z is an endpoint or if one side has no attachments. Since k≤2M−1 and M≥4, (1) gives g≤floor((5M−2)/(M+1))<M. For M=4 the quotient is 18/5, and for M≥5 it is less than 5. Thus there is a vertex x of H which is the label of no singleton group. Its Q-neighbors are exclusively multiple-neighbor positions. It needs at least k−M+1 of them by its degree. In particular at least k−M+1 groups are multiple-neighbor positions. From (1),

    n≥(M+1)(k−M+2).                                               (2)

If M≤k−2, the product in (2) is minimized on the allowed interval at M=k−2. Indeed its value at M+1 minus its value at M is k−2M≤0. Consequently n≥4(k−1)>3k+2 for k≥7. For k=6, this case forces M=4, n=20 and equality in (1) with g=3.

If M=k−1, then (1) gives g≤floor((2k+2)/k)=2. At least two groups must be multiple-neighbor positions, so these are the only attachments, say p,q. Every vertex of H must see both and all the other M−1 clique vertices to have degree at least k. Thus H is a clique complete to p,q.

## The pure two-port clique for k≥6

Neither p nor q sees z. Indeed if either did, all k−1 vertices of H would be in the second distance layer of z. At least one of the three neighbors of z lies outside {p,q}, hence misses the whole clique. Its degree is at most 3+k−(k−1)=4: its neighbors are z, at most two other neighbors of z, and vertices of the second layer. This contradicts k≥6.

Write C=G−(H∪{p,q}), of order c≤2k+1. Call a vertex of C a contact when it sees p or q. All contacts are high. Any such vertex has all k−1 clique vertices at distance two in G, leaving room for at most one other distance-two vertex. At least one component exists, as z belongs to C.

We use the ordinary cycle-closure theorem at threshold N in a graph of order N. Its path version at threshold N−1 follows by adjoining a universal vertex and applying cycle closure. Adding a degree-two marker attached to two specified vertices in an m-vertex graph gives Hamilton-connectedness if its minimum degree d satisfies 2d≥m+1, by completing the original vertices via cycle closure at the augmented order. In another application, adding a leaf at a specified start r in an m-vertex graph allows path closure at threshold m. Any spanning path of the resulting original augmented graph then has the leaf as an endpoint.

**Disconnected C.** Each component has a high vertex, as a component consisting of z would give z degree zero. Each has order at least k−1 because a high vertex loses at most two neighbors. Since 3(k−1)>2k+1 for k≥6, there are exactly two components, each of order at most k+2. Each sees both ports by 2-connectivity of G. Choose a contact of p in one component and of q in the other. In either component L, add a leaf at the chosen high contact r. Every high has internal degree at least k−2, so path closure at threshold |L|≤k+2 completes all high vertices, since 2(k−2)≥k+2. In an all-high component the closed graph has a spanning path beginning with the leaf and r. In the component containing z, the same path traverses the high clique ending at a neighbor of z other than r and appends z; all three neighbors of z are internal. Reversing closure and deleting the leaf yields a spanning path beginning at the original r. Join the two paths through p, the clique H and q.

**Connected C with a cut vertex w.** In every component L of C−w, the vertices through which L sees {p,q,w} include at least two distinct vertices. Indeed a singleton high would have degree at most three; a singleton z could see only w. For any part of order at least two, deleting a sole such boundary vertex would separate its remaining vertices from H, contradicting 2-connectivity of G.

No vertex x of L sees all three of p,q,w. Otherwise the two distinct boundary vertices of any other part are at distance two from x through that set; the k−1 vertices of H are also at distance two from x. This exceeds k. Thus each high vertex of L has internal degree at least k−2. Each part contains a high vertex and has order at least k−1. As |C−w|≤2k and 3(k−1)>2k, there are exactly two parts, each of order at most k+1.

At least one part A is all high. It contains a contact of w since C is connected and a contact of {p,q} since G−w is connected. These two contacts can be chosen distinct, by the boundary observation. As 2(k−2)≥k+2≥|A|+1, A has a spanning path between them. Enter A from a port and exit to w. In the other part L choose a neighbor r of w, taking r=z if possible. A spanning path of L from r follows by the leaf closure already used, since |L|≤k+1≤2(k−2). If r=z, traverse the leaf, z, an internal neighbor of z, and the remaining high clique. If z lies in L and misses w, all three of its neighbors lie in L, and the previous construction appends z. If L is all high, the construction just uses its clique. Prefix the traversal of A,w,L by a path starting at the unused port, traversing H and ending at the selected port. This spans G.

**2-connected C.** For any contact x, its second distance layer within C has at most one vertex, as every such vertex is a distance-two vertex of G distinct from H. A vertex in a third layer would make the sole second-layer vertex a cut vertex. Consequently each contact has degree at least c−2 in C. There are distinct contacts u,v of p,q respectively: both ports see C and no sole common contact can separate the other vertices of C from H. Add a marker r adjacent exactly to u,v. Apply path closure only to pairs inside C at the augmented threshold c. Every contact becomes universal in C since every vertex initially has degree at least two there. Every high noncontact has degree at least k in C. For c≤2k closure therefore completes the high vertices to a clique.

Suppose c=2k+1. Continue the same closure to saturation. A high vertex of final degree exactly k must be a noncontact and have gained no edge; thus its original neighbors include every contact. If a high noncontact x of final degree k exists, it misses at least k−1 high peers in the saturated graph, counting the 2k−1 high peers and its at most k neighbors. Each missed high peer also has degree exactly k by saturation and likewise originally saw u. These peers all have distance two from x through u; both p and q also have distance two through u,v. This contradicts the cap. Hence all high vertices now have degree at least k+1 and closure completes the high clique.

In the closure take the path r,u, then the remaining high vertices ending at v, then z. The edge vz exists because every contact became universal. Reverse closure to get an original spanning path in the augmented graph. If r is internal, replace the segment u,r,v by u,p,H,q,v in the appropriate direction, traversing all of H. If r is an endpoint whose path neighbor is u, replace r by q,H,p; for neighbor v reverse the roles of p,q. This proves traceability.

## The equality at k=6, M=4

Equation (1) is tight with three groups, each a multiple-neighbor position. Every H vertex needs at least three external neighbors, so H is a four-clique joined to all three positions U and has no other neighbors outside H. Equality in (1) leaves no spare vertex on the occupied sides of Q. The innermost attachment on each occupied side is therefore adjacent to z along Q. Thus all four vertices of H have distance two from z. A neighbor of z missing H would have degree at most 3+6−4=5, a contradiction. It follows that N(z)=U.

Now C=G−(H∪U∪{z}) has 12 vertices. At most two vertices X of C see U since they too have distance two from z. As before each component of C has at least two distinct contacts, since a singleton would have degree at most three and a single contact vertex in a larger component would separate it from H. Hence C is connected. If w cut C, every part of C−w would contain a noncontact: a part consisting solely of contacts has size at most two and any of them would have degree at most 1+1+3=5. A noncontact has all neighbors within its part and {w}, so that part has at least six vertices. Two parts would exceed |C−w|=11. Thus C is 2-connected. For a contact x, the four clique vertices and z all have distance two via a port, leaving at most one distance-two vertex in C. This gives degree at least ten in C by the same second-layer argument. Every noncontact has all its at least six neighbors in C. By Dirac's theorem C has a Hamilton cycle. Open it at a contact x and label a neighboring port u₃, with other ports u₁,u₂. Prefix the opened cycle with

    z, u₁, h₁, u₂, h₂, h₃, h₄, u₃, x,

where h₁,h₂,h₃,h₄ enumerate H and x is written only once. This spans G.

Thus k=6 and k=7 also close; the only boundary case of (2) required its own three-port argument. The result is for the bounded-order degree-three branch. It does not settle the full Graph Conjecture 189.
