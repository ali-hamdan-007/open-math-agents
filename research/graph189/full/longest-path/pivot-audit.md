# Independent audit of the four-attachment endpoint-rotation sieve

Suppose a nontraceable graph has a path P on all vertices except the unique low vertex z of degree four. Write L=n−1 for its length in vertices and m for the minimum number of omitted vertices over all paths through z. Every attachment is internal. For each of the three gaps between successive attachments, splicing via z omits the interior of that gap. Therefore each gap has at least m internal vertices and 3(m+1)≤n−4. Equivalently m≤⌊(n−4)/3⌋−1, exactly the exclusive Python upper bound used in rotation_core.py. The lower bound m≥⌊k/2⌋+1 and the packing bound m(k−m)≤n−4 were separately audited.

An endpoint v₁ has all its at least k neighbors on the path and cannot be adjacent to v_L, since a cycle on G−z can be opened at any neighbor of z and extended to z. Each of its neighbors v_j, 2≤j<L, gives the path v_(j−1),…,v₁,v_j,…,v_L. This maps an original position t<j to j−t and fixes every t≥j. The j=2 case is the existing path, correctly counted as one feasible pivot. Distinct pivots may yield the same binary word and must be counted with multiplicity. Reversing orientation gives the other endpoint test. Both tests are necessary even for a reflection-symmetric state.

Fix the underlying graph and m. The set of attachment words realized by paths on exactly G−z is contained in the initial gap-admissible family. If it is contained in the family at round i, every genuinely realizable word for either orientation has at least k pivots back into the genuinely realizable family, hence into round i. All genuinely realizable words therefore survive round i+1. This induction justifies iteration to the greatest fixed point. Quotienting by path reversal preserves both endpoint tests. The two tails and three internal gap excesses are nonnegative after subtracting the minimal tails (one each) and internal gaps (m each); their sum is L−4−2−3m. The script enumerates all such compositions.

For k≥15, m must be at least k−3: m>k/2 and 4(k−4)>3k−2 force this by monotonicity of m(k−m). Orient P so the shorter unmarked tail has length T and the other length R≥T. If E is the total excess of the internal gap lengths over m, then T+R+E=n−5−3m≤6. There are at most T pivots no later than the first attachment. In the region immediately after attachment a_i up to and including a_(i+1), a pivot j must satisfy j≥a_i+2 to keep the new first mark internal and j≤a_(i+1)+a₁−(m+1) to maintain the transformed middle gap. This gives at most T+e_i pivots, where e_i=a_(i+1)−a_i−(m+1). After the last mark there are at most max(R−2,0) pivots. Thus the total is at most 4T+E+max(R−2,0)≤13: T≤3, with T=3 forcing R=3,E=0; when T≤2 and R≥2 the bound is ≤3T+4, and R=1 forces T=1. This contradicts k≥15.

For 8≤k≤14 and every order 1≤n≤3k+2, audit-rotation.py independently enumerates ordered binary words on the path and physically reverses allowable prefixes. It stores both orientations during iteration. The surviving triples (k,n,m) and the counts of oriented / reflection-identified words are (9,28,5):210/110, (9,29,5):330/170, (10,32,6):330/170, (11,34,6):715/365, (11,35,6):1001/511. Each listed triple is a necessary surviving possibility. The complete all-orders scan shows no other triples for n≤3k. The resulting 2-connected theorem requires only the distance-two cap throughout, independently of the published 2k+3 bound and the prior fork 3k bound.

## Final exclusion of the five triples

For each triple C is a clique by its internal minimum degree. On a side containing S>0 singleton attachment positions and M multiple positions, every consecutive gap has at least one empty position, and at least M gaps have at least m empty positions. This gives L≥2S+(m+1)M−1, so its incidence total is at most L−S−M+1. The other side gives at most L'+m−S'−M', using the same bound if S'>0 and the original multiple-only bound if S'=0. Hence if any singleton exists the total ≤n−1−b, where b is the total number of attachment positions. Every vertex of C needs k−m+1 such positions. Substituting b≥k−m+1, the incidence bound is inadequate in every triple. Formula (5) for multiple-only positions caps b at five. This excludes k=11; the other cases give five positions A shared by every member of C.

Since d(z)=4<m, z lies outside A. To show five positions cannot be on a single side, including if z is initially a path endpoint, use the bound n−m−2 on the number of internal positions. This is strictly below the required span 4(m+1)+1. Therefore z is internal and both sides contain positions of A. Excluding z now leaves n−m−3 internal positions. The minimum required count over both sides is 3(m+1)+2, with at most one position to spare. This forces one of the positions immediately next to z to belong to A. All of C is therefore at distance two from z; at most k−m=4 other vertices are at distance two. A neighbor u of z outside A would miss C by definition and have at most 1+3+4 possible neighbors, fewer than k. Hence U=N(z) consists of four of the five contacts and A∖U is a singleton {a}.

On a path P spanning G−z at least four of the eight shifted witnesses from z lie in C. A consecutive run in C bounded by two U vertices would be the entire open gap between successive z attachments. Its size would be at least m. As C has exactly m elements this would be its only run, supplying at most two shifted witnesses in C. Thus every C-run contributes at most one and there are at least four runs. An internal C-run is bounded by two A positions and hence abuts a. There are at most two such internal runs since a occurs once. At least four runs thus require both P endpoints in C. These endpoints are adjacent in its clique, making a cycle on G−z. Opening that cycle at any z-neighbor and adjoining z produces a spanning path, the contradiction.
