# A clique of order \(k-1\) with exactly two ports in the minimum-degree-three residual

Let \(G\) be a 2-connected graph on \(n\leq3k+2\) vertices with minimum degree three, second-smallest degree \(k\geq10\), and at most \(k\) vertices at distance exactly two from every vertex. In the nontraceable residual of `degree-three.md`, suppose the omitted component \(H\) has order \(M=k-1\) and exactly two neighbors \(p,q\) in the rest of the graph. Then \(G\) is traceable, a contradiction. The proof uses the omitted-component conclusions of `degree-three.md`, ordinary path closure, and the marker proof of Hamilton-connectedness in `gap-packing.md`. The closure argument when the residual component is 2-connected is due to the parallel separator analysis.

Every vertex of \(H\) is high and has degree at most \((k-2)+2=k\). Therefore \(H\) is a clique and all its vertices see both \(p,q\). The unique low vertex \(z\) is outside \(H\cup\{p,q\}\), since \(p,q\) have at least \(k-1>3\) neighbors. Moreover \(z\) has no edge to either \(p\) or \(q\), since it otherwise sees all \(k-1\) vertices of \(H\) at distance two, contradicting \(|V(H)\cap D_2(z)|\leq4\) from the Hamilton-connected \(M=k-1\) case in `degree-three.md`.

## If deleting the two ports leaves two other components

Let \(C_1,\ldots,C_t\) be the components of \(G-\{p,q\}\) other than \(H\). Each contains a high vertex: a singleton low \(z\) would have no neighbors. Each consequently has order at least \(k-1\) by degree, and their total order is at most \(2k+1\). Thus \(t\leq2\). If \(t=2\), each has order at most \(k+2\). Every component has a neighbor of both \(p\) and \(q\) by 2-connectivity of \(G\).

An all-high component has internal minimum degree at least \(k-2\) and hence is Hamilton-connected by the usual two-endpoint marker and cycle-closure argument. In the component containing \(z\), deleting \(z\) leaves minimum degree at least \(k-3\) on at most \(k+1\) vertices, again Hamilton-connected. From any prescribed port contact in that component, a spanning path ends at \(z\): take a spanning path of its high subgraph from the prescribed contact to a distinct neighbor of \(z\), and append \(z\). Such a distinct choice exists because \(z\) has three neighbors in the component. The two spanning paths can therefore be joined through \(p\), a spanning path of \(H\), and \(q\), using the required endpoint contacts. This spans \(G\).

## If there is one other component with a cut vertex

Write \(C\) for the single component other than \(H\). Suppose \(w\) is a cut vertex in \(C\). Every component of \(C-w\) has a high vertex, because a singleton low vertex \(z\neq w\) would have no neighbors besides \(w\). It has order at least \(k-2\) because a high vertex can have at most three neighbors in \(\{p,q,w\}\). As \(|C|-1\leq2k\) and \(k\geq10\), there are exactly two components, each of order at most \(k+2\). At least one is all high: both are if \(z=w\).

In an all-high component, minimum internal degree at least \(k-3\) gives Hamilton-connectedness. This component has a contact of \(w\) because \(C\) is connected, and a contact of \(p\) or \(q\) because \(w\) is not a cut vertex of \(G\). They can be chosen at distinct vertices. Otherwise the only vertex making either contact would itself be a cut vertex of \(G\), since this component has order at least two. Choose a spanning path between the two contacts. If the other component is all high, choose a spanning path from any contact of \(w\). If it contains \(z\), its high subgraph has order at most \(k+1\) and minimum degree at least \(k-4\), so is Hamilton-connected. When \(w\) is adjacent to \(z\), start that component at \(z\), then any of its high neighbors, and traverse the high subgraph. Otherwise start at a high contact of \(w\), end the high subgraph at a distinct neighbor of \(z\), and append \(z\).

Start the global path at the port opposite the chosen contact in the first component, traverse \(H\), then the other port. Continue through the first component to its contact of \(w\), pass through \(w\), and take the spanning path of the second component just chosen. All used cross edges are original, and all vertices are covered.

## If the one other component is 2-connected

For each vertex \(x\) of \(C\) touching \(\{p,q\}\), all the \(k-1\) vertices of \(H\) lie at distance two from \(x\). Thus \(C\) itself has at most one vertex at distance two from \(x\). Since \(C\) is 2-connected, it cannot have a vertex at distance at least three from \(x\), as then the unique distance-two vertex would be a cut vertex in \(C\). Hence every contact has degree at least \(|C|-2\) within \(C\).

Choose distinct contacts \(u,v\in V(C)\) of \(p,q\) respectively. Such contacts exist by 2-connectivity of \(G\): each port sees \(C\), and one sole contact for both would be a cut vertex. Add a marker \(r\) adjacent only to \(u,v\). In this augmented graph use ordinary path closure on pairs of vertices from \(C\), so the threshold is \(|C|\), the augmented order minus one. Since every vertex in \(C\) has degree at least two there, each contact of \(p\) or \(q\) can be made universal in \(C\) by closure. Keep applying this closure on \(C\) until no more edges qualify. If \(|C|\leq2k\), the high noncontacts, whose degree is at least \(k\) within \(C\), then form a clique with the high contacts.

If \(|C|=2k+1\), let \(S\) be the high noncontacts of final degree exactly \(k\). They already had degree at least \(k\) in \(C\), so no new edge is incident to any member of \(S\). Every high vertex outside \(S\) has final degree at least \(k+1\), and hence sees every member of \(S\) after closure and already did so originally. Let \(a\) be the number of high vertices outside \(S\). For any \(x\in S\), one has \(|S|=2k-a\) and original internal degree on \(S\) at most \(k-a\), so \(x\) misses at least \(k-1\) other vertices in \(S\). Each such vertex is at distance two from \(x\) in \(G\) through the high contact \(u\), since every vertex of \(S\) sees every original contact. Further, both \(p\) and \(q\) are at distance two from \(x\), through \(u,v\) respectively, as \(x\) is a noncontact. This contradicts the distance-two cap. Therefore \(S\) is empty and all the high vertices form a clique after closure in this case too.

In the augmented closure, start at \(r,u\), traverse the remaining high vertices ending at another contact, and then append \(z\), which sees every contact after closure. Reversing closure yields a spanning path with all original edges in the augmented graph. If \(r\) is internal on that path, its two neighbors on the path are \(u,v\), and replace that segment by \(u,p\), a spanning path of \(H\), then \(q,v\), with the orientation reversed if needed. If \(r\) is an endpoint, its one path neighbor is one of \(u,v\); replace the first edge by starting at the opposite port, traversing \(H\), then the designated port and the given contact. This yields a spanning path of \(G\) in all cases.
