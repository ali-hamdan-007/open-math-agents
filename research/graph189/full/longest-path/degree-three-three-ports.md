# Three-port closure in the minimum-degree-three order residual

Retain the hypotheses of `degree-three.md`. Let the omitted component \(H\) of a longest path through \(z\) have order \(M=k-1\), and assume we are in the case of exactly three positions with multiple contacts and no other contacts. Then \(H\) has exactly three neighbors outside itself, forming a set \(A\) of three ports. Each port has at least two neighbors in \(H\), each vertex of \(H\) sees at least two ports, and \(H\) is a clique with at most a matching removed. Assume \(H\) is Hamilton-connected, as proved in `degree-three.md` for \(k\geq9\), so \(|V(H)\cap D_2(z)|\leq4\). The three-port path argument below is from the parallel audit by `perfectcover`.

Throughout, assume \(k\geq10\). The vertex \(z\) is outside \(H\cup A\) because a port has at least two neighbors in \(H\) and lies at an internal position of \(Q\) (an endpoint with a neighbor in \(H\) would extend the path). Its two path neighbors are outside \(H\), so its degree is at least four. Also \(z\) sees at most one port: any two ports jointly see every vertex of \(H\), so two port neighbors would give \(|V(H)\cap D_2(z)|=k-1>4\). Thus \(z\) has at least two neighbors outside the ports and \(H\).

## Routing all three ports through the exterior

For any two prescribed endpoints \(p,q\) from \(A=\{p,q,r\}\), the graph on \(V(H)\cup A\) has a spanning path from \(p\) to \(q\). In fact every vertex of \(H\) has all its ambient neighbors in this block and degree at least \(k\). If \(r\) has at least three block neighbors, add a degree-two marker adjacent to \(p,q\). Each port has degree at least three in the augmented graph, and each vertex of \(H\) still has degree at least \(k\); hence every missing edge with an endpoint in \(H\) qualifies for ordinary cycle closure at augmented order \(k+3\). Once these edges are added, the port pairs qualify as well, since \(2(k-1)\geq k+3\). A Hamilton cycle in the resulting clique plus marker reverses to one in the original augmented graph. Removing the marker yields the desired path.

Otherwise \(r\) has exactly its two \(H\)-neighbors \(a,b\) in the block. Every other vertex of \(H\) misses only \(r\) among the other \(k+1\) block vertices, so sees both \(p,q\) and every vertex of \(H\). Choose distinct \(x,y\in V(H)\setminus\{a,b\}\). Traverse \(p,x,a,r,b\), then all still unused vertices of \(H\), finishing at \(y,q\). This uses only original edges and gives the required path.

## Multiple components outside the exterior and its ports

Let the components of \(G-A\) other than \(H\) be \(C_1,\ldots,C_t\). Each contains a high vertex, since a singleton \(z\) would have at most one port neighbor and no other neighbor. Each has at least \(k-2\) vertices by the degree bound, and their total order is at most \(2k\). Thus \(t\leq2\). If \(t=2\), each order is at most \(k+2\). Each component sees at least two distinct ports by 2-connectivity. In particular, the component containing \(z\) has a high contact of some port, since \(z\) sees at most one port. Select that contact in the low component and a contact to a different port in the all-high one.

The all-high component has minimum degree at least \(k-3\), so is Hamilton-connected since its order is at most \(k+2\) and \(k\geq10\). It supplies a spanning path ending at its selected contact. In the low component, deleting \(z\) leaves minimum degree at least \(k-4\) on at most \(k+1\) vertices, hence also Hamilton-connected. Start its spanning path at the selected high contact, end at a distinct high neighbor of \(z\), and append \(z\). There is a distinct choice because \(z\) has at least two neighbors in that component. Join these two paths through the spanning path of \(H\) and all three ports, prescribed to start and end at the two selected ports. This gives a spanning path of \(G\).

## One other component with a cut vertex

Suppose there is one remaining component \(C\) and it has a cut vertex \(w\), and assume \(k\geq12\). Each component of \(C-w\) has a high vertex: a singleton low \(z\neq w\) would have at most one port neighbor and the possible neighbor \(w\), contradicting its degree three. Consequently each component has order at least \(k-3\), and their total order is at most \(2k-1\). There are exactly two, each of order at most \(k+2\). At least one is all high, including both if \(z=w\).

An all-high component has minimum internal degree at least \(k-4\), hence is Hamilton-connected. It has a contact of \(w\) by connectivity of \(C\) and a contact of some port by 2-connectivity of \(G\). They can be selected at distinct vertices, since a sole common contact for both purposes would be a cut vertex of \(G\). Span the all-high component between the two selected contacts. The other component, if all high, admits a spanning path starting at any \(w\)-contact. If it contains \(z\), its high subgraph has order at most \(k+1\) and minimum degree at least \(k-5\), giving Hamilton-connectedness. If \(wz\) is an edge, start at \(z\) and then at a high neighbor in this component, which exists since \(z\) sees at most one port. Otherwise take a spanning high path from a contact of \(w\) to a distinct high neighbor of \(z\), and append \(z\). There are at least two choices of high neighbors of \(z\) inside the component in this latter case.

Use a spanning path of the block on \(H\) and all ports ending at the selected port, continue with the all-high component to its contact of \(w\), use \(w\), and finish through the other component. Thus this case is traceable too.

## One other 2-connected component with a contact to two ports

Suppose there is one other component \(C\), it is 2-connected, and some \(y\in V(C)\) sees at least two ports. Every such vertex has all \(k-1\) vertices of \(H\) at distance two, since any two ports jointly see \(H\). Hence it has at most one distance-two vertex within \(C\) and therefore misses at most one other vertex of \(C\). To see the latter implication, any vertex at distance at least three would be separated from it by its unique distance-two vertex, contradicting 2-connectivity of \(C\). Call these vertices the contacts to two or three ports. They are high because \(z\) sees at most one port.

Add a leaf marker adjacent only to \(y\), and apply ordinary path closure only to pairs of vertices from \(C\), at threshold \(|C|\leq2k\). Since \(C\) has minimum degree at least two and each contact to two or three ports has degree at least \(|C|-2\), those contacts may all be made universal within \(C\). Continue until closure adds no further edge. Every other high vertex has initial degree at least \(k-1\) within \(C\), since it initially loses at most one port neighbor. When \(|C|\leq2k-2\), all high vertices form a clique in the closure immediately.

For the remaining values \(|C|=2k-1\) or \(2k\), suppose \(x\) is high and has final degree \(k-1\). This forces \(x\) to be an original contact to exactly one port, with no edge added at \(x\); a noncontact already had degree at least \(k\). In particular \(x\) sees \(y\) originally. If \(|C|=2k-1\), every missing high peer of \(x\) in the closure has final degree \(k-1\) too, since any high vertex of degree at least \(k\) would have qualifying degree sum. Thus all missing high peers see \(y\) originally, and there are at least \(|C|-2-(k-1)=k-2\) of them. If \(|C|=2k\), \(x\) misses at least \(k-1\) high peers and \(y\) misses at most one of those originally, so again at least \(k-2\) missing high peers are at distance two from \(x\) through \(y\). In both cases, there are at least two vertices of \(H\) at distance two via the sole port of \(x\), and at least one additional port at distance two via \(y\), which sees at least two ports. These disjoint witnesses total at least \(k+1\), a contradiction.

Thus all high vertices have final degree at least \(k\) at these two boundary orders. Consequently the high vertices form a clique in the closure for every order in this section. Start a spanning path of the augmented closure at the leaf marker, then \(y\), then traverse the high clique ending at a high neighbor of \(z\) different from \(y\), and append \(z\). This distinct choice exists because \(z\) has at least two high neighbors in \(C\). Reverse path closure. The marker is a leaf in the original augmented graph, so the resulting path has it as an endpoint followed by \(y\). Replace the marker by a spanning path of the exterior block and all three ports, ending at any port originally adjacent to \(y\). This spans \(G\).

## One other 2-connected component whose contacts are all private

Now suppose each contact in \(C\) sees just one port. First let \(|C|\leq2k-1\). In the coordinator's construction, add a marker adjacent to every contact in \(C\), and attach one new leaf only to the marker. Every high vertex of \(C\) has augmented degree at least \(k\), since the marker replaces its lost port adjacency if it had one. Apply path closure only among vertices of \(C\), at threshold \(|C|+1\leq2k\), to complete the high vertices to a clique. In that closure a spanning path begins at the new leaf, uses the marker and an original high contact, and then spans the high clique ending at a distinct high neighbor of \(z\) to append \(z\). A high contact exists because \(C\) meets at least two ports and \(z\) sees at most one. Also \(z\) has at least two high neighbors in \(C\), so one differs from the chosen start contact. Reversing closure recovers an original augmented spanning path. Its leaf and marker occur first, next to one original contact which may now be high or low. Replace the two marker vertices by a spanning path through \(H\) and all ports ending at the unique port touched by that contact. This spans \(G\).

It remains to handle \(|C|=2k\), for which assume \(k\geq12\). If all high vertices have degree at least \(k\) within \(C\), one leaf marker at any high contact suffices, since ordinary path closure at threshold \(|C|=2k\) completes the high vertices to a clique. Append \(z\) as before, reverse closure, and replace the leaf by a path through the exterior and its ports.

Otherwise choose a high vertex \(x\) with degree \(k-1\) within \(C\). It touches one port \(a\), because its degree in \(G\) is at least \(k\). Write \(s_a=|N_H(a)|\geq2\). Among its at least \(k-1\) nonadjacent high peers in \(C\), at least \(s_a-1\) have distance at least three from it in \(G\), because \(s_a\) vertices of \(H\) already occupy distance-two slots. Let \(y\) be such a peer. Their closed neighborhoods in \(C\) are disjoint, each of size at least \(k\). As \(|C|=2k\), these neighborhoods have size exactly \(k\) and partition \(C\). More generally for any high vertex of degree \(k-1\) in \(C\), all its high peers at ambient distance at least three have the complementary closed neighborhood in \(C\), and each touches a port different from the original vertex’s port (a shared port would give ambient distance two).

Write \(X=N_C[x]\) and \(Y=N_C[y]\) for the two halves, exchanging their names if necessary so \(z\in X\), and let \(X_0\) and \(Y_0\) be the sets of vertices with those respective closed neighborhoods. Every vertex of these sets has degree \(k-1\) in \(C\), so is high and touches a single port. Vertices of \(X_0\) are adjacent to \(z\), so \(|X_0|\leq3\). For any vertex of \(Y_0\) with port \(b\), the preceding count forces \(s_b-1\leq |X_0|\), namely \(s_b\leq4\). At most one port has \(s_b\leq4\), since the neighborhoods of any two ports cover the \(k-1>8\) vertices of \(H\). Therefore all of \(Y_0\) touches the same port \(b\). No member of \(X_0\) can touch \(b\), since each must have at least one high peer at ambient distance at least three and all of its possible peers are in \(Y_0\), which then would share its port.

A member \(x'\in X_0\) of port \(a'\neq b\) misses all \(k\) high vertices of \(Y\). Through its port it has \(s_{a'}\) distance-two witnesses in \(H\), so at least \(s_{a'}\) of the missed high vertices have ambient distance at least three. They belong to \(Y_0\). Since any two port neighborhoods cover \(H\), if \(s_b\leq3\) this gives \(|Y_0|\geq s_{a'}\geq k-4\).

If instead \(s_b=4\), then \(|X_0|=3\). Every \(y'\in Y_0\) has all its distance-two budget used up by its four witnesses in \(H\) and the \(k-4\) high vertices of \(X\setminus X_0\), which are nonadjacent to \(y'\) and have distance two because their closed neighborhood cannot be its complement. Thus no vertex of \(Y\) touches either port distinct from \(b\), as \(y'\) is adjacent to every other vertex of \(Y\) and such a port would be another distance-two witness. Both other ports have a high contact somewhere in \(C\): each is an internal marked position of \(Q\), its path neighbors cannot be other marked positions (the same-side gaps are at least \(M+1\), and \(z\) lies between positions on opposite sides), and at most one of its path neighbors is \(z\). Hence both ports distinct from \(b\) have contacts in \(X\). Each \(x'\in X_0\) now sees the other non-\(b\) port at distance two through one of its \(X\)-contacts, gaining a distance-two witness beyond the \(s_{a'}\) from \(H\). This forces \(|Y_0|\geq s_{a'}+1\geq k-4\) here too.

Every vertex of \(Y_0\) is universal within \(Y\), so \(G[Y]\) has minimum degree at least \(k-4\) and is Hamilton-connected on \(k\) vertices. Every high vertex in \(X\setminus\{z\}\) has degree at least \(k-6\) in that subgraph: it loses at most one port neighbor, at most four neighbors in \(Y\setminus Y_0\), and the vertex \(z\). Since \(2(k-6)\geq k\), this subgraph of order \(k-1\) is Hamilton-connected as well.

We may choose an original cross edge \(uv\) with \(u\in X\setminus\{z\}\), \(v\in Y\), and a neighbor \(w\) of \(z\) in \(X\) distinct from \(u\). A cross edge exists because \(C-z\) is connected. There is a neighbor of \(z\) in \(X\), since each vertex of \(X_0\) sees \(z\). If \(z\) has two such neighbors, choose one different from \(u\). If its sole \(X\)-neighbor \(w\) were the endpoint of every cross edge from \(X-z\) to \(Y\), removing \(w\) would disconnect \(X\setminus\{z,w\}\) from both \(Y\) and \(z\), contradicting 2-connectivity of \(C\). Thus the distinct choices exist in either case.

Start with any vertex of \(Y_0\), which differs from \(v\) because it has no edge to \(X\). Span \(Y\) to \(v\), take the cross edge to \(u\), span \(X-z\) to \(w\), and append \(z\). Prepend a spanning path of \(H\) and the three ports ending at \(b\), which meets the starting vertex of \(Y_0\). This completes the private-contact equality case.

Together the four remaining-component arguments eliminate the exact three-multi-contact pattern for all \(k\geq12\). For \(k=10,11\), a cut vertex in the sole remaining component and the private-contact equality case are still open here. Cases with a singleton-contact group are outside this manuscript.
