# Audit of the minimum-degree-three exterior

Let G be a finite simple 2-connected graph of minimum degree three and second-smallest degree k. Suppose its order is at most 3k+2 and every vertex has at most k vertices at distance two. In a hypothetical nontraceable G, let z be its vertex of degree three and Q a longest path containing z. Write M for the number of vertices outside Q. Under these assumptions, the exterior of every such Q is a single Hamilton-connected graph H with floor(k/2)+1 ≤ M ≤ k−1.

## The shortest-tail bound

The near-spanning theorem supplies a longest path on all vertices except z and gives 6 ≤ k. This makes z unique. Every longest unrooted path omits z, since the unrooted omitted-component theorem excludes a singleton high vertex. On any such path of order n−1, write i₁<i₂<i₃ for the indices of z's neighbors. They are internal, and every gap is at least M+1, since splicing z across the gap omits precisely its former interior.

Minimize the shorter endpoint tail over all longest unrooted paths and orient a minimizing path so l=i₁−1 ≤ r=n−1−i₃. If l>M, then n−2=l+r+(i₂−i₁)+(i₃−i₂)≥4M+4, proving M<k. Otherwise an endpoint pivot at t within (iⱼ,iⱼ₊₁] reverses the prefix of length t−1. The new starting tail has length t−iⱼ−1, ruling out the first l pivot positions. The last attachment in the reversed prefix comes from i₁ and now has position t−i₁. Its next attachment is at iⱼ₊₁. Their difference iⱼ₊₁+i₁−t rules out the final M−l pivot positions. The two forbidden ranges are disjoint since the interval has at least M+1 positions. The l positions after i₃ are also forbidden by shortest-tail minimality. The initial endpoint has at least k neighbors, all on the path, so k+2M+l≤n−2≤3k. Since l≥1, M≤k−1.

The rooted degree bound supplies internal degree at least h=floor(k/2) for every exterior vertex, and hence at least h+1 vertices per component. There can be only one component H. A cut vertex would leave at least two parts of size at least h, giving M≥2h+1≥k. Dirac supplies a Hamilton cycle. For even k or M≤k−2, one has 2h≥M+1. Add a marker adjacent to any two prescribed endpoints. Closure at the augmented order M+1 completes all original vertices and hence gives a spanning path between the prescribed endpoints in H. In particular k=6 is covered, including M=5. The incidence inequality M(k−M)≤3k−2 for M≤k−2 gives M≥k−3 when k≥15, since M>k/2 and 4(k−4)>3k−2.

## The odd boundary

Let k=2h+1 and M=2h, and assume H is not Hamilton-connected. The set S of internal degree-h vertices has size at least h. Indeed, if there were at most h−1 such vertices, the other h+1 or more vertices could each be joined to every original vertex by endpoint-marker closure at order 2h+1. All remaining vertices would then have degree at least h+1 and the closure would complete. Each vertex outside S has a neighbor in S, by its internal degree.

For x∈S the rooted attachment calculation forces x and every H-neighbor of x to attach to *both* path neighbors u,v of z. Namely its number of path neighbors must equal h+1, and for each H-neighbor y equality holds in the overlap bound |Aₓ∩Aᵧ|≤rₓ+1. This requires the run across z to give both positions flanking z. Thus z is internal and both u and v are adjacent to every vertex of H. The third neighbor w of z is on Q by the manuscript's omitted-neighbor splice. Its contact set C=N(w)∩V(H) has size at least 2h−3: all of H is in the second distance layer of z, and each member of N(z) misses at most three of these vertices.

Once a fourth distinct attachment position t can use a spanning path of H to reach w, every pair in {u,v,w,t} can be joined by a spanning splice. For a pair involving u or v, begin with any contact of the other position and open a Hamilton cycle at this contact. Its other endpoint is adjacent to the universal contact position. Two positions on the same side of z permitting a splice must differ by at least M+1: the replaced segment contains no z and omits only the old gap interior. All four positions are internal, distinct, and different from z. They require at least 2(M+1)+2 positions in total, whether split 3+1 or 2+2. If on one side, the requirement is at least 3(M+1)+1. Both exceed the n−M−2≤2k+1 available internal positions. Thus a fourth t is impossible whenever such a splice to w exists.

For each x∈H, let E(x) be the possible other endpoints of spanning paths in H starting at x. One has |E(x)|≥h. Open a Hamilton cycle at x to give a spanning path from x. At the opposite endpoint, its at least h neighboring pivot positions yield distinct successor endpoints, including the unchanged endpoint from the preceding path vertex. For h≥4, even if x belongs to C, the subsets E(x) and C∖{x} of H∖{x} have sizes at least h and 2h−4, with sum greater than 2h−1. Thus every fourth attachment can splice to w. There must be a fourth attachment since any s∈S has at least k−h=h+1≥4 path neighbors. This contradiction covers odd k≥9.

**The remaining k=7 also gives Hamilton-connectedness.** Here h=3, M=6, |S|≥3, |C|≥3, and |E(x)|≥3. Call x bad if E(x)∩C is empty. Every contact of every fourth attachment position is bad, by the four-position argument. Every s∈S needs at least four distinct path neighbors, so S consists of bad vertices. If |C|≥4, then E(x) and C∖{x} each have at least three elements in the five-element set H∖{x}. Hence there are no bad vertices. If |C|=3, the same count rules out every x outside C. Consequently |C|=3 and S=C is exactly the set of bad vertices.

The other three vertices have degree at least four inside H. If any s∈S misses one of them, endpoint-marker closure at order seven first joins the three high vertices to all originals. Vertex s thereby reaches degree four and can also be joined to every original. The four resulting universal original vertices give all remaining originals degree at least four, completing the closure and proving Hamilton-connectedness. Otherwise S is independent and complete to the three remaining vertices, two of which, say b₁,b₂, are adjacent since their degrees are at least four. Denote the third by b₃. For distinct s,t∈S and its third member r, the spanning path s,b₁,b₂,r,b₃,t gives t∈E(s)∩C. This contradicts badness and closes the k=7 case.

## Groups and an independent finite check

At M=k−1 every exterior vertex requires at least two path neighbors. Within either side of z, collapse maximal runs of successive attachment positions with an identical singleton contact; each position with multiple contacts is its own group. Consecutive groups on a side have representatives with distinct H-neighbors, so Hamilton-connectedness forces a spacing of at least M+1 between the groups. Four groups violate the same packing bound. There must be at least two multiple-contact positions: otherwise each of the M≥5 exterior vertices needs a singleton group of its own. Three multiple-contact positions imply that every vertex has internal degree at least M−2, giving a clique with at most a matching deleted. With two such positions, at most one exterior vertex can also use a singleton group. All other vertices are adjacent to both positions and to every other H vertex, so H itself is a clique.

The paper argument at k=7 was also checked on all 32,768 labeled six-vertex simple graphs. Of 1,858 graphs of minimum degree at least three, 240 lack Hamilton-connectedness. Across these graphs all 10,080 choices of contact set C of size at least three had some degree-three vertex s with E(s)∩C nonempty. The check used all spanning vertex orders and is reproducible with check_six_boundary.py.
