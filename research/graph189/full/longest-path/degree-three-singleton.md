# The singleton attachment group at exterior order k−1

Assume the degree-three residual of degree-three-audit.md. Thus G is 2-connected, |G|≤3k+2, z has degree three, every other vertex has degree at least k, and each vertex has at most k vertices at distance two. Suppose that the exterior H of a longest path Q through z has order k−1. In the two-multiple-contact case, there are two attachment positions p,q with multiple H-neighbors and a nonempty group of positions with sole H-neighbor e. The exterior classification makes H a clique and every H vertex other than e sees both p and q and no other Q vertex. We prove this pattern impossible for k≥14.

Put W=H−e, U={p,q,e}, and B=W∪U. W is a clique of order k−2 complete to U, with no neighbors beyond B. The low vertex z sees no member of U. It misses e because all its neighbors lie on Q; a neighbor p or q would place all k−2 vertices of W among the at most four vertices of H at distance two from z. Each member of U has a contact outside B. This holds for e by the nonempty singleton group, and for p,q from their path neighbors on Q: p and q cannot be successive on Q, by same-side spanning splice or the intervening z on opposite sides. Between any two prescribed members of U, B has a spanning path: insert the third member between two clique vertices and order the other clique vertices along the path.

Write C=G−B, of order c≤2k+1. All three neighbors of z lie in C. A spanning path of C starting at a contact of U expands to a spanning path of G by prefixing the B-path ending at that port.

## Disconnected and cut-vertex cases

If C is disconnected, each component contains a high vertex (a singleton z is impossible), and therefore has order at least k−2 since its high vertices can lose only three port neighbors. At k≥14 there are exactly two, each of order at most k+3. The all-high component has internal minimum degree at least k−3 and is Hamilton-connected. The other component after removing z has minimum degree at least k−4 on at most k+2 vertices and is Hamilton-connected as well. The criterion used is 2δ≥order+1, proved by adding a degree-two marker at any desired pair of endpoints and using ordinary Hamilton-cycle closure. Each component meets at least two members of U by 2-connectivity of G, and all the contacts are high. Choose distinct ports, one per component. Span the all-high component from its designated contact. In the other span the high subgraph from the designated contact to a distinct neighbor of z and append z. Join these paths through the full B-path between the selected ports.

If C is connected with cut vertex w, each part of C−w contains a high vertex and has order at least k−3, since a high vertex loses at most four neighbors. A singleton z would have at most w as neighbor. Their total order at most 2k gives exactly two parts, each of order at most k+3. An all-high part has minimum degree at least k−4, and any part containing z after deleting z has minimum degree at least k−5 on at most k+2 vertices. Both high graphs are Hamilton-connected at k≥14. An all-high part has distinct vertices seeing w and a port: it sees w by connectedness of C, a port by connectedness of G−w, and if every such contact were the same vertex its deletion would disconnect the remaining vertices of the part. Span it from its port contact to the w-contact. If the other part is high, enter it from w and take a spanning path. If it contains z and w sees z, enter at z, continue to an internal high neighbor, and span the high graph. Otherwise start at a high w-contact, end the high path at a different internal neighbor of z, and append z. Prefix a full B-path ending at the port of the first part.

## The 3-connected case

Every U-contact in C has all k−2 vertices of W at distance two and therefore at most two distance-two vertices within C. In a 3-connected C its eccentricity is at most two: a third layer would be separated by the second layer of size at most two. Thus it has degree at least c−3 within C. Add a leaf marker at one original contact y. Ordinary Hamilton-path closure at threshold c may make each U-contact universal in C because δ(C)≥3. A noncontact high initially has degree at least k in C. When c≤2k the high vertices can consequently be completed to a clique (contacts already universal). If c=2k+1, continue closure and suppose a high x has final degree k. It is a noncontact gaining no edge; each contact is originally adjacent to x. The at least c−2−k=k−1 high peers missed by x in the closure also have final degree k and likewise originally see y. They supply k−1 distinct distance-two witnesses from x. Two distinct members of U give two more via their original C contacts, a contradiction. Thus the high clique is present in all cases. Starting at the leaf and y, span the clique ending at a different neighbor of z and append z. Reversal of path closure and removal of the leaf recovers a spanning path of C starting at y.

## The 2-connected case with a two-vertex cut

Choose a separating pair a,b in C. Each part of C−{a,b} contains a high vertex, since a singleton z could see at most a,b. Each part consequently has order at least k−4, with at most five outside neighbors available to a high vertex. The parts have total order at most 2k−1. There are exactly two and each has order at most k+3. Each part has neighbors of both a and b by 2-connectivity of C, and a pair of contacts for a and b can be chosen distinct: if the only contacts for both were the same vertex, that vertex would cut off the remaining part vertices.

We construct a spanning path of each part L with one endpoint seeing a and the other seeing b. Add a degree-two marker at any specified distinct pair of endpoints in L. Every high vertex has degree at least k−5 within L. Since the augmented order is at most k+4≤2(k−5) for k≥14, ordinary Hamilton-cycle closure completes all high vertices of L to a clique. The marker edges and any z-edges are unchanged. It suffices to construct the prescribed spanning path after these high-high closures, close it using the marker, reverse closure, and remove the marker.

In an all-high part take distinct a,b contacts as endpoints. This also handles both parts if z is one of the separators. If z belongs to L and sees just one separator, say a, take z as its contact and any high contact y of b as the other endpoint. There are two internal neighbors of z, so choose one different from y and traverse the high clique from it to y. If z sees both separators, let t be its sole internal neighbor. Some high vertex y different from t sees a or b: otherwise C−t would separate L−{z,t} from both z and the other part. Use y for that separator and z for the other, then traverse from z,t through the clique to y.

Finally, if z sees neither separator, choose any distinct high a,b contacts x,y. There are three distinct internal high neighbors of z. If x sees z, start x,z,t with a neighbor t different from x,y, then traverse the remaining high clique to y. The case where y sees z is symmetric. If neither sees z, take any two distinct internal z-neighbors s,t and traverse the high clique from x to y with the segment s,z,t inserted. These constructions give the required two original spanning paths. Joining them to the two separators gives a Hamilton cycle in C. Open it at any U-contact and prepend the full exterior block.

These four cases exclude the singleton-group pattern for all k≥14. Together with the separately verified pure two-port and three-multiple-port cases, they exclude |H|=k−1 at k≥14 in the degree-three residual. The proof does not settle smaller exterior orders or the full conjecture.
