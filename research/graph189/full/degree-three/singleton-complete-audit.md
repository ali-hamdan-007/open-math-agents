# Independent audit of the singleton-T exclusion

In the d=1 triangle template, each private meets W. A private missing all W sees every member of W via its own anchor, and its degree forces an edge to another private since it has at most k−2 remote neighbors. The other private's exclusive remotes then overflow the distance-two cap, after counting z and both other anchors. Each w meets at most one private: meeting two would put at distance two the root, the missed private, and all remotes outside T and the missed private's exclusive class. The cap would require c≤2k−4, contrary to c≥2k−2. Meeting all three also breaches the cap via the exclusive remotes alone. When |T|=1, degree then forces each w to meet one private, t, and every other w.

The three common representatives yield |E|+2s−|E∩S|+c≤3k−8 by counting the remote sets they see via their private and t. The cap at t implies |H|≤k−5+q+s. The case q=3 would force s≥c+2−k≥k, violating 2s≤k−6. Every represented private has |R_i∩H|≥k−a_i−2, since a member of S∩R_i has at least k−2 remote neighbors and its closed remote neighborhood lies in both H and H_i. If represented by t instead, all of R_i lies in H and the exclusive-neighbor bound from H_i is stronger. Summing over three represented privates yields at least 2k−2, whereas multiplicity counting gives at most |H|+|E|+q−1≤2k−8. Hence some class h is unrepresented. Any pair-visible member of S would omit h and reach all remotes outside U_h. At most two such remotes lie outside H by the cap after counting all W and two anchors. The representative w_h gives u_h+s≤k−3, so c≤2k−6+q, a contradiction. If q=2, all remotes outside U_h already belong to H, giving an even stronger contradiction. Thus S consists of exclusive remotes, q≤1, and s>0 by the degree of t.

For the final count, fix x∈S and put K=N_C[x], D=N_C(K)\K, and L=C\(K∪D). The remote degree at x gives |K|≥k, while every member of D contributes a distance-two partner at x, together with W and its own anchor. Hence |D|≤3. Since K⊆H, |L|≥k−1−s>0 by 2s≤k−6. Also t∈K forces S⊆K∪D. There are no edges between K and L.

If one private p_i met both K\{t} and L, its closed remote neighborhood H_i would contain two closed remote balls of sizes at least k−1, intersecting only in D. This gives |H_i|≥2k−5. Meanwhile the cap at p_i gives |H_i\R_i|≤a_i+1 after counting z, two anchors, and the missed W. The anchor cap gives |R_i|≤k−2, and nonempty other W classes give a_i≤k−6, so |H_i|≤2k−7. This rules out such a private. At least two privates meet K outside t because |K\{t}|≥k−1 and each private covers at most k−2. Every L vertex must therefore be exclusive to the same remaining private p_h. Its degree gives |L|+|D|≥k. The representative w_h sees L via p_h and S via t, disjointly, plus z and the other two privates. Thus |L|+s≤k−3, contradicting |L|≥k−3 and s>0.
