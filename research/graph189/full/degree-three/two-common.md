# Two triple-visible remotes, one adjacent to every common middle vertex

Use the d=1 triangle notation of singleton-complete.md and assume T={t,t'} with t adjacent to all W. The arbitrary-T arguments there show that each private touches W and each w meets at most one private. Let W_i be those w touching p_i. All three sets are nonempty, but their union need not be all W. Put R_i=N_C(p_i), H_i its closed C-neighborhood. Again |H_i|<=|R_i|+|W_i|+1<=2k-7.

Put S=N_C(t) minus T and s=|S|. Each w_i in W_i sees z, the other two privates, and (V_i minus T) union S at distance two. Summing over i, counting a nontriple remote once or twice according to its number of visible anchors, gives c-2+|E|+2s-|S intersect E|<=3k-9 and so 2s<=k-5. If s<=1, the k-4 common vertices and at most one t' neighbor force t to touch at least two privates. Take two such privates, with complementary index h. Then t sees at distance two every nonneighbor outside U_h and T in C, and sees the three anchors. This gives c-|U_h|-2-s+3<=k, impossible since |U_h|<=k-4 and c>=2k-2. Thus s>=2.

Choose x in S, K=N_C[x], D its external boundary and L=C minus (K union D). Again |D|<=3 because x sees all W and at least one anchor at distance two, and |K|<=k-1+s by the cap at t, whose C-neighbors can include t' as well. Hence |L|>=k-4-s>1 by k>=9. The set S is disjoint from L. No R_i touches K and L both outside T, since the two closed C-neighborhoods would give |H_i|>=2(k-1)-3=2k-5. At least two privates touch K outside T, either directly through x or because exclusive x yields |K|>=k with at least k-2 nontriples, whereas one class supports at most |V_i|-2<=k-4 of those. Thus L minus T is nonempty and entirely in some U_h. For such a vertex the remote degree gives |L|+|D|>=k, hence |L minus T|>=k-4 (t lies in K). The representative w_h sees disjoint L minus T and S at distance two plus z and the two missed privates, forcing |L minus T|+s<=k-3, impossible as s>=2.

## Relaxing completeness

It suffices that t touches at least one representative of each W_i and that m=|W intersect N(t)| exceeds max_i |W_i|. Put r=k-4-m. Each w missing t must meet a private, t' and all the other W by its degree, so every such w is distance two from t. The same representative sum yields 2s<=k-5. If s<=r+1, degree forces t to touch two privates. As above the remote partners outside the complementary U_h, T, S, the three anchors and the r missed W already give s>=r+3, impossible. So s>=r+2. For x in S we have |D|<=3+r and |K|<=k-1+s-r, hence |L|>=k-4-s>1. No R_i can cross K and L outside T, because then |H_i|>=2k-2-(3+r)=k+m-1>k+|W_i|-1. Therefore L minus T consists of one exclusive class h as above, with |L minus T|>=k-4-r. The representative w_h adjacent to t sees L minus T and S alongside the three fixed partners, impossible since s>=r+2.

## No nontriple remote can touch both triples

This additional exclusion has no assumption on m or W_i incidence. If a nontriple remote x touched both t and t', every W would be distance two from x since degree forces each W to meet some triple. Again |D|<=3. Taking a private p_i adjacent to x gives K contained in H_i, whence |K|<=2k-7 and |L|>=2. Both triples lie in K. A crossing R_i needs at least 2k-5 places in H_i, impossible. At least two privates touch K outside T, either already through x or because |K|>=k and one private supports at most k-4 nontriples. Thus all L are exclusive h, and |L|>=k-3. Each representative w_h touches at least one triple, so x is at distance two from it, alongside L and the three fixed partners, contradicting the cap.

If m>= (k-4)/2, the hypothesis m>max |W_i| in the preceding relaxation can be omitted. At most one large class A has a_A=|W_A|>=m, since the other class is nonempty and the three W_i are disjoint. When no large class occurs the preceding proof applies. If S meets R_A, use x in their intersection. The union of all W_A and all t-neighbor W lies at distance two from x, and is strictly larger than a_A because t has a representative of another class. Thus |D|<=k-a_A-2, and any crossing H_i would contain at least k+a_A vertices, above its cap. If S misses R_A, then the representative w_A sees disjoint S and R_A minus T, implying |R_A|<=k-1-s. Hence |H_A|<=k+a_A-s<=k+m-4, using a_A<=k-6=m+r-2 and s>=r+2. A crossing A would need at least k+m-1 members, again impossible. This proves that a triple adjacent to at least half of W cannot have representatives in all three W_i.
