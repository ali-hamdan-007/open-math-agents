# Audit of the nonadjacent two-anchor cover at minimum degree three

The paper proof in nonadjacent-cover.md checks for \(k\ge9\), \(\operatorname{ecc}(z)\le3\), and \(n>3k\), with a covering pair \(a,b\) of nonadjacent neighbors of \(z\) and with every vertex besides \(z\) of degree at least \(k\). No global diameter bound is needed.

The counted slots at the two covering anchors are disjoint within each anchor's cap. The remotes contribute \(|C|+t\), the visible missed middle vertices contribute \(g\), and the incidences within the anchors contribute \(h\ge2\). Hence \(d+t+g\le1\). The degree count at a common middle vertex is valid because any edge to a private middle vertex makes that private vertex visible from the opposite covering anchor. A common middle vertex cannot see an exclusive remote by the definition of visibility. The first bound on misses ensures the common middle class exists before it is used. Thus \(2\le m\le3\) and the full remote order before transfers is at most \(2k\). Each exclusive remote class has at least \(k-2\) vertices.

In the remote augmentation, the possible transferred overlap has no exclusive neighbor. Transferring a weak doubleton member loses at most one edge from at most one exclusive vertex, and if this happens the remote order has dropped by at least one. Therefore the unchanged exclusive vertices can be completed first, and after their clique is complete their degree is at least \(k\), which pairs with the one possible degree-\((k-1)\) vertex at the reduced closure threshold.

For the endpoint choices, reserve the port of a retained overlap before selecting others. If both doubleton members remain, each has at least one port left and their union has size at least \(k-3\ge6\). The larger neighborhood therefore has at least three ports. Choose the starting port from the smaller one, leaving at least two distinct ports in the larger one for the internal doubleton member. The internal singleton uses a disjoint exclusive class and has enough ports after the reservation. If a weak doubleton member was transferred, the remaining member covers all but at most one of its exclusive class and supplies the starting port. The two-singleton case has two disjoint exclusive classes of size at least \(k-2\). In all cases there is at least one internal special. Every unused exclusive vertex can be inserted between the port leaving the starting special and the first incoming port of an internal special, since both are distinct members of the completed clique.

The core degree accounting allows one exception to the loss of \(s-l\), namely the transferred weak doubleton member, with at most one extra lost edge. Thus pair sums are bounded below by \(2k-2s\) with no transfers and \(2k-2s+2l-1\) otherwise. These meet the core path-closure threshold, including equality at \(k=9,m=3,t=1\). The common middle class has at least \(k-4\) members, so distinct start and end vertices are available in this class when traversing the completed core clique. Both endpoint leaves preserve the specified ends when reversing their closures. The two disjoint original paths join by the designated anchor edge.

This audit concerns the handwritten proof only. No finite solver was run.
