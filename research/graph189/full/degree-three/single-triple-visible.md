Assume the d=1 triangle template and the hypotheses of residual.md. Every private p_i has a neighbor in W. Otherwise its at most k-2 remote neighbors (anchor i already sees both foreign privates at distance two) and its one anchor neighbor force a p_i p_j edge. At distance two from p_i are all k-4 W through anchor i, the other two anchors and z, and across p_j all u_j >= 2 exclusive remotes. This exceeds k.

Let T={t}. Every common w misses at least one private, since otherwise the exclusive remotes (at least k+2) are all at distance two. Degree forces w to miss at most two other middle vertices. If w meets two privates and omits p_i, then it sees all C outside U_i and {t} at distance two, because every such remote meets a private other than p_i. The root and p_i each use a further slot. Thus u_i >= c-k+1. Two different omitted classes cannot occur, since 2(c-k+1)+3 > c by c >= 2k-2 and u_h >= c-2k+5 >=3. If every common w met two privates, their shared omitted private would have no W neighbor. Consequently some w meets exactly one private. Its degree forces adjacency to t and every other W.

Strengthening for arbitrary |T|=b. A w meeting two privates and omitting p_i has all C outside U_i and T at distance two, plus z and p_i. Thus u_i >= c-b-k+2, whereas u_i <= |V_i|-b <= k-2-b. Together these would require c <= 2k-4, impossible. No w meets three privates by the exclusive-remote cap. Consequently each common w meets at most one private, for arbitrary b. For b=1, every w must therefore meet exactly one private, t, and every other W. All three private classes have W representatives as above.

For b=1 write S=N(t) intersect C, s=|S|, and let E count remotes visible to exactly two anchors. At each representative w of type i, the distance-two set contains z, the two missed privates, and (V_i minus {t}) union S, via p_i and t. Hence |S minus V_i| <= k-2-|V_i|. The sum over i gives 2s - |S intersect E| <= 3k-8-c-|E|, interpreting E as a set. In particular 2s <= 3k-8-c <= k-6. If t meets q privates then s >= 4-q by its degree. If q=3, all C except t and S and the three anchors are at distance two from t, so s >= c+2-k >= k. If q=2, with omitted class h, t sees all C outside U_h, {t}, S, all three anchors and p_h (via W_h) at distance two, giving s >= c-u_h+3-k >= 4. Thus s >= 3 always, proving c <= 3k-14 and excluding k=9,10,11.

Suppose s=3. Then q=1 by the previous bounds. At t the three anchors and the two missed privates (via the corresponding W) use five distance-two slots, so H={t} union S union (C intersect N_2(t)) has size at most k-1. For every x in S all remote neighbors lie in H, so x needs at least two private neighbors. Hence every x in S is pair-visible. Fix one, omitting class h. Via its two privates x reaches all of C outside U_h within two steps (t is adjacent). Through t it sees all k-4 common vertices at distance two, and through its two privates it sees two anchors. Consequently at most two vertices outside H in C are reachable from x within two steps. Thus c-u_h <= |H|+2 <= k+1, or u_h >= k-3. But a representative w_h sees all S and U_h at distance two, disjoint since every x in S is pair-visible, as well as z and the two missed privates. This forces u_h <= k-6. Hence s >= 4, c <= 3k-16, and k>=14.

Let a_i=|W_i|, R_i=N_C(p_i), H_i its closed C-neighborhood, and H={t} union S union (C intersect N_2(t)). Then |H_i minus R_i| <= a_i+1 by counting the missed W, z and two anchors at distance two from p_i. Also |H| <= k-5+q+s by the anchors and missed privates at t. Call i represented if R_i meets S or t meets p_i. In the former case any x in R_i intersect S has its closed C-neighborhood of size >=k-1 inside H intersect H_i, so |R_i intersect H|>=k-a_i-2. In the latter case R_i lies in H and an exclusive remote has closed C-neighborhood of size >=k inside H_i. If all three classes are represented, summing gives >=2k-2. But the sum is at most |H| + |E| + q-1 <= k-6+2q+s+|E| <= 2k-12+2q-s+|E intersect S| <= 2k-8. Thus some h is absent.

If some x in S is pair-visible, its two private neighbors constitute precisely the classes other than h. Its radius-two ball contains C outside U_h, since these remotes other than t share a private neighbor with x, and t is adjacent to x. At distance two from x are all W and two anchors, so at most two remotes outside H are reached. Therefore c-u_h <= |H|+2 <= k-3+q+s. But w_h sees disjoint S and U_h and its three nonremote distance-two partners so u_h<=k-3-s. This implies c<=2k-6+q, impossible. Thus S is all exclusive; also q cannot be 2, since then already C outside U_h lies in H. In particular |E|+2s+c<=3k-8.

For s=4, every x in S has closed C-neighborhood of size >=k and equal to H, since |H|<=k-5+1+4=k. Thus |H|=k, q=1. Every vertex of the external C-boundary of H is at distance two from x, and at most three exist (count W and x's anchor). There are at least c-k-3-|E| >=9 exclusive vertices outside H and that boundary, since |E|<=k-14. Each such exclusive vertex has >=k-1 C neighbors, all outside H, forcing c>=2k. Substitution in |E|+2s+c<=3k-8 gives k>=16. For s>=5 already k>=16. Hence altogether k>=16.
