# A Hamiltonian nontriple layer and spanning path for the doubled-private single-triple case

Use the doubled-private notation with unique triple-visible remote t. The previous disconnected and cut-vertex cases provide spanning paths. We treat 2-connected F, the induced graph on the other c−1 remotes. Existing bounds give k≥14, 2c≤5k−13, a_P,a_Q≥4, a_B,a_C≥2, and at least one common middle neighbor of t. For each private J let R_J denote its remote neighbors and H_J the closed neighborhood of R_J in the remote layer. The boundary estimate is |H_J|≤|R_J|+a_J+1. For B,C, the private support has at most k−3 members by the visible-remote bound at the corresponding anchor. Every nontriple remote has between one and three private neighbors, no common middle neighbor, and degree at least k. Thus its full closed remote neighborhood has at least k−2 vertices and its degree in F is at least k−4.

Suppose F has an independent set I of size k−3, taking a subset if necessary. Its complement in F has size at most (5k−15)/2−(k−3)=(3k−9)/2. Each member has at least k−4 neighbors in this complement. Since 2(k−4)>(3k−9)/2 for k>7, pairs in I have a common F-neighbor. For x∈I with supporting private J, the other k−4 members of I, the a_J common middles incident to J, and the anchor of J are all distinct vertices at distance two from x. The latter claims use the absence of edges from x to a common middle or anchor. Thus a_J≤3, ruling out both P and Q. For each remaining support J=B,C meeting I, |H_J|≤(k−3)+3+1=k+1. Take x∈I∩R_J. Its full closed remote neighborhood is contained in H_J and misses (I∩R_J)−{x}. Its size is at least k−2. It follows that |I∩R_J|≤4. The two supports therefore cover at most eight vertices, contradicting k−3≥11. We obtain α(F)≤k−4.

Moreover |F|+2≤(5k−11)/2≤3(k−4) for k≥13. Therefore δ(F)≥max(α(F),(|F|+2)/3). By the 1971 Nash-Williams theorem, F has a Hamiltonian cycle. The precise classical theorem is stated in [Büyükçolak et al., p. 11](https://bilmuh.gtu.edu.tr/~dgozupek/Gozupek_DAM19.pdf).

To absorb the root and the middle, use a common neighbor w of t. If w meets C, start t,w,C. Otherwise select a common neighbor w' of C adjacent to w and start t,w,w',C. This selection is possible even using only a_C≥2 because the complement of W is a matching. Continue from C through its anchor, the root, the anchor of B, and B. To ensure B still has an available common neighbor, use the strengthened bound a_B≥3 from the strict support inequality. Alternatively dispense with the strengthening: when w misses C, if deleting w and w' could exhaust all of N_W(B), both would have to meet B, contradicting that w' meets C and no common middle meets both B,C. When w meets C, removing only w also causes no issue. In the remaining common vertices choose a neighbor of B and a different neighbor of P, possible since at most two vertices were used and a_P≥4. Traverse all remaining common vertices from the chosen B neighbor to the chosen P neighbor. Such a path exists with any prescribed endpoints: the remaining graph on m≥8 vertices has minimum degree ≥m−2, hence is Hamilton-connected by Ore's Hamilton-connected degree-sum condition. Continue through P, its anchor, Q and any solo q of Q, then traverse the Hamiltonian cycle of F cut at q. This is a spanning path of the original graph.

The argument uses no upper bound on k. Combining it with the inherited disconnected and cut-vertex proofs settles the doubled-private triangle with exactly one triple-visible remote at every allowed order.
