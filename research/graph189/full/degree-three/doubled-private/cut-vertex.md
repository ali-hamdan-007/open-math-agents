# A cut vertex in the nontriple remote layer gives a spanning path

Assume the doubled-private triangle with T={t}; k>=11 by doubled-solo.md. Write F=G[C minus {t}]. The disconnected case was proved separately; suppose r is a cut vertex of connected F. Write c=|C|, U for single-anchor remotes, and a_i for the number of W-neighbors of private i. Each exclusive vertex belongs to the external remote boundary of some foreign private, since its closed remote neighborhood has size at least k-1>|V_i|. Using |D_i|<=a_i+1 and the fact that W meets at most two privates gives |U|<=sum_i(a_i+1)<=2k-4. On the other hand |U|>=2c+1-sum_i |V_i|>=2c-3k+9. Thus 2c<=5k-13.

For a private touching two components of F-r, the closed F-neighborhoods of the ports each have size >=k-3 and can meet only at r. This would require |H_i|>=2k-7>2k-8. All supports therefore belong to a single component off r. Each component needs at least two: a component with only one support would consist of solo remotes, each having at least k-3 neighbors inside, yielding at least k-2 vertices while one support has at most k-3 nontriple ports. Hence there are exactly two components X,Y with two of the four private supports in each. Every support has >=3 solos, so after deleting r each of its classes still provides at least two distinct candidate endpoints in the corresponding component.

In either component, minimum degree is >=k-4: outside a component there are at most two private neighbors, t and r. Each component contains a solo, whose closed neighborhood there has at least k-2 members. Consequently the component order m is at most c-k, since the two deleted remotes and the other component already use k vertices. We have 2(k-4)>=m+3: indeed m<=c-k<=(3k-13)/2 and k>=11 suffices.

Each of the two component paths must connect its two private classes. Since r has at least k-4 neighbors in F-r, one component has at least four neighbors of r. Assign r to such a component. If t has a W neighbor, plan to append t after W; otherwise t has >=k-5 neighbors in F-r, so some component has at least two such neighbors. In the latter case select two neighbors for inserting t in that component first. Then select two neighbors for inserting r in its assigned component, disjoint from the t pair if assigned there. These one or two pairs form a matching. Choose a solo endpoint for each private class such that the endpoints are not the two ends of the same matching edge: after choosing one endpoint, its matching block has at most one member of the other class and that other class has at least two candidates.

For each component add a marker adjacent to its chosen endpoints and insert each vertex assigned to the component (r and possibly t) with only its selected pair of neighbors. There are at most m+3 vertices in this auxiliary graph. For every pair of originally nonadjacent component vertices the degree sum is >=2(k-4), at least the auxiliary order. Bondy-Chvatal closure allows adding all edges within the underlying component. In the resulting clique, order the blocks of the prescribed matching with the block of one endpoint first and that of the other last, oriented with the respective endpoint outside. This gives a clique path containing all matching edges; subdividing those edges by their assigned vertices and closing the cycle through the marker gives a Hamiltonian cycle. Closure yields a Hamiltonian cycle in the unaugmented-edge auxiliary graph, hence the desired endpoint path.

When the component pairs are P,b and Q,c (up to symmetry), join them as q,Y,c,anchor C,z,anchor B,b,X,P,anchor A,W. When they are P,Q and b,c respectively, use P,X,Q,anchor A,z,anchor B,b,Y,c,anchor C,W. Each route uses both component paths exactly once. The complement of G[W] has maximum degree one, so W has a Hamiltonian cycle. End its path at a W-neighbor of t if t was reserved, and append t. This proves traceability.
