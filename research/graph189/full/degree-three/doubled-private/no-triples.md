# Doubled-private triangle with no triple-visible remote

Write P,Q for the two private middles with anchor A, and B,C for the singleton privates of the other two anchors, using R_J for the remote neighbors of private J, H_J for its closed neighborhood in the induced remote graph, and a_J for its common neighbors in W. Write n_R for the number of remotes, at least 2k-3. The anchor visible sets have sizes at most k-2,k-3,k-3, respectively (each foreign private is distance two). Thus every exclusive class has at least two members. As usual |H_J minus R_J|<=a_J+1 from the k-4-a_J missed W and three nonremote partners at J. For singleton privates B,C an exclusive remote has closed remote neighborhood of size >=k contained in H_J, whereas |R_J|<=k-3. Hence a_B,a_C>=2.

Suppose T empty. Every w needs at least two private neighbors, since it has no remote neighbors. No w can touch B and C: all remotes outside U_A would then be distance two, counting also z and at least one missed private A-support if there is one, and n_R-|U_A|+2>=k+1. If all four privates are met then all remotes are at distance two, impossible. Similarly w cannot touch P,Q and B (or C): all remotes outside U_C (or U_B) would then be distance two besides z and the missed singleton, which exceeds k. Thus w meets exactly two privates and is complete to all other W. Its two remote-support sets have union size at most k-3 by z and its two missed privates. Two complementary two-private choices therefore cannot occur, as their four supports cover all n_R>=2k-3 remotes. Since B and C are each used but never together, their partners must be the same doubled-class private, say P. Any additional W cannot meet Q with B or C, since that would be complementary to an existing edge. Thus every W is adjacent to P and exactly one of Q,B,C.

The bounds on the four H_J are all at most 2k-8. Indeed a_Q<=k-8 since a_B,a_C>=2, and |R_Q|<=k-2. For J=B,C one has a_J<=k-6 and |R_J|<=k-3. For P, a_P=k-4 but |R_P union R_B|<=k-3, and R_B minus R_P contains the >=2 exclusive B remotes, giving |R_P|<=k-5. In each case the claimed bound follows by adding a_J+1. Moreover R_P is nonempty: otherwise P would need edges to all three other privates to attain degree k, putting all remotes at distance two from P.

Choose x in R_P and let K be its closed remote neighborhood, D the external remote boundary of K, L the remaining remote vertices. All W and at least one anchor are at distance two from x, so |D|<=3. We have |K|<=|H_P|<=2k-8 and hence |L|>=2. Every nontriple remote touches at most three privates and has closed remote neighborhood of size >=k-2. If a support R_J met both K and L, it would contain ports whose closed remote neighborhoods lie in K union D and L union D, respectively. Their union in H_J would have size >=2(k-2)-3=2k-7, impossible. Thus each private is used on at most one side.

At least two supports touch L. Otherwise L is exclusive to one satellite J, and its degree gives |L|>=k-3. If J touches W, the common vertex with neighbors P and J sees all L, x, z and its two missed privates, exceeding k. The only satellite that might miss W entirely is Q. In this case Q cannot be adjacent to B or C, else their >=2 exclusive remotes together with all W, z and the foreign anchors exceed the cap at Q. Thus Q's degree forces an edge to P and |R_Q|=k-2, whence R_Q fills the entire visible set of A and contains x. It cannot touch L.

Hence K uses at most two supports, one of them P. If its other support is a satellite J touching W, the common vertex attached to P and J sees all K at distance two beside z and its two missed privates; |K|>=k-2 makes this impossible. Otherwise K is covered by P and at most Q and x itself has at most those two private neighbors. Then |K|>=k-1 but all K belong to the visible set of A of size at most k-2. This contradiction excludes T empty.
