# Removing two nontriple remotes cannot disconnect a nontraceable singleton-T example

Assume the doubled-private triangle and T={t}, k>=11. Let F be the induced nontriple remote graph and suppose F-S is disconnected for S={r1,r2}. Use the boundary order bound 2c<=5k-13 from doubled-cut-vertex.md, where c counts all remotes. A private touching two different components would put two closed F-neighborhoods of size at least k-3 into H_i, overlapping only in S. This only gives 2k-8; instead count the full closed C-neighborhoods, of size >=k-2, which can meet in at most S union {t}. Thus |H_i|>=2k-7>2k-8. Each private is confined to one component off S. Each touches such a component since it has at least three solo remotes. A component with only one private would be all solo, with minimum degree there at least k-4. Its order would be at least k-3, exceeding the number of nontriple ports of any one private: for b,c their anchors see at most k-3 remotes including t; for P,Q at most k-5 nontriple ports occur by the three solos of the twin and |V_A minus T|<=k-3. Thus there are exactly two components X,Y, each covered by two private supports.

Every component has minimum degree >=k-5, and contains a solo which has at least k-4 neighbors there (one private, t and S outside). Each order is at least k-3. Hence any component order m<=c-k<=floor((3k-13)/2). This gives 2(k-5)>=m+2 for k>=11 and >=m+3 for k>=12.

The triple t has a W neighbor: otherwise every w needs two private neighbors, and the singleton-T all-double case in doubled-solo.md is impossible. Reserve t to append after W. Each r in S has at least k-5>=6 neighbors outside S in F since it has at most three private neighbors and possibly t. Assign the two r to different components in which they have at least two neighbors if this is possible. If impossible, both have the same unique eligible component X. Then each has at least five neighbors in X, so disjoint neighbor pairs can be reserved there. Moreover the other component Y receives at most two edges from S in total. Its two supports have at least six solos jointly before deleting S and at least four in Y afterwards. Some solo in Y avoids S, so its degree forces |Y|>=k-1. In this case the bound on |X| improves to c-k-2, giving 2(k-5)>=|X|+3 even for k=11.

The reserved neighbor pairs form a matching in each component. Choose endpoints from the two solo classes in different matching blocks. Each class has an available endpoint; at least one of the two classes has two or more, since it would take four deletions to leave both with only one. Thus choose a representative of the smaller class first and avoid its matching block when choosing from the larger class. Augment the component with an endpoint marker and the assigned r vertices (only the reserved pair of neighbors for each). The preceding bounds ensure its order is at most 2(k-5), even when both r vertices are assigned to the same component. Bondy-Chvatal closure completes the underlying component to a clique. Ordering the matching blocks with endpoints in the first and last distinct blocks, and subdividing the prescribed edges, gives a Hamiltonian cycle via the marker. Therefore the original incident-edge graph has a spanning path with the selected endpoints containing its assigned vertices.

Join the two component paths by the relevant one of the two private/anchor routes in doubled-cut-vertex.md. End the W path at a neighbor of t and append t. Hence deleting two vertices of F can never disconnect F in a nontraceable graph of this template.
