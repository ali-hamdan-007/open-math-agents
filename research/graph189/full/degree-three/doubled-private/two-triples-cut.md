# Two triple-visible remotes when the nontriple remote layer has a separator of size at most one

**Theorem.** In the doubled-private triangle with precisely two triple-visible remotes, assume $k≥26$. If the nontriple remote graph $F$ is disconnected or has a cut vertex, the whole graph has a spanning path.

Use the setup and arbitrary-triple port lemmas of *A spanning path for the doubled-private triangle with a fixed triple layer*. In particular $P,Q$ share an anchor, $B,C$ each have their own, and the common middles $W$ have size $k-4$. Write $T$ for the two triples, $c$ for the total remote order, $R_J$ for a private's remote neighbors and $H_J$ for their closed remote neighborhood. The three visible remote sets have sizes at most $k-2,k-3,k-3$ and $2c≤5k-14$. Each private has at least three solos. We also use the bound $|H_J|≤2k-8$. For twins this follows from $|R_J|≤k-5$, $a_J≤k-4$ and boundary size at most $a_J+1$. For singletons $|R_J|≤k-3$ and $a_J≤k-6$ since $B,C$ have disjoint common neighborhoods of sizes at least two.

Let $S$ be empty if $F$ is disconnected and otherwise consist of a cut vertex; let $s=|S|≤1$. If a private touched vertices in two components of $F-S$, each one's closed full remote neighborhood would have at least $k-2$ vertices and their intersection would lie in $T∪S$. Their union in $H_J$ would thus have at least $2k-4-(2+s)≥2k-7$ members, a contradiction. Each private is represented in a component, since it has at least three solos. A component with only one supporting private would consist of solos, with at most one private, two triples and $s$ separator vertices as neighbors outside. Its order would be at least $k-2-s$, exceeding the $k-4$ maximum number of nontriples seen by its supporting anchor. Hence there are exactly two components $X,Y$, with a distinct pair of supports in each.

In each component select one solo for each of its two private classes as prescribed path endpoints; at least two candidates per class remain. Every component vertex has at most two supporting privates, hence local degree at least $k-4-s$. Using a solo shows its component order is at least $k-2-s$. Subtracting the other component and $2+s$ remotes from $c$ gives the upper bound $m≤c-k≤(3k-14)/2$ on either component order.

Call a triple high if it has at least six common-middle neighbors. Each other triple has at least $k-11$ neighbors in the two components together, subtracting at most five common middles, four privates, the other triple and $s≤1$ separator vertices. A separator vertex has at least $k-5$ neighbors there. Assign each low triple and the separator vertex, when present, to a component where it has at least eight neighbors (since $\lceil(k-11)/2\rceil≥8$). Reserve two distinct neighbors there for each assigned vertex, avoiding the two marked endpoints and all earlier reservations. There are at most three assignments, so at most six vertices are already unavailable.

For each component form an auxiliary graph containing its original edges, a marker adjacent to its two endpoints, and each assigned vertex adjacent only to its two reserved neighbors. It has at most $m+4$ vertices. Two nonadjacent base vertices have degree sum at least $2(k-5)≥m+4$ for $k≥14$. Successive Bondy–Chvátal closure steps may therefore complete the base component to a clique while preserving Hamiltonicity of the auxiliary graph. In that clique order the disjoint reserved pairs as consecutive blocks between the two unreserved marked endpoints, including all unused vertices as singleton blocks. Subdivide each pair by its assigned vertex and close through the marker. This cycle certifies a Hamilton cycle before closure; removing its marker gives a path between the two prescribed endpoints. [Bondy and Chvátal, *A method in graph theory*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(76)90078-9).

Orient the component paths to match their private ports. In the split case label them so that $X$ connects $B,P$ and $Y$ connects $Q,C$. Concatenate $Q,Y,C,A_C,z,A_B,B,X,P,A$. When $X$ connects $P,Q$ and $Y$ connects $B,C$, use $P,X,Q,A,z,A_B,B,Y,C,A_C$. The last vertex is an anchor in both cases.

Reserve disjoint pairs in $W$ for the at most two high triples, possible since each has at least six choices. Form an oriented three-vertex block for each such triple with its reserved neighbors on the ends; all other common middles are singleton blocks. Each common middle misses at most two others, by counting its possible external neighbors. Two blocks can occur consecutively in either order whenever each tail is adjacent to the other's head. At most four other blocks fail this test for any given block. The number of blocks is at least $k-6≥20$, and their compatibility graph has minimum degree at least its order minus five. Dirac's theorem supplies a Hamilton cycle; cut it and expand the blocks. Either end begins with a common middle adjacent to the last anchor of the initial path. Appending the block path completes the spanning path.
