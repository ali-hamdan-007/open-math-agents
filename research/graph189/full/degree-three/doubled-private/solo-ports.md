# Both doubled privates need three solo exclusive ports

In the doubled-private triangle of doubled-empty.md let T be arbitrary. Write E for the set of remotes whose only middle neighbor is P. Suppose |E|<=2, and use Q,B,C as three effective supports. Every exclusive x belongs to an external remote boundary of one of Q,B,C. If x is A-exclusive, it has at most two middle neighbors, so its closed remote neighborhood has size >=k-1>|V_A|, giving a neighbor outside V_A supported by B or C. For x exclusive to B or C, its closed remote neighborhood has size >=k and its visible anchor set V_i has at most k-3 members. Among at least three neighbors outside V_i choose one outside E. This neighbor is nontriple and some different effective support touches it, placing x in that support's boundary.

No common w meets both B and C. If it did and missed either P or Q, every remote outside V_A would be distance two through B/C, alongside z and the missed private. If it met all four it would see all exclusive remotes and z. Neither is possible. Nor can w meet P,Q and B (or C), since then all remotes outside V_C (or V_B) are distance two, plus z (and the missed private if necessary). If w meets Q,B it therefore misses P,C. Every remote outside V_C and E touches Q or B and lies at distance two from w, since it is nontriple. Adding z and the two missed privates forces n_R<=k-3+|V_C|+|E|<=2k-4, impossible. The same holds for Q,C. Thus sum of the three effective a's is at most k-4. The three external boundaries have total size <=k-1, contradicting |U|>=k+2. By symmetry each of P,Q has at least three solo exclusive ports.

Consequences: each R_P and R_Q has at most k-5 members, because the other twin's three solo ports belong to V_A but not that support. Hence a_P,a_Q>=4, since their closed remote neighborhoods contain the size-k closed remote neighborhood of a solo port. Also a_B,a_C>=2. Further |H_P|,|H_Q|<=2k-8 even for arbitrary T; |H_B|,|H_C|<=2k-8 since a_B+a_C<=k-4. In particular k>=10 since 2|W|>=sum of the four a's>=12. For k=10, equality forces every w to meet exactly two privates. As |T|<=|V_A|-6<=2, complementary choices are impossible (each nontriple support union has at most k-3 members). The occurring pairs therefore form the star centered at P or Q, because B,C both occur, never together. The three satellite classes would demand at least 4+2+2=8 members of W whereas |W|=6. Thus k>=11.

For |T|=1 this also excludes the branch where every common vertex meets two privates. The complementary-pair obstruction gives the same star centered at P, and all three satellites have representatives. Choose a P-only solo x and form its closed remote neighborhood K, external boundary D and complement L as in doubled-empty.md. Then |K|>=k, |D|<=3, |L|>=2; a private cannot touch nontriple vertices in both K and L since their closed remote neighborhoods would give |H_J|>=2k-7. If K outside T used only P and one satellite J, the P,J common representative would see all its >=k-1 nontriple remotes plus three fixed partners. Thus K uses at least three supports. The nonempty L outside T uses only the remaining satellite h and consists entirely of h-only remotes, forcing |L|>=k-3. But the P,h common representative sees every member of L outside T (>=k-4), all >=3 P-only solos (disjoint), and three fixed partners, a contradiction.
