# A disconnected nontriple remote layer is traceable

Assume the doubled-private triangle with singleton T={t}. Suppose the induced graph F on the nontriple remotes is disconnected. Each nontriple vertex has at most three private neighbors and at most one other neighbor outside F, namely t, so its closed F-neighborhood has at least k-3 members. No private can touch two components: the disjoint closed F-neighborhoods of one port in each would require |H_i|>=2k-6>2k-8. Every component needs at least two private supports, as a component touched by only one has minimum degree >=k-2 and hence size >=k-1, exceeding that private's available nontriple ports. Consequently there are exactly two components with exactly two supports each. They cannot put both duplicate privates together, for that component would have minimum degree >=k-3 and size >=k-2 while anchor A sees at most k-3 nontriples. Relabel the two support pairs as P,b in X and Q,c in Y.

Both components have minimum degree >=k-3 and size >=k-2. Write n_R for the number of all remotes. Their visible-anchor counts give n_R+2<=sum_i |V_i|<=3k-8. Subtracting t and the other component gives |X|,|Y|<=2k-9. In particular any two vertices of a component have degree sum at least 2k-6, exceeding its order by at least three.

Choose in each component endpoints which are solo remotes of its two private classes. In the nontriple components these classes each contain >=3 members (doubled-solo.md and the singleton-class visible set count). To obtain a spanning path with these endpoints, add a marker adjacent to just the endpoints. If t has two neighbors in a component, also include t adjacent just to two such neighbors there, choosing endpoints outside those two. Repeat adding every absent edge within the original component: the degree sum for each pair is at least 2k-6, which is at least the order of the augmented graph. Thus Bondy-Chvatal closure preserves Hamiltonicity throughout. Once the component has become a clique a Hamiltonian cycle exists: reserve one consecutive pair for the marker and one for t if present, and traverse the other vertices. Deleting the marker from a cycle in the original augmented graph proves the required spanning path, including t when assigned.

Concatenate q, the Y spanning path, c, anchor C, z, anchor B, b, the X spanning path, P, anchor A, the W spanning path. If t has at most one neighbor in each component, the degree bound gives at least k-6 neighbors in W; finish the W path at one and append t. The complement in W has maximum degree one because each common vertex has at most two private neighbors and one possible remote neighbor, so W has a Hamiltonian cycle and its path may end at any chosen vertex.
