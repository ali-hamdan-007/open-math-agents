# Two triple-visible remotes with two common-middle neighbors each

In the doubled-private triangle suppose the triple-visible remote set T consists of t1,t2, each having at least two neighbors in the common middle set W. Let F be the induced graph on the c−2 other remotes. If F is 2-connected, the full graph is traceable at every admissible order.

The visible remote sets at the twin anchor and other two anchors have respective sizes at most k−2,k−3,k−3. Let U denote remotes visible from exactly one anchor. Counting visible incidences gives |U|≥2c+2−(3k−8). Each x∈U belongs to an external remote boundary of some private: x has at most two middle neighbors (both privates at its anchor), hence its closed remote neighborhood has at least k−1 members, strictly more than the size of the visible set at its anchor. A neighbor outside that visible set has a supporting private J at a different anchor and places x in the boundary of R_J. The four boundary sizes are at most a_J+1, where a_J counts W-neighbors of J. Each W vertex has at most two private neighbors, so |U|≤2|W|+4=2k−4. Consequently 2c≤5k−14, giving |F|≤floor((5k−18)/2).

For clarity a support bound holds for arbitrary h=|T| in this template. Writing S_J=R_J−T, one has |S_J|+a_J+h+1≥k+1. Indeed a solo has at least k vertices in its closed remote neighborhood, contained in H_J, and |H_J|≤|R_J|+a_J+1≤|S_J|+a_J+h+1. Equality at k would give H_J of size k, all of T inside R_J, and boundary D=H_J−R_J of size a_J+1. The root, two other anchors, the |W|−a_J missed common middles, and the boundary are k distinct distance-two witnesses for J. Every remote m outside H_J has a supporting private with a W neighbor and can be reached in at most four steps through the anchor of J, that common middle and that private. Distance four is excluded by the exhausted even-distance budget; distance one and two are excluded as well. In a shortest three-step path the penultimate vertex must be a boundary vertex: m is nontriple and has neither a W neighbor nor a neighbor in the first two root layers. A solo of J has its entire closed remote neighborhood equal to H_J, so sees all c−k outsiders at distance two via D. It also sees a_J common middles and the anchor of J via J, and one different private via a boundary vertex (every such vertex is nontriple and lies outside R_J). This gives c−k+a_J+2≥k+1 as c≥2k−3 and a_J≥2, a contradiction. The nontriple supports of each twin and each singleton respectively have at most k−2−h−3 and k−3−h vertices, using the visible-remote bounds and three solos of the other twin. Hence a_P,a_Q≥5 and a_B,a_C≥3.

For h=2, if a common middle meets privates I,J, then |S_I∪S_J|≤k−3 by counting these remotes, the root and the two missed privates at distance two. Thus |S_I|≤k−6 by the three solos of J, and the strict support bound gives a_I≥4. Complementary pairs are excluded since |F|≥2k−5 exceeds 2(k−3) and every member of F has a supporting private. If both B,C occur in pairs, they pair with the same twin, say P, and Q,B,C have disjoint W-neighborhoods (B,C never co-occur). Hence |W|≥5+4+4 and k≥17. If neither occurs in a pair, their lone neighborhoods and the neighborhood of P are disjoint, giving |W|≥3+3+5. If only B occurs in a pair, C uses three or more lone common middles, and P,Q,B demand at least 5+5+4 incidences on the rest, yielding |W|≥3+7. Thus always k≥14. At k=14 at least one of B,C occurs only singly, reducing the total incidences in W by at least three below 2|W|. The boundary counting above improves to 2c≤5k−17 in this case.

Every F vertex has at most three private neighbors, no W neighbors, and at most two neighbors in T. Its F-degree is at least k−5 and its closed full remote neighborhood has at least k−2 vertices. Suppose I⊂F is independent of size k−4. Pairs in I share a neighbor, since 2(k−5)>(3k−10)/2≥|F|−|I| for k>10. For each x∈I and supporting private J, the k−5 other members of I, the a_J common neighbors, and the anchor of J all lie at distance two from x. There is another witness. If x sees two anchors through its supports, use the second anchor. Otherwise x has at most two private neighbors and has a remote neighbor outside the visible set of its anchor by the preceding count; a private supporting that neighbor is at distance two from x. The positive even-distance budget k gives a_J≤3. Neither twin can support I, since each has a_J≥4. The remaining supports J=B,C each satisfy |R_J|≤k−3, and if they meet I, |H_J|≤|R_J|+a_J+1≤k+1. For x∈I∩R_J its closed remote neighborhood uses at least k−2 vertices in H_J and misses all other members of I∩R_J. Thus |I∩R_J|≤4 and |I|≤8, a contradiction. Hence α(F)≤k−5. For k≥16 we have (5k−18)/2+2≤3(k−5); for k=15 the rounded |F|≤28 also gives |F|+2≤3(k−5). For k=14 the improved bound gives |F|≤24 and |F|+2≤27. The 1971 Nash-Williams theorem yields a Hamiltonian cycle in F.

For the assembly, the complement of W has maximum degree at most two: its vertices have at most three anchor, two private and two triple neighbors off W and degree at least k, with |W|=k−4. Write N_i=N_W(t_i). No w meets both B,C. Each of B,C has at least three W-neighbors, and each twin has at least five. A chosen W neighbor b of one of B,C meets at most one twin, so one twin J avoids b.

First suppose N_1,N_2 intersect. Choose u in the intersection and v∈N_2−{u}. Start with t1,u,t2,v. If v meets a singleton private X∈{B,C}, put s=v and attach to X there. Otherwise choose X not adjacent to u, and take s∈N_W(X) adjacent to v, possible since this set has at least three members, contains neither u nor v, and v misses at most two common middles. Let Y be the other singleton. There is an unused b∈N_W(Y): in the first case v meets X, and in the second case v meets neither and s meets X, so only u might have been used from N_W(Y). Choose the twin J avoiding b and an unused p∈N_W(J), which is possible since u,v,s use at most three vertices and b avoids J. This initial segment ends at X via s.

Continue X, its anchor, the root, the anchor of Y, Y, b. The remaining W vertices, including b,p, induce a graph on m≥7 vertices of minimum degree ≥m−3, so they are Hamilton-connected by Ore's degree-sum criterion (2(m−3)≥m+1). Traverse them from b to p and continue p,J,the twin anchor,the other twin. End by stepping to a solo remote of that twin and following the F-cycle cut at this solo.

Suppose N_1,N_2 are disjoint. If either contains a neighbor w of a singleton private X, put that triple first (rename it t1) and set s=w. Otherwise take w∈N_1, X=C, and s∈N_W(C) adjacent to w, possible since this support has at least three W-neighbors, all outside both triple neighborhoods. Start t1,w and continue via s to X, using the edge ws when w,s differ. These selected w,s preserve N_2 in full. They use at most one neighbor of the other singleton Y. Choose unused b∈N_W(Y), take a twin J avoiding b, and reserve two different d,e∈N_2. At least two unused candidates for p∈N_W(J) remain after w,s, distinct from b. If b∈{d,e}, choose p outside that pair, possible since the pair has only one vertex different from b. Thus the marked endpoints b,p are never exactly the pair d,e.

Again pass through X, its anchor, the root, the anchor of Y, and Y to b. At least m=8 W vertices remain, including b,p,d,e. On these m vertices add an endpoint marker adjacent only to b,p, and add t2 adjacent only to d,e. Every two originally nonadjacent underlying W vertices have degree sum ≥2(m−3)≥m+2, so Bondy–Chvátal closure permits completing W. In the clique there is a b-to-p Hamiltonian path traversing the edge de: treat de as a block, orienting it suitably if one endpoint of the path is in the block. The two path endpoints are not the whole block. Subdivide de by t2 and close through the marker. Reversing the closure yields a b-to-p path through the original remaining W and t2. Finish through J, the twin anchor, the other twin and the remote cycle as before.

The Nash-Williams theorem used here is stated in [Büyükçolak et al., p. 11](https://bilmuh.gtu.edu.tr/~dgozupek/Gozupek_DAM19.pdf).
