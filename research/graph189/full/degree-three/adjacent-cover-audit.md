# Independent audit of the three-anchor adjacent-cover branch

I checked `../split-ball/three/adjacent-cover.md`. Its handwritten proof works for k≥8 and uses only the distance-two bound, rooted eccentricity at most three at the degree-three vertex z and degree at least k at all other vertices. The covering anchors must be adjacent and must together see every vertex of B=N₂(z). The branch is n>3k, so the separate lower-order theorem is not used in the proof.

## Counting and separation

Write B=L disjoint union M for the common and exclusive middle vertices, and C=E disjoint union T for the exclusive and common remotes. Exclusive M vertices missed by a are at distance two via the covering edge ab, and the missed third anchor is at distance two via z. These witnesses are disjoint from C. Thus |C|+|T|+|M|+h≤2k by summing the two caps, where h counts the missing edges to the third anchor. If one covering anchor missed none of B, then all C would be at distance two from it and n≤2k+4, impossible. Both exclusive middle classes are therefore positive. Since |C|≥2k−3+(k−|B|), the multiplicities listed in the proof exhaust all cases. An E vertex cannot see a common L vertex, the anchors, or z. Its M neighbors can be only on its own side, and there is at least one by distance from z. This shows the required separation without any assumption about 2-connectivity or the diameter away from z.

## Closure thresholds and endpoint assignments

For a component or block of order B₀ and a marker leaf placed at the designated endpoint, the augmented path-closure threshold is B₀. This follows by adding a universal vertex to the augmented graph and applying the ordinary cycle-closure theorem. At both uses only pairs within the designated clique are added, so the marker remains a leaf through every step. Reverse closure, orient the recovered spanning path with that leaf at its required end, and delete it. The other endpoint can change freely.

For the remote block, its order R=|C|+|M|−l is at most 2k−l, where l is the number of transferred vertices. Each E vertex loses at most one neighbor and at most one E vertex loses any neighbor at all. The unaffected E vertices of degree at least k can be made a clique. Their count is at least |C|−|T|−1≥2k−5≥k+1. The possibly affected vertex has some degree ≥k−l, so its degree sum with any clique vertex is at least 2k−l. The closure therefore makes all of E a clique. No special vertices or the marker receive added incidences.

- Multiplicities (1,1). Each exclusive remote class is complete to its own single M vertex and has size at least k−3. Use one port for the starting M vertex and two for the internal M vertex. If T remains remote, it has an existing E port by the transfer criterion. Reserve that port first and choose the other ports distinct afterward. The two exclusive classes are disjoint, and each has at least five available members before the reservation. Put any surplus E vertices on the clique segment between the start port and the first port of the internal special. A T transferred for lack of an E port costs no E degrees.
- Multiplicities (1,2). Equality in the caps gives exclusive class sizes k−2 for the singleton M vertex and k−1 for the two M vertices. At most one double-side vertex can have at most one E neighbor, since the union of the two neighborhoods covers the larger class. If one is transferred, its counterpart has a port, and the singleton side provides two distinct internal ports. If both remain remote, either double-side vertex is suitable as start. If the internal one's neighborhood has at least three elements, remove the chosen start port and select two internal ports. If it has exactly two, choose the start port outside that pair, which is possible because the union has at least three elements. The singleton's ports are in a disjoint class. Any surplus E vertices again fit on the clique segment between the start port and an internal port.

For the core, X=L plus any transferred vertex. Common vertices lose at most |M|+|T|≤3 neighbors, since they have none in E. A transferred M vertex loses at most one E neighbor and the two remaining M vertices; there is no T in that case. A transferred T has no E neighbor and loses at most the two M vertices. With transfer of T, core order is |B|+3≤k+3 and residual degrees in X are at least k−2. Otherwise core order is at most |B|+2≤k+2, and residual degrees are at least k−3. Thus the respective edge sums 2k−4 and 2k−6 reach the closure thresholds. There are at least k−4 members of L because (k−|B|)+|M|≤3. The written path uses two distinct common vertices at the ends of its traverse of closed X. The anchors are adjacent to them by definition, and c−z−b′ are original edges. The core marker forces attachment at a′. The edge to the remote start is an original edge because the start lies in M on the side of a′. This completes the gluing.

The audit found no dependence on arbitrary adjacency of a special to the core clique, nor on a prescribed second endpoint after closure. The source result is handwritten and has not been kernel-formalized.

## Strengthening to k≥4

The following changes lower the threshold of the checked proof to k≥4. In particular they cover the whole adjacent-cover branch with a unique low vertex of degree three. Keep the same notation and the first distance-two count. The proof that m_a,m_b are positive works because 2k+4≤3k. The class sizes and equality cases in the count are unchanged.

**Case (1,1).** Write p,q for the unique supports of their respective exclusive remote classes. When t=0, both classes have at least k−3≥1 members and their sum is at least 2k−3≥5. Use a support with at least two ports as the internal one and the other as the start. A common L vertex cannot see both p and q, since otherwise all |E|≥2k−3>k exclusive remotes, and none of them adjacent to L, are at distance two from it. Hence each L vertex retains degree at least k−1 in the core. Its order is at most k+2, so the marker path-closure threshold is met by all L pairs when k≥4.

When t=1, equality forces |B|=k, both edges from the covering anchors to c, and |C|=2k−3. Each of the two private remote classes has size exactly k−2, since each anchor sees at most k−1 remotes and the common remote vertex consumes one place in each anchor's cap. Call that common remote vertex u. It must have an E-neighbor: otherwise all its neighbors lie in B, and its degree forces it to see all of B. Via p and q it would then see all 2k−4 exclusive remotes and the two covering anchors at distance two, exceeding k. So no T vertex needs to be transferred.

Reserve an E-port for u first. Make the support from that exclusive class the remote start and the support from the other class internal. Both classes have k−2≥2 members, so the required one and two distinct ports are available. The remote path can finish at u. In the core, a common L vertex x seeing neither p nor q retains degree at least k−1. Otherwise it sees all k−2 members of at least one exclusive class at distance two via its support. Every core nonneighbor of x other than x is also at distance two via the covering anchors, because they are complete to L and each is adjacent to c and z. The core order is k+2. Therefore (k−2)+(k+1−d_core(x))≤k, so again d_core(x)≥k−1. The same closure threshold is met.

**Case (1,2).** Let p support the k−2 member class and q,r the k−1 member class. If no vertex is transferred, a common L vertex cannot see all p,q,r since it would see the full E of 2k−3>k vertices at distance two. Its core degree is at least k−2. The core order is k+1, so its marker closure works for k≥5. At k=4 the common L is the singleton {x}, and the core path is c,z,b′,x,a′.

If q is transferred, it has at most one port and r sees at least k−2 of the k−1 member class. Every L vertex seeing both p and r would see at least 2k−4 exclusive remotes and z at distance two, exceeding k. Therefore each x∈L loses at most one neighbor in the remote block and retains degree at least k−1. The transferred q cannot see both p and r either. Through them q would see at least 2k−5 members of the two remote classes at distance two, having removed its at most one port. It also sees z and the covering anchor it misses at distance two through its own covering anchor. The resulting 2k−3 exceeds k. Thus q loses at most one other M neighbor and at most one E-neighbor and has core degree at least k−2. Since there is only one transferred vertex, every sum tested for closure is at least 2k−3. The core order is k+2, so closure works for k≥5. At k=4 write L={x}. If xq exists use c,z,b′,x,q,a′. Otherwise the core degree of q is at least two, so besides its own anchor a′ it sees c. The core degree of x is at least three, so besides the two covering anchors it sees c as well. In this case use z,b′,x,c,q,a′. Every edge in either path is original.

**Remote closure for all these cases.** There is no affected E vertex except possibly the single neighbor of the transferred q in the last case. Every E-pair therefore has degree sum at least 2k−l even before edge additions, since at most one endpoint has degree k−l and all others retain degree k. This meets the remote threshold R≤2k−l without a bound on clique size during closure. The port choices in the original (1,2) proof work for k≥4 because the classes have sizes k−2≥2 and k−1≥3. Where a core uses a singleton L and no transfer, its unique vertex directly joins the two covering anchors. The original leaf and gluing argument applies unchanged in the remaining cases.
