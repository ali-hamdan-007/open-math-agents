# Three anchors with an adjacent covering pair

Let G be a finite simple connected graph, let k≥8, suppose at most one vertex has degree below k, and every vertex has at most k positive even-distance partners. Let z have degree three and eccentricity at most three. Write its neighbors as a,b,c, B=N₂(z), C=N₃(z). Suppose ab is an edge, B⊆N(a)∪N(b), and n>3k. Then G is traceable. Only the bound on distance-two partners is required.

Put b₀=|B|, d=k−b₀≥0, L=B∩N(a)∩N(b), and M=B−L. Let m_a,m_b count the vertices in B missed by a,b respectively. Both are positive, since otherwise every C is within two of one anchor, giving n≤2k+4≤3k. Members of C are visible to an anchor if they are at distance two. Every member of C is visible to a or b. Write T for those visible to both and E=C−T, split into E_a,E_b by their unique covering anchor. Put h=1[ac absent]+1[bc absent]. The two anchor caps give

|C|+|T|+m_a+m_b+h≤2k.

Indeed, the missed B vertices are distance two via ab, and the missed third anchor is distance two via z. Since |C|≥2k−3+d, we have m_a+m_b+|T|+h+d≤3. Write t=|T|. The only multiplicities (up to exchanging a,b) are (1,1) with t≤1, and (1,2) with t=h=d=0. Every vertex of E has all neighbors in E∪M∪T, and its B neighbors lie precisely on its own side of M.

Partition the graph into a core and a remote side. The core starts with z,a,b,c,L. The remote side starts with E,M,T. Transfer at most one special vertex ℓ from M∪T to the core as follows.

* For (1,1), write p,q for the two vertices of M. Each is adjacent to every vertex of its own exclusive class. Both classes have size at least k−3, since the other anchor sees at most k−1 members of C and |C|≥2k−3. If t=1 and its vertex has no E neighbor, transfer it to the core. Otherwise make no transfer.
* For (1,2), write p for the singleton on one side, q,r for the pair on the other. The two exclusive classes have sizes k−2 and k−1, respectively, paired with p and with q,r. This follows because C has size 2k−3 and both anchor capacity bounds are equalities. Transfer from q,r any vertex having at most one E neighbor. At most one qualifies because their E-neighborhoods cover their full class of size k−1.

Write l=0 or 1 for the number transferred. We will join a remote spanning path starting at one of its M vertices to a core spanning path ending at the corresponding covering anchor.

**Remote closure.** The remote side has order R=|C|+|M|−l≤2k−l. In it, every vertex of E retains degree at least k−l. At most one vertex of E has lost a neighbor since the vertex transferred has at most one E-neighbor. Fix the starting M vertex as specified below. Add a leaf adjacent only to that starting vertex. In the graph of order R+1, a nonedge can be added without changing traceability whenever the current degree sum is at least R. This is the usual path form of Bondy–Chvátal closure, obtained by adding a universal vertex and applying cycle closure. First fill the E vertices that have lost no neighbors into a clique, since their degrees are at least k. This clique has size at least |C|−t−1≥2k−5≥k+1. Thus each member has degree at least k, and every missing edge to the possible remaining E vertex can be added. After closure E is a clique. The added leaf forces the resulting spanning path to start at the chosen M vertex when we remove it after reversing closure.

In the following constructions a port is an E neighbor of a special vertex. Distinct ports allow arbitrary traversal of all remaining vertices in the E clique between any two selected port vertices.

* For (1,1), start at p. Use one port for p and two for q in their disjoint exclusive classes. If the T vertex remains remote, use it as the terminal special, choosing its one port first. Each exclusive class has at least k−3≥5 vertices, so the other ports are still available. In the absence of a terminal T vertex, finish in E after traversing q.
* For (1,2), if one of q,r was transferred, start at the remaining one; traverse p internally using two ports in its own class. If both remain remote, select one of q,r as the starting vertex and traverse the other internally. There are distinct ports for these two vertices, one for the start and two for the other: both neighborhoods have size at least two and their union has size k−1≥3. In fact either choice of start works, by choosing its port outside the internal vertex's neighborhood when that neighborhood has size two and the two sets differ. The p ports belong to a disjoint class.

In all constructions form the path through the specials in the indicated roles, using edges of the E clique between the selected ports. Insert any unused E vertices between consecutive ports from distinct specials (or after an internal special on the final E segment). The leaf is put immediately before the starting vertex. Thus we obtain the desired spanning path in the original remote graph.

**Core closure.** Call the anchor adjacent to the remote starting vertex a' and the other covering anchor b'. Put X=L together with the possible transferred vertex. Every common vertex has lost at most |M|+t≤3 neighbors: none sees E. The transferred vertex, if from M, lost at most one neighbor in E and at most two other special vertices; if from T, it had no E neighbors and lost at most two M vertices. Thus every member of X has degree at least k−3 in the core. If (1,1) transfers its T vertex, the core order is at most k+3 and every member of X retains degree at least k−2. In all other cases the core order is at most k+2. Add a leaf adjacent only to a'. The path closure cliques X, since in these two cases respectively 2k−4≥k+3 and 2k−6≥k+2. There are at least b₀−|M|≥k−4≥2 members of L. Start at c, follow z,b', traverse X starting and ending at distinct members of L, then take a' and the leaf. Reverse closure and delete the leaf to obtain a core spanning path ending at a'. Its edge to the chosen M vertex joins the two spanning paths.

The proof permits diameter greater than three as long as the specific z has eccentricity at most three. The checks for k=5,6,7 in exhaust.py instead impose diameter at most three globally, so these are different hypotheses.
