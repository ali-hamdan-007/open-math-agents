# Graph 189, six exceptional ports and empty triple-visible remote layer

This continuation closes the omitted-star case with at least two distinct omissions for integer k ≥ 9. In the rooted degree-three setting, k is the second-smallest degree and every vertex other than the root has degree at least k. The Graph 189 definition of distEven counts the vertex itself at distance zero. Its bound k+1 therefore allows at most k *other* vertices at positive even distance. The argument below uses this bound at distance four.

Use the notation and reduction in [six-ports-partial.md](https://github.com/ali-hamdan-007/open-math-agents/blob/research/parallel-lean-results/research/graph189/full/degree-three/six-ports-partial.md). The exact statement is [FormalConjectures.WrittenOnTheWallII.GraphConjecture189](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/9097e2f90e66d2f6244209cb1c07f7120d17ec53/FormalConjectures/WrittenOnTheWallII/GraphConjecture189.lean).

The triangular anchors see six exceptional ports A,B,C,D,E,F with masks 1,2,4,3,5,6. Each of the k−6 common middle vertices in W sees all three anchors. No remote can meet W because such a remote would be visible from all three anchors. Every remote therefore touches an exceptional port, and the root and anchors have no remote neighbor. There are c ≥ 2k−3 remotes. Write R_P for the set of remotes touching P, and H_P for its closed neighborhood inside the remote layer.

By the inherited reduction, each common middle vertex omits exactly two exceptional ports. Since |W|=k−6≥3, there is at least one omitted pair. In the star case with at least two distinct omitted pairs, the common endpoint is a singleton, say A. Every other port meets some w, because only A belongs to every omitted pair. The possible pairs are AB, AC, AF. Thus both D and E meet every w and have nonempty supports, as proved in the reduction.

For completeness, each anchor has at most k−3 visible remotes because its three missed exceptional ports are already at distance two via the anchor triangle. At least c−2(k−3)≥3 remotes avoid the visible sets of anchors 2 and 3. They can meet only A. For any such A-solo s, its remote closed neighborhood has size at least k by its degree bound, and is contained in H_A. Port A misses all k−6 members of W, sees them at distance two via anchor 1, and also sees the root and the two other anchors at distance two. Hence |H_A|−|R_A|≤3. As R_A lies in the visible set of anchor 1, |R_A|≤k−3. The inequalities force |H_A|=k, |R_A|=k−3, and R_A equals the entire visible remote set of anchor 1. In particular R_D and R_E lie inside R_A. The closed remote neighborhood of every A-solo is exactly H_A. The distance-two layer of A consists precisely of W, the root, the two other anchors, and the three remotes in H_A outside R_A. These disjoint sets already total k.

Take any remote m outside H_A and a supporting port P. We have P≠A, so some w touches P. The walk from A through anchor 1, w and P to m has length four. Distance one and two are excluded by the preceding descriptions. Distance four is excluded because the entire positive even-distance budget of A is spent at distance two. Therefore m is at distance three from A. The penultimate vertex on a shortest path cannot be in W, be the root, or be an anchor, as none has remote neighbors. It belongs to H_A outside R_A. Consequently every remote outside H_A touches one of these three vertices.

Fix an A-solo s. It is adjacent to all three vertices in H_A outside R_A, so all c−k≥k−3 remotes outside H_A lie at distance exactly two from s. Four further vertices occur at that distance. Anchor 1 is reached via A. Each of D and E is reached via a member of its nonempty support inside R_A, necessarily distinct from s because s is solo. Finally take any vertex g in H_A outside R_A and any exceptional port J supporting it. Since R_D and R_E lie inside R_A, J differs from A,D,E. Thus s reaches J via g. None of these four vertices is a neighbor of s, whose only nonremote neighbor is A. This gives at least k+1 vertices at distance two from s, a contradiction.

This proof replaces the proposed continuation using separated remote sets K,L and a small boundary. No claim about which side contains a support is needed in the multi-omission star argument. The single-omission star and the omitted triangle are covered separately in the inherited note. The conclusion concerns only this six-port, no-triple-visible template under the full Graph 189 hypothesis.
