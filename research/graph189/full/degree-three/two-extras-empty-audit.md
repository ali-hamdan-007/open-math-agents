# Audit of the five-port triangle templates with empty T

I checked both mask lists (1,2,3,4,5) and (1,1,2,4,6). The visible-set caps are respectively (k−2,k−3,k−3) and (k−3,k−3,k−3). A remote has at least one port and at most three, since the allowed union of its port masks has at most two anchors. Its closed remote neighborhood has at least k−2 vertices, improved to k−1 or k when it has at most two or one port.

Every common middle meets exactly three ports. Degree gives at least three, while four ports leave at most k−2 remotes unseen and thus exceed the distance-two cap after counting the root and the omitted port. The three touched supports have union at most k−3. The missed set has at least k remotes supported only on the omitted pair. If the omitted pair's masks share an anchor, its missed set is contained in a visible set of size at most k−2. Hence the masks are disjoint. Removing one port's support from the missed set gives at least k−|R_J| solos of the other port. Two disjoint omitted pairs cannot occur because their complementary touched triples would cover all remotes and have combined capacity at most 2k−6.

For an omission triangle the allowed-mask pairs give exactly the listed triples, and in both templates each satellite gets at least three solos. The anchors of the universal double D contribute two separate distance-two vertices at any chosen x∈R_D, and all W contribute another k−5. Hence at most three vertices lie in the external remote boundary of K=N_F[x]. The degree estimate makes R_D nonempty: with no remotes a universal double needs three other ports, whose supports leave at most k−2 remotes uncovered. The boundary bounds imply that every port's closed support neighborhood has size at most 2k−8. Two closed remote neighborhoods centered on opposite sides can overlap only in the external boundary, so their size at least 2k−7 rules out the same port meeting both sides.

The far side L must use at least two ports. A sole port would force |L|≥k−3 by the solo degree bound, while a touched triple containing that port and D would cover L together with x, a contradiction. The near side K hits every omitted pair because otherwise the complementary touched triple would see all K, whose size is at least k−2. Thus K uses D and two satellites. L uses the other universal port and the remaining satellite J, and there is a touched triple containing those two ports and D. In particular this triple sees L and x, forcing |L|≤k−4. No member of L can be solo, and the resulting saturation makes |L|=k−4. The three J-solos lie in the external boundary, giving |R_J|≥k−1 and contradicting the visible cap.

For an omission star its center has a solo by the missed-set count and a=0 since all common middles miss it. All centers except A in the first template are excluded by the closed-support bound: the singleton cap for all those singletons is k−3, and the cap for doubles loses the B- or C-solos. In the remaining A-star both AB and AC occur. A's solo forces its own support to fill V_1, so R_D and R_E are subsets of R_A. The only ordinary support disjointness used is R_B∩R_E=∅ and R_C∩R_D=∅. Both follow because the corresponding mask unions are all three anchors. Since both universal double supports are nonempty, the common cap gives |R_B|,|R_C|≤k−4 and their closed support neighborhoods have size at most 2k−8. No port can cross the cut from K to L. If neither B nor C occurs in K, then K lies in R_A and x has at most two ports, since D and E cannot share a remote. This gives |K|≥k−1>|R_A|. If at least one occurs in K, L has only the other available, contradicting its solo degree bound in the same way as above. All counts remain valid with overlaps other than the specifically forbidden mask unions.
