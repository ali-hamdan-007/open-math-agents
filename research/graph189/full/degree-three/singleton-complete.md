# The singleton triple-visible remote is impossible

In the triangle template with d=1 from residual.md, write C for the remote layer, c=|C|>=2k-2, W for the k-4 universal middle vertices and p_i for the three private middle vertices. Let V_i be the remotes visible to anchor i, U_i the remotes visible only to i, and T the remotes visible to all three. Each |V_i|<=k-2, since the anchor also sees both foreign privates at distance two. Each |U_i|>=c-2k+4>=2. Every non-T remote sees only private middle vertices, and their classes are exactly its visible anchors. All arguments use only the degree lower bound and the distance-two cap.

Every p_i meets W. Otherwise its at most k-2 remote neighbors and its one anchor neighbor force a private neighbor p_j. The k-4 common vertices, z, and two foreign anchors are all at distance two via anchor i, and all |U_j|>=2 exclusive remotes are at distance two via p_j, contradicting the cap.

Every common w meets at most one private, for arbitrary T. If w meets two, say p_j,p_l, and misses p_i, all C outside U_i and T lie at distance two, in addition to p_i and z. Thus c-|U_i|-|T|+2<=k. Since |U_i|+|T|<=|V_i|<=k-2 this implies c<=2k-4. If w meets all three, all exclusive remotes are at distance two; there are at least 2c-sum |V_i|>=k+2 such remotes.

Now suppose T={t}. Each w misses at least two privates. From its three anchor neighbors, at most k-5 other W, at most one private and at most one remote, its degree forces equality throughout. Thus W is a clique partitioned into nonempty W_i by its unique private neighbor, and t sees all W. Set a_i=|W_i| and S=N_C(t), s=|S|. Since t has at most k-1 nonremote neighbors, s>=1. Choose a representative w_i for each class. Its distance-two set includes z, its two missed privates and (V_i minus {t}) union S. Consequently |S minus V_i|<=k-2-|V_i|. If E denotes the set of exactly-two-visible remotes, summing gives 2s-|S intersect E|<=3k-8-c-|E|, whence 2s<=k-6.

Choose x in S, let K be its closed neighborhood in the induced graph G[C], and D the vertices outside K with a neighbor in K in G[C]. Put L=C minus (K union D). Every member of D is distance two from x, as are all k-4 common vertices through t and at least one anchor through a private. Thus |D|<=3. The set K is inside the closed radius-two ball of t in G, restricted to C. All three anchors are at distance two from t, hence |K|<=1+s+k-3=k-2+s. So |L|>=k-3-s>0. There are no edges from K to L. Moreover S is disjoint from L because t lies in K.

Write R_i=N_C(p_i) and H_i for its closed neighborhood in G[C]. Every vertex in H_i minus R_i is at distance two from p_i, as are its k-4-a_i missed common vertices, z, and the two other anchors. Therefore |H_i|<=|R_i|+a_i+1<=k-2+(k-6)+1=2k-7 because the other two common classes are nonempty. If R_i contained non-t vertices in both K and L, the closed C-neighborhood of each would have at least k-1 members since each has at most two middle neighbors. These neighborhoods lie respectively in K union D and L union D and meet in at most |D|<=3 vertices. Their union in H_i would have size >=2k-5, impossible.

At least two privates touch K outside t. If x has two private neighbors this follows immediately. Otherwise |K|>=k from the degree of x, and covering its at least k-1 non-t vertices needs two privates since each |R_i|<=k-2. Thus at most the third private p_h can touch L. Every member of L is therefore exclusive to h and has at least k-1 remote neighbors, all in L union D, so |L|>=k-3. Yet w_h sees disjoint L and S at distance two, along with z and its two missed privates. Hence |L|+s<=k-3, contrary to s>=1.
