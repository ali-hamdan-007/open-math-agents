# The path-centered mask-6 family with a single triple-visible remote

Let z be the degree-three root, of eccentricity at most three, of a graph with order n>3k, second-smallest degree k, and at most k other vertices at positive even distance from any vertex. Label the neighbors of z by 1,2,4, with edges 2–1–4 and no edge 2–4. The middle has k vertices: the four ports A,B,C,D with masks 1,2,4,6 and the k−4 common middles W. Let R be the remote graph of order c≥2k−3. Suppose that t is its unique remote visible (at distance two) from all three anchors. If F=R−t is 2-connected and k≥16, the original graph has a spanning path.

Write V_i for the visible set at i, R_J for the remote neighbors of port J, H_J for their closed neighborhood in R, and a_J=|N_W(J)|. The central anchor sees B,C,D at distance two, giving |V_1|≤k−3; each leaf sees the other leaf and A, giving |V_2|,|V_4|≤k−2. At least c−(|V_2|+|V_4|−1)≥2 remotes are visible from anchor 1 only, and at least three are visible from each individual leaf only. Such remotes meet only their singleton port. A nontriple has no W neighbor, at most three port neighbors, and if it meets A, at most two port neighbors. Hence every nontriple has closed remote neighborhood of size at least k−2, improving to k for solos and k−1 for nontriples on A.

For J=A,B,C the boundary H_J−R_J has size at most a_J+1; for D the bound is a_D+2. Indeed each missed common middle is at distance two, as is the root. Port A also sees both leaves at distance two. Port B also sees the center and either D (if BD is absent) or the opposite leaf (if BD is present); apply symmetry to C. Port D also sees the center. In particular a_A≥2, a_B,a_C≥1 by the solo neighborhood sizes, and |R_D|≤k−5 by the three B-solos outside D in V_2.

Each w meets at most two ports. If it meets exactly three, all nontriples outside those supports have only the omitted port and there are at most k−3 of them: they are contained in a visible set of size ≤k−2 and exclude t. Of the at least 2k−4 nontriples at least k−1 are then at distance two from w, alongside z and the omitted port. Four ports are excluded in the same way by the coverage of all nontriples. Thus the complement of W has maximum degree one, by the degree of w and its at most three anchor, two port, and one triple neighbors. The sum of the four remote boundary sizes is at most 2(k−4)+5=2k−3. Write U for the single-anchor remotes. Each is a solo with a closed remote neighborhood of size at least k, strictly larger than its anchor's visible set. A remote neighbor outside this set is nontriple, with a port not supporting the solo. The solo thus belongs to the boundary of some port. Counting anchor incidence gives 2c+1−|U|≤3k−7, hence 2c≤5k−11.

We need a_A≥3. Equality a_A=2 forces |R_A|=k−3, H_A of size k and boundary of size three. Thus R_A equals V_1 and contains t. From A the positive even budget is exhausted at distance two by the root, both leaves, the k−6 missed common middles, and the three boundary remotes. Every remote outside H_A has distance at most four from A: use the central anchor, a leaf anchor, and a supporting leaf port (outside R_A there is no A-support, and the remote is nontriple). Its distance cannot be one, two or four; on a shortest three-edge path the penultimate distance-two vertex must therefore be a boundary remote (the outside remote has no W neighbor and no anchor/root neighbor). Every A-solo has closed remote neighborhood equal to H_A, and sees all c−k outsiders at distance two. It also sees the central anchor and two common neighbors via A, and a different private port via any boundary remote, since all such remotes are nontriple outside R_A. The c−k+4≥k+1 witnesses contradict the bound.

Consider the graph L=F when t has at least two W-neighbors, and otherwise L=R. In F the minimum degree is at least k−4. In the second case F-vertices have degree ≥k−3 in R, and t has degree at least k−4 in R: with at most three port neighbors and one W neighbor this follows by degree. If t meets all four ports then every F vertex is adjacent to t or at distance two from it, and its three anchors are also at distance two, giving d_R(t)≥c−k+2. In particular t has at least two neighbors in F and R is 2-connected. In both cases L is 2-connected, has minimum degree ≥k−4, and its order satisfies |L|≤floor((5k−11)/2)≤3(k−4)−2 for k≥16 (use the integer rounding at 16).

Suppose I is an independent set of L of size k−3. Any pair shares a neighbor since 2(k−4)>|L|−(k−3). For any nontriple x∈I and supporting port J, x sees k−4 other members of I, all a_J common neighbors of J and an anchor of J at distance two, and at least one more vertex. Indeed if x sees multiple anchors, use the second one. If it sees a unique anchor then it is a singleton solo and has a neighbor outside that anchor's visible set, with a different supporting port. Therefore a_J≤2. This excludes A. If D supports any member of I∩F, |H_D|≤(k−5)+2+2=k−1. Taking such a member x, its closed remote neighborhood of size ≥k−2 misses every other member of I supported by D, so at most two are in I∩R_D. Outside D the remaining nontriple members meet only B,C. For either of these supports meeting that remainder, |H_J|≤(k−2)+2+1=k+1. A member in the remainder has at most two ports and hence closed remote neighborhood size ≥k−1, so at most three members there use J. There are thus at most eight nontriple members and at most one triple in I, fewer than k−3. Consequently α(L)≤k−4. By the 1971 Nash-Williams theorem (2-connected, minimum degree at least the independence number and (|L|+2)/3), L has a Hamiltonian cycle. See [Büyükçolak et al., p. 11](https://bilmuh.gtu.edu.tr/~dgozupek/Gozupek_DAM19.pdf).

Cut the cycle to finish at a B-solo; continue through B, leaf 2, D, leaf 4, z, center 1 and A. Traverse W from some A-neighbor p to a different C-neighbor q and finish at C. If L=R this is possible since a_A≥3 and a_C≥1 and W is Hamilton-connected by its complement-degree bound. If L=F, reserve two distinct W-neighbors d,e of t. First choose q, then p≠q with {p,q}≠{d,e}, possible by a_A≥3. To find the W-path including t, augment W by an endpoint marker adjacent only to p,q, and by t adjacent only to d,e. Every missing underlying W pair has degree sum ≥2(|W|−2)≥|W|+2, so Bondy–Chvátal closure completes W. In the clique order the block de in a p-to-q spanning path, possible since p,q are not both ends of the block. Subdivide de by t and close through the marker. Reversing closure gives the required path in the original graph, completing the proof.

## Removing the connectivity assumption

In fact the result holds with no connectivity assumption on F. Select L by the same W-degree rule. Its bound δ(L)≥k−4 used no connectivity. The proof above applies whenever L is 2-connected. Otherwise let S be empty if L is disconnected, or a singleton {s} consisting of a cut vertex. Every component of L−S has minimum degree at least k−4−|S|, and therefore order at least k−3−|S|≥k−4. There are exactly two components X,Y since 3(k−4)>floor((5k−11)/2) for k≥16.

The nontriples in each component use at least two distinct port types. Indeed the component has nontriples by its order. If all use only J, each has just one middle neighbor and a closed full remote neighborhood of size at least k. All members of that neighborhood except possibly s and t are nontriples in the component, giving at least k−2 such vertices. But every port has at most k−3 nontriple neighbors by its anchor's visible bound, which includes t. Thus each component has at least one eligible endpoint port other than A. In the union of the two components both B and C occur, as each has at least three exclusive solos and only one vertex is ever removed. Therefore distinct eligible non-A ports may be selected in the two components (failure would make each candidate set the same singleton). Choose a nontriple remote incident to each selected port in its component.

If S is empty, each component of order m has δ≥k−4 and m≤floor((5k−11)/2)−(k−3)≤2(k−4), so each is Hamiltonian. For S={s}, choose two neighbors of s in one component, possible since d_L(s)≥k−4. Each component has order m≤floor((5k−11)/2)−1−(k−4)=floor((3k−5)/2). Hence 2(k−5)≥m+1, including k=16 by rounding. Augment the assigned component with s keeping just these two neighbors. Bondy–Chvátal closure completes the underlying component to a clique (every missing pair has degree sum at least m+1), and this clique has a cycle through the selected edge, which can be subdivided by s. The augmented component is Hamiltonian. The other component is Hamiltonian by Dirac with the same bound. In either case, cutting each cycle at an edge incident to the chosen remote gives a spanning component path ending there, with s absorbed exactly once if present.

Use one of the following connections between the chosen endpoint ports, reversing the paths if required:
```
B – 2 – D – 4 – z – 1 – A – W – C
B – 2 – z – 1 – A – W – C – 4 – D
C – 4 – z – 1 – A – W – B – 2 – D
```
Each traverses W between A and a singleton leaf port. Its prescribed endpoints and, when L=F, the insertion of t are handled exactly as in the connected proof. Joining the component paths to the indicated endpoint ports completes a spanning path of the original graph.

## Improved threshold k≥12, still without connectivity assumptions

Write e=5k−11−2c. Because every exclusive remote is in at least one port boundary, and the sum of the boundary sizes exceeds |U| by at most e, at most e exclusives belong to two or more boundaries. Let x be a nonexclusive remote incident to at least three ports. Every exclusive neighbor of x belongs to at least two boundaries (the exclusive has only one supporting port). Since the number of other nonexclusive remotes is c−|U|−1≤3k−9−c, one obtains
```
d_R(x) ≤ e + 3k−9−c = 8k−20−3c.
```
If |L|>3(k−4)−2, then c≥3k−13, and k≥12 implies 3c>7k−16. The displayed bound is then strictly below k−4. No nontriple can have three ports, since such a nontriple is nonexclusive and has d_R≥k−3. Also when L=R, t cannot have three or more ports because we proved d_R(t)≥k−4 in that case. Consequently δ(L)≥k−3. Since c≤floor((5k−11)/2)≤3(k−3)−2 for k≥11, the two size cases always give |L|≤3δ(L)−2.

The independence argument still rules out a stable set of b=k−3 vertices. Neighborhood intersection holds directly from 2(k−4)>floor((5k−11)/2)−(k−3) for k≥12. The D support accounts for at most two members, and A is excluded. Among remaining nontriples, if a B-only solo occurs, the closed remote neighborhood of this solo has size at least k in H_B of size at most k+1, so at most two members are supported by B. The same holds if a C-only solo occurs. If both occur this bounds the total remainder by four. If one occurs but the other does not, every member in the remainder belongs to the first one's support. If neither occurs, all remaining members have both B and C and there are at most three, by the previous bound. Including t if present gives |I|≤2+4+1=7, a contradiction.

For 2-connected L the same Nash-Williams test applies. In all other cases let δ=δ(L). After deleting at most one cut vertex each component has at least δ vertices and there are exactly two by |L|≤3δ−2. In the cut case its order m satisfies m≤|L|−1−δ≤2δ−3, enough to close the component augmented by s since its underlying minimum degree is ≥δ−1. In the disconnected case m≤|L|−(δ+1)<2δ suffices directly. All endpoint and W-path constructions above hold unchanged for k≥12 (|W|≥8). This proves traceability throughout the path-centered mask-6, single-triple template for k≥12.
