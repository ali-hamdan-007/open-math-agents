# Small orders k=9,10,11 in the single-triple, path-centered mask-6 family

Retain the hypotheses, notation, support bounds, route, and graph L from singleton-triple.md. This addendum, together with the k≥12 section there, settles every k≥9, without a connectivity assumption. Write g=k−4, N=|L|. We use Bondy's theorem that a 2-connected graph is Hamiltonian if the minimum degree sum σ₃ of an independent triple is at least max(N+2,3α), with infinite σ₃ if there is no such triple. See [Bauer et al., survey, Theorem 12 on p. 4](https://people.scs.carleton.ca/~kranakis/conferences/icdcn-papers/toughness-survey.pdf).

The established bounds are 2k−3≤c≤floor((5k−11)/2), δ(L)≥g, and d_R(x)≤M=8k−20−3c for each nonexclusive remote x meeting at least three ports. Recall that every three-port nontriple has the three leaf ports B,C,D, while a single-anchor remote is a solo. If M<k−3, no nontriple has three ports, so those vertices have R-degree ≥k−2 and F-degree ≥g+1. If M<g, the low-W choice L=R also has d_R(t)≥g+1: d_R(t)≥g already precludes t meeting three ports. The following exhaustive rows record the degree improvements; unmarked lower degrees are still available by the general estimates.

| k | c | nontriples improved | low-W t improved |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | 15 | no | no |
| 9 | 16,17 | yes | yes |
| 10 | 17 | no | no |
| 10 | 18 | yes | no |
| 10 | 19 | yes | yes |
| 11 | 19,20 | no | no |
| 11 | 21,22 | yes | yes |

For any independent set I of b=k−3 in L, pairs share a neighbor: in the unmarked rows 2g>c−b, also for the row (10,18); in the rows with both improvements 2(g+1)>c−b. Apply the support argument from the earlier note to its nontriple members, obtaining a_J≤2, hence no A support, at most two independent D members, and at most four independent members outside D. In fact at most five members are nontriple. If there are two D members, each has its closed remote neighborhood in H_D of order at most k−1, missing the other. Its size is therefore at most k−2, so its middle degree must be at least three. Both are B,C,D-ports. In this case every nontriple member of I belongs to B or C. When both solo classes occur, each of B,C contains at most two members (closed neighborhood of solo ≥k, |H_B|,|H_C|≤k+1). When only the B-solo class occurs, every nontriple outside D is supported by B, and so are the two D members, giving at most two; the C case is symmetric. If neither occurs, the outside-D members all have both B and C. If one exists, its closed remote neighborhood has size ≥k−1 and excludes every other member in the B support, giving at most three for the entire set of nontriples; otherwise there are just the two D members. This proves the asserted bound five.

For k≥10 it follows α(L)≤g since at most one further member can be t. For k=9 this gives α(F)≤5 when L=F; for L=R an independent six-set must contain t. If t has one W-neighbor w, then t sees at least two common middles at distance two via w (|W|=5, its complement is a matching, and t has only one W neighbor). Its three anchors and its five other independent remote vertices already add eight, impossible. Thus α(R)≤5 in this case. If t has no W-neighbor then δ(R)≥6: at most three port neighbors give it this degree, and meeting all four gives d_R(t)≥c−k+2 as before. Furthermore α(R)≤6 since a seven-set would include six independent nontriples. Therefore in all cases α(L)≤δ(L).

For L=R each nontriple has degree at least g+1 and t has degree at least g, so σ₃≥3g+2. In the rows where both improve this bound is ≥3(g+1)+2. These bounds give σ₃≥c+2 row by row (the (10,18) case uses 3g+2=20). For L=F, N=c−1≤3δ(F)−2 by the table except (9,15), where N=14, g=5. In that case suppose three independent vertices all have F-degree 5. Each must meet B,C,D and t by the degree k=9 assumption; each has R-degree at least 6. In the M bound on one of these vertices, two of the theoretically available nonexclusive neighbors are the other two independent members and must be removed. Thus its R-degree is at most M−2=72−20−45−2=5, impossible. Consequently every independent triple has degree sum at least 16=N+2. This proves Bondy's criterion whenever L is 2-connected.

For disconnected L or a graph L with a cut vertex use the same two-component argument as in the main note. Here are the component size checks at the small orders. For L=R put j=g in the unmarked and singly marked rows and j=g+1 in the doubly marked ones. We have c≤3j, δ(R)≥j, and all vertices except possibly t have degree ≥j+1. After deleting a cut vertex each component contains a non-t vertex and has size at least j+1. There are exactly two, each of order m≤c−1−(j+1)≤2j−2. Inside each component at most one vertex has degree below j, and that exception has degree ≥j−1. Every missing pair has degree sum at least 2j−1≥m+1, so the completion after adding s with two reserved neighbors works. If L is disconnected use empty S, giving even stronger degree and size bounds. For L=F the general estimate N≤3δ−2 settles all rows except (9,15). In this row a component of F−s of order five would consist of five vertices of F-degree exactly five (degree lower bound five, only four internal neighbors and s). Every such vertex meets D, whereas |R_D|≤k−5=4. Thus each component has at least six vertices and there are just two, each of order m≤14−1−6=7. Inside minimum degree ≥4 gives pair degree sum ≥8≥m+1. If disconnected, each component already has size at least six and order ≤8, and is Hamiltonian by Dirac. The existence of endpoint ports and the middle routes require only the previously proved solo counts, so apply unchanged.

Finally, the W augmentation works directly by closure for k≥10, where m=|W|≥6 gives 2(m−2)≥m+2. For k=9 low-W L=R, W is Hamilton-connected as before. For high-W L=F, if the degree of t into W is at most three, at most one W edge is missing: two missed edges must be disjoint, and every endpoint of a missing edge must touch t to reach degree k with three anchors, at most two ports and at most |W|−2 W neighbors. Reserve the missing pair for insertion of t if one exists, and otherwise reserve any two of its W-neighbors. The W graph is a clique apart from this possible reserved edge: the clique block ordering of the main proof directly lifts after subdividing that edge by t. If t has at least four W-neighbors, any path through the five common middles has consecutive neighbors of t. Choose prescribed distinct A and leaf-port endpoints and any W spanning path connecting them (Hamilton-connected by Ore); insert t between consecutive neighbors. This completes the proof at k=9.
