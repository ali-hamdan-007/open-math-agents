# Two remote component paths in the six-port singleton-triple triangle

Assume the degree-three triangular-anchor Graph 189 template with six exceptional ports of masks \(1,2,4,3,5,6\), \(k-6\) common middles \(W\), and one triple-visible remote \(t\). Let \(F\) be the induced nontriple remote graph. Every vertex other than the root has degree at least \(k\), each vertex has at most \(k\) positive even-distance partners, and the graph has order greater than \(3k\). If \(k\ge34\) and \(F\) is disconnected or has a cut vertex, then the full graph has a Hamilton path.

Write \(d\) for the number of vertices of \(F\) visible to exactly two anchors. No nontriple remote meets a common middle, and each has at most three port neighbors. Each anchor misses three ports, which already lie at distance two via the anchor triangle, hence has at most \(k-3\) visible remotes. Summing anchor visibility (the triple contributes three and each counted double contributes two) gives
\[
 2k-4\le |F|\le3k-12-d,\qquad d\le k-8,\qquad \delta(F)\ge k-4.
\]
A nontriple visible to just one anchor is a solo of the corresponding singleton port: it has no other port neighbor.

## Two components and distinct solo endpoints

If \(F\) is disconnected let \(Z=\varnothing\). Otherwise let \(Z=\{s\}\), where \(s\) is a cut vertex. Every component of \(F-Z\) has minimum degree at least \(k-5\), and at least \(k-4\) when \(Z=\varnothing\). Thus component orders are at least \(k-4\) and \(k-3\), respectively. Three components in the cut case would already have total order at least \(3k-12>|F|-1\), and three in the disconnected case would have total order at least \(3k-9>|F|\). There are therefore exactly two components. In both cases each component \(X\) has order at most \(2k-9-d\), by subtracting the lower bound for the other component and \(|Z|\) from the bound on \(|F|\).

Each component contains a solo because it has at least \(k-4\) vertices, while \(d\le k-8\). The same singleton port cannot have solos in both components. Indeed, for a singleton port \(P\) with \(a_P\) common neighbors, its total remote support \(R_P\) has size at most \(k-3\), and the external boundary of that support in the full remote graph has size at most \(a_P+3\). The boundary vertices are distance-two witnesses at \(P\) in addition to the root, the two other anchors, and the \(k-6-a_P\) missed common middles. Its closed support neighborhood therefore has size at most \(2k-6\). Two solos in different components would give two closed remote neighborhoods in it, each of size at least \(k\), overlapping only possibly in \(\{s,t\}\), for a union of size at least \(2k-2\). Choose a solo \(x\) in the first component, a solo \(y\) in the second, and their distinct singleton ports \(P,Q\).

## Cycles through the components, the separator and possibly the triple

If \(t\) has two common neighbors, reserve such a pair for \(t\) in the common layer. Otherwise assign \(t\) to a component with at least two neighbors in it. In the latter case \(d_F(t)\ge k-7\), since only six ports and at most one common middle lie outside \(F\) among its possible neighbors. Also assign \(s\), if present, to a component with at least two neighbors in it, using \(d_F(s)\ge k-4\). A suitable component exists for each assignment with only two components and with the possible removal of \(s\) from \(N_F(t)\).

If both vertices need to be assigned, assign them to different eligible components when possible. Otherwise they have the same unique eligible component and each has at least \(k-9\) neighbors there, subtracting at most one neighbor in the other component and at most the other assigned vertex. In this case choose disjoint pairs of neighbors for the two insertions. Thus the reserved pairs within each component form a matching.

For a component \(X\) add the assigned vertices with only their reserved incident pairs, forming an auxiliary graph on \(|X|+q\) vertices, with \(q\le2\). It suffices to apply Bondy–Chvátal closure to make the underlying component a clique. If \(d\ge3\), all underlying pairs have degree sum at least \(2k-10\ge |X|+q\), because \(|X|\le2k-9-d\). If \(d=0\), all underlying vertices are solos and each has degree in its component at least \(k-3\), losing at most its support, \(s\), and \(t\). This again gives the needed closure inequality. If \(d=1\) or \(2\), any pair involving a solo has degree sum at least \((k-3)+(k-5)=2k-8\ge |X|+q\). Add all missing edges incident to solos first. Every remaining vertex now has at least \(|X|-d\) underlying neighbors, so every remaining pair can be completed, since \(2(|X|-d)\ge |X|+q\) from \(|X|\ge k-4\). This works for both components.

In each completed component clique choose a Hamilton cycle traversing each matching block as a consecutive pair, and subdivide those pairs by their assigned vertices. The resulting cycle is Hamiltonian in the completed auxiliary graph, and closure gives a Hamilton cycle in the original auxiliary graph. Thus in the full graph we have two disjoint cycles covering all nontriples and also \(t\) if assigned to a component. Both selected solos are on their respective cycles.

## Insert remaining ports and join through the common middles

Apart from \(P,Q\), classify each of the four remaining ports \(J\) by its number \(a_J\) of common neighbors. For \(a_J\le9\) insert it in a remote cycle. Its support \(X_J\) among the two remote cycles has size at least \(k-a_J-8\): outside the cycles it can neighbor at most \(a_J\) common middles, two anchors, five other ports, and possibly \(t\) if \(t\) was reserved in \(W\). The external boundary of \(X_J\) inside the union of these cycles is at most \(a_J+4\); each such remote is at distance two from \(J\), in addition to the root, at least one missed anchor, and the \(k-6-a_J\) missed common middles. Orient the two cycles. Distinct members of \(X_J\) have distinct successors, and any successor outside \(X_J\) belongs to that boundary. Hence there are at least \(k-2a_J-12\ge4\) eligible edges on which to insert \(J\). Choose different eligible edges for all the low ports and subdivide. Neither oriented edge entering \(x\) or \(y\) is eligible for these low ports, since \(x,y\) each have only their reserved singleton as a port neighbor. Cut the expanded cycles at these entering edges to give two spanning paths beginning at \(x\) and \(y\).

For each high port, with \(a_J\ge10\), choose a pair of common neighbors, all pairs disjoint and avoiding the pair reserved for \(t\) when applicable. At most eight common middles are excluded when the final high pair is chosen, leaving at least two choices. Make an oriented block from each chosen pair with its port or \(t\) between, and make each unused common middle a singleton block. Every common middle misses at most three others: it has at most three anchors, six ports and \(t\) outside \(W\), with degree at least \(k\). Make blocks compatible if the tail of either is adjacent to the head of the other. Distinct heads and distinct tails imply each block is incompatible with at most six others. There are at least \((k-6)-5=k-11\ge23\) blocks. Their compatibility graph thus satisfies Dirac's degree bound and has a Hamilton path, which lifts to a path through \(W\), all high ports, and \(t\) if reserved there.

Let \(A,B\) be the anchors of \(P,Q\), and \(C\) the third. Follow the first remote path in reverse to \(x\), then traverse \(P,A,z,C\), the common-layer path, \(B,Q,y\), and the second remote path. The anchors touch all common middles and the root touches all anchors, so the connections exist. All vertices are used exactly once.

## Independent threshold refinement for the separator case

The separator conclusion holds for every $k≥31$. Keep the two solo endpoint ports $P,Q$, the two genuine remote cycles obtained by reversing closure, and the placement rule for $t$ from the preceding proof. Sort the four remaining ports in decreasing order of their common-neighbor counts $a_J$. Choose the largest $q∈\{0,1,2,3,4\}$ for which each of the first $q$ counts is at least $2q+2$, allowing $q=0$.

Assign the first $q$ ports disjoint pairs of common neighbors. At the last assignment at most two common middles are reserved for $t$ and at most $2q-2$ have been used for earlier pairs, leaving at least two choices. If $q=4$, all ports have been placed. Otherwise every remaining port has $a_J≤2q+3$: if the next count were at least $2q+4$, it and all preceding counts would qualify for $q+1$. The number of eligible original remote-cycle edges for each remaining port is consequently at least
$$
 k-2a_J-12≥k-4q-18≥4-q.
$$
The last inequality needs $k≥22+3q$, which holds at $k=31$ because $q≤3$. Assign distinct eligible edges greedily to the $4-q$ ports. Each cut edge contains a solo of $P$ or $Q$, so neither cut consumes an eligible edge. Subdivisions and the final path use exactly the preceding construction.

For clarity the earlier component estimates still hold at the new endpoint. A component has at least $k-4≥27$ vertices. If the separator and triple require the same unique eligible component, each has at least $k-9≥22$ candidate neighbors there, enough for disjoint pairs. For $d=1,2$ the final closure inequality $2(|X|-d)≥|X|+2$ holds since $|X|≥27$; the other closure cases use only the already established bounds on $|X|$ and its internal degrees. The common-middle compatibility graph has at least $k-11≥20$ blocks and minimum degree at least its order minus seven, sufficient for Dirac's theorem. This proves the claimed $k≥31$ separator threshold. Together with the $k≥32$ result for 2-connected $F$, the six-port singleton-triple template is traceable for every $k≥32$ without any connectivity assumption on $F$.
