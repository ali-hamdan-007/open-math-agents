# A saturated independent set gives a spanning path

**Theorem.** Let G be a finite connected simple graph whose second-smallest degree is k. Suppose each vertex has at most k other vertices at positive even distance. If an independent set I of size k+1 has all its pairs at distance two, then G is traceable.

Write Y=V∖I and m=|Y|−k, so n=2k+m+1. When m≤1, Easwar–Johari's Theorem B already gives traceability for n≤2k+2; this includes m≤0. For the rest, m≥2. Let i₀ be the unique vertex of degree below k when it belongs to I, and otherwise choose any i₀∈I. Then all k vertices of H=I∖{i₀} have degree at least k.

**Structure of Y.** For every i∈I, the other k vertices of I exhaust its even-distance allowance. In particular it has no distance-two vertex in Y. For adjacent x,y∈Y this implies N(x)∩I=N(y)∩I: adjacency of i to exactly one would give distance two to the other. Partition Y according to these I-neighborhoods, called profiles. Each profile has a nonempty I-neighborhood by connectedness: an entire component of G[Y] has the same profile, and every such component has an edge to I. Different profiles have no edges between them.

Two distinct nonadjacent vertices of Y are at even distance. For the same profile they share a neighbor in I. For different profiles, a shortest path joining them alternates between Y and I. An internal Y–Y edge could be removed: every maximal run of consecutive Y vertices along such a path lies in a single profile; an adjacent I vertex sees every member of the run, so one keeps just one Y vertex, choosing the endpoint if the run contains one. The same shortening works if the path has both endpoints in different profiles, as it must then use at least one I vertex. Hence all nonneighbors of y in Y, except y, are at positive even distance from y. It follows that y has at least |Y|−1−k=m−1 neighbors within its profile. Every profile therefore has size at least m, and every profile of size m is a clique.

A high vertex h∈H, all of whose neighbors are in Y, misses at most |Y|−k=m elements of Y. If it misses any profile, that profile has size exactly m and is a clique, and it sees all other profiles. For any profile of size m, some vertex in it has degree at least k (there is at most one low vertex and m≥2). That vertex has only m−1 neighbors in Y and sees at most one member of I outside H. Thus all vertices of that profile see at least k−m members of H. Any profile of size exceeding m is complete to H.

**When 2m<k.** Choose a neighbor y₀ of i₀. Starting at y₀, extend a path within its profile to exactly m vertices. This is possible since its internal minimum degree is m−1. Delete these m vertices from Y, leaving Y' of size k. The balanced bipartite incidence graph on H,Y' has minimum degree at least k−m in each part: each h missed at most m vertices originally, and every remaining y has the H-neighbor bound just proved. Because 2(k−m)>k, the Moon–Moser bipartite Ore condition gives a Hamilton cycle. Equivalently, any missing cross edge has endpoint degree sum at least k+1 and one may apply balanced bipartite cycle closure. The last of the m selected vertices has an H-neighbor h. Delete an edge of the cycle at h to start the spanning path there. Prefixing i₀ and the m selected vertices gives the desired path.

**When 2m≥k.** The |Y|=k+m vertices form at most three profiles.

- With one profile, all H sees every Y vertex. Take the same prefix of m vertices from a neighbor of i₀. Its last vertex sees every h∈H, so continue directly with h₁,y₁,h₂,y₂,…,hₖ,yₖ for arbitrary enumerations of H and the remaining Y'. This also works for k=1, with just the single edge h₁y₁. The same argument applies whenever no profile is missed by H.
- Suppose there are two profiles and one is missed by H. Call it C. It is an m-clique, while the other profile D has size k≥m. If k=m, both are k-cliques. Some i∈I sees both since G is connected. Reserve this vertex as a connector and assign the other k vertices of I to any adjacent clique. Each clique receives at most k. In a k-clique together with up to k independent vertices complete to it, there is a spanning path with a clique endpoint, by alternating the independent vertices with distinct clique vertices and putting the remaining clique vertices together. Join the two clique endpoints through the connector.
- Still with two profiles, suppose k>m. Then D is complete to H. If i₀ sees D, all I sees D; traverse C, continue with any I-neighbor of C, then alternate through the k members of D and the remaining k members of I. If i₀ misses D, it sees C. Start at i₀, traverse C, enter an H-neighbor of its last vertex and then alternate through all k members of H and D. Such an H-neighbor exists because k−m>0.
- With three profiles, their size lower bound gives 3m≤k+m, so 2m=k. All are m-cliques. Every h∈H sees at least two of them, since its degree is at least 2m. Each clique sees at least m members of H. Pick h₁∈H and two cliques it sees. Pick h₂≠h₁ seeing the third clique, which is possible since m≥2; it also sees at least one of the first two. Thus h₁,h₂ link the three groups in an order, using one independent vertex for each link. Assign the 2m−1 other members of I to adjacent cliques with capacities m, m−1, m in that order. Hall's theorem on the individual capacity slots applies: any collection whose union of adjacent cliques includes two has at least 2m−1 available slots, and a collection restricted to one clique consists only of the possible vertex i₀, which has at least one slot. Interleave the assigned independent vertices in each clique. At an end clique one clique endpoint is needed and its capacity is m; in the middle both endpoints must be clique vertices and its capacity is m−1. The two reserved links now concatenate the three paths.

This covers all cases. The Moon–Moser criterion used above is the classical Hamiltonicity condition for balanced bipartite graphs with each missing cross pair having degree sum greater than the part size. All other constructions are given explicitly.

## Consequence at n=δ+2k+1

Let δ be the minimum degree and suppose G is 2-connected. If α(G)≤k, put H'=G plus a universal vertex and c=κ(H')≥3. The Chiba–Furuya–Ozeki–Tsugaki–Yamashita threshold from order-bound.md is still met at this order because replacing α≤k+1 by α≤k saves c−2≥1. Hence G is traceable. If α≥k+2, some independent pair has distance at least three and its neighborhoods together with the independent set require n≥δ+2k+2, impossible. Thus a remaining graph has α=k+1. The theorem above deals with any such independent set having all pairs at distance two.

Consequently, in a remaining graph, *every* independent I of size k+1 contains some x,y at distance at least three. Their neighborhoods are disjoint and avoid I. At this order all the inequalities are equalities, giving a partition V=I⊔N(x)⊔N(y), with degrees δ and k in either order. If δ<k, every such far pair must contain the unique minimum-degree vertex. In particular every maximum independent set contains it. Easwar–Johari handle the case where G is not 2-connected.
