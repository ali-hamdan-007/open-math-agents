# Every minimum-degree-two graph satisfying Conjecture 189 is traceable

Let G be a finite connected simple graph of minimum degree two. Write k for its second-smallest degree and suppose each vertex has at most k other vertices at positive even distance. Then G has a Hamilton path.

Take z of degree two with neighbors a,b. We may suppose G is 2-connected, by Easwar–Johari, Theorem B. When k=2, G has diameter at most three: an endpoint of a distance-four pair would have a distance-two layer of size at least two, since that layer separates it from its opposite endpoint, and a nonempty distance-four layer. This exceeds its positive even-distance bound. The checked Graph189LowDegreeTwo.traceable_min_two_sigma_two handles k=2, with orders at most five covered by Easwar–Johari. Hence take k≥3; all vertices other than z now have degree at least k.

We use two earlier lemmas from the upstream Graph 189 reduction. Under the full even-distance hypothesis, the diameter is at most five, and the endpoints of a distance-five pair cannot both have degree at least k. These are the geodesic bounds in WrittenOnTheWallII.GraphConjecture189.conjecture189.R1.lean (lemmas five_endpoints and diameter_le_five). The two new upper bounds below treat eccentricity at z directly.

**Eccentricity at most three.** The kernel-checked rooted extension in rooted/Graph189DegreeTwoRooted.lean gives n≤3k−1 for k≥4 and n≤3k for k≥3. It takes the hypothesis ∀x, dist(z,x)≤3 and covers both incidences of a,b. To adapt the earlier diameter-bound proof, the nonadjacent-anchor case groups remote vertices by anchor distance two or greater than two, permitting distances four and higher for the latter group. For k=3 this gives n≤9 and for k=4 n≤11; both are within Easwar–Johari's range. For k≥5 use the traceability theorem up to n≤3k−1 in degree-two.md.

**Eccentricity four.** Write B,C,D for the respective distance-two, three and four layers at z. One has |B|+|D|≤k. Each anchor sees only z, optionally the other anchor, and B. Thus each misses at most 1+𝟙[ab is an edge]−|D| members of B. If either anchor sees all B, every C is at distance two from it, and n=3+|B|+|D|+|C|≤2k+3, giving traceability. This includes the nonadjacent-anchor case and the case |D|≥2. Otherwise a,b are adjacent, D={f}, |B|=k−1, and the anchors miss distinct single members of B. Name the private vertices p,q and the remaining vertices common, each adjacent to both anchors.

For k≥4 suppose n≥3k. Then |C|≥2k−3. Call a remote in C exclusive to p or q if its neighborhood in B consists precisely of that private vertex. Every remote has a neighbor in B. The two sets of remotes at distance two from the respective anchors cover C. Each has size at most k−1 because the opposite private vertex uses one distance-two slot. A remote in only one of the two sets is exclusive to the corresponding private vertex. Hence at most one remote is in both sets. If none is in both, each of the k−3≥1 common vertices has no neighbor in C and must see all k−2 other vertices of B to reach degree k. It then sees both p and q, hence all of C and also z at distance two, impossible.

Thus the intersection has exactly one member t and |C|=2k−3. The two exclusive classes P and Q each have size k−2. A common vertex c sees at most t in C and so needs at least k−3 of the other k−2 vertices of B. It sees at least one of p,q. Seeing both would give 2k−4 exclusive remotes and z at distance two, exceeding k. Exchange labels to have c see p and miss q. It must then see t to reach degree k. The vertices z,q and all k−2 members of P are at distance two from c and exhaust its allowance. Therefore t has no neighbor in Q, as that would put a further vertex at distance two from c. Each y∈Q must have a neighbor in P: in their absence its possible neighbors are q, f and k−3 others of Q, only k−1 vertices. Consequently all Q are at distance two from p through P. Together with z and the anchor missing p these account for k distance-two vertices at p. But the singleton-layer vertex f, which has degree at least k, can have neighbors only in C, of which just k−1 are outside P. Hence f sees P and is one more vertex at distance two from p. This contradiction proves n≤3k−1. Apply degree-two.md for k≥5 and Easwar–Johari for k=4.

For k=3 in the residual one-miss layout, B={p,q}, D={f}, and |C|≤4. Orders up to nine are already covered, so |C|=4. The remote classes P,Q each have two vertices and cover C disjointly. Vertex f has at least three neighbors in C, hence a neighbor in each class. Each private vertex already sees z, the missing anchor and f at distance two. There can be no pq edge and no P–Q edge, either of which would add a distance-two member of the other private class. Each exclusive remote now has only three possible neighbors: its own private vertex, its classmate and f. All three edges are required. If a sees p, a spanning path is b,z,a,p,P₁,P₂,f,Q₁,Q₂,q. This is the earlier hand argument in the diameter-four work, now applied at the rooted eccentricity.

**Eccentricity five.** With B,C,D as above, write F for the nonempty layer five. The cap at z gives |B|+|D|≤k. Anchor degree gives |B|≥k−2, so |D|≤2. Each f∈F has degree at least k and can have neighbors only in D∪F. Thus |F|≥k−|D|+1≥k−1. Both anchors are at distance four from every f: distance is at least four and at most five, and neither endpoint has degree below k. A shortest anchor-to-f walk increases the z-layer at each step (from one to five in four steps). Its midpoint lies in C, at distance two from the anchor. The cap at that anchor now gives |F|≤k−1. Equality forces |D|=2, |B|=k−2, and both anchors to see all B and each other to reach degree k. They consequently see every C at distance two. Since all k−1 vertices of F are at distance four, this implies |C|≤1. But C is a nonempty separating layer in a 2-connected graph and must have at least two vertices. Thus eccentricity five is impossible.

These cases exhaust the eccentricities by the upstream diameter bound. The traceability argument at large k uses 1-tough circumference bounds, a saturated independent-set construction and the two-attachment closure argument documented in degree-two.md. The rooted order inequalities are kernel-checked; the full theorem here is a paper proof.
