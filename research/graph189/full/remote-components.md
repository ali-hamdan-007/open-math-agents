# The remote vertices lie in one component at larger orders

Let G be a qualifying 2-connected Graph 189 graph on n vertices. Let z be its unique vertex of degree d<k, where k is the second-smallest degree. Assume k≥3. Put A=N(z), let T be the distance-two layer of z, and let R consist of all vertices at distance at least three from z. A port is a vertex of T. If

    n > 3k + d − 3,

all vertices of R lie in the same component of G minus N[z]. If k≥2d−3 and G minus N[z] is disconnected, it has exactly two components. One consists entirely of ports, has at least k−d+1 vertices, and the other contains at most d−1 ports. The eccentricity of z is at most four in the disconnected case.

To prove this, note that |R|=n−1−d−|T|>2k−4, since |T|≤k. Any two remote vertices in distinct components of G minus N[z] are at distance exactly four: every path between them meets A, each is at least two steps from A, and high-degree vertices are at most four steps apart. Thus a remote vertex x sees every remote in another component at distance four, and at least one anchor in A at distance two. If there are at least k−1 opposite remotes, this exhausts its even-distance budget. Then x has no distance-two vertex within its own component. A shortest internal path to any missed vertex would give one, so x is adjacent to all other vertices of its component. That component touches at least two anchors by 2-connectivity, thus x now sees two anchors at distance two, a contradiction. If two distinct components contain remotes, adding the two upper bounds k−2 on their outside remote counts already bounds the entire remote population by 2k−4. This proves the first assertion.

Each component has size at least k−d+1, since its members only have external neighbors in A. This is at least two, so 2-connectivity also forces at least two distinct port vertices in each component. If there were two entirely port components and one remote-bearing component, then |T|≥2(k−d+1)+2>k for k≥2d−3. Hence there is exactly one port component K when disconnected in this degree range. Even without that degree-range condition, if disconnected, there is at least one such K. For any vertex outside K and N[z], take a path of length at most four to a vertex of K. It must visit A and then use at least one more edge. Thus the vertex has distance at most three from A and at most four from z. All vertices of K are already at distance two. This proves the eccentricity claim.

For d=5 this applies at every order above 3k+2, precisely where the previous order proof for minimum degree at least four stops. When k≥7 the only disconnected case to study is a single all-port side opposite the remote component with no more than four ports. This is a structural reduction; traceability in the remaining degree-five case is open here.
