# Audit of the saturated independent-set reduction for Graph 189

The theorem and its stated consequence follow from the argument after one small endpoint correction. In the one-profile case and the more general case where \(H\) sees all profiles, use a spanning alternating path in the remaining \(K_{k,k}\), starting at any vertex of \(H\). This works for \(k=1\) as well. A Hamilton-cycle argument in \(K_{k,k}\) would require \(k\ge2\). The large-order regime has \(k\ge1\) by connectedness, since it has at least two vertices.

## Structure and low-degree bookkeeping

- For each \(i\in I\), the other \(k\) vertices of \(I\) exhaust its positive even-distance allowance. Every induced edge of \(Y\) therefore joins vertices with the same profile. Every component of \(G[Y]\) has an edge into \(I\), so all profiles are nonempty as subsets of \(I\).
- For vertices in distinct profiles, a shortest path has to visit \(I\). Any consecutive run of at least two vertices in \(Y\) along it could be shortened, since the neighboring vertex or vertices in \(I\) see the entire run. If the run contains a path endpoint, retain that endpoint. A run cannot contain both endpoints because their profiles differ. Thus such a shortest path alternates and has even length. Same-profile nonneighbors have distance two via their common neighbor in \(I\).
- Counting nonneighbors in \(Y\) gives each profile internal minimum degree at least \(m-1\). A missed profile has at least \(m\) vertices and a high vertex in \(I\) misses at most \(m\) vertices total. Therefore any missed profile is an \(m\)-clique and a high vertex misses at most one profile.
- An \(m\)-clique contains a vertex of degree at least \(k\) even if the unique low vertex lies in \(Y\), since \(m\ge2\). The high member has exactly \(m-1\) neighbors in \(Y\), and at most one neighbor in \(I\setminus H\). Its common profile therefore sees at least \(k-m\) members of \(H\). A larger profile sees all \(H\), regardless of the location of the low vertex.

## Paths and endpoint checks

- If \(2m<k\), a longest path within the initial profile and starting at the chosen neighbor of \(i_0\) has at least \(m\) vertices by internal minimum degree. Take its first \(m\) vertices. The last has an \(H\)-neighbor. Each part of the remaining balanced incidence graph has minimum degree at least \(k-m\), and \(2(k-m)>k\). Moon–Moser supplies a Hamilton cycle, because in this regime \(k\ge5\). Removing a cycle edge incident to the specified \(H\)-neighbor leaves a spanning path with that neighbor as an endpoint.
- With two profiles, \(k=m\) and a missed profile, both profiles are \(k\)-cliques. Connectedness implies a vertex of \(I\) sees both. Reserve it, then any assignment of the other \(k\) independent vertices to neighboring cliques respects each clique's capacity \(k\). In a clique with \(r\) vertices and \(s\le r\) assigned independent vertices, the ordering \(i_1,c_1,\ldots,i_s,c_s,c_{s+1},\ldots,c_r\) gives a clique endpoint. Reverse one group before joining through the connector. The empty-assignment case uses the clique path.
- With two profiles and \(k>m\), the larger profile \(D\) sees all \(H\). When \(i_0\) also sees \(D\), take any independent vertex that sees \(C\) after traversing \(C\), and follow it with a \(D,I,D,I,\ldots,D,I\) sequence of \(k\) pairs. When \(i_0\) misses \(D\), it sees \(C\) by connectedness. Traverse \(i_0,C\), then take an \(H,D,H,D,\ldots,H,D\) sequence. The first \(H\)-vertex exists because the profile of \(C\) has at least \(k-m>0\) high neighbors.
- With three profiles, \(2m=k\) and all profiles are \(m\)-cliques. Each high independent vertex sees at least two. Each clique sees at least \(m\) high vertices, so the second connector seeing the third clique can be chosen distinct from the first. Its other incident clique determines which clique is in the middle. The unreserved independent vertices have cardinality \(2m-1\). A subset seeing at least two cliques has at least \(m+(m-1)=2m-1\) permissible slots. A subset seeing exactly one clique has size at most one, because its only possible member is \(i_0\), and all capacities are positive. Hall therefore applies with capacities \(m,m-1,m\). In the middle use the ordering \(c_1,i_1,c_2,\ldots,i_s,c_{s+1},\ldots,c_m\), giving two distinct clique endpoints since \(m\ge2\). Orient the end cliques toward and away from their connector as needed. All transitions join disjoint vertex blocks and the two reserved independent vertices.

## Equality at \(n=\delta+2k+1\)

An independent set of size \(k+2\) has a pair at distance at least three by the even-distance bound. Their disjoint neighborhoods avoid the independent set, and at most one endpoint has degree below \(k\). This gives \(n\ge\delta+2k+2\), a contradiction. If \(\alpha\le k\), the universal-vertex augmentation saves \(c-2\ge1\) in the cited degree-sum threshold, offsetting the extra vertex of the boundary \(n=\delta+2k+1\). In applying the threshold one may assume an independent set of size \(c+1\) exists. Otherwise Hamiltonicity follows from the Chvátal–Erdős condition already invoked in the order-bound argument. The remaining case has \(\alpha=k+1\). Each maximum independent set contains a distant pair unless the saturated-independent-set theorem already applies. The same neighborhood count now meets equality, giving the partition and the degree pair \(\{\delta,k\}\). If \(\delta<k\), the unique low vertex belongs to every maximum independent set.
