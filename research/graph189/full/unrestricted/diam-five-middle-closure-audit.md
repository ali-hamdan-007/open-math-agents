At order n=3s+3 every qualifying graph of diameter five is traceable, with arbitrary size of the fourth-layer subset D0 having no far-layer neighbor. At order n=3s+3+e it suffices that 0≤e≤g and |D0|≤h−1, with the layer slacks defined in the source.

**Closure and expansion.** On w+2 vertices the ordinary path-closure threshold is w+1. This follows from cycle closure by adjoining a universal vertex. Closure only adds pairs between original vertices. In the completed graph pick distinct neighbors for the two marker endpoints, possible because the left marker has at least two choices and the right marker at least one. The left marker would have only one neighbor if all c>1 core vertices had the same sole B neighbor; its adjacent anchor would see all c>h core vertices at distance two in addition to all f far vertices at distance four. As closure unwinds, marker endpoints can move. At an internal marker, two retained singleton choices have different unique contacts by construction, and any pair containing a multiple choice has a system of distinct representatives. The outer blocks are Hamilton-connected, hence an internal marker expands to a path with the chosen ends; an endpoint marker can start at its single chosen contact. No restored path uses a marker-marker edge. Even if they are separated by just one original vertex, the expansions of the two markers use disjoint blocks and each concatenation is valid.

**Order 3s+3.** The earlier refined lower bound on c, from a hidden nonproductive vertex, forces both nonnegative gaps i−m and j−d0 to vanish. Every far vertex is thus complete to D+ and the remaining far vertices. Every productive core vertex sees all F through D+, and cannot see all A. A D0 vertex without a D+ neighbor would have degree at most w−1<s. Its D+ neighbor witnesses distance two to every far vertex. Consequently Y is visible from every far vertex and |Y|≤l, while |X|≤h−m by any anchor's allowance. Every remaining original is a nonproductive C vertex missed by an anchor, so has ≤i outside neighbors. Substituting s=w+i−1 shows it is complete inside T.

For X and Y the T-degree lower bounds simplify independently to h+1+j+g and l+j+g. An unmarked X or Y has one contact on its own side and T-degree respectively ≥w−1 or ≥w−2. Every other original has augmented degree at least three, since l≥2 and w>h≥8, so even the latter exception can close to each other original. An X–Y pair with both class markers has degree sum ≥h+l+3+2j+2g≥w+1 using d0=j. Once these cross pairs are filled, every marked vertex has degree ≥w−t+1 and ≥t+1 for its class size t. Either bound combination suffices for the remaining same-class pairs. This proves the complete middle closes.

**General window.** Each marked all-A-visible member of X misses some far vertex at distance two, so all its D+ contacts are among that far vertex's at most j omissions. For Y a type H has at most i B contacts and a D0 vertex has none. This recovers the augmented rX=h+g+2 and rY=l+g+d0+1. An omitted all-A vertex has exactly one B contact, an omitted H vertex has exactly one D+ contact, a middle vertex visible from neither entire endpoint layer is nonproductive, and an omitted D0 vertex has at most one D+ contact. These give rZ=s−max(i,j)−1 in every case. Inequality (3) then fills all Z pairs, followed by all X–Y pairs. For class sizes use |X|≤h−m and |Y|≤l+d0 to repeat the preceding closure. Finally the X–Y surplus is g−e. One has rZ≥h+l+g and min(rX,rY,rZ)≥e+d0+3 from e≤g, d0≤h−1 and l≥2. These give exactly the required degree sum w+1. This covers the asserted higher orders without a bound on s.

**Sharpening.** The condition d0≤h−1 can be removed throughout 0≤e≤g. For every all-A-visible C vertex x choose a far vertex y from which x is not at distance two; such a y exists since a+f>s. The far vertex y misses all D0 in its possible neighborhood D∪(F−{y}). Its remaining omission allowance is at most j−d0. Any D+ neighbor of x must be missing at y. This improves its number of D+ contacts to j−d0. Apply this both to left-marked X and to an unmarked all-A-visible vertex of Z. The four cases for Z consequently give rZ'=s−max(i,j−d0)−1=h+l+g+min(j,i+d0)≥h+l+g+d0. Retain the original rX and rY. Their sum still exceeds or equals w+1 at e≤g. Each of rX, rY, rZ' is at least e+3, using h≥8, l≥2, d0≥0. Therefore rZ'+min(rX,rY,rZ')≥h+l+g+d0+e+3=w+1. The preceding marker closure proves traceability for every e≤g with arbitrary d0.
