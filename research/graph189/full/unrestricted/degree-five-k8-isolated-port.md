# (4,4) at k=8: isolated Q-port closes

Let p be Q-isolated, X=S_p and C=Σ_{j≠p}c_j. The k=9 double count gives |X|(8−|X|)≤9, and 5≤|X|≤7, so |X|=7. As X is covered by the three other boundaries, C≤2. Two isolated ports are impossible (14 private vertices >12), so the other triple is connected in Q. If all three were ordinary singletons, two nonadjacent overlaps would cost ≥4 in C. If they form an ordinary edge ab plus singleton c, there must be an overlap of c with exactly one endpoint, say a; otherwise C≥4. Then C=2. The total allowance of remote-even witnesses over the three ports is exactly seven slots, already consumed by the seven distinct vertices of X because each is seen by some port. Yet any common vertex of S_c and S_a is omitted by S_b, and is at distance two from b by ordinary edge ba; it is outside X. This is impossible. Thus the three ports have connected ordinary adjacency.

Write H=R−X. If C≤1, the ordinary graph of three ports is a triangle; otherwise a nonadjacent pair at the ends of the ordinary path would spend two in C. At least one port q sees three members of X at distance two. This exhausts its remote-even allowance, so the union of the neighborhoods of its ordinary mates is contained in S_q and H=S_q. If C=2, every port's remote-even allowance is already spent on X, so any q adjacent to the two other ports similarly has S_q=H. The other two are both adjacent to q and hence miss at most two vertices each of H. Indeed, after X is witnessed, just 2−C≤2 remote-even slots remain in total over the three ports, and any H nonneighbor of such a port has distance two via q. Therefore 6≤|H|=h−7≤7.

The singleton block X∪{p} of order eight is Hamilton-connected: every core vertex loses at most three neighbors outside, so has internal degree ≥5, and p is universal. In L=H together with the other port triple every core vertex retains degree ≥5 by its own port's boundary. Each port has ≥|H|−2+1≥5 neighbors. Since |L|≤10, Dirac gives a Hamilton cycle. Connected D has a cross-core edge ab with a∈X and b∈H. Starting in the shell, enter p, follow a Hamilton path of the singleton block to a, cross to b and follow a Hamilton path opened at b on the L-cycle.
