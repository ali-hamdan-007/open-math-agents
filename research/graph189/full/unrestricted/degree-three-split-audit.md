# Independent audit of separated common-middle degrees, degree-three root

The theorem in degree-three-split-common-neighborhoods.md is valid subject to its stated two inequalities and the hypotheses inherited from the six established high-order templates. Here d is a nonnegative integer. The proof does not require connectivity of the induced remote graph. It does require the ambient template's visibility and support-boundary estimates. Its range and the related mixed theorem each require a degree-profile hypothesis.

For L=R∖B the visibility inequality c+b+2h≤3k−f and the removal of g triples give |L|≤3K−f−b−2r. Since each L vertex has ≤d W-neighbors, δ(L)≥K−d−e=D. The numerical hypotheses imply |L|≤3D−2, K≥100, e≤6, and 2h≤k−6. If I is independent of size D+1, its complement in L contains at most 2D−3 vertices, so the D-neighborhoods of each pair intersect.

A neighbor in B would produce at least g+d+e+1 distance-two W witnesses along with a visible anchor and D peers, exceeding k. If x touched w in W, a W-neighborhood count subtracting its ≤d direct W neighbors and at most g(w)+r(w)+2 W vertices nonadjacent to w leaves at least |W|−d−g(w)−r(w)−2 further W witnesses. Add g(w) distinct B witnesses, three anchors and D peers. The sum exceeds k since k−h−2d−2e+1≥(k+6)/2−2d−2e+1>0. Thus I misses B,W. An I member in exceptional support J has ≥k−e+1 vertices in its closed L-neighborhood. The support has ≤K−2 vertices with boundary ≤e−2+a_J. The stated subtraction gives |I∩S_J|≤d+3e−5, using a_J≤g+d+e−1. The e supports cannot cover D+1 independent members because K≥(e+1)d+3e²−4e. Thus α(L)≤D. With |L|+2≤3D the quoted Hamiltonicity criterion for 2-connected graphs applies; equivalently, the independent-triple degree sum is ≥max(|L|+2,3α(L)), the available criterion in the earlier audited manuscript.

When L is disconnected or cut, every part after removing zero or one vertex has order at least D+1 or D, respectively. There are exactly two, of order at most 2D−3, with internal degree ≥D−1. This suffices for any prescribed spanning path endpoints by marker closure; a cut vertex has ≥D neighbors in the two parts, so it can be included in a cycle using two neighbors on one side. Two cycles can reserve distinct ports with distinct incident anchors: each side contains nontriples since D>r. A single anchor or a single port meets at most k−h−2 nontriples, while the two sides together have at least c−h−1>k−h−2. If the initially selected distinct anchors use the same port on both sides, choose a different port on one side and use the common port with the other anchor on the opposite side. The single-cycle case retains a true singleton class's exclusive remote, unaffected by deletion of B. A low unreserved J has ≥K−4e+1≥e−1 distinct eligible cycle edges by the stated successor count. Distinct-edge insertion and opening cycles at their reserved contacts are valid, even when a subdivided edge was originally incident with a reserved contact.

The marker graph has order N≤k+g. For W vertices with r(w)≤K/3, their initial sums with every W vertex reach N: r(w')≤K/2−3, and K≥12e+18. At most 3rd/K≤3d/2 other W vertices exist. After low-to-all closure only those high vertices can miss one another, giving each W degree ≥k−e−1−3d/2. Doubled, this reaches N since K≥3d+2e+2. The two subsequent closure waves are sound: a B vertex has ≥g+e+1 W neighbors, and after B-to-W completion a high unreserved exceptional has ≥e+1, enough in both cases at threshold k+g. K≥2e provides enough separate W gaps for up to g+e−1 outside vertices. Reversing the cycle closure preserves the specified W-ended path through the marker. The two published global routes use all vertices once.

Scope: the hypothesis requires a gap for a single chosen d and group size g between W degrees ≤d and W degrees ≥g+2d+e+1. Arbitrary intermediate profiles are not covered. Even with the gap, K≥7d+100 and f+b+2r≥3(d+e)+2 remain necessary assumptions of this argument. The simpler 2r≥3d+13 is sufficient for the second inequality; it does not characterize the allowed range. No universal degree-three or Graph189 conclusion follows from this result alone.
