# All two-port cases at k=6

The component and shell manuscript supplies the prescribed-anchor spanning path for every two-port case. Each port has at most five third-layer contacts, both port anchor sets are nonempty and disjoint, and the third layer has at least nine vertices. Indeed, an anchor touching both ports would see the whole third layer at distance two.

For |D|≤12, the usual path closure in D with an extra degree-two marker between the two ports makes the remote core a clique. The threshold is |D|≤12 and each core vertex has degree at least six. Both ports are active and have different available representatives by 2-connectivity. After reversing closure, replace an internal marker with the shell between the ports' distinct anchors, or replace an endpoint marker with a shell path attaching to its sole port neighbor on the path. This includes the whole (2,2) case.

The larger (3,2) and (4,2) orders use the following replacement for the side Hamilton-cycle step. A graph on four or five vertices of minimum degree at least two is traceable. On four vertices it is Hamiltonian. On five vertices, if it has no Hamilton cycle, the endpoints of every Hamilton path have degree exactly two, by the endpoint degree-sum cycle argument. Consider such a five-vertex side W with core ambient degrees at least six and a fourth-layer vertex g meeting W. Suppose that for every x∈W, its possible external neighbors are contained in a set D_x of at most four vertices which includes g. Then W has a spanning path with an endpoint adjacent to g. If W has a Hamilton cycle, break at a g-contact. Otherwise a degree-two endpoint needs at least four actual external neighbors and therefore meets every member of D_x, including g. This works separately for each requested g.

For the (3,2) extreme, the sharp cross-endpoint inequalities remain valid:
    
    |N_V(U)|≤|A_u|−1,      |N_U(V)|≤|A_v|−1,

where U,V are the disjoint five-vertex sides and A_u,A_v are the port-anchor sets. The single fourth vertex f meets both sides. If both port-anchor sets have size at most three, the external possibilities for any x in U are u,f and at most two opposite cross endpoints; symmetrically for V. The side lemma applies and gives u,U,f,V,v. If an anchor set has size four, the opposite one has size one, so at least one cross-endpoint bound is zero. The absence of cross edges forces both sides to be cliques complete to f by the degree requirement, giving the same route.

For the (4,2) extreme with one fourth vertex, each disjoint five-vertex side again has only its port, f and at most two cross endpoints as external possibilities. This follows from the three even slots at each port after K and z, one slot used by f. The side lemma gives the same route.

With two fourth-layer vertices f,g, the previous four-two extreme argument partitions the third layer into X,Y of sizes four or five, assigning a potential single shared port contact w to X. Each side is wholly adjacent to its own port. Let F_X denote the fourth vertices meeting X and E_Y the vertices of Y incident with cross-edges; define F_Y,E_X symmetrically. On the X side there are at most four external possibilities counting u, the potential additional port v at w, F_X, and E_Y. Indeed the cap at u gives |F_X|+|E_Y|+c≤3, where c counts the possible overlap. On the Y side the external possibilities are v,F_Y,E_X. The cap at v gives |F_Y|+|E_X|≤3: when w is adjacent into Y it need not be counted as an even witness itself, but the opposite port u is then at distance two via w and pays for it; without overlap, the inequality is immediate. The two ports are nonadjacent, since otherwise the at least three nonshared contacts of the opposite side would lie at distance two, in addition to K and z; more precisely, in the disjoint case the opposite side has at least four, and in the overlap case both port neighborhoods have size five, leaving four opposite nonshared contacts, exceeding three slots. Thus each side has minimum degree at least two, and its external-possibility bound includes all fourth vertices meeting it.

If the incidence graph between {f,g} and {X,Y} has a matching, use the side lemma for the two matched contacts. With b the two-port marker, this gives a spanning path f,X,u,b,v,Y,g (interchange f,g as needed). With no matching both fourth vertices meet only one side, say X. The required degree six forces |X|=5, fg an edge, and both f,g complete to X. Then f,g,X,u,b,v,Y works with any side paths. Expand the marker using the prescribed-anchors shell.

Consequently all three two-port parameter pairs at k=6 are traceable. Combined with the component and three-port notes, only (a,s)=(4,4) remains for the degree-five disconnected-neighborhood problem at this k.
