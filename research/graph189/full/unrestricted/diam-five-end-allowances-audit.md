The claimed necessary bounds pass, using the earlier layer obstruction and its strict-cover audit (in /tmp/full189nov28/diam5/layer-obstruction.md and /tmp/full189nov28/verify/diam5-audit.md).

Every B vertex has an A neighbor. That neighbor's missed B vertices at distance two, its ≥f distance-four F vertices and its adjacent B-vertex's C neighbors at distance two are disjoint, yielding d_C≤h−m. Subtracting the ≤b−1 side neighbors from degree s consequently gives d_A≥s−b+1−h+m=j+g+m+2. Each C vertex therefore has that many visible A vertices through any B neighbor, while each anchor sees ≤h−m C vertices. The double count is valid, as is multiplying this with the strict earlier cover (h−m>0). It yields c/(h−m)<sqrt(a) and s<a−1+(a−3)sqrt(a). D+ has the symmetric C capacity l from an incident F vertex and hence ≥i+g+1 F neighbors by degree. Each L midpoint needs the latter to fit l; each H midpoint needs the former A-neighborhood to fit h. When l=1 each far vertex can see exactly one C midpoint, which must serve all A, contradicting i+g+1≥2. The earlier h≥5 bound plus c≤floor((h+1)²/4)−1 excludes h=5 at n>3s+2.

If both midpoint types occur, adding their two inequalities into a=i+h+1+j+g gives the displayed a≤2h+l−g−m−2. In the H-only case every B vertex not touching K=N₂(y)∩C for a far y lies at distance four, since a length-three geodesic from B to F has to increase the z-layer at every step and both endpoints are high. Consequently ≥i+|K| B vertices touch K and each sees ≤h anchors. Counting B misses at anchors gives (i+1)(a−h)≤a(h−1); when a>2h this bounds i<2h, then a<4h. In the L-only case every D+ vertex touches a type-L C midpoint and has ≤l F neighbors and ≤l C neighbors, hence ≥a−l D neighbors. At most l among the latter miss its adjacent F vertex, giving |D+|=t≥a−2l. Within D∪F the degrees of y∈F and w∈D+ are ≥s and ≥a, whose sum exceeds d+f−2; if nonadjacent they must share a neighbor, so y misses ≤l D+ vertices. The incidence inequality f(t−l)≤tl follows. If t≤2l then a≤4l; otherwise f<2l and a=f+h−l<h+l. This establishes a≤4max(h,l), s=a+l≤5max(h,l).

At n>3s+2 the order identity implies c≥h+l+g+2. Together with earlier c upper bound this yields the cited upper bound on l in terms of h, hence numerical bounds on a,s,n for each fixed h. It leaves h unbounded and does not settle diameter-five traceability.
