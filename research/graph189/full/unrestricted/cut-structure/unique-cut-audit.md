The entire exterior, all e(S,c) even S-vertices as measured at c, and the separate contact x (odd from c) lie in the union of these two even sets, yielding (1). Its right side is strictly below n and in particular |S|≥2. With uniqueness of a possible low vertex there is a high member of S, so |S|≥k. Zero e(S,c) makes every S vertex a contact because any missed vertex has along a path to c a first transition from nonneighbors to neighbors, leaving a distance-two witness in S. Three disjoint gate sides each have exterior size ≥2k+1, forcing all three e terms zero by (1), so a contact sees ≥2k opposite contacts at distance two. This rules out branching.

For distinct cuts, a shared member of their selected opposite sides would have additive gate-distance identities in both directions, contradicting the positive inter-gate distance. High u in the resulting middle Z away from both gates has all neighbors inside Z and forces |Z|≥k+1. Applying (1) on both sides gives |A|=|B|=k, |Z|=k+1, and e=0 on each side. Such u is adjacent to both gates and sees the 2k side vertices at distance two. Hence any middle nongate vertex is low and unique. A bridge splits parity at its endpoints on every vertex, forcing n≤2k+2; a singleton leaf is ruled out already by (1). Thus if the middle is just the two gates there is a forbidden bridge. If middle u exists, both gates must be its neighbors, and the gates are adjacent because otherwise cu is a bridge. On A,B and the two gates, exactly one gate is at even distance, forcing |A|=|B|=k. Since u is the only low vertex, all their members have every possible neighbor inside side plus gate. Hence u sees 2k at distance two. Thus there is at most one cut.
