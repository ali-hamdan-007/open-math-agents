# Many triple-visible remotes with large common-middle neighborhoods

In the six degree-three templates of `degree-three-nontriple-cycle.md`, suppose

    k−h ≥ e(e+1)+3,    and    |N(t)∩W| ≥ h+e+1 for every triple t.

Then G has a Hamiltonian path. This bound permits h of order k/2 and assumes no connectivity of F. Here e∈{4,5,6} counts exceptional middle ports, W consists of the other k−e middle vertices, h counts triple-visible remotes, and F is the induced nontriple remote graph. The order of G is greater than 3k+2. Retain b,f,Vᵢ,Sⱼ,Hⱼ,aⱼ from the nontriple-cycle argument. In particular m=|F|≤3(k−h)−f−b, each fᵢ≥2 and f≥7. A nontriple has at most three middle neighbors, an exclusive has at most two (just one in both possible f=7 templates), and δ(F)≥D=k−h−3.

**The 2-connected case.** Every pair in an independent set I of D+1 nontriples has a common original F-neighbor. For clarity, let sₓ be the number of middle neighbors of x. The total F-degrees of x,y∈I are at least 2(k−h)−sₓ−sᵧ, while m−|I|≤2(k−h)−f−b+2. One has sₓ+sᵧ<f+b−2: for f≥8 use min(6,4+b); for f=7 use min(6,2+2b), splitting b≤2 and b≥3. All independent peers thus have distance two. A member x of I cannot have a triple neighbor: it would have at least h+e+1 W witnesses via that triple, one visible anchor and the D peers at distance two, exceeding k. Thus |N_F[x]|≥k−2.

For an exceptional support J meeting x one has aⱼ≤h+2 by counting the aⱼ common neighbors of J, the anchor and the D peers at distance two from x. Its nontriple support Sⱼ∩F lies in Vᵢ minus all triples, for any anchor in its mask, and hence has size at most k−h−2. Its external boundary in R has size at most e−2+aⱼ as before. Its closed support neighborhood in F contains N_F[x] and misses no F vertex of the support itself. The closed neighborhood of x misses all other independent members of the support. Consequently

    |I∩Sⱼ|≤1+(k−h−2+e−2+aⱼ)−(k−2)≤e+1.

The e supports cover I, contradicting |I|=k−h−2>e(e+1). Hence α(F)≤D.

For independent triples the earlier sum bound is unchanged. Explicitly, the sum s of the three middle degrees need only satisfy s≤f+b−2 to ensure F-degree sum ≥m+2. For f≥8 one has s≤min(9,6+b); for f=7 use min(9,3+2b), which suffices except at b=3. If h=0, the preceding nontriple-cycle theorem handles F since k≥e(e+1)+3. Let h≥1. In the exceptional profile f=7,b=3 a failure forces m=3(k−h)−10 and every member of an offending independent triple to have F-degree exactly D, three supporting ports, and all h triples as neighbors. Indeed m+2≤3D+1 and all degrees are at least D. Both f=7 templates have three true privates and one double port; the only possible three supporting ports of a nontriple are that double port and the two privates of its mask i,j. Thus all three pair-visible remotes are the offending independent triple with this same support. For the third class l there are at most k−h−2 exclusive remotes. The i,j exclusive classes therefore have combined size at least

    m−3−(k−h−2)=2(k−h)−11.

Any one x of the three offending vertices sees each of these exclusives within distance two via the corresponding private, and at most D of them are adjacent to x. This gives at least k−h−8 distance-two exclusive remotes. Also at distance two are h+e+1 or more common middles through a triple neighbor, two visible anchors, and the other two independent pair-visible remotes through a shared supporting port. The disjoint total is at least k+e−3>k. The independent-triple degree sum is therefore at least m+2 in all cases; it is also at least 3D≥3α(F). The Bauer–Morgana–Schmeichel–Veldman criterion supplies a Hamilton cycle when F is 2-connected.

**Disconnected or cut F.** In the disconnected case take its components, j=0; in the cut case take components after deleting a cut vertex w, j=1. Each part has order at least D+1−j. As m≤3D+2, there cannot be three parts when disconnected. Three parts after a cut would require m≥3D+1, so f+b≤8 by the order bound and b≤1. Every part then contains an exclusive (its order is ≥D>1). Every exclusive has degree ≥k−h−2=D+1 in F, giving at least D+1 vertices in each part even after deletion of w. This contradicts m≤3D+2. Thus there are exactly two parts.

Each part is Hamilton-connected by the following endpoint-marker closure. Write d=k−h and let q=2 if a private is doubled and q=1 otherwise. Use s=3 except that s=2 when q=2 and b=0. The entire F has minimum degree ≥d−s. The other part has at least d−s+1−j vertices, so either part has order m₀≤2d−f−b+s−1. All its vertices have internal degree at least d−s−1. If f+b≥3s+2, then m₀+1≤2d−2s−2, and closure in the graph with an endpoint marker completes the entire part. Otherwise b≤3s+1−f≤3, so at most three part vertices are nonexclusive. Each exclusive has degree ≥d−q−1 in the part, and f+b≥s+2q+2. The latter holds because f≥7 for q=1; for q=2 one has f≥8 and either b≥1 or s=2. Consequently 2(d−q−1)≥m₀+1, completing all exclusives. The at most p≤3 other vertices have minimum degree d−s−1≥19. The exclusive clique now has degrees ≥m₀−p−1, enough to complete all cross edges at threshold m₀+1. Remaining exceptional pairs qualify either by original minimum degree d−s−1 or by the new degree m₀−p: if 2(d−s−1)<m₀+1 then m₀≥2(d−s−1)≥2p+1. This proves Hamilton-connectedness for specified endpoints.

Thus in the cut or disconnected cases take two disjoint cycles covering F, one on each part. When w exists, include it in the cycle on a part where it has two neighbors by spanning that part between these neighbors and closing through w. Such a part exists since d_F(w)≥D>2.

**Remote insertions and the common-middle path.** For a single F-cycle reserve a unique-class private P and a solo remote q at this port, possible since the exclusive class has size ≥h+3. For two cycles reserve distinct ports P,Q touching the respective parts at x,y with distinct incident anchors a,b, as in `degree-three-all-triples-connectivity.md`. Indeed the combined part size is at least 2D>k−h−2, whereas nontriple neighbors of any exceptional port and nontriple remotes visible to any anchor each number at most k−h−2. Thus choose distinct anchors for the two sides; if an initial choice uses a single common port J, its mask contains both anchors and J touches both parts. Pair any different touching port on its own part with J on the other, choosing J's anchor differently.

For each unreserved exceptional J with aⱼ≤e there are at least k−2aⱼ−2e−h+1≥d−4e+1≥e−1 eligible oriented edges across the one or two F-cycles with both ends adjacent to J, by the support-boundary successor count. The final inequality follows from d≥e(e+1)+3≥5e−2 for e≥4. Assign distinct eligible edges and subdivide them. The remaining unreserved high ports each have at least e+1 W-neighbors.

It remains to find a spanning path of the graph on W, all h triples, and these p≤e−1 high ports, with both endpoints in W. Choose any distinct W vertices as endpoints and add a degree-two marker joining them. The augmented order N is at most (k−e)+h+(e−1)+1=k+h. Every W vertex originally has degree at least k−3−e in this graph since all triples are present and its other possible missing outside neighbors are the three anchors and e exceptional ports. As d=k−h≥2e+6, cycle closure at threshold N completes W to a clique. Each triple has degree at least h+e+1 and each W vertex now has degree at least |W|−1, so every triple–W edge can be added: the sum is at least |W|+h+e≥N. Then the W degrees are ≥|W|−1+h, and the high ports have degree ≥e+1, so all high port–W pairs can also be completed. In this closure each satellite (triple or high port) can be placed between consecutive W vertices on a W spanning path with the desired endpoints. There are |W|−1≥h+p distinct available gaps because d≥2e. Close the resulting spanning path through the marker. Reversing closure and deleting the marker supplies the original spanning path with its W endpoints.

In the one-cycle case, use this W path, then the anchor/z spanning path ending at P's anchor, then P,q and the opened expanded remote cycle. In the two-cycle case traverse the first opened expanded cycle ending at x, then P,a,z,c where c is the third anchor, the W path, then b,Q and the other opened cycle beginning at y. This spans every vertex.
