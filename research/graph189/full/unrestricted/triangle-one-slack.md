# Triangle transversals at k=3t+1

For t≥1, a qualifying graph with k=3t+1 is traceable if some t vertices meet every triangle. Combined with the larger-parameter results, this forces at least ceil(k/3) vertices to meet all triangles of a counterexample, and at least ceil(k/9) vertex-disjoint triangles. The empty-transversal case uses the prior triangle-free theorem.

Use the notation from triangle-boundary.md. If intersection among the high neighborhoods of H=G−X is transitive, its odd-cycle argument makes H bipartite. The separate disconnected-equality.md component proof at k=3t+1 and the preceding connected-component bound then give traceability. It suffices here to bound the order when that intersection relation fails. Put A=N_H(b0), F=N_H(b), B=N_H(b2), with A and B disjoint and each meeting F. Put Ω=A∪B, r=k−t=2t+1, and

    α=|A|−r, β=|B|−r, φ=|F|−r, o=|F∖Ω|,
    u=k+1−|A∪F|, v=k+1−|B∪F|.

These nonnegative integers sum to one. Indeed |F|+o=|F∖A|+|F∖B|, and the two differences can be computed by subtracting |A| and |B| from the respective union sizes. Call the A side saturated if u=0, and likewise for B. Every a∈A∩F on a saturated side has its entire closed even set A∪F, because triangle-freeness forces all other union members to distance two. It has eccentricity at most three and is adjacent to every x∈X adjacent to b, via the two-step route through b if needed. The corresponding statements hold on the B side. At least one side is saturated; hence b has eccentricity at most four. Its degree is at most k+φ. Apart from b itself and its neighbors, at most k vertices have positive even distance from b. It remains to bound its third layer L.

**Both sides saturated.** Every q∈L reached on a shortest path beginning with a member of (A∩F)∪(B∩F) belongs to Ω∪F. This holds also for any shortest path b,x,p,q with x∈X. Indeed p∉F and the intersection of the two saturated unions is F. Choose a∈A∩F or a∈B∩F on a side whose union misses p. As a is adjacent to x, its odd distance to p must equal one. Then q is at distance two from a. If o=0 all paths are accounted for and |L|≤|Ω∖F|=r+α+β−φ. Therefore n≤1+2k+r+α+β≤3k+2.

If o=1, all other parameters vanish. The one element of F outside Ω has its whole set F inside its even set, since F is an independent neighborhood in H. It accounts for at most k+1−r=t+1 extra vertices in L. Moreover b2∈A and b0∈B. To see this, the three index vertices each have exactly r neighbors in H, so all are adjacent to every x∈X. The vertex b2 lies outside F because b2 and b share a neighbor and H is triangle-free. If b2 were outside A as well, pick a∈A∩F. Then b2 and a share an x∈X and are nonadjacent since a∉B, contrary to saturation. The other direction follows symmetrically (or by adjacency symmetry). Both b0 and b2 are at distance two from b. Thus |L|≤|Ω∖F|−2+t+1=(r+1)−2+t+1=k, as required.

**One side unsaturated.** Swap A and B if needed to have u=1. All other parameters vanish. In particular F⊆Ω, its group F_A=A∩F has size t+1, and its group F_B=B∩F has size t. All three indices are adjacent to every x in X. Using one vertex of F_B in the preceding saturation argument shows b0∈B and b2∈A. Put Z=L∖Ω. For a∈F_A the even set already contains A∪F, of size k, leaving at most one extra even vertex. For a∈F_B there is no space at all.

If each z∈Z is at distance two from a member of F_A, then |Z|≤t+1. Otherwise fix q∈Z for which this fails. A shortest path must have the form b,x,p,q, x∈X: starting via F_A would violate the choice of q, and starting via F_B contradicts saturation. We have p∈B∖F, since if p were outside B∪F, then any a∈F_B would be adjacent to p via the same saturation argument, making q even from a. Also p cannot be adjacent to any a∈F_A, by choice of q. For every a∈F_A its one extra even vertex is now accounted for. If a is adjacent to x then p is at distance two from a, and otherwise x is at distance two from a via b. Both x and p lie outside A∪F and outside Z. Consequently no member of Z can be reached in two steps from F_A. Thus *every* member of Z must be reached via a shortest path with the same form and with penultimate vertex in B∖F (the x and p may vary). It lies at distance two from b2. Vertex b2 already sees |A|−1 members of A∖{b2} at distance two through b0, since A∩B is empty. It also sees b at distance two through F_B; b∉A because b and b0 share a neighbor in F_A in the triangle-free graph. None of these r vertices belongs to Z. Hence |Z|≤k−r=t in this alternative. In both alternatives |L|≤|Ω∖F|+t+1=r+t+1=k+1. With φ=0 we obtain n≤3k+2.

The previous bounded-order theorem therefore supplies a spanning path. This is a graph-theoretic argument at the first slack case, not a proof that H must always be bipartite.
