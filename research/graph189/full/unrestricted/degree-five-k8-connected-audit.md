In the initial overlap lemma, for a disjoint outer pair the other outer port is beyond the counted j mask neighbors in each of the two transverse overlaps. Thus each has ≥4 vertices, disjoint in the middle support of size ≤7. Failed propagation through ordinary ab fills all three remote-even slots at b and forces its c_b=0 as stated. The connected ordinary case uses either a single center or two central vertices of a spanning P4, both with an additional distance-two port when there is no dominating vertex.

For (3,1), a proper singleton overlap set O with an ordinary edge exiting it makes the entire three-set F incident with every member of O, with exact c counts. When |O|=1 both remaining ordinary ports are adjacent to p or would cost a second c_p; both thereby spend all remote slots on F. When |O|=2, every singleton-t private vertex needs the unique possible member of B_t as neighbor; any ordinary port at that neighbor witnesses all these private vertices and is either saturated by F or has at most two slots. These prove h≤12. The all-active singleton has B_t empty and cannot support privates.

For (2,2), all active cross-pairs overlap because failure leaves the opposite ordinary mate with c=0. With a deficiency, all cross intersections coincide as the three witnesses seen from an inactive mate. At any F vertex its mask has exactly s+t ports, and the two appropriate boundaries have respective upper sizes 3−t,3−s. Including its two possible F peers gives at most eight possible neighbors; all are needed, so F is complete. The deficient side's W_1 equals its inactive port's support, precluding all W_1–W_2 edges because the three F vertices already exhaust that port's slots. Both sides have ≤7 W vertices. Every core vertex in its block loses at most three F neighbors, and ports in the block miss at most two W_i vertices. The marker degree sums reach |W_i|+3, and using a distinct mate of an active port as the shell entrance gives the stated three-block route. If both sides are fully active, equality in each freely chosen spanning tree gives all supports of size 7, mate differences of size 1 and transverse differences of size 4. Thus the common three transverse contacts all have mask size four. Each port's sole remote-even witness is its mate's one private-in-the-pair difference, and the two such differences are distinct. An exterior neighbor of any common vertex would have to be in both distinct singleton witnesses of an opposite pair, impossible. Degree eight then fails.

For (2,1,1), failure of ordinary propagation at ab forces the two singleton overlaps with a to be the same three-set F; the three-component lemma supplies an overlap with a even if the other singleton first joined via its mate. Their intersection is closed under core adjacency and has size ≥5. Each potential c-only or d-only vertex has ≤2 same-support exterior vertices to use and no edge to the intersection, insufficient. The remaining ordinary pair has at most six further supported vertices and each would have core degree ≤5 and at most two ports, likewise insufficient. The four-singleton case follows directly by mask containment and minimum mask-class size five once all boundaries are empty.
