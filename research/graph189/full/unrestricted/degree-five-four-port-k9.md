# Extension of the four-port theorem to k=9

Keep the notation and results of degree-five-four-port-k10.md. At k=9 the high-order assumption gives h≥15 and the port neighborhoods have size at most eight. We check the changes to its three Q cases.

For connected Q, propagation along ordinary edges still works because an intercomponent overlap has size at least k−5=4>3. To prove the quotient transitivity, suppose S_p∩S_q and S_q∩S_r are nonempty with p,q,r in distinct ordinary components and S_p∩S_r empty. At x∈S_p∩S_q the lower bound c_p+c_q≥j=|N_P(x)| counts only ports in N_P(x). The port r is outside N_P(x), since it misses S_p, and is at distance two from q. Thus c_p+c_q≥j+1, whence |S_p∩S_q|≥k−4=5. But S_q misses S_r in at most four vertices. The resulting contradiction proves transitivity. All the connected-Q case counts are unchanged: h≤k+5=14<15.

For an isolated p put X=S_p, of order at least k−3=6. Every x∈X needs k−|X| neighbors outside X∪{p}, and all of them lie in B_p of size at most three. Conversely every y∈B_p has at most three X-neighbors: choose a port q touching y, and observe that each of those X-neighbors lies in B_q. Counting edges between X and B_p gives |X|(k−|X|)≤9. Since 6≤|X|≤8, this forces |X|=8. The private boundary count now gives C=∑_{q≠p}c_q≤1. A second isolated port likewise has eight private neighbors, impossible since there are at most twelve private vertices in total. The remaining three ports therefore form a connected Q component and constitute an ordinary clique (any nonadjacent overlap would contribute at least two to C; a three-vertex ordinary path would contribute at least two also). Some port q in the triangle sees at least three vertices of X at distance two, so its core-neighborhood H=S_q covers all of R−X. The two-block argument above still works. In particular N=|H|+3≤11. Core degree ≥6 and port degree ≥N−5 suffice for marked closure since 12≥N+1 and 6+(N−5)=N+1. The singleton block has order nine with universal p and core minimum degree at least six.

For two port pairs, a nonadjacent pair has both its ports at distance two from the other and therefore |B_p|≤2 for both. In its block N≤k+5=14 and all core degrees are at least k−2=7. Each port has at least N−6 core neighbors. Thus the first closure step joins the ports to the original core, then joins the ports together. Original high vertices have degree at least k=9, sufficient for the next wave since 9+7≥N+1. As in the previous argument at most six possible low vertices remain, whence 2 max(7,N−6)≥N+1 completes the closure.

For an adjacent pair p,r, their core neighborhoods directionally differ by at most three. Thus N≤k+4=13, internal port degrees are at least N−4 (the ordinary mate also contributes), and all core degrees are at least six. Hence the ports and all core vertices of original degree at least nine become universal within the original vertex set. At most six core vertices can still be nonuniversal. For N≤11 the minimum degree six suffices and for N≥13 the bound N−6 does. At N=12, fewer than six nonuniversal vertices suffice by the same bound, and a nonuniversal vertex of degree at least seven can be made universal since the others have degree at least six. The sole possible failure is therefore a set X of exactly six original core vertices, each still of degree six and complete to the other six original vertices. Each has original internal degree at most six, so it needs at least three cross edges.

Let Y be the vertices in the opposite block with a neighbor in X. These vertices belong to B_p∪B_r, so |Y|≤6. Every y∈Y has at most three neighbors in X by the boundary of its own port. The resulting 18≤e(X,Y)≤3|Y|≤18 forces |Y|=6, forcing B_p,B_r to be disjoint three-sets covering Y. A double-contact member of X would have all its cross-neighbors in their empty intersection. Thus X consists of private vertices. Both private types occur, since otherwise all cross-neighbors of X lie in just one boundary, contradicting |Y|=6. But then p sees three members of Y through one of its own neighbors in X and a fourth vertex, private to its adjacent mate r, through r. This contradicts the three-slot bound. Thus both marked blocks close and the previous shell splice spans G.

This proves the full (4,4) case for k≥9, leaving k=7,8 and 2k−3≤h≤2k+4, at most sixteen parameter pairs with n≤34.
