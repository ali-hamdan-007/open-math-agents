# Two deficient unions at k=3t

In the notation of triangle-one-slack.md let u=v=1 and all other slack parameters vanish. Thus the three index vertices b0,b,b2 see all X. Each F_A=A∩F and F_B=B∩F has t members. For a in F_A, the closed even set contains A∪F, with exactly one remaining slot; likewise B∪F for F_B. The index vertices b0,b2 lie in L2(b), and b has degree k. Below a spare means a positive even vertex outside the baseline union.

## Fifth layer

An F vertex has eccentricity at most five, since otherwise its distances four and six would both be spares. Thus b has eccentricity at most six. If q∈L5(b), for every a∈F its distance to q is four or five, and its spare s_a is q itself or the neighbor of q at distance four from a along a geodesic. In either case s_a lies in a layer at least four from b. Hence all F see all X. Any q′∈L3(b) outside Ω cannot have a path b,a,p,q′ via F, since q′ could not be a spare. Along a shortest b,x,p,q′ via X, choose an a whose baseline excludes p, using p∉F. Then a sees x, and p cannot be its spare (p is in L2), so a sees p, again impossible. Thus L3⊆Ω. For any fixed a, every q∈L5 equals s_a or neighbors it. If s_a is outside L5, triangle-freeness makes L5 independent, and every q therein has at most |L4∪L6|≤k−2 neighbors (b has two distinct L2 index witnesses), so at most one such q exists. If s_a is in L5, the other L5 vertices form an independent set and have at most one additional neighbor s_a, so there is at most one of them. Hence |L3|+|L5|≤2t+2≤3t+1, giving n≤3k+2.

## Nonadjacent outer indices, t≥2

For a∈F_A, at most one X vertex is missed (each missed X is a spare via b). Thus a sees some X vertex and sees b2 at distance two through it, since b2∉A∪F and is nonadjacent to a. Hence the spare is b2 and a sees all X; symmetric on the other side. Both F sets have eccentricity at most three. Any external L3 vertex q must use a shortest b,x,p,q via X. Choose the F side whose baseline excludes p. If p is not an outer index, adjacency a−x forces a−p as otherwise p is an unavailable spare. This makes q an unavailable spare. If p is b0 or b2, all its H-neighbors lie in Ω and q cannot be external. Thus L3⊆Ω and n≤3k+2.

## Adjacent outer indices, fourth layer or less

Write Z=L3(b)∖Ω and S_A for its vertices with a neighbor in A, similarly B. Let C_A be all positive even vertices of b0 outside Ω∪{b}. Since b0 sees B∖{b0} and b at distance two, |C_A|≤t, and S_A⊆C_A; similarly for B.

Call q direct if reachable in two steps from some a∈F. Assign each direct q to one witnessing side, say F_A, and choose a shortest a,p,q. Here p lies in L2(b) and is outside A∪F. If p∈B, q is in C_B. Otherwise p is in C_A via b0,a,p. Such penultimate vertices p cannot be reused by different q: a single F neighbor of p would see both such q at distance two. When q∈S_A, q is also in C_A and distinct from the first witness (located on the other side or in L2).

If a side has three or more assigned q, at most one assigned q can lack neighbors in its own baseline. Indeed, for q lacking such neighbors and any other assigned q′ whose spare witness is a′, d(a′,q)=3 (q cannot be this vertex's even spare and is in L3(b)). The penultimate vertex of a shortest a′–q path cannot be in the baseline, so it is q′. Hence q is adjacent to every other assigned q′. Two such q and any third would be a triangle in H. If a side has at most two assignments, trivially at most two lack neighbors in the baseline. Thus among all direct q, at most four lack their additional witness. The resource witnesses allocated above are disjoint as counted across both C sets: direct q themselves are distinct, and non-Ω penultimates are distinct and in L2. If all Z vertices are direct, 2|Z|−4≤|C_A|+|C_B|≤2t, so |Z|≤t+2 and n≤3k+2.

For an indirect vertex q, a shortest path b,x,p,q must start via X. On each side whose baseline excludes p, every spare is now either x (when an F vertex misses x) or p (otherwise, since any adjacency a−p would make q direct). In particular that whole side has no spare in Z. If any vertex on that side uses x as its spare, every Z vertex must have a baseline neighbor: for an external Z vertex at odd distance three from that a, its geodesic predecessor must lie in the baseline or be x, and x cannot be adjacent to a L3 vertex. This yields |Z|≤t. Otherwise all F vertices of the burned side have the same spare p, and every Z vertex not neighboring that side's baseline is adjacent to p. This last concentration case is open in this argument.
