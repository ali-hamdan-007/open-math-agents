# Independent audit of the (4,4) cutoff k≥13

The largeorder/degree-five-four-port-overlap.md manuscript with heading “Four-port overlap and the cutoff k≥13” passes. The relevant order high-range condition is h≥2k−3.

The anchor-union cap k−1 and port's ≤3 residual even slots follow as stated. For pq adjacent, S_q−S_p is at distance two from p disjointly from the counted c_p ports. For an overlap across distinct ordinary adjacency components, each of the j−2 additional ports incident with x cannot be adjacent to both p and q; whenever nonadjacent it contributes to the corresponding port count through x. Counting p,q themselves as a mutual pair gives c_p+c_q≥j. Every R-neighbor of x outside the intersection lies in B_p∪B_q, so the +1 for x itself in the intersection estimate is justified. The two directional neighborhood differences are at most four, improving to three when the two c counts total at least j+1.

For connected Q, a spanning tree of ordinary components and linking overlaps has total increments ≤10 if it has at least two ordinary edges. With one ordinary edge ab, some endpoint has an external overlap and hence c≥1; rooting at that endpoint gives increments ≤2,4,4. With zero ordinary edges, the j≥3 case gives c_p+c_q≥2(j−1)≥j+1, while at j=2 at least one endpoint of every Q edge has another neighbor in connected Q on four vertices. Thus all increments are ≤3. Even using the weakest total gives h≤k+9, strictly less than 2k−3 for k≥13.

For an isolated Q-port p no ordinary edges or shared R contacts are possible. It has at least k−a private neighbors. Each has a remote neighbor outside S_p because its only port contributes just one and |S_p|≤k−1. Its membership in another B_q gives the 9−d bound, where all d nonadjacent anchor-sharing ports each spend a slot on p. At k≥13 this forces a≥4+d; a=5 means all three ports share anchors with p, inconsistent. The remaining d=0 single-complement anchor covers R−S_p, yielding h≤k+8. Thus no isolated Q-port exists.

Two remaining components are disjoint port pairs and their disjoint H_i cover R. Within each pair either the ordinary edge or the cross-adjacency-component overlap difference bound applies. Hence |H_i|≤k+3 and each port misses at most four H_i vertices. On either side all remote endpoints of cross edges lie in the union of the opposite block's two B sets, of order at most six. All core vertices in L_i retain degree ≥k−6, and all except at most six retain ≥k. Both port degrees are ≥N−6 even if the pair has no ordinary edge. At threshold N+1 high-degree core vertices qualify against every original vertex since 2k−6≥k+6≥N+1. Ports qualify against degree ≥7. Once these vertices are universal, each of ≤6 residual core vertices sees at least N−6 originals; if N≥13, 2(N−6)≥N+1, and if N≤13 the degree-7 bound suffices. The endpoint marker thus establishes Hamilton-connectedness in original L_i. The indicated two-block route via any cross edge and the anchor-ended shell spans G.

For all k, every private vertex belongs to some B_p and hence there are ≤12. This gives h≤2k+4, so at most 6×8=48 integer pairs and n≤12+6+28=46 remain in (4,4). These bounds do not establish the existence of any surviving example.
