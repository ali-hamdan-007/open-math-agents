# The defect-four connected regime at k=6

Assume the setup of degree-five-connected-layer-defect-four.md with k=6, diameter at most four and n>20. The general anchor counting in its first paragraph excludes d=5. If d=4, then T={x,y}. Each anchor sees exactly one of x,y, and all five anchors form a clique. The groups A_x and A_y of anchors at the respective ports are both nonempty. Write S_x=N_R(x) and S_y=N_R(y), h=|R|, s=|F|. The two supports cover R and each has order at most five, as an anchor of the relevant group also sees the missed port at distance two.

The order gives h+s≥13 and the cap at z gives s≤4. Therefore h≥9, h≤10, and s≥3. If |S_x|=5, every member f of F meets S_x. Indeed an anchor of A_x has spent its six even slots on S_x and y, so its distance to f is three. By layers a three-step path from that anchor to f must go via x and a member of S_x. Thus all s vertices of F lie at distance two from x. In addition x sees z and all members of A_y at distance two. The six-even cap implies s≤5−|A_y|=|A_x|.

If both supports have size five the analogous inequality gives |A_x|+|A_y|≥2s≥6, a contradiction. Hence their sizes are five and four, and they are disjoint, with h=9 and s=4. Say |S_x|=5, so |A_x|≥4 and therefore |A_x|=4. At y, z and the four members of A_x leave just one additional even slot. Any r∈S_y has at most four neighbors in S_y∪{y} and is not adjacent to x. Every neighbor of r outside this set lies at distance two from y, by the disjointness of S_x,S_y and the layer definitions. There is at most one such neighbor, contradicting degree at least six. Thus d=4 is impossible at k=6. Combined with the companion theorem, any qualifying nontraceable degree-five graph of order above 3k+2 in the diameter-at-most-four regime satisfies |L₂(z)|≥k−3 for all k≥6.
