# Four-port overlap and the cutoff k≥13

In the disconnected degree-five-root case (a,s)=(4,4), every qualifying graph with k≥13 has a spanning path. The small-order theorem covers n≤3k+2. Assume otherwise, so the third layer R has order h≥2k−3. Every vertex in R has neighbors only in P∪R, where P is the four-port set. The pure component K has k−4 vertices, all complete to all five anchors.

Write S_p=N_R(p), B_p=N_R(S_p)−S_p, and c_p for the number of other ports at distance exactly two from p. Each |B_p|≤3−c_p, since K∪{z}, B_p and the c_p ports are disjoint sets of distance-two witnesses at p. Every port touches an anchor, and no anchor touches all ports, since the whole of R would then be at distance two from it. Each anchor α sees the union of the S_p for the ports p it touches at distance two, as well as at least one witness outside this union. Indeed, a missed anchor supplies that witness through z; if α sees every other anchor, a port it misses supplies the witness through its anchor. Thus any such union, in particular S_p alone, has at most k−1 members.

Define a graph Q on the four ports by linking ports that are adjacent in G or have a common neighbor in R. For an ordinary adjacency pq, |S_q−S_p|≤3−c_p by counting the new vertices at distance two from p. For an overlap between p,q in different components of the ordinary port-adjacency graph, take a common neighbor x and let j=|N_P(x)|. The two nonadjacent ports count one another in c_p and c_q. Each of the other j−2 ports incident with x is nonadjacent to at least one of p,q, since otherwise p,q would be in the same adjacency component. Thus c_p+c_q≥j. All R-neighbors of x outside S_p∩S_q lie in B_p∪B_q, whence

    |S_p∩S_q| ≥ 1+k−j−|B_p|−|B_q| ≥ k−5+c_p+c_q−j.

In particular |S_q−S_p|≤4 for such a link, and the bound improves to 3 if c_p+c_q≥j+1.

If Q were connected, h≤k+9 as follows. Start with any spanning forest of ordinary port-adjacency and join its components along overlap links, always using ordinary edges inside adjacency components. If the forest already has at least two edges, the total possible new vertices beyond the root neighborhood is at most 3+3+4=10. If it has exactly one ordinary edge ab, some endpoint, say a, has a common R-neighbor with a port outside {a,b}, since Q is connected. Thus c_a≥1. Root the tree at a, using at most two new vertices on ab and at most four apiece on the remaining links. If the forest has no edge, every overlap link has c_p+c_q≥j+1. For j≥3 this follows from c_p,c_q≥j−1. For j=2, at least one endpoint has a second neighbor in the connected four-vertex Q, again counted at distance two. In this case all three links add at most three each. Starting with |S_root|≤k−1 proves the bound. Since h≥2k−3>k+9 for k≥13, Q is disconnected.

Q cannot have an isolated port p. Write a for its number of anchors and d for the number of other ports sharing an anchor with it. It has at least k−a core neighbors, each of which is private to p. Any private neighbor x has at most |S_p|≤k−1 neighbors in S_p∪P, so it has a neighbor y outside S_p. Each port q touching y then has x∈B_q. At least d of the other ports count the nonadjacent p at distance two. Consequently

    k−a ≤ |S_p| ≤ ∑_{q≠p} |B_q| ≤ 9−d.

For k≥13 this requires a≥4+d. If d≥1, then a=5, making d=3, an impossibility. If d=0, all three other ports get their anchors in the complement of the a≥4 anchors of p. This complement must be a singleton, and its one anchor sees every member of R−S_p at distance two. The anchor-union bound above gives |R−S_p|≤k−1, hence h≤9+k−1=k+8<2k−3. This excludes an isolated port.

Thus Q consists of two port pairs. For each pair M_i let H_i be the portion of R it contacts. These portions partition R, and |H_i|≤k+3 by the link bounds above. Within H_i each of its two ports misses at most four vertices because the two port neighborhoods differ directionally by at most four. Put L_i=G[H_i∪M_i] and N=|L_i|≤k+5. Cross edges of R between the two portions have at most six possible endpoints on each side: every such endpoint opposite M_i lies in the union of the two B_p for p∈M_i. A core vertex in L_i therefore retains internal degree at least k−6. Except for at most six cross-edge endpoints, core vertices have degree at least k. The two ports have internal degree at least k−5 since they lose only anchor neighbors, and also at least N−6 because each misses at most four vertices of H_i.

Each L_i is Hamilton-connected. Adjoin a marker of degree two at any prescribed distinct endpoints and apply Hamilton-cycle closure at threshold N+1, only to original vertex pairs. The minimum original degree is at least k−6≥7. Every core vertex of degree at least k can therefore be made universal to originals since k+(k−6)≥N+1. Each port can also be made universal because (N−6)+7≥N+1. The at most six remaining original vertices now have degree at least N−6. For N≥13, 2(N−6)≥N+1 and these pairs close too. For N≤13, 2·7≥N+1 already suffices. The original vertices now form a clique. Reverse closure on a marker cycle and remove the marker to obtain the prescribed spanning path.

Connectedness of D supplies an edge ab between L_1 and L_2, with a in L_1. Choose either port of L_1 different from a. End the prescribed-anchor spanning shell path at an anchor of this port. Continue through the port and L_1 up to a, cross to b, and span L_2 starting at b. This spans G.

At every k, all private vertices lie in the union of the four B_p, of order at most twelve, exactly as in the isolated-port count. Thus 2h−12≤∑|S_p|≤4(k−1). The outstanding parameters satisfy 7≤k≤12 and 2k−3≤h≤2k+4: at most 48 integer pairs, with n≤46.
