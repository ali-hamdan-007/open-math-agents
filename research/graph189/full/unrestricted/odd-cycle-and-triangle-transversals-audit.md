# Independent audit of the global transversal results

For an induced connected bipartite H with classes A,B, take B to avoid the possible single low vertex. Distinct vertices in the union of two intersecting E sets, excluding an intersection witness a, are all at genuine distance two from a: the class A is independent in G. This bounds the union by k+1. Thus when k>3t+2, three sets with the consecutive-intersection property have intersecting outer sets by removing at most 2(t+1) of ≥k−t points from the middle set. Chains of the B-neighborhoods are connected because H is connected, giving |B|≤k+1. For A, any two high members share a B neighbor, since 2(k−t)>k+1. If there were k+1 high A vertices and an additional low one, the latter's H-neighbor has a high A-neighbor who sees the other k high A vertices and the low vertex at distance two. This proves both class bounds.

For the sharpening to k≥3t+1 in connected H, the indicators q_i record actual extra witnesses outside A: an x in the appropriate intersections of removed-neighbor sets is at distance two from a if not adjacent, and otherwise the opposite-index b is at distance two from a via x. The disjoint outer sets ensure a is not adjacent to that b. Then k≤2+Σp_i−q_0−q_2 is necessary. Either Σp_i≤3t−2, or at most one individual membership in X is missing, giving q_0=q_2=1 for t≥2. Both contradict k≥3t+1. For t=1 the manuscript's four boundary choices include the only equality k=4,e_0≠e_2 and exclude it using e_1=1. This part uses bipartiteness and should not be transferred directly to merely triangle-free H.

For arbitrary H=G−X in the bipartite case, every vertex of a nonsingleton component has a high neighbor, yielding at least r−1 distance-two peers and only t+1 available distance-two slots outside its component. A high x in X consequently could not touch two such components: two witnesses in different components bound its H-neighbors total by 2t+2<k−t+1. It has at least r=k−t neighbors in its one component. If adjacent high x,y were assigned to different components, r neighbors of y would consume r distance-two slots of x. Inside x's component choose two adjacent high x-neighbors u,v, possible since at most t of the ≥r H-neighbors of u fail adjacency to x. Opposite classes give ≥2r distinct H-neighbors of u,v, of whom ≥2r−t>k are adjacent to x, all witnessed at distance two from y. Removing the unique low vertex leaves G connected and hence all nonsingleton components coincide. An isolated H-vertex is necessarily the unique low one. Thus n≤2k+t+3 when k≥3t+2. For t≥2, n≤3k; this earlier order result and its independent audit are at /tmp/full189nov28/verify/three-k{,-audit}.md. The more restrictive k≥3t+3 gives n≤3k−4 because t≥2. The prior 2k+3 and cut-vertex inputs are recorded in the same earlier audit as Easwar–Johari Theorem B.

For t=1,k=3,min degree 3, using the previously established diameter ≤4, four neighbors of x in one class would have all three distance-two slots occupied by each other. Connectivity prevents a fifth same-class vertex because no H-neighbor of that set can meet the rest of its class. In a class of size at least five, any nonneighbor a of x missing distance two from x cannot reach a classmate in three steps: an odd walk in H is impossible, and a three-edge path through x would have to hit x at step two. Thus a sees ≥4 even classmates, impossible. The manuscript's order-eight conclusion for H then follows by the pigeonhole argument in its smaller class. Earlier minimum-degree-two reductions handle the complementary ranges. The separate one-edge deletion proof uses the triangle's third vertex as a source at which the extra edge never lies on a geodesic; its parity and class counts are valid.

For the triangle-transversal theorem, H is only assumed triangle-free at the outset. For high-index neighborhoods E,F with common vertex a, any other u in their union shares a neighbor with a and cannot be adjacent to a because H is induced and triangle-free. Thus the ordinary k+1 union bound still holds, and k≥3t+3 gives consecutive-intersection transitivity. On any odd cycle the step-two cyclic order is a permutation with intersecting E sets on successive indices. Deleting a possible low index and beginning after that index leaves one chain of all high cycle vertices. Transitivity then supplies a common neighbor for two adjacent high cycle vertices; such an original adjacent pair exists on a cycle of length ≥5 with ≤1 low vertex. This contradicts triangle-freeness. Hence H is bipartite and the connected-component and order results above apply.

Writing τ_odd for minimum vertex odd-cycle transversal, τ_triangle for minimum vertex triangle transversal, and ν_triangle for the maximum number of pairwise vertex-disjoint triangles, the valid necessary bounds on a counterexample are respectively ceil((k−1)/3), ceil((k−2)/3) and ceil((k−2)/9). The packing inference uses the independently established triangle-transversal lower bound: the union of the 3m vertices of any maximal set of m disjoint triangles meets every triangle. It does not use the odd-cycle-transversal lower bound by itself. The previously handled triangle-free branch also forces ν_triangle≥1. These are necessary counterexample restrictions and do not prove the full conjecture.
