# Three-port first order with an optional fourth-layer vertex

**Theorem.** In the degree-five disconnected case (a,s)=(4,3), if R=D−P has order 2k−2 then G has a spanning path for every k≥7. This includes both possibilities for the fourth layer.

Use the setup and shell lemma of the degree-five analysis. Write f for the sole fourth-layer vertex when it exists. Every core vertex other than f meets at least one of the three ports. When present, f has all its neighbors in R, so d_R(f)≥k.

For each port p, let S_p=N(p)∩R. Then |S_p|≤k−1. Any anchor α meeting p sees S_p at distance two. If α misses another anchor, that anchor is an additional even witness through z. Otherwise α sees an additional missed port through some other anchor. There must be a missed port, as an anchor meeting all ports would see the entire third layer, of size at least 2k−3>k, at distance two.

We have δ(G[R])≥k−2. This follows directly from ambient degree for core vertices with at most two port neighbors. A triple-contact vertex x shares a port with every other third-layer vertex. Thus at least 2k−4−d_R(x) core vertices are at distance two, allowing for the potential omission of f. The at least two distinct anchors incident to the ports are additional distance-two witnesses, so 2k−4−d_R(x)+2≤k as desired.

In a disconnected core, this degree bound forces precisely two cliques on k−1 vertices each. In particular f cannot be present there. The three-port two-clique matching construction from the companion proof gives a spanning path. In more detail, both cliques have usable port pairs, meaning pairs admitting distinct contacts. This follows from 2-connectivity, which requires at least two different ports and two different contacts per clique. If distinct pairs can be chosen, the route p,C_1,q,C_2,t uses all three ports, and one appends the shell at p. If both cliques have the same unique usable pair {p,q}, the last port t has no clique contact. Otherwise its contact and a different contact from the matching for {p,q} would give a further usable pair. By connectivity of D, label a port neighbor of t as p. The route t,p,C_1,q,C_2 then accepts the shell at t.

Suppose R is connected. By the elementary longest-path equality lemma in the companion proof, R has a Hamilton path unless it is a join of a (k−2)-vertex graph and an independent k-set I. Each x∈I has d_R(x)=k−2, and in particular differs from f. Its at least two port neighbors collectively meet just one anchor since all k−1 other members of I lie at distance two. As the three ports collectively meet at least two different anchors, there is at most one pair of ports with the same singleton anchor neighborhood. Every member of I must meet that pair, contradicting |S_p|≤k−1.

It remains to find a Hamilton path of R with endpoints assignable to two distinct ports. At least one endpoint x has a port contact because at most one vertex is f. Fix x. If the other endpoint y=f, then the one-step rotations at f produce d_R(f)≥k possible endpoints, including the original f, all different from x. If x has a unique port p and all the other possible endpoints have only p, then S_p contains x and at least k−1 other vertices, a contradiction. If x has two or more ports, any possible endpoint other than f immediately supplies a distinct assignment.

Otherwise both endpoints meet ports, and unless they can already be assigned distinctly they share the same singleton port p. One-step rotations at y now give d_R(y)≥k−1 possible endpoints, all different from x. If one accepts a port other than p, the assignment is found. If f occurs as an endpoint, use the preceding rotation argument on that Hamilton path. In the remaining case S_p would contain x and all k−1 or more possible endpoints, again impossible.

Write p,t for the ports assigned to the endpoints of the resulting Hamilton path, and q for the third port. At least one of the pairs {q,p}, {q,t} can be assigned two different anchors, since failure of both forces all three ports to share a sole anchor. Orient the path so that q and p have distinct assigned anchors. Use q, the shell between those anchors, p, the core Hamilton path, and t. This completes the proof.
