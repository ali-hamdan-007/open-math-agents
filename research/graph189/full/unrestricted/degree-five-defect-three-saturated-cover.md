# At d=s=3 a covering pair cannot contain a double-missing anchor

Assume the high-order degree-five root, diameter at most four, k≥7, |T|=|L₂|=k−3, and |F|=|L₄|=3. Then |R|=|L₃|≥2k−3. The other degree-five connected manuscript shows that every anchor misses one or two T-vertices. An anchor missing m has at least m even witnesses outside R: for a double miss it must be complete to the other anchors and reaches both missed T-vertices through their anchors, and for a single miss it has a missed anchor or its missed T vertex through another anchor.

Thus disjoint anchor misses of sizes m,n force m+n≤3 by covering all R. Suppose a misses {x}, and b misses {y,t} disjoint from it. Their visible R sets (those reached in two steps) cover all R and have orders at most k−1 and k−2. Equality is forced throughout: |R|=2k−3, the two visible sets have those precise respective sizes, and their intersection is empty.

Every w∈W=T−{x,y,t} touches both anchors, hence has no R-neighbor. The set W has order k−6≥1. The only potential neighbors of w are A and the other T vertices, totaling k+1. It cannot meet all its T peers, for it would then see all of R at distance two. Therefore it is complete to A and misses exactly one T peer p. Both z and p are now distance-two witnesses. Every R vertex that is not private to p (i.e. its T-neighbors are not the singleton {p}) is a further distance-two witness via some T neighbor of w. Hence at least |R|−(k−2)=k−1 vertices of R are private to p.

The b-visible side is exactly S_x=N_R(x), since W has no R contacts. It has only k−2 vertices, so p cannot be x. Nor can p lie in W, whose members all lack R-contacts. Relabel y,t if necessary so p=y. Its k−1 private vertices fill the entire a-visible side, proving that t has no R contact. Now t already misses the anchor b. Among A and its other T peers, it therefore has at most k available neighbors. To reach degree k it must touch all its T peers, in particular both x and y. Their supports cover R, so t sees |R|=2k−3>k vertices at distance two, a contradiction.

Consequently the only remaining miss-set patterns at d=s=3 either have pairwise intersection (the previously classified three-pair triangle), or have a disjoint pair of two singleton misses. In the latter case |R| cannot equal 2k−2: both visible sets would then be disjoint of size k−1. A common neighbor w outside the two singleton misses has no R-contact and must miss exactly one of the two active ports. It sees z, that missed port via an anchor, and all k−1 contacts of the other port, again exceeding k. Hence a surviving disjoint-singletons configuration has exactly |R|=2k−3, with at most one common-visible R vertex.
