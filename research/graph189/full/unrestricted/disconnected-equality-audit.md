# Independent audit of induced bipartite remainder at k=3t+1

Audited global/disconnected-equality.md SHA-256 503268aa308a714bde6c55126fd6079334dd960674e59413709c1eec363d7a79. The equality-case argument passes.

At a contact u in a nonsingleton H-component a high H-neighbor supplies r−1 same-color peers at exact distance two. Therefore t+1 external peers remain possible. A high x touching two components has ≥2t+2 H-neighbors and is limited to ≤t+1 neighbors outside each component. Equality implies exactly t+1 x-neighbors on either component and none elsewhere. In an all-high touched component C, fix one x-contact u. Its each H-neighbor v is high and d_H(v)−1 peers at exact distance two, together with the t+1 contacts on the other component, bound d_H(v)≤r=2t+1. As ambient degree ≥k=r+t, v must touch every vertex of X, in particular x. All d_H(u)≥r neighbors would therefore lie among the t other x-contacts of C, impossible.

The separate prohibition on adjacent high X vertices assigned to distinct components still works at equality: at least r of y's contacts remain in its assigned component, leaving ≤t C-witnesses from x, while opposite-color high neighbors u,v adjacent to x provide at least 2r−t=3t+2>k other two-step witnesses at y. Deleting the possible low vertex preserves connectivity of G and joins no distinct assigned components, so H has at most one nonsingleton component. The prior connected component class bound applies at k≥3t+1 and at most one isolated low vertex remains. The resulting n≤2k+t+3=7t+5≤3k for t≥1 is within the earlier order theorem. The improved triangle-transversal bound at k=3t+1 would need its own nonbipartite-remainder argument.
