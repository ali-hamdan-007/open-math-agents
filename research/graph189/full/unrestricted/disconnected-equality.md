# Bipartite deletion at k=3t+1

Let G be finite and 2-connected. Let t=|X|≥1, H=G−X bipartite, and k=3t+1 its ambient second-smallest degree. Suppose every vertex has at most k vertices at distance exactly two in G. Then H has at most one nonsingleton component. If the stronger even-distance condition of Graph 189 holds, G is traceable.

Put r=k−t=2t+1. Every high vertex, namely one of degree at least k in G, has at least r H-neighbors if it lies in H. Each member u of a nonsingleton H-component C has a high H-neighbor. For a high u this follows from r≥2 and the uniqueness of a possible low vertex. A low u has an H-neighbor by assumption, and all other vertices are high. If v is such a high neighbor, at least r−1 other vertices in N_H(v) share u's color in C and have distance exactly two from u in G. Therefore at most k−(r−1)=t+1 vertices outside C have distance two from u.

Suppose a high x∈X touches two nonsingleton components C,D. It has at least k−(t−1)=2t+2 neighbors in H. Take an x-neighbor in each component. From the contact in C, at most t+1 of the H-neighbors of x can lie outside C. The contact in D gives the corresponding bound outside D. Thus there are exactly t+1 x-contacts in each C,D and no H-neighbors of x elsewhere. Choose C to avoid the possible low vertex, swapping C and D if needed, and fix u∈N(x)∩C. For every v∈N_H(u), the d_H(v)−1 other vertices in N_H(v) together with the t+1 x-contacts in D are distinct distance-two vertices of u. Thus d_H(v)≤2t+1=r. The vertex v is high, so it has exactly r H-neighbors and is adjacent to all t vertices of X, including x. Hence every H-neighbor of u is among the t+1 x-contacts in C and differs from u. This gives d_H(u)≤t, contrary to d_H(u)≥r. A high x consequently touches a unique nonsingleton component whenever H has such components; its at least 2t+2 H-neighbors cannot be all isolated since at most one H-vertex is isolated.

For completeness, if high x,y∈X are adjacent and assigned to different components C and D, at least r H-neighbors of y in D are at distance two from x, since at most one of y's H-neighbors could be an isolated low vertex. Thus x has at most t distance-two vertices in C. It has at least r neighbors in C, so choose a high such neighbor u. Of the at least r opposite-color H-neighbors of u, at least r−t=t+1≥2 are also x-neighbors. Choose a high one v. The two H-neighborhoods of u,v have opposite colors and total size at least 2r. At most t of their union can be outside N(x), as these would be distance-two vertices of x. Hence x has at least 2r−t=3t+2>k neighbors in C. They are all at distance two from y, impossible. Every remaining edge stays inside a single assigned component after deleting the unique low vertex, if present. Two such components would separate the remaining graph, contrary to 2-connectivity. This proves the component claim.

The proved bipartite neighborhood intersection theorem at k≥3t+1 bounds each color class of the unique nonsingleton component by k+1. The possible isolated low vertex contributes at most one more. Therefore n≤2k+t+3=7t+5≤3k for t≥1. The earlier paper order theorem for n≤3k gives traceability under the full metric condition. If G is not 2-connected, use the cut-vertex case of Easwar and Johari. The 2-connected component obstruction itself uses only the distance-two bound and has no dependence on the order or circumference theorems.
