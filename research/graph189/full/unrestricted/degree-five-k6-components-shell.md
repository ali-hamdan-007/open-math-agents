# Component reduction and prescribed shell routes at k=6

Suppose z is the unique vertex of degree five, the second-smallest degree is six, the even-distance cap is six, n>20, and G−N[z] is disconnected. The earlier 2-connectivity and remote-component results apply. In particular all vertices at distance at least three belong to one component D, and the eccentricity of z is at most four. Let A=N(z) be the five anchors. Any component of G−N[z] has at least 6−5+1=2 vertices and at least two vertices adjacent to A by 2-connectivity. There are at most six vertices in the second layer, so there are at most three components altogether.

If there are three components, the two pure components each have order two and are edges complete to all five anchors. The remote component has precisely two ports u,v. There are six second-layer vertices so there is no fourth layer. The third layer T has at least 21−6−6=9 vertices. Its subsets S_u,S_v are the contacts of the two ports and cover T. An anchor cannot touch both ports because |T|>6. For any anchor of u, all S_u are at distance two and there is at least one other witness: a missed anchor through z, or, if complete to the other anchors, the missed port v via one of its anchors. Thus |S_u|,|S_v|≤5, and |D|≤12. The two sets are nonempty by 2-connectivity and have distinct representatives because their union has order at least nine. Add one marker adjacent to u,v and close only core pairs for Hamilton paths, at threshold |D|≤12. All core vertices have degree at least six there, so the core becomes a clique. A spanning path runs from the marker through u, the whole core between distinct contacts, and v. Reverse closure. An internal marker can be expanded through the shell between distinct anchors of u,v, and an endpoint marker can be replaced by a shell path ending at an anchor of its one path neighbor. The requisite shell path between any prescribed anchors a_1,a_2 is, with the other anchors labeled a_3,a_4,a_5,

    a_1,x_1,a_3,x_2,a_4,z,a_5,x_3,x_4,a_2,

where x_3,x_4 form one pure component and x_1,x_2 the other.

Suppose there is exactly one pure component K. With r=|K|, s the number of ports of D, and a=6−r, the inequalities 2≤s≤a≤4 give exactly the usual six pairs. Write F for the fourth layer and T for the third. Since r+s+|F|≤6 by the even cap at z, |T|≥21−6−6=9. No anchor meets all ports, and each port has at most five contacts in T by the same additional-witness argument used above.

The shell on A∪{z}∪K has a spanning path between *any* prescribed distinct anchors, for all six parameter pairs.

* For r=4 there are two ports, so an anchor loses at most one neighbor on restriction to the shell. Hence every shell anchor has degree at least five. Members of K retain degree at least six; after adjoining a degree-two endpoint marker, the order is eleven, so every missing anchor–K edge and every missing K–K edge closes for Hamilton cycles.
* For r=3 and s≤3 an anchor loses at most two neighbors. Thus its shell degree is at least four, and the same argument works at marker order ten.
* In both cases the completed K-clique is joined fully to A. Order the five anchors with the prescribed ones at the ends and put z in one of the four gaps, the remaining three gaps containing nonempty runs of K. Add the marker to form a cycle and reverse the closure edges. Deleting the marker yields the prescribed path of the original shell.
* For r=2, K is an edge complete to the five anchors. No anchor can be isolated in G[A]. Such an anchor would have just z and the two K-vertices available in the shell, so it would have to touch at least three ports. The four other anchors at distance two would leave at most two even slots for all the contacts of those ports in T. At most one port would remain, giving |T|≤2+5=7, a contradiction. Consequently G[A] has at least three edges. Given distinct prescribed anchors, choose any anchor edge different from their own pair. Arrange the five anchors with the prescribed two at the ends and the chosen edge consecutive (possible since the edge does not consist of both endpoints). Use z and the two K-vertices to separate the remaining three consecutive anchor pairs. This path uses only original edges.

Thus the exceptional three-component case is traceable, and in the two-component case every marked construction from the larger-degree argument can expand through a prescribed shell path also at k=6.
