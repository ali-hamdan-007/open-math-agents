The anchor degree bound and the lower bound h≥2k−3+d−s>k force every anchor to miss at least one T vertex, eliminate d=5, and at d=4 require exactly one miss plus adjacency to every other anchor. Two unequal misses cover T between their anchors; their two sets of visible remotes have overlap ≤2k−2−h≤1. If there are ≥3 miss labels, any given t touches two anchors missing different other labels, forcing ≤1 neighbor in R per t and contradicting h>|T|. With exactly x,y missed, every W vertex must have an R-neighbor: without one it needs all other T vertices for degree k, then sees the entire R at distance two. Thus there is one common contact v, h=2k−3, s=4; the two anchor-visible sets each have k−1 members with intersection exactly {v}. The exclusive contacts X,Y have sizes k−2 and singleton T masks.

Any W member must miss exactly one of x,y and no other T peer. If it met both, its ≥2k−4 R nonneighbors would be distance two. For a W member on the x-side, z,y,X already consume k slots; thus v cannot meet F or Y. If both W types occurred, v would lose X and Y and have only k−4 potential neighbors. All W therefore choose x. Each anchor has precisely its unique missed T and its k−1 visible R vertices as even witnesses. Every f must lie at distance three from each anchor (layer difference ≥3, diameter ≤4, and distance four impossible), forcing a neighbor on both X and Y since v misses F. At y, W,z,A_x,F give k−1+|A_x| distance-two vertices; hence |A_x|=1 and no further witnesses may occur. In particular X–Y edges and xy are impossible. The vertex v must meet ≥4 X vertices by its degree; yv would therefore spend an additional y slot, so yv is absent.

For the partition objective, holding the other three assignments fixed gives the claimed score difference. Adding the two hypothetical scores gives d_X+d_Y−4−(3−d_F)=d_G−7≥0; at a maximum the assigned score is at least the alternate and thus nonnegative. This yields d_C(f)+d_assigned(f)≥|F_C|+1. If needed when the X-side is empty, a member with ≥2 X neighbors exists by pigeonhole from ≥5 X vertices all requiring at least one F neighbor. Moving the one member preserves every lower bound on the other side and satisfies the bound 2 on its new side.

Interpret each side graph before closure as the induced graph on its indicated vertices with a new leaf at p. For k=8,t=1 on X, the two core degrees are ≥4 each, matching threshold 8 on nine vertices. In general the condition is k+t≥9 on X and k+t≥7 on Y; the latter covers k=7,t=0. For k=7 moving v into the X core is justified because its only potential neighbors are five X vertices, x and the one W; it therefore meets all seven. X degrees after restricting are ≥3+t, so for t≥1 their sums are ≥7+t, exactly the correct threshold when the side core has six vertices. After the core clique is complete, every core-to-assigned-F pair meets threshold |C|+t+1, with |C|≥t+1. The displayed leaf-rooted traversal therefore exists in the closure. During Hamilton-path reversal the leaf still has degree one and remains an endpoint; both restored side paths have their original specified port endpoint. The shell with x,w_1,v,w_2,... for k≥8 or x,w_1 for k=7 uses only original edges and all five anchors and z can be ordered as claimed, ending next to y. A wording improvement would replace “Join each to all its assigned F_C and its own port” with “Take the induced graph on C∪F_C∪{p}”; the degree and closure argument already uses this latter meaning. No assumption of a complete join to F_C is needed. This result reduces prospective degree-five survivors of diameter ≤4 to d≤3 for k≥7; it does not cover k=6 or all connected-neighborhood cases.

Since every f has anchor distance three or four by layers and diameter, it must be reached in three steps through x and a member of S_x. Thus every f has x-distance two. Together with z and A_y this gives s≤|A_x|. Two saturated supports would force 2s≤5. Therefore they must have sizes five and four, disjoint, with h=9 and s=4. Then the size-five support forces |A_x|=4, so at y, z and A_x leave only one even slot. Any remote of the smaller support has at most four neighbors counting that support and y; every possible other neighbor is a different-layer-F or opposite-support remote, both missed by y, hence counts toward the one remaining slot. Degree ≥6 is impossible. At k=6 the claimed branch itself is impossible above order 20. Across all k≥6 the necessary d≤3 conclusion follows under the stated diameter-at-most-four assumption.
