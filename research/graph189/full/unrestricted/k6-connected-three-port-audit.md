# Independent audit of the three second-layer vertices at k = 6

The census and its stated residual conclusion pass. Assume a unique degree-five vertex z, positive even-distance cap six, all five of its neighbors and every other vertex of degree at least six, diameter at most four, and order at least 21. The three-port, three-fourth-layer configuration is impossible. Together with the earlier defect-four and three-exception arguments, every nontraceable graph in this range has at least four vertices in the second layer of z and at most two in its fourth layer.

I checked the submitted C++17 source, SHA-256 e647296cded42ae2ddd4a464a96486a83909b8b6bc48c7a4c9be5245ee43145a, against k6-connected-three-port-proof.md, SHA-256 c3e1a5853a5315b412aedcaf72a56c89d79d4349ba2d91c25c400d674096f33c. Compiling it independently with g++ -O2 -g -std=c++17 -fsanitize=undefined,bounds -fno-sanitize-recover=all reproduced all seven rows in k6-connected-probe-checked.log with no diagnostics. This replays the original enumeration.

* Relabel all five anchors jointly and all three second-layer vertices jointly. Sorting port-contact masks up to these permutations loses no configuration because the program then iterates every anchor graph and every port graph on the chosen labels. Individual anchors meet at least one port by degree, and miss at least one since all h≥9 core vertices have a port. The five subsets jointly cover the port set by the definition of second layer. A complete-to-anchors anchor sees any missed port in two steps. This gives the cap of five on its union of core contacts, also valid if an anchor misses some other anchor. It follows directly that h≤15, justifying all array lengths and the bit shifts on h.
* The computed anchor and port shell distance-two witnesses have explicit two-edge paths inside the shell. They are nonneighbors of the vertex charged. Counting z uses a separate slot for each port. The lower support bound enforces the port degree. Each port-adjacent support outside its own creates an additional even witness. Downward propagation of upper bounds and addition across two complementary submasks are both one-sided necessary estimates. The multisets of core contact masks and fourth-layer witness masks may be sorted independently.
* A fourth-layer vertex f has at least four core neighbors. Every such neighbor's port mask is contained in f's mask. If the anchor and f have disjoint masks, their distance is four. Any three-edge route between them must increase layers at every step, since it goes from layer one to layer four. Overlap of two nonadjacent ports in the core causes a new distance-two witness only when the shell has not already charged it. The core distance-two port masks are disjoint from their contact masks and must contain missed ports adjacent to a contact.
* If a vertex i touches only p, it has at most |S_p| neighbors among its own support and the three ports combined. It has at most the number of f masks containing p additional neighbors in the fourth layer. When this total falls below six it must have a core neighbor outside S_p. Such a neighbor belongs to a different port, guaranteeing the required nonempty distance-two port mask for i. Sorting these masks among identical contact types is valid since neither incidence to individual fourth-layer vertices nor core edges have yet been fixed.
* Every actual core-core edge survives the two mask-containment tests. Every actual core-f edge survives its single mask-containment test. Counting all of these and both possible f-f neighbors yields upper bounds on achievable degrees. The final option-set test excludes an f mask containing a port not attained by any of f's potential core neighbors. This cannot exclude an actual graph, since the intermediate vertex on a two-edge route from f to a port lies in the third layer.

For h=9 the shell, core-mask, fourth-mask, further-distance, remote-even-mask, degree and final tallies were respectively 302, 312, 15,645, 9, 8, 7 and 0. For h=10 they were 32, 14, 784 and four zeros. For each h=11 through 15 all seven tallies were zero.

I also checked the dependency degree-five-connected-three-exceptions.md for the case with three ports and at most two fourth-layer vertices. The bound h≥12−s makes a disjoint pair of anchor misses possible only with singleton misses, h=10 and s=2; the third port then misses at most one of its possible neighbors among anchors and other ports. It cannot see all ten core vertices in two steps and therefore misses one of the two exceptional ports, exceeding its cap via the remaining port, z and the missing port. Intersecting miss pairs with empty total intersection force precisely the three exceptional pairs. Each port then has at most four core contacts. A private core vertex that cannot meet a core vertex outside its support needs two neighbors in the fourth layer. At s≤1 this is impossible for at least one of the at least ten private vertices. At s=2 the two fourth-layer vertices consume at least four of the five port slots, leaving at least seven private vertices needing both fourth-layer neighbors. They require two distinct supports of multiplicity at least two. Each of these supports then exhausts its port's slots, precluding sufficient degree at a private vertex of the last port. The fourth-layer size never exceeds three when the second layer has three members by the even cap at z.
