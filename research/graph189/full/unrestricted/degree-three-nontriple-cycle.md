# A remote cycle and a spanning path in the six remaining degree-three templates

Assume the preceding high-order degree-three reduction, n>3k+2. The root has eccentricity three. The three templates whose only exceptional ports are the three true privates have already been excluded by the three-private-port boundary argument. In the remaining six templates the second layer has k vertices. Let W be the ports seeing all three anchors and let e∈{4,5,6} be the number of other, exceptional ports. These templates are the anchor path with masks (1,2,4,6), and the five triangles with one, two or three extra exceptions from the six-mask list in `degree-three-two-anchor-order.md`. For each anchor i let Vᵢ consist of the third-layer vertices visible at distance two to i. Put R=L₃, c=|R|≥2k−1. Let h count remotes in all three Vᵢ, b those in exactly two, and F the induced graph on the remotes in at most two. Put m=|F|=c−h.

**Theorem.** Suppose F is 2-connected and k≥(e+1)h+e(e+1)+3 (uniformly, k≥7h+45 suffices). Then F has a Hamilton cycle. Let B be the set of the h triple-visible remotes. If every subset S⊆B has at least 2|S|−1 distinct neighbors in W, then G has a spanning path. In particular it suffices that each triple has at least 2h−1 W-neighbors. Under the connectivity and degree bounds, a nontraceable graph therefore has a nonempty S⊆B with at most 2|S|−2 common-layer neighbors between its members. Here this is the cardinality of the union of their W-neighborhoods.

For each anchor let fᵢ be its number of positive even-distance witnesses outside R; write f=Σfᵢ. Each fᵢ≥2: in a triangle the two foreign true privates suffice, and in the anchor path the center sees the two leaf privates while each leaf sees the center private and the opposite anchor. In a triangle all missed ports are at distance two, so f is at least the total number of missing incidences of exceptional ports with A. For the anchor path just listed the center additionally sees the mask-6 port at distance two, giving f≥7. Consequently f≥7 throughout. The only templates in which f could equal seven are this path and the triangle with three true privates and a single extra double mask. In both there is exactly one private per anchor. The remaining templates each have at least eight missing exceptional incidences. By counting anchor visibilities,

    m+b+3h=Σᵢ |Vᵢ|≤3k−f.                                        (1)

Every nontriple x has only exceptional ports as its second-layer neighbors. If it misses anchor i's visibility, all its port neighbors miss i, of which there are at most three by the anchor degree. Consequently x has at most three port neighbors. An exclusive vertex has at most two, since every template has at most two privates of one type; for either possible f=7 template it has just one. Therefore δ(F)≥D=k−h−3.

**Independence.** Suppose I⊆F is independent with |I|=D+1. For distinct x,y∈I write sₓ,sᵧ for their numbers of port neighbors. The sum of their F-degrees is at least 2k−2h−sₓ−sᵧ. Their neighbors all lie in the m−|I|≤2k−2h−f−b+2 vertices outside I. In all cases sₓ+sᵧ<f+b−2. Indeed for f≥8 the upper bound min(6,4+b) is less than f+b−2. For f=7 the bound min(6,2+2b) is less than 5+b for b=0,1 and for b≥2. Thus every two members of I have a common neighbor.

For an exceptional port J let Sⱼ=N(J)∩R, let Hⱼ consist of Sⱼ together with all its neighbors in R, and put aⱼ=|N(J)∩W|. Its support Sⱼ is contained in some Vᵢ, so |Sⱼ|≤k−2. Each remote in Hⱼ∖Sⱼ has distance two from J. Other disjoint distance-two witnesses are the k−e−aⱼ missed W, z, and at least one missed anchor; the latter is adjacent to an anchor in J's proper mask because the anchor graph is connected. Thus |Hⱼ∖Sⱼ|≤e−2+aⱼ. If x∈I∩Sⱼ, all of N_R[x] is within Hⱼ and |N_R[x]|≥k−2 by its at most three supporting ports. This closed neighborhood misses every other member of I∩Sⱼ. Furthermore x has at distance two the aⱼ common neighbors of J, at least one anchor, and all |I|−1 of its independent peers, in disjoint sets. It follows that

    aⱼ≤h+2,    |I∩Sⱼ|≤1+|Hⱼ|−(k−2)≤e−1+aⱼ≤h+e+1.

Since the e exceptional supports cover I, this would give k−h−2≤e(h+e+1), contrary to the assumed bound. Hence α(F)≤D.

**Independent triples.** We show that every independent triple in F has F-degree sum at least m+2. Write s for the sum of its three port-neighbor counts. By (1), s≤f+b−2 suffices. For f≥8 use s≤min(9,6+b)≤f+b−2. For f=7 use s≤min(9,3+2b), which suffices for b=0,1,2 or b≥4. In the remaining case f=7, b=3, there are four exceptional ports, with exactly one private per anchor, and the following auxiliary bound gives m≤2k+3.

A common port w sees at most two exceptional ports. If it saw exactly three and omitted J, choose an anchor i in the mask of J. Every nontriple outside Vᵢ sees an exceptional port other than J and is at distance two from w. Their number is at least m−(|Vᵢ|−h)≥c−k+2≥k+1. If w saw all four, every nontriple would be at distance two; m≥2k−1−h>k under the theorem's hypothesis. Thus Σⱼ aⱼ≤2(k−4). The four external remote boundaries Hⱼ∖Sⱼ have combined size at most Σⱼ(aⱼ+2)≤2k. Every exclusive x has a single supporting private, so |N_R[x]|≥k>|Vᵢ| for its visible anchor i. It has a remote neighbor y outside Vᵢ, supported by an exceptional port J that x misses. Thus x belongs to Hⱼ∖Sⱼ. There are at most 2k exclusives and just three pair-visible vertices in F, giving m≤2k+3 as claimed. The hypothesis k≥5h+23 in this four-port case implies 3D≥m+2.

Finally F is 2-connected by hypothesis. The independent-triple degree-sum criterion of Bauer, Morgana, Schmeichel and Veldman gives Hamiltonicity when every independent triple has degree sum at least max(m+2,3α(F)); the case without an independent triple also satisfies the criterion. We have proved the first bound, and 3δ(F)≥3D≥3α(F) gives the second. This proves the remote-cycle assertion. The original degree-sum theorem is Theorem 10 of their 1990 paper on long cycles, as cited in the prior six-port remote-cycle proof.

## From the remote cycle to a spanning path

At least two anchor classes have exactly one true private. Choose one such class i and call its private P. At least c−|Vⱼ|−|Vₗ|+h≥(2k−1)−2(k−2)+h=h+3 remotes are visible only to i, since the other two visibility sets overlap in all h triples. Choose such a remote q. Its sole port neighbor is P, and q lies on the Hamilton cycle of F just proved. Reserve P for entering the cycle at q.

Let M=2(h+e−1). For each unreserved exceptional port J with aⱼ<M, consider X=N(J)∩F on the oriented F-cycle. Its degree in G is at least k, and it can lose at most aⱼ common ports, two anchors, e−1 other exceptionals and h triples when restricted to F. Thus |X|≥k−aⱼ−e−h−1. Any cycle successor outside X of an X vertex lies in Hⱼ∖Sⱼ, of size at most e−2+aⱼ. The oriented cycle therefore has at least

    |X|−|Hⱼ∖Sⱼ| ≥ k−2aⱼ−2e−h+1 ≥ k−5h−6e+7

edges whose endpoints both see J. This is at least e−1: the assumed lower bound on k exceeds or equals 5h+7e−8, since the difference is (e−4)h+e²−6e+11≥0 for e=4,5,6. Choose one distinct eligible edge per such J greedily, as there are at most e−1 such ports. Subdividing the chosen edges by their corresponding ports yields a simple expanded cycle. Sharing a cycle endpoint between consecutive chosen edges creates no repetition. Since the sole port neighbor of q is the reserved P, neither incident cycle edge at q was chosen. Open the expanded cycle at q to obtain a spanning path on its expanded vertex set starting there.

The neighborhood hypothesis on B allows distinct W partners for all but at most one of the two slots at each triple. Indeed form the bipartite graph between two copies of every member of B on the left and W on the right, with adjacency inherited from G. Add one new right vertex adjacent to every left vertex. For a nonempty set of left copies with set S of underlying triples, the original W-neighborhood has size at least 2|S|−1, so the augmented neighborhood has size at least 2|S| and hence at least the number of copies. Hall supplies a matching of every left copy; removing the new right vertex leaves at most a single unmatched copy in total. If there is one, its triple t₀ still has its other slot matched to a W vertex w₀. For h=0 this construction is empty.

Assign each remaining unreserved exceptional port, with aⱼ≥M, a disjoint pair of W-neighbors of J. This is possible after the triple assignments: before the last such selection at most 2h+2(e−2)=M−2 vertices are unavailable. For every full pair, orient its two W endpoints arbitrarily and form a block consisting of the first W vertex, its assigned triple or port, and the second W vertex, in that order. Every unused W vertex is a one-vertex block. If a partly matched triple t₀ exists, take w₀ as its own one-vertex block and reserve t₀ to prepend later. Let B₀ be the number of blocks. Every full pair reduces the initial count |W|=k−e by one; there are at most h+e−1 such pairs. Hence B₀≥k−h−2e+1.

Each w∈W misses at most h+2 other W vertices: outside W its neighbors comprise at most three anchors, e exceptional ports, and h triples. On the blocks form an undirected compatibility graph in which two blocks are adjacent when the last vertex of each sees the first of the other in G. The first W endpoints of distinct blocks are distinct, as are their last endpoints. Therefore for each block at most 2(h+2) other blocks fail compatibility. The compatibility graph has minimum degree at least B₀−1−2(h+2)≥B₀/2. Indeed B₀≥4h+10 follows from the degree hypothesis, because k−h−2e+1≥eh+e²−e+4≥4h+16 for e≥4. By Dirac this graph has a Hamilton cycle. Open it at the block w₀ if one was designated, and otherwise at any block. Traverse the resulting ordered blocks in their fixed orientations. All joining edges are original. If necessary prepend t₀ ahead of w₀ to cover the remaining triple. The last vertex of this path lies in W.

The graph on the anchors together with z has a spanning path ending at anchor i and beginning at an anchor: if the other two anchors are adjacent, start with their edge and append z,i. Otherwise i belongs to an anchor edge, so start at the anchor outside that edge, traverse z and then the edge ending at i. The anchor graph has an edge in all six templates. Join the last W vertex to the first anchor, traverse the four-vertex shell, continue through i,P,q, and then follow the opened expanded remote cycle (q is used once). This covers every vertex exactly once. This proves the spanning-path assertion.

Within the stated k and F-connectivity range, failure of this sufficient matching condition therefore leaves a nonempty set S of triples whose union of W-neighbors has size at most 2|S|−2. For example, when h=1 the sole remaining obstruction to this routing is a triple with no W-neighbor. The proof makes no traceability claim in that obstruction or for disconnected F.
