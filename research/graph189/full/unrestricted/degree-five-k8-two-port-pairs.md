# (4,4) at k=8: two Q-pairs close

For a pair p,r write m=|H_i|, N=m+2, a=|S_p−S_r|, b=|S_r−S_p|. Every S has size ≤7. There are no cross-port or cross-incidence edges; D must have a cross-core edge between its two blocks.

If p,r are adjacent then a,b≤3 and min(a,b)≥m−7. At p, the b private r vertices are remote-even witnesses disjoint from all opposite-block cross-endpoints. Thus any x touching p loses at most 3−b core neighbors to the opposite block; for x touching r use 3−a. Core degrees in the block are ≥5+min(a,b)≥m−2. Each port also has degree ≥m−2 because it misses at most three core vertices and sees its mate. For m≥7, twice m−2 is ≥m+3=N+1, proving Hamilton-connectedness by marked closure. For m≤6 the original core minimum is at least five and hence m≥4. At m=4 all core vertices are complete internally since N=6, and the ports are adjacent too. At m=5 or 6 the core pairs qualify initially. After those pairs close, each has degree at least m by its own port neighbor; with port degrees ≥m−2 all remaining core–port pairs qualify as 2m−2≥m+3. This proves Hamilton-connectedness of the adjacent-pair block.

If p,r are nonadjacent then they overlap, spend one slot on each other and have |B_p|,|B_r|≤2. Each directional difference is initially ≤4. Equality b=4 would force exactly three common vertices and |S_r|=7 by the lower bound |S_p∩S_r|≥3. A common x has exactly two port neighbors, hence ≥6 core neighbors. It has only two common peers and at most four other possible core neighbors in B_p∪B_r. Equality forces disjoint B_p and B_r of size two and all four adjacent to x. No such boundary vertex can belong to the opposite block as then it would lie in both boundaries through x. Thus B_p,B_r both lie in H_i. No cross edge from H_i would be possible since every opposite neighbor of any H_i vertex lies in one of these two boundaries. This contradicts connected D. Interchange p,r to exclude a=4 as well.

It follows that a,b≤3, m≤10, N≤12. Every port has at least m−3=N−5 core neighbors and each core vertex has internal degree ≥6 by its own port's boundary. All port–core pairs qualify at marker threshold N+1; the port pair then qualifies as m≥5 (core min six). High core vertices (degree ≥8) qualify against all others. At most six low vertices are possible since all vertices losing a cross edge belong to the union of two opposite boundaries. If N≤11, minimum core degree six suffices. The sole other failure is N=12 and six residual vertices X all still of degree six: with fewer, every residual vertex sees ≥7 originals, and once a residual vertex has degree seven it qualifies with every remaining one. Each x∈X requires ≥2 cross neighbors in Y, where Y is contained in B_p∪B_r and hence |Y|≤4. Any y has at most three X-neighbors by its own port's boundary. Thus 12≤e(X,Y)≤3|Y|≤12. Equality forces Y of size four and B_p,B_r to be disjoint two-sets lying entirely in the opposite block. At m=10, a,b≤3 and |S_p|,|S_r|≤7 force a=b=3 and four common vertices. No common vertex can touch a private vertex of its own block because that private vertex would be in B_p or B_r. It cannot have any cross neighbor, as that neighbor would be in both disjoint boundaries. It therefore has at most three common peers and two port neighbors, below required degree eight. Contradiction. This proves Hamilton-connectedness for each nonadjacent-pair block as well.

The existing connected-D crossing and shell splice completes the path. In particular, the split of the four ports into two Q-pairs is settled at k=8 for every h.
