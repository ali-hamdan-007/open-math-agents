# All three-port cases at k=6

Use the k=6 prescribed-shell result and component reduction of the companion note. For (a,s)=(3,3), the uniform order count applies unchanged: when the third layer has order h>6, at most six private contacts and at most five contacts per port give h≤⌊21/2⌋=10. The first-order h=9 clique closure and the h=10 core equality and rotation proofs are unchanged at k=6. In the former argument the closure threshold is twelve and all core degrees are at least six. In the latter the connected-core equality lemma has m=4 and the disconnected case consists of two five-cliques. Nothing beyond the shell used the previous lower cutoff. Thus (3,3) is traceable.

For (4,3), the optional-fourth-layer first-order proof at |R|=10 also uses m=4 and remains valid with the new shell. For |R|>10, the saturation and private-contact bounds apply unchanged: a third layer of order H≥11 would give at least k+4=10 private contacts, exceeding nine. Thus the only higher possibility is |R|=11, consisting of H=10 third-layer vertices and a fourth-layer vertex f. If every port had at most four third-layer contacts, there would be at least k+2=8 private contacts, exceeding the bound seven when f is present. The saturation argument therefore supplies a partition into two five-sets E and V with ports p,q,r such that

    E=S_p,  V=S_q∪S_r,  E∩V=∅.

The vertex f touches both sets because d(f)≥6. The vertices in either set see only ports and remote-core vertices. Every port has at most three even witnesses besides the two vertices of K and z.

We will use the elementary odd-order case of the longest-path bound: a graph on 2m+1 vertices of minimum degree at least m has a spanning path. Indeed it is connected, and a longest path that omits a vertex cannot close into a cycle by connectedness. The shifted endpoint-neighborhood argument then gives at least 2m+1 path vertices. Moreover if the graph has no Hamilton cycle then every Hamilton path has both endpoint degrees exactly m, since a larger endpoint degree sum closes the path into a cycle.

E has minimum degree at least two by the (4,3) higher-order bound. Thus E has a spanning path with one endpoint contacting f. For a Hamilton cycle, break an edge incident to any f-contact. Otherwise take any spanning path. Each endpoint x has degree two in E. Since it touches only p, it needs three more neighbors in V∪{f}, all in B_p. The set B_p has at most three elements and includes f, so x meets f. The other endpoint can be attached to p since E=S_p.

Suppose q and r are nonadjacent. The general argument yields δ(V)≥2, so V is connected and traceable. In fact every vertex x∈V with just one port has d_V(x)≥3. If S_q and S_r meet, the ports see each other at distance two, leaving only two external core slots at either port. If S_q and S_r are disjoint with both nonempty, connectedness of V gives an edge between the sets, consuming at least one even slot at each port inside V. Again any singleton x has at most two neighbors outside V∪P. Both sets are nonempty: an inactive q, being nonadjacent to r, could reach degree six only through all five anchors and p. But the edge qp then puts the five E-vertices at distance two from q in addition to K∪{z}, a contradiction.

If the ends of a spanning V-path cannot be assigned distinctly to q,r, both are singletons and have degree at least three in V. Their degree sum closes the path into a Hamilton cycle. Any Hamilton cycle in V has an edge whose endpoints can be assigned to distinct ports, because S_q,S_r are nonempty and cover V. Break that edge. Thus a q-to-r traversal of V is available. Choose which of q,r to call q so that q and p admit distinct anchor representatives, possible by 2-connectivity. The route r,V,q,shell,p,E,f spans G.

If qr is an edge, J=G[V∪{q,r}] has order seven and minimum degree at least three, as in the higher-order manuscript. Hence it has a spanning path. If it has a Hamilton cycle, break it at any V-neighbor of f and use shell,p,E,f,J. If it has no Hamilton cycle then both endpoints of any spanning J-path have degree three in J. If one such endpoint x lies in V, choose a port t touching it. The vertex x needs three additional neighbors in E∪{f}, all even witnesses of t, fully consuming its three available remote slots. It follows that t meets every member of V, as any member it misses is another even witness through the other port. Since f meets V and t is complete to V, f itself is an even witness of t and hence one of those three actual neighbors of x. Use shell,p,E,f,x,J. If both endpoints of the J-path are ports, at least one can be assigned an anchor different from an assigned anchor of p. Orient the path to end at that port and continue through the shell,p,E,f. This proves (4,3) at k=6.
