# Arbitrarily many triples of bounded common-middle degree

Use the six unrestricted-order degree-three templates and notation of `degree-three-all-triples-routing.md`. Put d=max |N(t)∩W| over all triple-visible remotes t, taking d=0 for an empty triple set. If k≥11d+130, then G is traceable, with no restriction on h or on connectivity of the induced remote graphs. This is complementary to the theorem assuming large W-neighborhoods.

If k≥7h+84 the all-triples routing and connectivity theorems apply. Otherwise h>(k−84)/7≥(11d+46)/7. Hence 2h>3d+13, because 22d+92≥21d+91. Put D=k−d−e. Every vertex in the remote layer R has internal degree at least D: nontriples see only exceptional ports, and triples see at most d common and e exceptional ports. The order bound c≤3k−f−b−2h, with f≥7, e≤6, now implies c+2≤3D. Also 2h≤k−6 by c≥2k−1 and the same visibility bound.

If R is 2-connected, consider an independent set I of D+1 vertices. Each two have a common R-neighbor since c−|I|≤2D−3. Suppose a member t sees some w∈W. It must be a triple; by degree w misses at most h+2 other common middles. At distance two from t are at least k−e−d−h−2 common middles through w, its three visible anchors, and the D independent peers. These are disjoint, and their sum exceeds k since k−h≥(k+6)/2>2d+2e−1. Thus every member of I has no W neighbor, is covered by some exceptional support J, and has |N_R[x]|≥k−e+1. At x∈I∩Sⱼ its visible anchor plus the aⱼ W-neighbors of J and all D peers are disjoint even witnesses, so aⱼ≤d+e−1. Since |Hⱼ|≤k+e−4+aⱼ and N_R[x] misses other independent members of the support,

    |I∩Sⱼ|≤1+|Hⱼ|−(k−e+1)≤d+3e−5.

The e supports cover I; this contradicts k≥(e+1)d+3e²−4e, which follows from k≥11d+130 and e≤6. Thus α(R)≤D and 3δ(R)≥c+2; the Nash-Williams degree-and-independence criterion gives a Hamilton cycle in R.

If R is disconnected or connected with a cut vertex, take its components after deleting zero or one cut vertex as appropriate. Their sizes are at least D+1 in the disconnected case and D in the cut case. Since c≤3D−2 there are exactly two. Each has order m≤c−D−1≤2D−3 and minimum internal degree ≥D−1. An endpoint-marker cycle closure at order m+1 yields Hamilton-connectedness. Take a spanning cycle in each part, including the cut vertex in the cycle of a part where it has two neighbors by Hamilton-connectedness. Each part contains nontriples since D>h by k−h≥(k+6)/2>d+e. The total population of nontriples in the two parts is at least c−h−1>k−h−2, the maximum nontriple visibility of any one anchor or support of any exceptional port. Consequently choose distinct ports P,Q on the two parts and incident distinct anchors as in the two-cycle routing proof: start with distinct visible anchors; if the ports coincide, its two choices of anchor permit using it on the opposite side from any other port on either part. For a single R-cycle simply reserve the usual unique-class private and a solo.

Every unreserved exceptional port with aⱼ≤e has at least k−aⱼ−e−1 remote neighbors, and the external remote boundary has order ≤aⱼ+e−2. It can therefore be assigned a distinct subdivided edge on the one or two oriented cycles, since at least k−4e+1≥e−1 edges are eligible.

It remains to traverse W and the at most e−1 unreserved ports with aⱼ≥e+1, starting and ending in W. Choose distinct W endpoints and adjoin their endpoint marker to this induced graph, of augmented order N≤k. Let μ(w) count other W vertices missed by w and t(w) count its triple neighbors. Its degree gives μ(w)≤t(w)+2, and hence

    max μ≤h+2≤k/2−1,       Σ μ≤hd+2|W|.

Two W vertices can be completed at cycle-closure threshold N whenever their missed counts sum to at most k−2e−2, since |W|=k−e. Every W vertex of missed count ≤k/3 can first be completed to all W vertices: k/3+k/2−1≤k−2e−2 follows from k≥130 and e≤6. The number of remaining W vertices with original missed count >k/3 is less than 3hd/k+6≤3d/2+6. After the first completions only these vertices can miss one another. Thus the sum of any two remaining missed counts is less than 3d+12≤k−2e−2, completing W. The high exceptional ports can each be completed to every W vertex since their degree ≥e+1 and the W clique gives W degrees ≥k−e−1. Place these at most e−1 ports in distinct gaps between consecutive vertices of a W spanning path with the chosen endpoints. Close with the marker, reverse closure and remove the marker. This gives the required spanning path in the original outer part.

Finally concatenate this W path, the anchor-root frame, reserved ports, and opened expanded remote cycle or cycles exactly as in `degree-three-all-triples-connectivity.md` and `degree-three-all-triples-routing.md`. In the one-cycle case the W path precedes the four-vertex anchor-root path and reserved private; in the two-cycle case the two reserved ports attach their respective cycles to different anchors, with the third anchor, z and W between them. All vertices are covered.
