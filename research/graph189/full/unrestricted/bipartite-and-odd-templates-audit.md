# Independent audit of bipartite and odd-cycle blowup theorems

Both the high-order spanning-path claim for all connected bipartite bases and the high-order Hamilton-cycle claim for odd-cycle bases of length ≥5 pass. Modules may independently be cliques or independent sets, and all template edges must induce complete joins. The strict-expansion input used for mixed odd-cycle modules is the earlier theorem in /tmp/g189-2319/strict-expansion.md.

In the bipartite case, all other modules in one's part give positive even witnesses; an independent module counts its own twins as well. Under n>3k+2, one independent module in A implies W_A≤k+1 and W_B>2k+1. The minimum B weight then exceeds k+1, so a second B module would already yield too many even witnesses. The sole B clique can separate every A vertex individually. When all modules are cliques, m_A+m_B≥k+3 and at least one part has at most two modules by the two simultaneous minimum-weight cap inequalities. In a star the smallest peripheral module is either high or the only low singleton; in both cases the perimeter cap and a suitable high degree give (b−2)r≤p−1, hence b≤p+1. With |A|=2 and b≥2 both minimum weights are ≥3. If b≥3 the inequalities m_A≥k/2+3 and b≤k/3+1 make every clique large enough for the degree of its vertex in the base. Doubling a base spanning tree and taking one copy off its chosen endpoint path uses at most the base degree many module visits, so an open Euler traversal expands to a spanning path.

For an odd cycle, k=2 forces all module sizes to be one using the two distinct distance-two modules. If all modules are independent, summing counts over one representative in each module gives 3W−l≤lk and 2W≥(l−1)k+2; their combination forces W≤l. Otherwise deletion of any single graph vertex preserves connectedness (a path of nonempty modules remains if an entire singleton is deleted). For a nonempty independent set of independent-module indices, the incidence graph with its adjacent module indices has path components, since a cycle component could only be the entire odd base cycle and the incidence graph is bipartite. In each path the neighbor side has one more index than the selected side. Strict expansion of the corresponding independent set of blown-up vertices, followed by subtraction of module counts on that component, proves the required inequalities for a_i=w_i−1.

The fractional edge-amount LP has the stated covering dual. The negative levels of z=y−1_I lie in I and are independent; all their neighbors have corresponding positive level. Integrating the independently established a-neighborhood inequalities gives nonnegative dual surplus. The bounded primal therefore saturates all independent-module capacities. In an extremal feasible solution, a maximal noninteger path could be perturbed with alternating signs since its endpoints have nonintegral, hence slack, bounds and its edge amounts are positive. If all cycle edges are noninteger but some vertex sum is slack, alternating perturbation on the odd cycle preserves the other sums, also contradicting extremality. Thus all sums are integral and all fractional parts are one half. Alternating rounding down on the two edges incident with a clique module preserves every other sum and reduces only a nonrequired sum by one. Using multiplicities 1+2x_e then gives a connected Eulerian multigraph with each independent-module occurrence exact and each clique-module occurrence within capacity. Its cyclic traversal expands into the claimed Hamilton cycle.
