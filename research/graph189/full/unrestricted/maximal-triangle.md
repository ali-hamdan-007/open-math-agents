# A necessary metric witness at every triangle

Let G be a finite connected simple graph, k its second-smallest degree. Include each vertex itself in its set E(v) of vertices at even distance. Assume |E(v)| ≤ k+1 for every v, as in Graph 189.

**Claim.** If a triangle K has no vertex at odd distance equidistant from all three of its vertices, then n ≤ 3k+2. In particular, every triangle of a counterexample has an equidistant odd witness. If K is maximal as a clique, that witness has distance at least three from every vertex of K.

For every x, distances to the three members of K differ by at most one. Consequently all three distances are odd only when all three equal the same odd integer. By hypothesis the three even sets therefore cover G, giving n ≤ 3k+3. Suppose equality. The three sets then partition the vertices, so every vertex outside K at distance one from K is adjacent to exactly two members of K. If some vertex is at distance two from K, its neighbor on a shortest path to K is adjacent to two members. Both members are at distance two from the original vertex, contrary to the partition. Connectedness now implies that K dominates G. Every member of K has eccentricity at most two. Choose a minimum-degree vertex z. If z belongs to K, n ≤ 1+d(z)+k. Otherwise choose its neighbor c in K. The closed neighborhood of c lies in the radius-two ball at z, so 1+d(c) ≤ 1+d(z)+k. The radius-two ball about c is all of G, giving n ≤ 1+d(z)+2k ≤ 3k+1. Both cases contradict equality. This proves the claim. The independently reviewed traceability theorem through 3k+2 now applies. A witness at distance one from all of K would extend K to a four-clique.

**Chordal consequence.** Every qualifying connected chordal graph with a maximal clique of size at most three is traceable, with unrestricted diameter and order at the outset. Here a maximal clique means inclusion-maximal, irrespective of other clique sizes. To see this, no vertex can be equidistant to all members of any maximal clique K of size at least two in a finite connected chordal graph. Suppose all lie in the same positive distance layer r from x. Let C be their component in the graph on vertices of distance at least r. Every neighbor of C outside C is in layer r−1. The set S of such neighbors is a clique. Indeed, if nonadjacent a,b belonged to S, join them by a shortest path with interior in C, and by a second shortest path with interior at distance at most r−2, using the geodesics back to x. The induced paths have no edges between their interiors by the distance gap. They form a chordless cycle of length at least four. For r=1 the boundary is just x.

Every u in K has a neighbor in S on a shortest path to x. These nonempty sets N(u)∩S are nested: incomparable sets for u,v would give distinct a,b in S with au and bv present but av and bu absent, so a,u,v,b would form a chordless four-cycle. A member of the smallest set therefore touches all of K, contradicting maximality. If x is in K, its distances to itself and to a different member already differ. Thus a maximal triangle in a chordal graph has no equidistant odd witness, and the claim applies. For a maximal edge its two even sets cover G, so n ≤ 2k+2 ≤ 3k+2. A maximal singleton in a connected graph gives the trivial one-vertex case. As one example, this covers every qualifying chordal graph of treewidth at most two. It also covers arbitrarily large cliques elsewhere in the graph.

The metric witness conclusion applies to arbitrary qualifying graphs through the earlier bounded-order paper theorem. This manuscript establishes the new order estimates directly. No full-target kernel verdict is asserted.
