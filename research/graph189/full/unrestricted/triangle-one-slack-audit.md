# Independent audit of the triangle-transversal one-slack theorem

The nontransitive order bound, transversal threshold k≥3t+1 and disjoint packing consequence all pass.

The six nonnegative slacks sum to one from |F−A|+|F−B|=|F|+|F−(A∪B)|. Thus one can exhaust the cases by the position of the only unit. A saturated side has entire even set equal to the corresponding union. Its witnesses lie within distance three of everyone and see all X-neighbors of b, which is enough whenever a three-geodesic from b starts in X. At least one saturated side supplies eccentricity ≤4 for b. The ambient degree bound is d(b)≤|F|+t=k+φ.

With both sides saturated, if a three-geodesic begins at an a of either overlapping side, its endpoint lies in Ω∪F. If it begins at x∈X, its penultimate p is outside F, so one of the two saturated even sets omits p. An appropriate a adjacent to x must then be adjacent to p, placing the endpoint at distance two from a. For o=0 this gives exactly the claimed bound. When o=1, the one remaining possible initial neighbor a₀∈F−Ω already counts all r of F in its even set and supplies at most t+1 further third-layer vertices. Both outer indices are in Ω−F and have b-distance two: the middle and outer indices meet all X, so a saturated a on the first side sees the opposite index through X and cannot be adjacent to it, forcing it into that union but outside F. The reverse follows by symmetry. The subtraction by two is valid.

If only the B side is saturated, F_A has size t+1 and F_B has size t≥1. Using a member of F_B forces b₀∈B, thus b₂∈A by adjacency symmetry. An exception q outside Ω and three steps from b, not two steps from any F_A vertex, has shortest path b,x,p,q with x∈X. Its penultimate vertex p must be in B−F; otherwise a saturated F_B member sees p in one and q in two, contradicting q∉Ω. No F_A member is adjacent to p since q is not two steps from one. Each such member consequently spends its one extra even slot either on p through x if adjacent to x, or on x through b otherwise. Neither is in Z. Thus if an exception exists, every Z vertex follows the same geodesic pattern. Its penultimate point belongs to B−F, so it is at most two from b₂. It is not b₂, nor adjacent to b₂: all X are adjacent to b, hence Z⊂H, and any H neighbor of b₂ is by definition in B. Thus it is exactly two from b₂. The r−1 vertices of A−{b₂} and b itself are separate two-step witnesses (A∩B=∅ and both overlaps F_A,F_B are nonempty). This proves the stated |Z| restriction. If no exception exists, the t+1 F_A extra slots directly give |Z|≤t+1. The order and packing inequalities follow as written.

Optional strengthening: all nontransitive cases actually have n≤3k. For one unsaturated side, both outer indices in Ω−F have b-distance two, allowing their subtraction from the printed bound on L, giving n≤3k. For both saturated, o=0, the default bound is n≤3k+1−t+α+β, already ≤3k except when t=1 and α+β=1. If α=1 then the opposite index b₂ and b have all of X as neighbors; a saturated a∈A∩F is adjacent to all of X and is nonadjacent to b₂ (as a∉B), forcing b₂∈A and b₀∈B. Both indices can be subtracted; the β case is symmetric. Finally, when o=1, a₀∈F−Ω necessarily spends at least one of its extra even slots outside L: either on a missed X vertex through b, or if it sees all of X, on b₀ through any x (since a₀∉A and b₀∉F). These vertices have b-distance one or two. This improves the o=1 bound by one to n≤3k. Thus the older independently audited order theorem through 3k suffices in every case.

The transversal conclusion implies the disjoint packing conclusion by taking the union of any maximal disjoint triangle packing. It does not assert bipartiteness of the remainder in the nontransitive cases.
