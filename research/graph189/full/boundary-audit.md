# Independent audit of the order bound for Graph 189

I checked the November 28 versions of order-bound.md, saturated-independent.md and boundary.md. The stated implication holds: a connected finite simple graph satisfying the conjecture's even-distance hypothesis is traceable when its order n is at most δ+2k+1, where δ is the minimum and k is the second-smallest degree. The stronger distance-two-only assertion is established through δ+2k. The final extra order uses the full even-distance hypothesis in the saturated-independent-set and published small-order/non-2-connected cases. This is a handwritten theorem invoking published graph results.

## Endpoint of the previous order range

For an independent set of size k+2 or more, fix a member. It cannot see every other member at distance two. Choose a member at distance at least three. Their neighborhoods are disjoint and avoid the independent set; their degrees sum to at least δ+k. Thus n≥δ+2k+2, confirming α(G)≤k+1 throughout the claimed range. For n≤δ+2k the cone has connectivity c≥2. Its independent (c+1)-sets have degree sum at least δ+ck+c+1. The Chiba–Furuya–Ozeki–Tsugaki–Yamashita threshold is N+c+(c−2)(α−1), with N=n+1, and the bounds give the required inequality. When α≤c, Chvátal–Erdős applies directly. At the boundary n=δ+2k+1, 2-connectivity of G gives c≥3 in its cone, so α≤k still suffices. The statements at order two, for the complete cone or with no independent (c+1)-set, create no exceptional obstruction.

The saturated independent-set proof was checked separately, including its profile size lower bound, the balanced bipartite Hamilton cycle in the strict 2m<k case, and reserved connectors and slot counts for two or three profile classes. At the boundary, without a saturated maximum set and with α=k+1, every maximum set has a pair x,y at distance at least three. The three disjoint sets I,N(x),N(y) exhaust n by equality. If δ<k, the pair contains the unique degree-δ vertex z, so α(G−z)≤k.

## Toughness

Easwar and Johari's Theorem B settles graphs that fail 2-connectivity and also all cases with δ≤2 at the boundary. Hence assume G is 2-connected, k≥δ≥3 and n=3k+1−h, h=k−δ. If the cone is not 1-tough, its universal vertex belongs to a failing cut. With s additional cut vertices and t components, 2≤s, s+2≤t≤α≤k+1. Every component containing a high vertex has size at least k−s+1. Only the unique low vertex could form an exceptional component, for which one may subtract h from this lower bound. Thus n≥s+(s+2)(k−s+1)−h. On 2≤s≤k−2 the concave quadratic is at least 4k−2, strictly greater than 3k+1; a nonempty interval requires k≥4.

If s=k−1, then t=k+1 and the number of vertices outside the cut is 2k+2−h. All components have at least two vertices except possibly a singleton consisting of the unique low vertex; hence h≤1. When h=0 all components are pairs. When h=1 there are k pairs and one singleton. Each component vertex sees all k−1 cut vertices by its degree lower bound (δ=k−1 for the singleton), and every paired vertex sees its mate. A paired vertex therefore has 2k or 2k−1 distance-two vertices in the other components, in either case more than k. This verifies the cone toughness.

## The two circumference bounds

Vu Dinh Hoa's original article gives, for a 1-tough graph of order N≥3 with σ₃≥N, circumference at least min(N,N+σ₃/3−α+1). The division is by three as a real number, with no floor, and the final +1 is present. The cone's independent triple degree sum is at least δ+2k+3=N+1. Its independence number is at most k+1, so the lower bound is at least min(N,N+1−h/3). For integral h≤5 this is strictly larger than N−1. With no independent triple, use Chvátal–Erdős instead. Removing the cone vertex from a Hamilton cycle gives the spanning path.

For h≥5 after eliminating α≤k and saturated maximum sets, let J=G−z, M=|J|=δ+2k. Then α(J)≤k, minimum degree at least k−1, and M≤3k−5. If J is 2-connected, apply Theorem 10 of Bauer, Veldman, Morgana and Schmeichel with s₀=3(k−1)≥M+2. The precise conclusion is circumference at least min(M,M+s₀/3−α(J))≥M−1. It does not require J to be 1-tough. A missing independent triple is handled by Chvátal–Erdős. The regimes h≤5 and h≥5 overlap at 5, without a gap.

## Lifting back from J

If J has a spanning cycle, open it at a neighbor of z. If a longest cycle C of J misses one vertex w, every J-neighbor of w lies on C. Orient C. No two neighbors of w are consecutive, since then w inserts. Their successors form an independent set: a chord joining two successors lets one traverse the two complementary directed arcs in opposite orientations and join through w to obtain a spanning cycle. The successors are also nonadjacent to w. Hence d_J(w)+1≤α(J)≤k and, since d_G(w)≥k, wz is present. The path z,w followed by an opened C at any J-neighbor of w is spanning. The argument needs neither z to have two different contacts on C nor a Hamilton-connected C.

If J has a cutvertex v, J is connected because G is 2-connected. Each component of J−v has at least k−1 vertices, since every surviving vertex had at least k neighbors before removing z,v. Three components would require at least 3k−3 vertices in J−v, which has at most 3k−6. There are therefore two. Each has order r≤δ+k≤2k−5 and internal minimum degree at least k−2, whence every nonadjacent degree sum is at least 2k−4≥r+1. This is the correct Hamilton-connected threshold; alternatively the degree-two marker proof in boundary.md uses closure at threshold r+1 in the augmented order. Each component has a v-neighbor by connectivity of J and a z-neighbor by connectivity of G−v. If distinct choices from the two attachment sets were impossible, both sets would be the same singleton; that vertex would cut the component's remaining vertices from G. The components have size at least k−1≥2, so this really is a contradiction. Two Hamilton paths with distinct chosen ends concatenate through z and v into a spanning cycle of G.
