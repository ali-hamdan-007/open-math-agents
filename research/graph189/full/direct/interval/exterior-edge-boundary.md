# Every high–high exterior edge of a longest path lies in a triangle

Let G be a hypothetical 2-connected counterexample under the full Graph 189 hypotheses with k≥3. Let H be the vertex set of a longest nonspanning path. If x and y are adjacent omitted vertices and both have degree at least k, then x and y have a common neighbor in G.

Suppose they have no common neighbor. Write W=N[x]∪N[y], let B be the set of vertices outside W adjacent to some member of W, and put Z=V(G)−(W∪B). The d(y)−1 vertices in N(y) other than x are at distance exactly two from x. The analogous d(x)−1 vertices use even-distance places of y. Thus outside W at most one vertex can have positive even distance from x, and likewise from y. Every member of B has distance exactly two from at least one of x,y since its neighbor in W belongs to N[x] or N[y]. Hence |B|≤2.

Every attainable endpoint misses x and y, so the attainable endpoint set R is disjoint from W. By the endpoint-linear-growth theorem |R|≥k+ceil(k/2)+1>k+2. Consequently Z is nonempty. Since B separates the nonempty W and Z, 2-connectivity gives |B|≥2. Thus |B|=2, and the two vertices in it exhaust the respective even quotas outside W. Write b for the unique member of B at distance two from x, and b' for the other, which is uniquely at distance two from y. The quotas also imply d(x)=d(y)=k. No vertex can be at distance four from x because W lies in its radius-two ball and b already takes the only outside-W even place. Connectedness then gives eccentricity at most three for x. Each z∈Z is at distance three from x; its penultimate vertex on a geodesic cannot lie in W and must be b. Hence b is adjacent to every vertex of Z. Similarly b' is adjacent to every vertex of Z.

Choose a neighbor w∈W of b. By the definition of Z, w has no neighbor in Z. It therefore sees every vertex of Z at distance two via b, giving |Z|≤k. But then |R|≤|Z|+2≤k+2, a contradiction.

The same argument for an arbitrary adjacent omitted high pair gives a small separator: with c=|N(x)∩N(y)|, the number of vertices outside W at positive even distance from x is at most k−d(y)+1+c, and at most k−d(x)+1+c for y. Therefore |B|≤2(c+1)−(d(x)−k)−(d(y)−k). If R has more vertices than this boundary, deleting B separates W from at least |R|−|B| attainable endpoints. Taking k to be the second-smallest degree gives κ(G)≤k<|R|. If |B|≥|R| then |B|≥κ(G) holds directly; the separator gives |B|≥κ(G) in the complementary case. Thus every such edge satisfies c≥ceil((κ(G)+(d(x)−k)+(d(y)−k))/2)−1.
