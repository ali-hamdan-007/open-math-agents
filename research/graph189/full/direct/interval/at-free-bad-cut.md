# A component-count obstruction forces an asteroidal triple

Let G be a finite 2-connected graph and let k be a nonnegative integer. Suppose at most one vertex has degree below k, and every vertex has at most k vertices at distance exactly two. If some vertex set S satisfies c(G−S)>|S|+1, then G has an asteroidal triple consisting of vertices in three distinct components of G−S₀, where S₀ is any inclusion-minimal violating subset of S. In particular, every AT-free graph under these degree and distance-two hypotheses satisfies the component-count inequality for a spanning path.

An asteroidal triple is an independent triple for which each pair can be connected by a path avoiding the closed neighborhood of the third.

**Expansion and size comparison.** Write S for the inclusion-minimal violating cut, s=|S| and c=c(G−S)≥s+2. The elements of S are the anchors. An anchor touches a component if it has a neighbor in it. Every component touches at least two anchors by 2-connectivity. For nonempty U⊆S, the number of components touched by U is at least c−s+|U|≥|U|+2. Indeed, deleting the proper subset S−U leaves each component untouched by U as a separate component and leaves at least one further component containing a surviving anchor. Minimality bounds their number by s−|U|+1.

Call a vertex high if its degree is at least k. Suppose a high vertex x in C has r≥1 anchor neighbors and one such neighbor is complete to a different component D. The r anchors collectively touch at least r+2 components including C. From x one therefore sees all vertices of D and at least one in each of r further components, all at distance exactly two. Thus

    |D|+r ≤ k ≤ deg(x) ≤ |C|−1+r.

In particular an anchor cannot be complete to two high-containing components, by the opposite strict size inequalities. The only possible component without a high vertex is a singleton z comprising the unique low vertex.

**Limits on each anchor.** Suppose no asteroidal triple has representatives in three distinct components. Any anchor is partial to at most two touched components, where partial means the anchor has both a neighbor and a nonneighbor there. Otherwise choose a nonneighbor in each of three partially touched components. Each pair can connect inside their own two connected components through the anchor, avoiding the closed neighborhood of the third. It follows that each anchor touches at most four components. Every anchor touching four is complete to the low singleton z and to one high-containing component, and partial to the two others.

**A consecutive ordering.** Form the bipartite incidence graph B, with anchors on one side and components on the other. An asteroidal triple on its component side would lift to G. Choose arbitrary representatives of its three components. Every path in B between two components avoiding the closed neighborhood of the third uses only anchors that miss the third component entirely. Realize the incidences by edges of G and connect the consecutive contact vertices inside each component of the path. This gives a path between the two representatives avoiding the closed neighborhood of the third representative. Therefore B has no asteroidal triple on its component side. Tucker's consecutive-ones theorem gives an ordering C₁,...,C_c in which the components touched by each anchor are consecutive.

At least two anchors touch C₁. If each touched at most three components, their union of touched components would be contained in the first three, contrary to the expansion bound of four for this pair. Some anchor a therefore touches exactly the first four. Likewise an anchor b touches exactly the last four. Both have the low singleton z among their touched components. The two blocks of four consequently overlap and together cover all components. Let H be the high-containing component that a joins completely. From z every vertex of H is at distance two via a. In each of the other c−2 components besides z and H, at least one vertex is at distance two via a or b. We obtain k≥|H|+c−2≥|H|+s. A high vertex of H, however, has degree at most |H|+s−1. This proves the theorem.

The one-sided asteroidal triple formulation is also stated as Theorem 1.3 at https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/494/download.
