# The last mixed endblock at connectivity above half the second-smallest degree

Suppose G is a 2-connected qualifying nontraceable graph with k≥4 its second-smallest degree, δ its minimum degree, κ its connectivity, and κ>k/2. Write t=floor(k/2) and h=ceil(k/2). The results in `connectivity-and-exterior-cycles.md` reduce this to an arbitrary longest path P whose omitted graph is a single mixed component C with two leaf blocks, the unique below-k vertex z a noncut vertex of one leaf block. Let B be the other leaf block, w its unique cut vertex in C, b=|B|, and R=C∖B, r=|R|≥1. The earlier degree bound gives internal degree at least h in C for high vertices with a high exterior neighbor. The earlier Fan/long-cycle argument gives h+1≤b≤2h−1. Each member of B∖{w} has internal degree at least h in B. There is a matching from t distinct members of B∖{w} to t distinct vertices of P, as shown in the two-endblock argument by deleting w and a hypothetical smaller vertex cover.

## Routing through the good leaf block

For any two distinct ports in B add a new degree-two marker precisely at those ports. Adding missing edges between vertices other than w preserves Hamiltonicity in this graph by Bondy–Chvátal closure: both vertices originally have degree at least h and the enlarged order is b+1≤2h. Thus spanning paths between the marked ports can be checked after filling B∖{w} to a clique. The cut w has at least two original neighbors there, since B is not a bridge and is a block. If w is an endpoint, start through one neighbor distinct from the other port, then traverse the clique. Otherwise insert w between two neighbors in the clique path. This works for all port pairs except possibly the pair of its two neighbors, when there are exactly two. For that pair one can still use the longer arc of the previously established Hamilton cycle in B, giving at least ceil(b/2)+1 vertices. If b=h+1, B is a clique already, since each of its b−1 noncut vertices has degree h.

Read the t matched vertices in order on P. The contacts within B are distinct, so at most one consecutive pair is exceptional. For every other interior gap replace the interval by a spanning path of B between its contacts; the gap must have at least b unmarked vertices. Even in the exceptional gap the deficit against b is at most s=floor(b/2)−1. Each outer tail can be replaced through its matched port by a spanning path of B ending at w, followed by a neighbor of w in R. Thus each outer tail has at least b+1 vertices. Counting the t marks and every vertex of C gives

    n ≥ t+(t+2)b+r+2−s,

where s may be set to zero when b=h+1. If R contains any high vertex v then v's internal neighbors in C lie in R∪{w}, so r≥h from its internal degree bound. For this application, if v has no high internal neighbor then its only possible internal neighbor is z. The general high-leaf exclusion in the earlier longest-path attachment argument rules out such v in a component of order ≥3: its k−1 or more attachments force one run and exhaust its cap, while a second neighbor of z in C gives an additional witness. If z has no second C-neighbor, C would be an isolated pair. In the present block-chain configuration one can equivalently choose a high member of R with a high neighbor: choose a vertex of R adjacent to w; it is high unless it is z. A low noncut vertex adjacent to w cannot be the only neighbor of w in a component of R containing other vertices because it would cut C. Thus if R contains any high vertex, such a choice is possible.

For b=h+1 and r≥h the order lower bound is at least t+(t+2)(h+1)+h+2. This is t²+5t+4 for even k and t²+6t+7 for odd k. For b≥h+2 use s≤h−2 to give the lower bound t+(t+2)(h+2)+4, still larger. Both contradict the corresponding established upper bounds t²+5t+1 and t²+6t+4. Therefore R={z}. The only edge from z into C is zw.

## The pendant low vertex

The vertex w is high and has high neighbors within B, hence its internal degree q in C is at least h. All q−1 other C-neighbors of w have distance two from z, because z has no other C-neighbor. Write A_z and A_w for the positions of their neighbors on P. Since δ≥t+1, |A_z|=δ−1≥t. The marked positions avoid endpoints and consecutive positions by maximality of P. The shifts A_z−1 and A_z+1 together give |A_z|+u distance-two witnesses, where u≥1 is the number of step-two runs. Each member of A_w∖A_z is another distance-two witness via w, disjoint from the shifts: a consecutive pair of attachments of z and w would permit inserting zw into P. Hence

    k ≥ (q−1)+|A_z|+u+|A_w∖A_z| ≥ (h−1)+t+1 = k.

Equality forces q=h, |A_z|=t≥2, u=1, and A_w⊆A_z. Each of the t members of the single run has another member two positions away. If w attached to any member, the resulting pair of attachments two positions apart would allow substituting z,w for the intervening single vertex of P. Therefore A_w is empty. It follows that deg_G(w)=q=h<k, contradicting its high degree. This finishes the remaining branch. Together with the previous cases, every qualifying graph with κ>k/2 is traceable.
