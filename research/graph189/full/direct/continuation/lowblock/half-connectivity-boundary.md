# Equality in the half-degree connectivity threshold

Every qualifying graph of vertex-connectivity κ≥floor(k/2) is traceable, with k its second-smallest degree. The strict branch was proved in `high-connectivity-complete.md`; here κ=t=floor(k/2), h=ceil(k/2). Parameters k≤5 are settled by the small-degree and small-order results, so take k≥6. As before assume nontraceability and write P for a longest path. The maximum even-distance count, rooted order bound, internal-degree argument, block routes and exterior-cycle exclusion used below are as in the preceding proof. In particular the latter requires only t disjoint connectors (so κ=t suffices), every omitted high vertex has internal degree ≥h, and every nonsingleton exterior component has at least h+2 vertices by the previous parity-specific exterior results `even-exterior.md` and `odd-exterior.md`.

## Any exterior containing a high vertex

Suppose C is such a component. For a 2-connected C the Fan and marker arguments give h+2≤b=|C|≤2h−1, a Hamilton cycle, and spanning paths between all endpoint pairs with at most one exception. There are t matched C–P contacts since fewer deletions cannot disconnect G. If no exception exists, n≥t+(t+2)b as in the strict case. At even k this is at least t²+5t+4 and at odd k at least t²+6t+6, above the universal order bounds. An exception requires the unique low vertex z in C, with exactly two neighbors there. One of them, w, has at least h internal neighbors. At least h−2 of these are nonneighbors of z distinct from z, hence at distance two. Its δ−2 path contacts contribute at least δ−1 shift witnesses when δ≥3. Thus k≥h−2+δ−1, giving δ≤t+3. The exceptional gap loses at most floor(b/2)−1≤h−2 vertices against b, because of the longer arc on the Hamilton cycle. Consequently

    n≥t+(t+2)(h+2)−(h−2)>1+(t+3)+k+floor((k+1)²/4).

The margin on the right is two for even k and one for odd k. This contradicts the order bound with its actual δ.

If C has a cut vertex choose a good leaf block B with cut w and all noncut vertices high. As before h+1≤b≤2h−1. There are now t−1 distinct noncut ports matched to distinct P marks, by deleting w and any supposed smaller vertex cover. Let R=C∖B. If it contains only low z, z is an exterior leaf with only exterior neighbor w. More generally such a leaf forces deg_G(z)≤2 without any connectivity bound. Write q_w for the degree of w in C, a_z=|A_z| for the number of z contacts on P, and u for the number of their step-two runs. Shared contacts of z,w can only occur in singleton runs of A_z, since using a second contact two positions away would permit replacing the middle P vertex by z,w. The internal neighbors of w other than z, the shifted z contacts, and A_w∖A_z are separate distance-two sets from z. Therefore

    k≥q_w−1+a_z+u+|A_w|−|A_z∩A_w|≥k−1+a_z,

using high degree of w. Thus a_z≤1, contradicting δ≥3.

Otherwise R contains a high vertex, so r=|R|≥h. Each tail can add at least h−1 vertices outside B to its spanning route across B ending at w. To see this, take a component K of the graph induced by the high members of R and a shortest w-to-K path in C. Every member of K loses at most w and the single low vertex among its C-neighbors, so has degree at least h−2 in K. Follow a longest K-path rooted at the entrance to obtain at least h−1 additional vertices. When all vertices are high this improves to h because only w can be lost. The possible exceptional interior gap costs at most s=floor(b/2)−1≤h−2, and s=0 for b=h+1. Hence

    n≥(t−1)+(t+1)b+r+2(h−1)−s.

The least lower bound occurs at b=h+1 with s=0: a larger b adds at least t+1 before subtracting at most h−2. The lower bound is then t²+6t−2 for even k and t²+7t+2 for odd k. This exceeds the respective order caps t²+5t+1 and t²+6t+4 for even t≥4 and odd t≥3 (where even/odd refer to k). At k=6 the even expression ties the maximum cap only if δ=k, in which case the stronger high-only tails each give an additional vertex. This excludes all nonsingleton exterior components.

## When precisely the low vertex is omitted

Only the unique low z can remain outside P. Its attachments avoid consecutive positions and positions two apart, as the latter exchange would leave a high singleton outside another longest path. Hence 2δ≤k, and κ=t forces δ=t. Let m be the minimum number of omitted vertices for a path Q through z. By the previously proved near-spanning order bridge, t+1≤m≤k−δ+1, and the omitted vertices are a single Hamilton-connected H of minimum internal degree at least t. We recall the two ingredients: a longest path constrained to contain z has high exterior minimum internal degree at least floor(k/2), and every interval of P between consecutive attachments of z, as well as both tails, can be replaced by z to obtain a path through z. Thus all δ+1 intervals have at least m vertices; combining n≥1+δ+(δ+1)m with the order bound n≤1+δ+k+δ(k+1−δ) proves m≤k−δ+1.

For any such Q write A for all its positions touched by H. The support of a mark means its neighbors in H. Form groups in marked order by combining consecutive marks with the same singleton support; a mark with support of size at least two always forms its own group. Between different groups one can select distinct endpoints in H and use a spanning path of H. Either outside tail can likewise use all H, starting at a given contact. Thus at least g of the g+1 between-group gaps and outer tails contain m unmarked vertices each: only one can contain z, and replacing any of the others preserves z. In particular

    |Q|≥|A|+g m.

For even k=2t, m=t+1 and n≤t²+4t+1. Since all t+1 intervals of P have at least m vertices, some has exactly m (otherwise n≥1+t+(t+1)(t+2), above the cap). Replace this interval by z; the resulting Q is longest through z and H is precisely that interval. Each of its t+1 vertices requires at least t contacts in Q. The group bound gives g≤floor((t²+2t)/(t+1))=t. Some H vertex owns no singleton group since there are more H vertices than groups. This vertex must have ≥t multiply attached groups, so all g=t groups consist of single multiply attached marks. Every vertex of H must touch every mark. However, a bounding original z-attachment is a mark of Q, adjacent to an original endpoint of the removed interval. It cannot touch all H: then z would see H at distance two, but its 2t shifts on P exhaust its even cap and at most two belong to the interval. This contradiction applies also to an outer-tail interval, which has just one bounding attachment.

At odd k=2t+1, n≤t²+5t+2 and m∈{t+1,t+2}. If m=t+2, some P interval again has length m, since all ≥t+3 would force n≥1+t+(t+1)(t+3)>t²+5t+2. Replacing yields Q and H. Each H member needs ≥t contacts and the group bound gives g≤floor((t²+3t)/(t+2))=t. The owner-free vertex again implies exactly t multiply attached marks, all universal to H. A bounding z-attachment cannot see all H: at most two of the 2t shifted witnesses lie in this interval, and the total cap leaves room for only one more. Here t+2>3, a contradiction.

Finally suppose odd k and m=t+1. Take any longest Q through z. H is a clique of size t+1 because its minimum internal degree is t. Each vertex needs at least t+1 contacts. The group bound gives g≤floor((t²+3t)/(t+1))=t+1. If every H member owns a singleton group, then g=t+1 and every group is private to its owner, so |A|≥(t+1)². The group inequality forces n≥2(t+1)²+(t+1)>t²+5t+2. Otherwise a vertex owning no singleton group forces g=t+1 multiply attached marks, all universal to H. This holds for every longest Q through z in the present case.

There must be two marks at distance two on Q: if no such pair existed the shifts about the t+1 neighbors of any x∈H would give 2t+2 distinct distance-two vertices. To justify that shift count for a path constrained through z, a contact can never be at a Q endpoint or consecutive to another contact of x, as adjoining/inserting x preserves z. The midpoint of the step-two pair must be z; otherwise substituting two distinct clique vertices for it lengthens Q and retains z. Thus z sees all H at distance two. The remaining t+1 gaps/tails have at least m interior vertices. At least one has exactly m, since if all had at least t+2 then n≥(t+1)+(t+1)+1+(t+1)(t+2)>t²+5t+2. Replace such an interval J by H. This gives another longest path through z and its omitted vertices are J. By the same classification z is at distance two from all of J. The disjoint sets H and J alone supply 2t+2 even witnesses, a contradiction. This exhausts the singleton case and proves the theorem.
