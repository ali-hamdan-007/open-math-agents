# A longest-cycle exterior in every single-vertex completion

Let G have second-smallest degree k and satisfy the even-distance cap k, let w be any vertex, and let H=G−w. Suppose every other vertex had original degree at least D+1 and k≥2. Let P be any longest path of H. For integers d≥2 with k+2d≤2D, one of the following holds.

* At most two vertices of H are outside P.
* The induced graph H−V(P) contains a cycle on at least d+1 vertices.

If P omits at least three vertices of H, this supplies a cycle on at least d+1 vertices in the exterior of the corresponding longest cycle of the graph K obtained by making w universal. If every real path of G omits at least four vertices, the same assertion holds for every longest cycle of K.

**Exterior with an edge.** A distance-two pair in H remains a distance-two pair in G, since the two-edge path survives and its endpoints remain nonadjacent. Thus the exact distance-two count of every H vertex is at most k. For an exterior edge xy write a(x)=|N_H(x)∩P| and q(x)=|N_H(x)−P|. Every contact of x and every contact of y charges a distinct path vertex of H at distance two from x by the predecessor/successor count; the common contacts use their successors and the remaining contacts of y use themselves. Including the exterior neighbors of y that are neither x nor neighbors of x gives

    a(x)+deg_H(y) ≤ k+q(x).

The charges and their distinctness are formalized in Graph189ExteriorGap.cross_contact_degree. Since every H vertex has degree at least D, and a(x)+q(x)≥D, this gives 2q(x)≥2D−k≥2d. Discard the exterior's isolated vertices. The induced remaining graph has the same exterior degrees and has minimum degree at least d. A longest path beginning at an arbitrary vertex ends at a vertex whose neighbors all belong to that path. Starting at that endpoint, at least one neighbor is at path distance ≥d, since the initial d positions include the endpoint itself. Closing that segment gives a cycle with at least d+1 vertices. This argument applies to all nonisolated exterior vertices, regardless of the placement of the original low vertex.

**Independent exterior.** Consider any omitted vertex x whose original degree is at least k. Its neighbors in H all lie on P; write a=deg_H(x)≥k−1≥1. All contacts are internal and no two are consecutive. The predecessors of the a contacts and the successor of the final contact give a+1 distinct vertices on P at distance two from x in H and in G. Thus a=k−1, w is adjacent to x in G, and the entire ambient even budget of x occurs on P. Two distinct originally high omitted vertices x and y would therefore both be adjacent to w. Since the exterior is independent, y is an additional ambient distance-two witness at x outside P. Consequently there is at most one originally high omitted vertex and at most one originally low omitted vertex. No order inequality involving k(k−1)/4 is needed.

For an original minimum-degree completion take D=k−1. For the critical completion, writing δ=k−b, one may always take D=k−b−1. If the previously audited four-vertex path gap holds, a longest cycle of K has an exterior cycle of order at least ⌈(k−2b−2)/2⌉+1 whenever the lower bound on exterior degree is at least two. In particular the familiar condition ⌊(k−2b−2)/2⌋+1≥s+1 suffices uniformly. The paper bound for the critical lift can drop its independent-exterior fallback. The older numerical replay, which retained the fallback, therefore remains valid.

## The four-vertex gap when 2δ>k

Suppose k≥5, every degree exceeds k/2, and G is nontraceable. This implies δ≥3. If some path omits at most three vertices, take a longest such path P. Every originally high exterior vertex has at least half its original degree outside P by the ambient path-contact and low-neighbor argument, formalized in Graph189ExteriorGap.high_exterior_half. Yet at most two other exterior vertices remain. This would give k≤4. Nontraceability therefore forces the omitted set to consist of the single original low vertex z.

All neighbors of z are internal vertices of P, and no two of its contacts are consecutive. If two contacts occur two positions apart, replace the intervening vertex by z. The resulting path is again longest, and the omitted midpoint has high degree, contrary to the preceding paragraph. Consequently every two distinct contacts are at least three positions apart. All their predecessors and successors are distinct, giving 2deg(z) vertices at distance exactly two from z. This contradicts 2δ>k. Thus every real path omits at least four vertices. No large-order or global rooted-exterior structure enters this degree regime.

For any critical-vertex completion used with k>5s and an originally low vertex on a minimum s-cut, the opposite-side closed-neighborhood count gives δ≥k−2s+2>k/2. Thus this short gap proof directly covers the degree-sensitive lift, including arbitrary choices of the critical vertex. For the baseline minimum completion when δ≤k/2 the already audited full almost-spanning theorem gives the required gap; the only residual in the shorter proof is precisely a spanning path in the deletion of the unique low vertex.

## When a minimum deletion misses exactly one high vertex

Suppose w has minimum degree, H is not traceable, and one of its longest paths P has independent exterior. All vertices of H were originally high, so the exterior consists of one vertex x. Its k−1 contacts have k−1 distinct predecessors and k−1 distinct successors; their union lies in its original even shell and hence has size at most k. At least k−2 path positions are therefore both a predecessor and a successor. Choose one of these midpoints y, with a neighboring contact t. Replacing y by x is a genuine H path of the same length, now omitting just y. Saturation at the high y forces it adjacent to w in G. Since x is also adjacent to w, replace the edge ty of the original P with the path t,x,w,y. This includes every original vertex and contradicts nontraceability. The conclusion requires k≥3, and works at unrestricted minimum degree. The same count actually forces all k−2 swappable midpoints to be neighbors of w. Thus whenever H is nontraceable, every longest H path has an edge in its exterior. This direct argument uses neither the full almost-spanning result nor its high-minimum special case. The remaining empty-exterior alternative is precisely a Hamiltonian path in H.
