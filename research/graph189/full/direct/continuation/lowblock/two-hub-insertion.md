# Inserting the two exceptional high-degree vertices

Suppose κ(G)≥3, exactly two vertices X,Y exceed the second-smallest degree k, and the even cap is k. Let H=G−{X,Y} and K=N_G(X)∩N_G(Y). The order regimes n≤4k+1 and n≥5k are traceable by the moderate-order and trimmed-high-degrees results. Work in 4k+2≤n≤5k−1 with k≥6. When there is a low vertex z, take P to be any longest path starting at z in H. Otherwise take any unrooted longest P of H and orient it either way. If P spans H we are done by almost-spanning. Let D be the exterior vertices having H-neighbors on P.

## Forced equality

For an induced H-wedge with ordinary high endpoints the original degree excess E=d_G(w)+e_G(w)−d_G(u)−d_G(v) is at most zero. The refined wedge inequality gives j_G≤−1. Deleting X,Y raises j by at most two, so j_H≤1; equality requires j_G=−1, both endpoints in K, and E=0 since E≤−1 would force n≤4k+1. For any u∈D let A_u be its path marks and U_u their successors. The rooted local Ore proof gives sum of the corresponding j_H over the marks at least |A_u|. Its endpoints are u and successors distinct from z, so every mark attains equality. In particular, D and all successors belong to K. Each mark w is saturated in G and has d_G(w)=k. In the unique-low case this proves that the starting point z is not a mark. In the tied case a longest unrooted P has neither endpoint marked. Thus in all cases marks avoid both path endpoints.

The individual set-containment steps in the same rooted count are equalities: N_H(w) outside N_H(u)∪N_H(s(w)) consists exactly of u and N_H(w)∩U_u. In particular every mark w touches exactly one H−P component and its H-neighbors outside P form a clique. For an exterior neighbor y of w distinct from u, no neighbor y of w can also see s(w), since w,y,s(w) would insert y into P. If y is not adjacent to u it would therefore enlarge the equality set beyond u and the on-path successors. Thus every pair of exterior neighbors of w is adjacent.

## Every mark gives exactly one ordinary common neighbor

For any u∈D order its marks w₁,…,w_t along P. Write t_i and s_i for the predecessor and successor of w_i. Every mark has a predecessor by the endpoint observation above. The first predecessor t₁ is outside the successor set U_u, outside N_H(u), and distinct from u. The equality containment at w₁ therefore forces t₁ adjacent to s₁. If t_i is adjacent to s_i, the next mark cannot be two positions after w_i: otherwise replacing t_i,w_i,s_i,w_{i+1} by t_i,s_i,w_i,u,w_{i+1} extends the rooted path. Consecutive marks were already impossible. Thus t_{i+1} is outside U_u, and the same containment proves t_{i+1} adjacent to s_{i+1}. By induction all marks have these bypass edges and any two marks differ by at least three positions.

No s_i is adjacent to a different w_j. If j>i, follow P to w_i, take w_i,u,w_j,s_i, follow the old forward segment to t_j, then bypass w_j by t_j,s_j and continue. If j<i, follow P to t_j, bypass w_j by t_j,s_j, follow the old forward segment to w_i, then take w_i,u,w_j,s_i and continue. These simple paths contain all of P and the new vertex u, retaining the initial root. Every common H-neighbor of u,s_i already lies among the marks, because one outside P would allow w_i,u,y,s_i. Consequently

    N_H(u)∩N_H(s_i)={w_i},   N_G(u)∩N_G(s_i)={X,Y,w_i}.

Equality j_H=1 now says the center-only set N_H(w_i) outside the two endpoint neighborhoods consists precisely of u,s_i. This holds in both minimum-degree cases and on every rooted longest P as chosen above. The disjoint shifts of all marks alone give 2|A_u| even witnesses from u. A singleton exterior component would have |A_u|=k−2, impossible for k≥6. More generally 2|A_u|+|D∖C|≤k for u in exterior component C, since all boundary points in the other components are additional even witnesses via either hub.

## Absorbing two components

Let C be an exterior component and x one of its members neighboring a mark w. Whenever C has at least two members, there is a vertex y≠x of C adjacent to either prescribed hub T∈{X,Y}. If C has a second boundary vertex in D this follows from D⊆K. If x is its only boundary vertex and no other member sees T, deleting x and the other hub disconnects C−x from P in G, contradicting κ≥3. Consequently if C is Hamilton-connected, replace the edge w,s(w) by a route starting at w, then taking a spanning x-to-y path of C, and finally visiting T,s(w). A singleton uses w,x,T,s(w). All used edges lie in G and no original path vertex is removed. For two different components one can make both replacements using different hubs because their marks are distinct and their replaced path edges are distinct, including if these edges share a path vertex. More generally the same insertion accepts any x-to-y path of C, costing only that path's unvisited vertices. If the path after these insertions misses at most ceil(k/2)+1 vertices in G, the graph is traceable. Thus when two components are absorbed in full, only the total order of all other exterior components counts; when just one is absorbed in full one additionally counts the unused hub.

There is a minimum-degree bound making the criterion concrete. Each nonsingleton exterior component C has minimum degree at least h−2 with h=ceil(k/2). For a boundary point x take any neighbor y in C; write q_x,q_y for their C-degrees and a_x,a_y for their path contact counts. The standard shift count supplies at least a_x+a_y vertices on P at exact distance two from x in G: use A_x−1, (A_x∩A_y)+1, and A_y∖A_x. Cross contacts one or two positions apart would strictly lengthen P while retaining its root. At least q_y−q_x additional vertices in C have distance two via y. All H degrees are at least k−2 except possibly the chosen root on P. Thus k≥q_y−q_x+a_x+a_y≥2k−4−2q_x. If x has no path contacts then its C-degree is already at least k−2. A nonsingleton C of order at most 2h−5 is Hamilton-connected by the degree-sum marker criterion, so can be absorbed in full as above.

In fact at a boundary x, all D outside C also consume even places via either hub and are disjoint from the previous witnesses, whence 2q_x≥k−4+|D∖C|.
