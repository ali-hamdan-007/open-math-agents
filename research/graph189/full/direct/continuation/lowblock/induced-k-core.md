# Induced high-degree cores inherit the Graph 189 condition

Let G satisfy Graph 189, so at most k other vertices are at positive even distance from each vertex, where k is its second-smallest degree. Suppose H is a nonempty induced subgraph, d_H(a)≥k for every a∈H, and every exterior v has t(v)=|N_G(v)∩V(H)|≤k+1. Then H is connected and satisfies the Graph 189 condition using its own second-smallest degree. All ambient distances at most three between its vertices are preserved exactly.

For a vertex v outside H, two nonadjacent H-neighbors a,b of v have their H-neighborhoods in (B₂^G(v)∩H)∖{a,b}, of size at most t(v)+k−2. Their H-neighborhoods each have size at least k. Hence they have at least k−t(v)+2≥1 common neighbor in H. Thus a two-edge segment between H vertices through a single outside vertex can be replaced in H without increasing length.

For an adjacent exterior pair v,w with t(v)≤t(w), take a vertex a∈H touching v. If a does not touch w, its H-neighbors and the t(w) H-contacts of w all lie in (B₂^G(v)∩H)∖{a}, of size at most t(v)+k−1. Thus their intersection has size at least t(w)−t(v)+1≥1. In particular a geodesic a,v,w,b with both endpoints in H can be oriented to put the smaller contact count first; the claimed intersection replaces v by an H point adjacent to both a,w. The first paragraph then replaces the remaining w if necessary. This also proves the assertion for a length-three path with just one outside vertex, and retained edges already preserve distance one.

Every H vertex has original degree at least k. By the established Graph 189 high-pair distance bound, any two are at original distance at most four. If H were disconnected, every pair across two components would have to be at distance exactly four in G since all smaller distances transfer. Each component has at least k+1 vertices by its internal minimum degree, so one point of the other component would exceed the even cap. Thus H is connected. If a pair has even distance in H, its ambient distance could not have been one or three, and therefore was even in G. Every H vertex thus has at most k other vertices at positive even H-distance, at most its own second degree. No preservation of original distance four is required.

## Avalanche after deleting a seed

Let G be a minimum-order counterexample and h=ceil(k/2). After removing any nonempty seed A, repeatedly delete vertices whose remaining degree is strictly below k until the k-core H of G−A remains. If every seed vertex has at most k+1 neighbors in H, then the total number of removed vertices is at least h+2. This holds, in particular, for any initial seed of vertices of original degree at most k+1. If H is empty it is obvious since n>3k+2. Otherwise every vertex peeled after the seed has at most k−1 neighbors in the final H, so the core satisfies the preceding theorem. Since H is proper it has a spanning path by minimum order. A path missing at most h+1 vertices of G forces traceability by the almost-spanning and exterior-gap theorems. This proves the avalanche lower bound.

With a unique low vertex z one may take the ordinary original k-core, since initially z has degree less than k and is canonically peeled. A least counterexample thus has either empty original k-core or at least h+2 vertices outside it. With tied minimum degree, deleting any one vertex of degree k or k+1 and then taking the k-core again peels at least h+2 vertices in total. The seed set need not be independent or connected.
