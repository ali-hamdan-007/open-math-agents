# Induced high-degree cores inherit the Graph 189 condition

Let G satisfy Graph 189, so at most k other vertices are at positive even distance from each vertex, where k is its second-smallest degree. Suppose H is a nonempty induced subgraph, d_H(a)≥k for every a∈H, and every exterior v has t(v)=|N_G(v)∩V(H)|≤k+1. Then H is connected and satisfies the Graph 189 condition using its own second-smallest degree. All ambient distances at most three between its vertices are preserved exactly.

For a vertex v outside H, two nonadjacent H-neighbors a,b of v have their H-neighborhoods in (B₂^G(v)∩H)∖{a,b}, of size at most t(v)+k−2. Their H-neighborhoods each have size at least k. Hence they have at least k−t(v)+2≥1 common neighbor in H. Thus a two-edge segment between H vertices through a single outside vertex can be replaced in H without increasing length.

For an adjacent exterior pair v,w with t(v)≤t(w), take a vertex a∈H touching v. If a does not touch w, its H-neighbors and the t(w) H-contacts of w all lie in (B₂^G(v)∩H)∖{a}, of size at most t(v)+k−1. Thus their intersection has size at least t(w)−t(v)+1≥1. In particular a geodesic a,v,w,b with both endpoints in H can be oriented to put the smaller contact count first; the claimed intersection replaces v by an H point adjacent to both a,w. The first paragraph then replaces the remaining w if necessary. This also proves the assertion for a length-three path with just one outside vertex, and retained edges already preserve distance one.

Every H vertex has original degree at least k. By the established Graph 189 high-pair distance bound, any two are at original distance at most four. If H were disconnected, every pair across two components would have to be at distance exactly four in G since all smaller distances transfer. Each component has at least k+1 vertices by its internal minimum degree, so one point of the other component would exceed the even cap. Thus H is connected. If a pair has even distance in H, its ambient distance could not have been one or three, and therefore was even in G. Every H vertex thus has at most k other vertices at positive even H-distance, at most its own second degree. No preservation of original distance four is required.

## Avalanche after deleting a seed

Let G be a minimum-order counterexample and h=ceil(k/2). After removing any nonempty seed A, repeatedly delete vertices whose remaining degree is strictly below k until the k-core H of G−A remains. If every seed vertex has at most k+1 neighbors in H, then the total number of removed vertices is at least h+2. This holds, in particular, for any initial seed of vertices of original degree at most k+1. If H is empty it is obvious since n>3k+2. Otherwise every vertex peeled after the seed has at most k−1 neighbors in the final H, so the core satisfies the preceding theorem. Since H is proper it has a spanning path by minimum order. A path missing at most h+1 vertices of G forces traceability by the almost-spanning and exterior-gap theorems. This proves the avalanche lower bound.

With a unique low vertex z one may take the ordinary original k-core, since initially z has degree less than k and is canonically peeled. A least counterexample thus has either empty original k-core or at least h+2 vertices outside it. With tied minimum degree, deleting any one vertex of degree k or k+1 and then taking the k-core again peels at least h+2 vertices in total. The seed set need not be independent or connected.

## Arbitrary seeds and a contact obstruction

The numerical external-contact bound is only used to guarantee that every pair of H-neighbors of a fixed exterior point can be joined in H by at most two edges. If all outside vertices have this latter property, the adjacent-outside-pair argument works with their actual contact counts, with no upper bound. Thus a k-core after deleting arbitrary seed vertices still qualifies unless some seed v has two H-neighbors at intrinsic H-distance greater than two (including points in different H components). Every subsequently peeled vertex passes automatically.

There is a quantitative restriction on the incompatible contact pairs of such a seed. On A=N(v)∩H make a graph linking a,b when their H-distance exceeds two or they are in distinct components of H. Associate to each a its closed H-neighborhood F_a, of size at least k+1. These sets lie in U=B₂^G(v)∩H of size at most k+t, t=|A|. Incompatibility is exactly disjointness of F_a,F_b. In particular t≥k+2 if there is any edge. If t=k+2 then every incompatible pair consists of complementary (k+1)-sets in U, and each nontrivial component of the incompatible-pair graph is complete bipartite between two classes of H true twins. If t=k+c, c≥3, every odd cycle in the incompatibility graph has length at least

    2 ceil((k+1)/(c−2))+1.

Along two consecutive incompatibility edges with intermediate closed neighborhood F, both endpoint sets lie in U∖F of order at most k+c−1. Each has size at least k+1, so each misses at most c−2 points of the other. Along t' such two-edge steps one loses at most t'(c−2) members of the initial set. In an odd cycle of length 2t'+1 the terminal set after these steps is disjoint from the initial set, which proves the bound. In particular for t=k+3 the incompatible-pair graph is bipartite since its vertex set has fewer than 2k+3 points (k≥1 here). These restrictions apply at every degree, without assuming a bounded number of high vertices.

For a minimum-order counterexample, every seed whose k-core deletion cascade removes at most ceil(k/2)+1 vertices therefore admits such an incompatible pair at some seed. Otherwise the residual core qualifies and gives a forbidden almost-spanning path. The separate bound in global/near-degree-exterior.md also applies to all avalanches that do leave a qualifying core: if their size m≤k−2, an actual longest path omits a high-containing component of size b≤m and h+2≤b. Hence m(k−m+3)≤b(k−b+3)≤n+1, by monotonicity in this range. In particular m>k−sqrt(3k)−4 for k≥432 as in that earlier paper.

## Regrowth removes contact restrictions on the initial deletion

Starting with any nonempty induced H₀ of minimum degree ℓ≥k, repeatedly adjoin any outside vertex with at least 2ℓ−k+2 neighbors in the current set. The terminal induced set H still has minimum degree at least ℓ and every outside vertex has at most 2ℓ−k+1 contacts there. In the first transfer estimate, two H contacts of an outside v then have at least 2ℓ−(t(v)+k−2)≥1 common H neighbor when they are nonadjacent. The second estimate holds as before since ℓ≥k. All ambient distances at most three between survivors transfer, so H is connected and qualifying. In particular every nonempty ordinary ℓ-core for ℓ≥k qualifies, since points peeled originally had fewer than ℓ contacts to it.

A missing vertex having original degree at most 2ℓ−k+1 can never regrow. Thus in a minimum counterexample any induced H₀ of order at least n−h−1 and minimum degree at least ℓ≥k must already include every vertex of original degree at most 2ℓ−k+1. Otherwise the terminal H is a proper qualifying subgraph of that same near-spanning size. In particular, after deleting any arbitrary high-degree seeds and running downward k-core peeling, if even one removed vertex initially had degree at most k+1, regrowing at threshold k+2 cannot restore that vertex. The terminal qualifying core must miss at least h+2 vertices (or be empty).
