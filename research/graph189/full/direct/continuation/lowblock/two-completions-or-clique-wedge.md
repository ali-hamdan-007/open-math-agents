# Two distinct degree exceptions or a common-neighbor clique of saturated vertices

Let G be a minimum-order counterexample. Call a vertex exceptional here if it has degree below the second-smallest degree k, or if it has a clique of j≥2 saturated neighbors and its degree is at most k+j−2. Saturation means exactly k other vertices at even distance. Then at least one of the following holds:

* Some two nonadjacent vertices have a nonempty common-neighbor set consisting entirely of mutually adjacent saturated vertices.
* At least two distinct vertices are exceptional.

In particular in the tied-minimum case the second outcome requires two different low-degree completions of saturated cliques, with no bound or restriction on the other vertex degrees. In the unique-low case it requires at least one such completion distinct from the original low vertex. This complements the numerical large-saturation/clique-completion theorem. The proof uses the settled cut-vertex and two-cut cases and the paper theorem for connectivity at least floor(k/2) to exclude k≤7. The argument below treats all k≥8.

Assume both displayed outcomes fail. Any clique of saturated vertices deletes isometrically, one member at a time in any order. Indeed if a first single deletion failed, two nonadjacent surviving neighbors of that center would have no other surviving common neighbor, so their full common-neighbor set would be a nonempty subset of that original saturated clique. This includes a disconnecting deletion since some two neighbors would then be separated. Take the separated cliques K selected successively as maximal cliques of the remaining saturated pool in saturated-clique-deletions.md. The local detours of one such clique lie within distance two of that clique's current center, so all selected cliques delete simultaneously isometrically. Their union S meets the even-distance set of every remaining saturated vertex. A nonexceptional survivor adjacent to j vertices in S touches just one selected clique. It retains degree at least k−1 for j≤1, or because its initial degree was at least k+j−1 for j≥2. By assumption there is at most one exceptional survivor, so G−S qualifies. It has a spanning path P by minimum order. The existence of at least two survivors follows as before: a nonexceptional clique member has at least k−1 exterior neighbors for a block of size ≥2, and a singleton has at least three exterior neighbors. Every nonsingleton block has a nonexceptional member. The initial saturated pool is nonempty by the minimum-degree isometric deletion test.

Write h=ceil(k/2) and r for the number of blocks. As before, r<(k+1)/4+2 and r≤h. Every nonexceptional member x of a c-vertex block with c≥2 has degree at least k+c−2. For c=2 this is the original degree condition; for c≥3, apply nonexceptionality to its other c−1 saturated clique members. Consequently in the path-recovery algorithm each nonexceptional x in an unprocessed block has at least k−1−p contacts on the current path, where p counts the omissions accumulated while processing earlier blocks. Distinct unprocessed blocks have no edges between them.

For k≥9 we have r<k/2. Process the possible block containing the unique exceptional vertex last. All earlier blocks leave at most one omission apiece by the prior shift argument: after freely inserting any directly insertable vertices, two remaining vertices each with more than k/2 contacts must have cross contacts separated by one or two positions, so the entire residual clique can be inserted, losing at most one path vertex. At the last exceptional block use only nonexceptional vertices when testing for a direct insertion or a short cross contact. If at least two of them remain after direct insertions, each has more than k/2 contacts since p≤r−1. A short cross contact again inserts the entire residual clique, including the exceptional vertex. If at most one nonexceptional member remains, leave it and the possible exceptional vertex outside. The total omission is at most r+1≤h+1 in all cases, a contradiction.

At k=8 only r=4 can need separate attention. If every block has size at most two, skip the exceptional block (or any block in its absence) and insert the first three, where only at most two previous omissions occur. Their deficit plus the skipped block is at most five. If a block has size ≥3, it has a nonexceptional member of degree ≥9; every other block containing a vertex of degree at least eight has closed neighborhood size at least nine. A block of size at least three not containing the potential exceptional point can be processed among the first three, and the exceptional block may be skipped if its size is ≤2. There is such a small block when needed: if all four blocks had size at least three, each would have a nonexceptional member of degree ≥9, giving n≥40 in disjoint closed neighborhoods contrary to n≤37. Thus the only leftover situation is an exceptional vertex inside a block of size at least three. Each of the other three blocks contains only degree-at-least-eight vertices. The disjoint closed-neighborhood inequality then gives n≥3·9+(8+c−1)≥37, while n≤29+δ≤37. Equality forces c=3 and δ=8. It also forces every other block to have size at most two, since any additional size-three block would have a representative of degree ≥9. Process the exceptional size-three block first. Even its exceptional member has at least 8−2=6 contacts on the initial P, so every member has more than k/2 contacts and the full standard recovery leaves at most one omission. Process two other blocks and skip the last block of size at most two. Again at most five vertices are omitted. This finishes the proof.

If the first outcome holds, choose a smallest such common neighborhood W for nonadjacent a,b. For every v∈W the graph obtained by deleting W except v is isometric by minimality of |W|, since no smaller set can witness the first outcome. The numerical estimate in saturated-common-neighbor-wedge.md consequently applies to every member, irrespective of the number of exceptional vertices:

    d(v)≥d(a)+d(b)−k−|W|+1+ceil((n−3k−1−|W|+1)/k).

When there is at most one exceptional vertex and |W|≥2, at least one of a,b has degree at least k+|W|−1. This yields d(v)≥min(d(a),d(b))+ceil((n−3k−|W|)/k) for every v∈W. The summand is positive in the unrestricted high-order regime whenever |W|<n−3k.
