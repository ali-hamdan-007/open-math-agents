# Reduction of every deep small side, allowing every position of the exceptional vertex

Consider qualifying graphs subject to the necessary counterexample order and population bounds, with cut size s tending to infinity. Let a minimum separator S split G into A and the giant B, with a high nonport in A. Then for sufficiently large s the graph induced on H=B (or H=B−z if the unique subthreshold z is in B) is a proper connected qualifying graph, and a spanning H-path forces G traceable. Thus all these arbitrarily large cut sizes admit a smaller-instance reduction, including when A has only one high nonport and without requiring high neighbors on both sides at any anchor. The conclusion is an inductive reduction: absent an independent reduction at smaller orders one cannot infer nonexistence of every nonminimal counterexample in a sequence.

Write k for second-smallest ambient degree. The unconditional necessary bounds in ../root/universal-minimum-order.md and geodesic-width-and-order.md give
    n≥(s+1)(k−s+2)−1,    s≤(1+o(1))sqrt(k),   |A|=(1+o(1))k.
The high A nonport supplies |A|≥k+1. Omit the low vertex from H if it lies in B, and denote this omission count by l∈{0,1}. Write P_i=N_A(i) and Q_i=N_H(i), with orders p_i,q_i. Every q_i≤k because the opposite contact sees Q_i at distance two. Every nonport in H (meaning no S contact) is high and at original distance four from the fixed A nonport, so there are at most k−s of them. For v outside S let t_v count its S contacts. Therefore
    D=sk−#(H ports)=Σ_i(k−q_i)+Σ_{H ports v}(t_v−1)
      ≤s²−s+|A|+l−1 ≤(2+o(1))k.
Indeed n≥sk+k−s²+s+1 and |H|=n−s−|A|−l. Each A point sees the union of its Q-sets at distance two; that union is at least t_a k−D. In particular t_a≤3 eventually (using four throughout also suffices). All high A vertices have internal degree ≥k−4; every pair of nonadjacent high A points has a common A-neighbor because |A|=(1+o(1))k. The Hall argument of giant-side-degree-and-allowance.md gives ≥s−t_a−1 positive-even witnesses in A∪S at each high a∈A. Hence for every i with a high A contact, q_i≤K=k−s+5.

In fact every anchor has a high A contact. Otherwise P_e={z} for the unique low z in A. All triangle vertices on ez must lie in S: e has no other A contact and z has no B neighbor. But every edge in a qualifying graph belongs to at least ceil((n−3k−2)/(k+1)) triangles by ../root/edge-order.md. This lower bound tends to infinity with s by n≥(s+1)(k−s+2)−1, while z touches at most three labels. This is impossible. Thus every q_i≤K and
    D* = Σ_i(K−q_i)+Σ_{H ports v}(t_v−1) = D−s(s−5) ≤(1+o(1))k.
In particular for any set J of labels, |union Q_j|≥|J|K−D*. At every anchor i put R_i=N_H(Q_i)−Q_i. The closed A-neighborhood of a high P_i member yields |R_i|≤p_i+3.

All original odd distances between H points transfer. When l=1 first delete the global minimum z isometrically, as proved in minimal-counterexample-minors.md; this preserves all original distances and the cap k. Use large-side-metric.md in the resulting graph for the two-edge relays through S. The H internal degrees are ≥k−s−1, and at every anchor with any H contacts the relevant A contact degree is ≥k−O(s), including every possible location of the low vertex. The threshold k≥4s_B+2s_A−5 holds since s=o(k). No assumption on H connectedness is needed for the two-edge replacement proof. If an ambient three-step pair has no three-step H path, then after deleting z all its geodesics use two anchors, since a one-exterior path relays. For one such x,i,j,y the two disjoint common-neighbor sets measured in G lie in S together with the single removed point if any. Their total ≤s+1 contradicts the width bound 2(n−2k)/(k+1)≥(2−o(1))s. H is connected: pairs across distinct H components would all have original distance four; if one component has more than k points, all other components are impossible, and if all have at most k then a point in one sees at least |H|−k>k outside its component at distance four. Here |H|/k tends to infinity.

We give the remaining constant-degree details. For each H point y∈Q_i write W_y=N_H[y]. Its order is ≥k−s (up to the one lost z). A member of Q_i outside W_y lies at distance two from y along with all P_i, and W_y⊆Q_i∪R_i. Thus at all labels
    |Q_i−W_y|≤min(k−p_i,s+p_i+4)≤k/2+O(s).
For any |W_y|≤k sum these estimates over its incident labels; the deficits and repetitions may be charged to D*, giving t_y(k/2−O(s))≤k+D*. Hence t_y≤4+o(1), so t_y≤4 eventually and δ(H)≥k−5. At every i with 0<q_i and p_i<s, all N_S(Q_i) labels j obey |Q_i∩Q_j|≥K−max(0,K−q_j)−k/2−O(s), using their shared y and W_y⊆Q_i∪R_i with |R_i|≤s+3. Summing over j shows |N_S(Q_i)|≤4+o(1), hence at most four eventually. Its full boundary in G has size at most p_i+3+4+l, requiring p_i≥s−8. If q_i=0, an anchor may have touched just the omitted low, and is thus originally high (l=1). Its original degree then forces p_i≥k−s. Thus every anchor has p_i≥s−8, in particular every H port spends at least s−8 even places outside H.

If H fails to qualify, some nonport x must have w≤4 original outside-H even witnesses (all original odd distances between H points transfer and δ(H)≥k−5). Put I={i∈S:d_G(x,i)=2}. All high A points outside P(I) have distance four from x, apart from the possible length-three walks x,z,i,a when l=1. In the latter case global isometry avoiding z shows any original length-three path corresponds to an equal path through H and S: a shortest x-to-A path of length three begins at a non-anchor H neighbor and next reaches its anchor in I. Thus there are at most w−|I| high A points outside P(I). The high A nonport is one, so I is proper, and it is nonempty by |A|≥k+1. If I={i}, p_i≥|A|−w≥k−O(1). Choose y∈Q_i adjacent to x using global isometry when necessary; a shortest x-to-i path cannot go through A. All t_y labels belong to I. The full boundary of W_y consists of its at most t_y adjacent anchors, at most k−p_i further points at distance two from y outside P_i, and possibly the removed z. It has size O(1)<s, impossible. We obtain |I|≥2, and in particular w≥3.

With |I|≤4 and all p_j≥s−8, every j outside I shares an A contact with some i(j)∈I (at most five A points lie outside P(I)). Each of these shared contacts gives q_i(j)≤k−q_j+|Q_j∩Q_i(j)|. The positive deficits relative to k and duplicates in D show Σ_{j outside I} q_i(j)≤D. Hence one i₀∈I has q₀≤D/(s−|I|)=o(k). At this anchor p₀=(1−o(1))k: a high H contact and a high A contact respectively have internal degrees ≥k−5 and ≥k−4, and their disjoint internal second layers at the anchor yield p₀+q₀≥k−O(1).

The single i₀ deficit consumes (1−o(1))k of D*. All remaining q_j=k−o(k) uniformly and their duplications in H, including with Q₀, sum to o(k). No A point touches two of these remaining labels, since it would see >k points of their Q union, so all their P sets are disjoint. If |W_y|≤k and y touches i₀, it sees P₀ at distance two; each incident Q_j therefore misses only o(k) points from W_y, and at most one of the disjoint-up-to-o(k) full-sized Q_j can occur. If y misses i₀ then the union of its t incident Q sets has size ≥tk−o(k), and |Q_j−W_y|≤p_j+O(1) for each, by |W_y|≥k−4 and the R bound (an O(1) increase if l=1). The summed p_j ≤|A|=(1+o(1))k. Thus t≥3 would force |W_y|≥2k−o(k)>k. In both cases t_y≤2 whenever |W_y|≤k. Hence δ(H)≥k−2−l≥k−3. The previously identified nonport x would now need at most two outside witnesses to violate the cap, but all such x already have ≥3; the reasoning giving |I|≥2 applies to every nonport of outside allowance at most four. This contradiction proves H qualifies.

For the global join in all cases, if H has a spanning path its endpoints can be brought to ports as in small-side-unprescribed-join.md. Every nonport has at least k−1 neighbors in H (allowing removed z), while the number of nonports is ≤k−s. A path with two port ends is therefore obtainable. The all-but-one anchor Fan guarantee in A applies in the original G, where the cutoff is well above |A|=(1+o(1))k. If one joining anchor is unusable, an H spanning path touching only that label at its two ends is closed through it as before. When l=1 an anchor of H with no contacts can cause at most one issue: by Hall on the original B side at most one anchor contacted only z, so among s−1 good A labels choose one with an H neighbor. The resulting path spans H and A and omits ≤s−1+l≤ceil(k/2)+1 original vertices. The universal almost-spanning theorem finishes.
