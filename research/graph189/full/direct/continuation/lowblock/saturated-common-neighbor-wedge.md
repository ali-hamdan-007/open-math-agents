# Every minimum counterexample has a local saturated degree obstruction

With the parameters of saturated-clique-deletions.md, every minimum-order counterexample has at least one of the following:

1. There are nonadjacent a,b with a nonempty common-neighbor set W which is a clique of saturated vertices. Some v in W satisfies d(v)≥b(v)+q, where here b(v) denotes the earlier degree threshold (δ if v neighbors the unique low vertex, k otherwise).
2. There is a clique of j≥2 saturated vertices with a common neighbor x outside the clique such that d(x)≤k+j−2.

In particular the large-degree saturated witness can be required to lie in the common neighborhood of an induced two-edge path, every common neighbor of which is saturated and pairwise adjacent. The earlier quantitative dichotomy separately guarantees j≤q+1 if its numerical large-degree alternative fails. This stronger-localization statement does not impose that bound on j when other saturated vertices can already have large degree.

Use the pool of successively chosen maximal saturated cliques from the preceding paper, and suppose outcome 2 fails for every j≥2. The sequential path recovery there used d(v)≥k+c−2 at each high member of a selected clique of order c≥2, a consequence of this exclusion alone. The bound on the number r of selected cliques and all its case distinctions similarly had no need for an upper bound on their degrees, apart from the quick dismissal at k=6,r=3. We first replace that dismissal.

Suppose k=6, r=3, and all three cliques have order c≥3. For each clique K put B=N(K) outside K and U=K∪B. The U of different selected cliques are disjoint. Every member of K is high and has at least k+c−2 neighbors, hence at least five in B. Thus |U|≥c+5≥8. The order upper bound gives n≤25. There is at most one vertex available beyond the three lower bounds of eight, counting additional clique members, additional boundary vertices, and vertices outside all three U. In any U with |B|=5, K is complete to B. Every member of B, having ≥3 saturated clique neighbors, must have degree ≥7 by exclusion of outcome 2. If a U has one extra boundary vertex, then |B|=6 and each of its c clique members misses at most one boundary vertex. A boundary vertex of degree at most six has at most one neighbor in K, so misses at least c−1 of them. Since there are at most c missing incidences in total and c≥3, at most one such boundary vertex exists. Consequently there is globally at most one vertex of degree at most six, contradicting the definition of the second-smallest degree. This shows that k=6,r=3 also permits skipping a block of size at most two. The k=8,r=4 dismissal already only used the lower bound d(v)≥k+c−2.

If every selected clique were isometrically deletable one vertex at a time in order of nondecreasing original degree, the preceding paper would therefore prove a contradiction, without imposing any upper bound on saturated degrees. Indeed detours are local to the radius-two ball of the current center, simultaneous deletion preserves second degree at least k−1 by exclusion of outcome 2, the saturated vertices are covered at even distance, and the path-recovery argument now holds in every case. There is thus some selected clique K for which this deletion order first fails at a member v, after i prior members of K have been removed. In the current induced graph there are nonadjacent neighbors a,b of v whose only common neighbor is v. In the original graph their common neighbors can only be v and some of the deleted members of K. In particular their whole common-neighbor set is nonempty, saturated and a clique.

It remains to see that this very v has d(v)≥b(v)+q, even if larger-degree saturated members of K are present. The local isometry proof in the preceding paper applies under the sole assumption d(v)<b(v)+q at the current vertex. Explicitly, two exterior neighbors retain degree sum at least b(v)+k and so force the strict local wedge bound after i deletions. At i=0 the same bound works for any neighbor pair. At i≥1 with a remaining internal neighbor, c=|K|≥3 means k+c−2≤d(v)<b(v)+q≤k+q. Hence c≤q+1, q≥2 and i≤c−2≤q−1, using only v's upper bound. The remaining internal neighbor has current degree at least d(v)−i by deletion order. A high exterior neighbor gives local excess at most zero and n−i>3k+1. A low exterior neighbor gives k−δ≤q−c+1≤q−1−i from the same upper bound on v; this again satisfies the strict local wedge inequality. These contradictions exclude a failed deletion at v under its numerical upper bound, proving outcome 1.
