# Simultaneous cyclability of dense blocks

Let G be a finite graph of vertex connectivity s≥2. For 1≤j≤t let U_j be pairwise disjoint nonempty sets, of order m_j and with exterior vertex boundary of order b_j. Suppose the induced minimum degree of U_j is at least m_j−d_j and
    m_j≥2d_j+3(b_j+1)+1.
Then if t≤s a single cycle of G contains all vertices of all U_j. It may also be required to include any specified set of at most s−t vertices outside the blocks. There are no restrictions on mutual adjacency between blocks, and G need not satisfy a distance cap.

For one U form the bipartite graph between two copies of each boundary member and the vertices in U, with original incidences. Choose a maximum matching, and choose a vertex u of U unmatched by it. This is possible since m>2b. I claim that any cycle K containing u may be rerouted to cover all of U while keeping all original cycle vertices outside U. If K lies entirely in U, use Hamiltonicity of U. Otherwise its runs in U have prescribed boundary incidences at both ends and there are at most b such runs (each intervening nonempty outside segment uses a boundary vertex). When some run has two distinct terminals in U it can already be expanded by the dense-block interval routing lemma in ../order/dense-block-compression.md, whose local argument applies to cyclic lists of runs as well as paths. It remains the case where every run in U is a singleton.

From unmatched vertices of U traverse alternating paths, first along unmatched edges to the boundary copies and then along matched edges back into U. Every reached boundary copy is matched, or there is an augmenting path. All copies neighboring a reached U vertex are reached: unmatched incidences can be traversed, and a matched incidence to a reached U vertex is the one that led to it. The matched representatives of all reached boundary copies are distinct reached members of U. Retain singleton terminals outside the reached part. For every incidence at a reached singleton, use the matched representative of the corresponding boundary copy instead. Distinct copies can be allocated to the at most two cyclic incidences of each actual boundary vertex. The resulting terminals are distinct across changed incidences, and do not interfere with unchanged ones because all their representatives are reached vertices. In particular the singleton run at the chosen unmatched u now has two distinct terminals. If a single boundary vertex is the only outside point of the cycle, its two copies are available just as above; in fact no singleton-only cycle with that pattern can be a simple cycle. The dense-block lemma joins short runs and expands one flexible run through all the rest of U, retaining the exterior order.

Choose such an unmatched representative in each U_j. Dirac's cyclability theorem gives a cycle through these t representatives and all the prescribed outside vertices, since at most s vertices were designated. Successively apply the claim to each block. Earlier blocks and all remaining representatives stay on the cycle because every later change retains every point outside its own block. This proves the assertion.

In a qualifying graph the result gives a spanning path if the number M of vertices outside t such blocks satisfies M≤s−t+ceil(k/2)+1: designate min(M,s−t) remaining points, open the resulting cycle, then use the global almost-spanning gap. More generally, all vertices of each block are secured without using more than one of s designated positions per block, while keeping previously designated outside regions intact.
