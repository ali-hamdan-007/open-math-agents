# Independent check of the connectivity theorem for bounded local excess

Reviewed verify/local-excess-connectivity.md, including its extension of the 35 affine cases to r=1. The reduction to n≤(r+3)k+1 for a bound on wedges whose endpoints avoid one root is exactly the rooted local Ore theorem. For r=0 it combines with the previously proved bounded-order result. For positive r the half-connectivity case handles k≤4r+3.

In both kinds of block, the number of available distinct high ports after excluding one bad-pair endpoint is at least a=k−b+1. The terminal case b=h+1 needs no exclusion because each noncut vertex is adjacent to all other block vertices. A terminal tail can always traverse the entire block to its cut vertex: that prescribed pair cannot be the pair of the cut vertex's two neighbors. The other side then contributes ≥h−1 additional vertices on each tail, and the exterior count contributes ≥h more. This correctly compensates the one missing connectivity mark and gap in the terminal version of J. Both applications of the matching-cover duality have surviving vertices on each side since b−1≥2r+1 when k≥4r+4.

I checked all 35 stated affine inequalities independently at the r=1 boundary and their slopes. The large-k identity for the real endpoint βk and both endpoint positivity margins also hold. For r=1, k=8 through 14 are included directly in the affine table; k≥15 follows from the order-4k+1 theorem for k≥13.

At equality b=h+2 and a=h−1. After fixing an arbitrary high contact edge and discarding the possible low vertex and the bad-pair endpoint distinct from the prescribed contact, at least b−3≥a−1 eligible high ports remain. Every one has at least a−1 distinct eligible path marks after removal of the preassigned mark. Matching a−1 further marks and ports follows by the cover argument. Prescribing every high contact this way forces exactly a fixed marks and completeness of high-to-mark edges. Every high vertex must then meet all b−1 other block vertices, so the entire block is a clique. The same prescribed-edge argument now works for a low vertex's marks without any exceptional pair. All marks therefore have fixed indices under two-ended rotations on the same longest vertex set. The endpoint theorem supplies ≥k+1 high endpoints. Those adjacent to a mark have distance two to a fixed omitted high vertex, so a high endpoint avoiding all marks exists. A pivot past the first mark forces its own index to be a multiple of b+1; since |P|=(a+1)(b+1)−1 it must itself be a mark. This bounds the avoiding endpoint's degree by b−1<k. The full connectivity theorem follows, including r=Δ−k.
