# Joining the components of a separating set for an even-distance cover

Let S be a cardinality-minimum s-cut in a Graph 189 graph of order n, with exactly two sides A and B. Suppose both have a high vertex with no neighbor in S. A high vertex means ambient degree at least k. For each side C suppose some vertex c of C makes the graph induced by S plus c connected. Equivalently such a c touches each connected component of the graph induced by S. This includes disconnected S and does not require c to touch every anchor.

If S is disconnected then n≤(s+1)(k+1)−1, unless the stronger n≤4k−s+1 holds. In particular an s=4 cut of this kind proves traceability for k≥15 by the n≤6k−11 four-connected order theorem. The connected-S result remains in connected-separator-cover.md.

To prove the bound, a high nonport on either side is at distance two from S: a high nonport opposite is at distance at most four by the high-pair diameter theorem, and any path between them needs at least two steps on each side. Each side has at most k−s high nonports since any high opposite nonport sees them at distance four plus a distinct second layer of size at least s (that layer separates it from the nonempty fourth layer). A universal port is adjacent to every member of S. If universal ports exist on both sides, every port lies at distance two from the opposite universal port, yielding at most k ports and k−s high nonports per side. Add S and the one possible low vertex to obtain n≤4k−s+1.

Otherwise choose c in the side containing no universal port; if neither has one choose either. Set T=S∪{c}. All high nonports lie in the closed even-distance set of an anchor. Any port outside the union of the closed even-distance sets of T has odd distances to all of T. These distances are equal: adjacent roots differ in distance by at most one and T induces a connected graph. As the port touches S, the common distance is one. It is a universal port adjacent to c, which is impossible on the chosen side by its definition and on the opposite side by the cut. The closed even sets thus cover every vertex except possibly the low nonport. Their total size is at most (s+1)(k+1). Because S is disconnected, T is connected and not complete. Some two vertices of T are at distance exactly two in T and in G (they are nonadjacent in the induced graph). Both are counted twice, once by their own set and once by their partner's. The union plus the one exceptional vertex has the claimed bound.

More generally one can use any connected induced T containing S. The common odd distance argument still applies. If T uses vertices on both sides, every universal port is covered, and at most one vertex is missed; each internal unordered pair of roots at positive even ambient distance saves two duplicate incidences (count incidences at each root relative to the diagonal). Thus n≤|T|(k+1)−2e_T+1, where e_T is the number of such pairs. These estimates use the original even-distance hypothesis; the last inequality itself is independent of any traceability result.
