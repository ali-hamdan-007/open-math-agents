# Ali's contact-path argument and its first size obstruction

Ali Hamdan supplied the one-apex construction and the one-small-side joining argument in this Work chat on 28 November 2026. The coordinator and two independent reviewers checked the original. A reproducible exhaustion of labelled 2-connected graphs of orders 3,4,5,6 with a distinguished apex verifies the side-path bound in 1, 10, 238, and 11,368 instances respectively. See check-separator-apex.py and its log in this directory. These are mathematical arguments and finite tests.

Let S be a minimum vertex cut of order s, with precisely two components A,B after deletion. Write k for the second-smallest degree, d=k−s≥3, h=ceil(k/2), and m_X=|X|. A port in a component touches S. For either side B of order at least two, contract the outside to an apex b joined to every port. The resulting graph is 2-connected: after deleting any side vertex each surviving component still reaches a port (otherwise the deletion separates G); deleting b leaves B connected. A high vertex (original degree at least k) has augmented degree at least d+1. If the sole low vertex z belongs to B, temporarily join it to b when this edge is absent. Thus no two vertices of augmented degree less than d+1 are at distance two. Fan's theorem supplies a cycle on at least min(m_B+1,2d+2) vertices. A cycle through b opens into a B path with at least one genuine port endpoint, since only one apex edge could be artificial. A cycle avoiding b is wholly in B and can be reached along a shortest path from a genuine port, then traversed. The resulting B path from a port has at least min(m_B,2d+1) vertices. A singleton requires no augmentation.

If |A|≤2d−1, any chosen joining anchor r can start a path in A covering all its high vertices. If A is wholly high it has internal minimum degree d and a Hamilton cycle by Dirac. If it contains a low vertex z, the all-high part A−z has order between d and 2d−2, unless A is the singleton z, and minimum degree at least d−1; it too has a Hamilton cycle. Open the cycle at an r-neighbor or, if r touches only z, begin at z and enter the cycle. The total omissions are at most (s−1)+1_{low in A}+(m_B−2d−1)_+. Ali's cutoff follows exactly. A prescribed r can genuinely force omission of z, as shown in ../verify/prescribed-small-side.md.

## Choosing the joining anchor and trimming nonports

For each prescribed pair R of anchors the same augmentation may retain only ports touching R. Removing S−R and any one B-vertex costs ≤s−1 vertices, so every component after the one deletion reaches a genuine retained port. A high vertex losing all its apex contacts touches ≤s−2 anchors and retains degree at least d+2. The same proof works. Hence at most one anchor of S fails to start a side path of the displayed length. At s≥3 the anchor choices for the two sides intersect. The guaranteed total omissions are at most

    s−1 + (m_A−2d−1)_+ + (m_B−2d−1)_+.

This handles larger A as well as a low vertex on either side. It does not require either side to be internally 2-connected. See ../order/paths-through-two-sides.md.

For a different estimate suppose A has a high nonport x. Every high B nonport is at distance exactly four from x by the high-pair diameter bound; the intervening second layer from x has ≥s distinct vertices if such a B nonport exists. Thus B has at most N=k−s+1_{low nonport in B} nonports. If B is traceable and has a port, orient a spanning B path from the end with the shorter initial run of nonports. The two terminal runs are disjoint, so trimming costs at most floor(N/2), independently of m_B. For s=3 this is ≤h−2 if B is wholly high, ≤h−1 if B contains the low. In the second case A is wholly high. When |A|≤2d−1, joining the resulting path to A via the chosen anchor therefore loses at most h+1 vertices **across all G**. No lower bound on the number of contacts of that chosen anchor is needed. This conditional argument applies to arbitrarily large B. At a nonspanning B path with q vertices outside it of which t are nonports, the analogous bound on the B omissions is q+floor((N−t)/2), whenever the path contains a port. An independent argument establishing a sufficiently long ordinary path in B is needed to use this estimate.

## The discrete boundary at s=3

When k≥24, |A|≤2k−5, the bare two-apex guarantee and the fork's improved omitted-component bound first leave |B|=3k−9 as a numerical possibility. Even this boundary can be excluded without either side being wholly high. For n≤5k−11 any counterexample must omit at least k−1 vertices on every longest path. Indeed the prior near-degree exterior bound forces a high-containing omitted component C to have m≥k−2 (the concave estimate for m≤k−3 appears in ../order/paths-through-two-sides.md). If m=k−2 and all of C is high, its minimum internal degree h gives Hamilton-connectedness; each vertex has at least three distinct contacts on the longest path. Three disjoint matched edges give n≥5m+3>5k−11. If C contains the unique low z, H=C−z has minimum degree h−1 and order m−1. Since 2(h−1)≥m, it is Hamilton-connected. Its vertices still have at least three distinct path contacts; König's theorem supplies three disjoint matched edges, as |H|≥3. Both inner gaps and both tails have length at least m−1 outside the marks. At least one tail can use all m vertices: choose any neighbor of z in C, and use the tail whose matched H vertex differs from that neighbor, traverse H to it, then append z. Thus n≥m+3+4(m−1)+1=5m=5k−10, a contradiction. For |A|≤2k−5 and |B|=3k−9 one has n≤5k−11, yet the two-apex path omits at most k−2. This rules out that boundary. If |B| is smaller the same proof applies (or the coarser bound already settles it). Later moderate-order theorems may move the frontier farther; this calculation documents what the apex and omitted-component arguments themselves prove.
