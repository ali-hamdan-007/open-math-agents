# Moderate graphs at second-smallest degrees seven and eight

This extends [the degree-nine paper argument](moderate-degree-nine.md) to k=7 and k=8. Keep its hypotheses, symbols, longest H-path and exterior-component bounds. The two extra cases received independent mathematical review. Graph189ModerateBorder.lean checks the boundary sizes, order inequalities and forced positions. The routing uses mathematical results still being formalized.

For connectivity at least three H=G−z is two-connected as before. At connectivity two let d=k−2 and h=4. The three-component part of the minimum-cut proof still works: all three components have order at most k+2. For a wholly high middle, 2d≥k+3. A low-deleted high core at an end has order at most k+1≤2(d−1). In the two-component count the ordinary bound for |A|≤2d−1 still works as h≥3s−2. In the one extra possibility |A|=2d, we have |B|≤2d+1, so B spans from some contact. At k=8 the sides then both have order twelve and one is wholly high, hence has a Hamilton cycle by Dirac. At k=7 a path of at least d+1 vertices suffices in A, since the separator and A together then lose at most five vertices. Any prescribed contact can start such a path. From a high contact take a maximal path avoiding the low vertex; its high endpoint has at most one neighbor disallowed, so the path has at least d vertices. If it has exactly d, its endpoint must be adjacent to the missing low and the path extends. From the low contact, take a maximal path; its other endpoint is high and supplies the required degree. If there is also an omitted singleton it is the sole low, so both larger sides are wholly high and A spans by Dirac. Thus at either degree a counterexample of connectivity two still has unique minimum cut N(z), and H is two-connected.

In the large-block argument put q=|Q| and e=|H outside P and Q|. The two-link inequality forces q=k−2 and e≤1. If e=1 every cycle vertex has a direct P contact by its H-degree ≥k−1. Adjacent Q vertices with distinct contacts give n≥4q+4>3k+2. Otherwise all cycle vertices have the same single P contact: any edge with nonempty contact sets at both ends and no two distinct choices has the same singleton at both ends. Each cycle vertex must be adjacent to the sole extra vertex, since q−1 other cycle vertices plus that one P contact cannot supply degree k−1. Inserting the extra vertex on a cycle edge gives a cycle of order q+1, already excluded by the two-link bound.

If e=0 each cycle vertex has at least two distinct P contacts. Two adjacent Q vertices with different contacts give n≥4q+3, excluding k=8 immediately. At k=7 this gives equality: n=23, q=5 and P has 17 vertices. The two outside tails and inner gap must all have length five, counting unmarked vertices. Hence every such pair uses indices 5 and 11, starting at zero. For every contact of every Q vertex an adjacent Q vertex has two choices, so every Q vertex has exactly those two contacts. Its H-degree ≥6 forces it adjacent to the other four cycle vertices, and its G-degree ≥7 forces adjacency to z. Replacing the five interior vertices of P between the marks by the six vertices of the clique Q∪{z} yields a G-path missing only five, within the original gap.

For a short leaf block m≥4. At m=5, only relevant for k=8, three eligible contacts give n≥5m+4>26. At m=4 the block is a clique because every nonarticulation vertex has at least three neighbors there. At k=7 three marked contacts yield n≥24>23. At k=8 four suffice when this is the entire component. If it is a proper leaf, match three of its nonarticulation vertices. The usual count gives 24; each of the two outer detours can end at the articulation and continue to an outside neighbor in D, gaining one per tail, and that neighbor is also an extra graph vertex outside P and M. Thus n≥27>26.

The short proof of the moderate parent case now covers every k≥7. The independent focused Lean theorem for k≤2 leaves four values 3, 4, 5 and 6 to join or treat on this particular route; the earlier comprehensive paper proof used separate small-degree classifications.
