# Second-smallest degree six at connectivity at least three

Assume the graph satisfies the original even-distance cap and is three-connected. Its second-smallest degree is six. The rooted layer bound gives $n\le 1+\delta+6+12=19+\delta\le25$. The following argument specializes the three-link estimate and endpoint rotation in the full paper. The two-connected case with a separating pair has the separate side analysis. [Three-link argument](../lowblock/high-connectivity-complete.md), [endpoint pivot count](../../rot/even-exterior.md) and [small separating pairs](../sep/small-two-cuts.md)

Let $P$ be an ordinary longest path, and let $J$ be the induced graph of its omissions. Every omitted vertex of ambient degree at least six has degree at least three in $J$. There is at most one exceptional vertex. An exterior cycle on $q$ vertices admits three disjoint links to $P$. The two tails of $P$ contain at least $q$ vertices each. The two open intervals between the three marks each contain at least $\lceil q/2\rceil+1$ vertices by using the longer cycle arc. Counting the cycle and marks yields $n\ge3q+2\lceil q/2\rceil+5$. In particular an exterior five-cycle would force $n\ge26$. This inequality passed the pinned Lean kernel with only the three standard axioms. [Three trimmed links](../lowblock/Graph189ThreeExteriorLinks.lean)

If a high vertex is omitted, take a longest path of $J$ with a high endpoint. Endpoint rotations give a set $B$ with at most four vertices and at least three vertices $R\subseteq B$ whose entire $J$-neighborhoods lie in $B$. At least two of these vertices are high. Each such vertex sees all the three other members, so $|B|=4$. Let $D$ be the set of all high members of $B$ whose $J$-neighborhoods lie in $B$. [Rotation window](../lowblock/Graph189OrdinaryRotationWindow.lean)

## Three full high vertices

If $|D|\ge3$, the graph induced by $B$ is a clique. Each member of $D$ needs at least three neighbors on $P$. Choose three different members and match them to three distinct marks $i<j<k$. Paths through the clique give at least four vertices in each outer tail and each open interval. Every contact of every vertex of $D$ is also at least four positions from either endpoint, because a rooted four-vertex path is available there.

Fix $a\in D$ and a different $b\in D$. The cross-contact construction for adjacent omitted vertices gives at least $|A_a|+|A_b|\ge6$ distinct distance-two vertices from $a$, where $A_a,A_b$ are their mark sets. Every index in that construction is either a mark, one less than a mark, or one more than a mark. All these vertices are strictly internal to $P$. As the second layer has size at most six, an endpoint of $P$ cannot see any mark of $a$, since adjacency to such a mark would put the endpoint at distance two from $a$. This holds for every chosen mark. [Cross-contact indices](../sep/cover/final82/lean/Graph189ContactColumns.lean)

From the initial endpoint of $P$, a chord to any of the next four positions after a chosen mark would skip at most three intervening vertices. Traversing the four-vertex exterior path, joining at the mark, following the reversed prefix and taking the chord would lengthen $P$. Thus the three disjoint intervals $[i,i+4]$, $[j,j+4]$, $[k,k+4]$ each exclude five potential neighbors of the initial endpoint. These intervals avoid the initial endpoint. All its neighbors occur on $P$, so its degree is at most $|P|-16\le n-4-16\le5$. Reversing $P$ gives the same estimate for its other endpoint. Their distinctness contradicts the second-smallest degree. [Endpoint-chord splice](../../rot/Graph189RotationGap.lean)

## Two full high vertices

Now $D=\{a,b\}$. The three trapped vertices are $a,b,z$ with $z$ the unique low vertex. Write $c$ for the fourth member of $B$. This vertex is high, and it has a neighbor $w$ outside $B$ because it does not belong to $D$. Only $c$ can meet a member outside $B$. Every vertex of $J-B$ is high, so each has at least two neighbors in $J-B$ after losing at most $c$. Choose a three-vertex path $w,u,v$ there. In particular $J$ has at least seven vertices.

Match $a,b$ to two different path contacts. Each of $a,b$ begins a seven-vertex exterior path: start with $a,z,b,c$ or $b,z,a,c$ and continue with $w,u,v$. The three-vertex route $a,z,b$ fits between the marks. The two tails, the middle gap and the marks give $|P|\ge7+7+3+2=19$, so $n\ge26$. This completes the ordinary-path contradiction.

## The possible single omission

A nonspanning $P$ can therefore omit only the unique low vertex. For $n\le22$ the checked protected-path theorem inserts this vertex in a spanning path. For $n\ge23$ the rooted layer bound gives $\delta\ge4$, and the checked high-minimum longest-path gap rules out a single omission. The pinned kernel verified the original conclusion under three-connectivity and the connectivity-independent four-omission threshold, with only the three standard axioms. [Original graph statements](Graph189SixThreeConnected.lean), [four-window rotation](../lowblock/Graph189SixWideRotation.lean), [full-port contradiction](../lowblock/Graph189SixFullPorts.lean). [Protected insertion](../verify/Graph189SixProtected.lean) and [high-minimum path gap](../lowblock/Graph189HighMinimumGap.lean)

The same pivot count supplies the short-path threshold used at a separating pair. Suppose some path omits at most four vertices and $\delta\ge3$. Take an ordinary longest path. If exactly four are omitted, at least three are high, and each needs three omitted neighbors. The omissions form a clique with three trapped high vertices. The first part of the proof rules this out at both endpoints, independently of connectivity. At most three omissions give traceability from the high-minimum path gap for $n\ge23$ or the low-deletion and protected-insertion theorems for $n\le22$. Thus a two-cut route losing four vertices altogether suffices at degree six.
