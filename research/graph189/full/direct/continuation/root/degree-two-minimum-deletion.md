# Hamiltonian minimum deletion at degree two

Let G satisfy the registered hypotheses and let z be a vertex of degree two. Suppose G−z has a Hamiltonian path. Write k for the second-smallest degree, and assume k≥7. The following argument gives a Hamiltonian path in G. The full degree-two argument below was independently reviewed on paper. Its cited Lean modules cover the indicated portions.

Choose a longest path Q through z and let F be the graph induced by its omitted vertices, of order m. Assume G is nontraceable. The verified root bound gives n≤3k+1. The two neighbors on the Hamiltonian deletion path give three gaps, hence 3+3m≤n. Consequently m≤k−1. If m≤k−2, the three-index protected order theorem gives 4(k−1)≤n+1, impossible at k≥7. Thus m=k−1. All vertices in F are high and have at least two contacts on Q. The kernel-checked protected exterior degree theorem gives minimum degree at least floor(k/2) in F. [Protected order theorem](../lowblock/Graph189ProtectedOrder.lean) and [deletion gap](../lowblock/Graph189ProtectedExteriorSize.lean)

For even k, F is Hamilton-connected by prescribed-endpoint Ore closure. For odd k=2t+1, F has 2t vertices and minimum degree at least t. Suppose a has F-degree t. It has at least t+1 contacts on Q. For each neighbor b of a in F the protected contact inequality bounds the sum of this contact count and the ambient degree of b by k+2 plus the number of their common exterior neighbors. Hence a and b have at least t−1 common exterior neighbors. These exhaust the neighbors of a other than b, so a's neighbors form a clique. Any such a can be prescribed as one endpoint in F with any other endpoint y. To see this, close the missing edges incident to a at the endpoint threshold 2t. Once a is universal, every old nonneighbor has gained a neighbor. The remaining vertices of degree t lie in the original clique of neighbors of a, so every outstanding nonedge has degree sum at least 2t+1. Ordinary prescribed-endpoint closure completes the graph. [Simplicial endpoint proof](../lowblock/Graph189SimplicialEndpoint.lean) and [protected inequality](../lowblock/Graph189ProtectedExteriorDegree.lean)

If F has at least three vertices of degree t, match three of those vertices to distinct contacts. Each has at least t+1≥3 choices. Any two of the chosen vertices admit a spanning path between them. The three marks delimit the prefix, suffix and two inner gaps of Q. At most one gap contains z, and each other gap must contain at least m vertices, since it can be replaced by a spanning exterior path. Including the three marks and F gives n≥4m+3>3k+1. If F has at most two vertices of degree t, close all missing edges incident to a higher-degree vertex at the ordinary threshold 2t+1. Only one missing pair can remain. Both endpoints now have degree at least 2t−2, which closes the last edge. Thus the surviving alternative always gives Hamilton-connectedness of F. [Protected matching construction](../lowblock/Graph189ProtectedMatching.lean)

The two-port Hall sharpening now forces all of F to have the same two contacts on Q, since a third matched port again gives n≥4m+3. The exterior is a clique by the ambient degree count. Both conclusions hold for every longest path through z. The two marks on Q form three nonempty gaps, of which at least two avoid z and have length at least m. Since |Q|≤2m+4, there is an unmarked gap of exactly m avoiding z. Replace that gap by an exterior spanning path. The new longest path contains z and has the same uniformity property. A bounding mark sees one vertex of the old gap along the original Q and hence sees every vertex of the gap by the new uniformity. Every one of those m vertices is therefore at distance two from an original exterior vertex x. Among the predecessors and successors of the two original marks at least two distinct shifts lie outside the gap. Those are further distance-two vertices from x, giving m+2=k+1, contrary to the allowance k. If the deletion has no Hamiltonian path, the minimum-cut anchor degree bound puts z outside every minimum two-cut. Making z universal raises connectivity to at least three, so the linked-set theorem gives n≥4k−5, contradicting n≤3k+1 at k≥7. Together the two alternatives prove traceability at every order for a two-connected graph with a vertex of degree two. Combining this with the connectivity and minimum-degree bounds closes the entire moderate parent obligation for k≥12.
