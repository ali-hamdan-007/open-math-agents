# Exact terminal paths around a universal vertex

Let H have N vertices, a universal vertex x, and minimum degree at least d≥3. Path order counts vertices. These are elementary auxiliary results for the H* cores in sep/cover. They apply to arbitrary specified distinct endpoints. In the routing results below, whenever d≥3 and N≤3d−6 all mentioned pieces have enough vertices for the respective Dirac/Ore criteria.

For any endpoints a,b, there is a path on at least d+1 vertices. For a,b different from x, take a longest rooted path from a in H−{x,b}, whose terminal has at least d−2 neighbors there. Append x,b. For b=x use the same argument with only x deleted.

If a,b are distinct from x and there is no path between them on d+2 vertices, a,b must lie in the same complete component of H−x of size exactly d. To see this, the rooted path P above has exactly d−1 vertices. Its terminal sees all other P vertices. Holding a fixed, each point of P other than a can be made the terminal by a single rotation at its predecessor. Thus none has a neighbor off P in H−{x,b}, and the lower degree bound d−2 in that graph makes P a clique. If b had any neighbor outside P,x,b, we could traverse P, then x, that neighbor, and b. Consequently b is adjacent to x and every member of P and has degree exactly d. By symmetry a has degree exactly d too. Finally every interior P member must see b and x to have degree d. These vertices have no other neighbors in H−x, as asserted.

Suppose that H−x has a cut vertex c and is connected, and N≤3d−6. Each component of H−{x,c} has at least d−1 vertices. There are exactly two, U and V, since three would require N≥3d−1. Each has order at most N−2−(d−1)=N−d−1 and internal minimum degree at least d−2. Each is Hamilton-connected and stays Hamilton-connected after deleting any one vertex, by the endpoint degree-sum criterion. Both have neighbors of c. Any prescribed endpoints outside {x,c} in different components span U,V and x using Hamiltonian paths and x, losing only c. Same-side endpoints, say a,b∈U, can span all of H. Choose any c-neighbor u in U. If u differs from both a and b, traverse all of U−b from a to u, use c, traverse V starting at a c-neighbor, and finish x,b. If u equals a or b, begin there, use c, span V starting at a c-neighbor, use x and span the rest of U ending at the other prescribed endpoint. This yields paths on at least N−1 vertices for every terminal pair outside {x,c}.

If H−x is disconnected and N≤3d−6, it has exactly two components, both Hamilton-connected by the same count (minimum internal degree ≥d−1). Any terminals in different components have a spanning path via x. A rooted path with just one prescribed non-x endpoint can likewise span H. If H−x is 2-connected, H is 3-connected (assuming N≥4). The theorem in Long Paths Through Specified Vertices in 3-Connected Graphs gives paths between any prescribed endpoints of order at least min(N,2d−1), by taking its degree-sum threshold min(N+1,2d).
