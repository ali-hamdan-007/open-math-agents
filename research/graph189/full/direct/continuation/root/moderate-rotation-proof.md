The two-link cycle count below implements the large-block inequality in [the full moderate paper](moderate-degree-nine.md). The protected degree-seven deletion has passed the pinned kernel. The original theorem files below are still undergoing replay.

Let \(G\) be a qualifying connected graph, \(n=|V(G)|\), \(k\) its second-smallest degree, \(\delta\) its minimum degree and \(\kappa\) its vertex connectivity. The registered short-order and cut-vertex theorems reduce the argument to \(\kappa\ge2\).

## The lower order bound for \(k\ge7\)

Suppose \(G\) is nontraceable. At \(\kappa\ge3\), the linked-set order theorem at index three gives \(n\ge4k-5\). Let \(\kappa=2\) and choose a minimum-degree vertex \(z\). Degree two is settled at every order. If \(G-z\) is traceable then \(z\) is low and the protected path bound at index three again gives \(n\ge4k-5\). If \(G-z\) is nontraceable and \(z\) avoids every minimum cut, making \(z\) universal raises connectivity and the minimum-deletion order theorem gives the same bound. [Linked-set order theorem](../lowblock/Graph189RotationMinOrder.lean) and [degree-two original theorem](Graph189DegreeTwoOriginal.lean)

It remains to consider a minimum two-cut containing \(z\). Its two sides each have a neighbor of \(z\). The original distance cap gives \(\delta+2\ge k+1\). If \(\delta\ge k\), take any vertex \(w\) whose completion raises connectivity. If \(\delta=k-1\), choose a smallest side \(A\) among all minimum two-cuts. The atom lemma shows that every vertex of \(A\) avoids every minimum cut, since \(3\kappa<2(\delta+1)\). In particular \(z\) lies outside \(A\). Its high vertices force \(|A|\ge k-1\). If \(z\) were adjacent to all of \(A\), then \(z\) would be in the separator of \(A\), with another neighbor on the opposite side, forcing degree at least \(k\). Thus there is a nonneighbor \(w\in A\) whose completion raises connectivity. Making \(w\) universal also raises the degree of \(z\). In both cases the resulting graph \(K\) has minimum degree at least \(k\) and connectivity at least three. [Atom and completion](../order/Graph189AtomNonneighbor.lean)

The graph \(H=K-w=G-w\) has minimum degree at least \(k-1\). A longest cycle in \(K\) gives a longest path in \(H\). The high-minimum path gap and the independent-exterior bound supply an actual exterior edge. If \(a,b\) are adjacent in this exterior, the cross-contact inequality gives \(\deg_H(a)+\deg_H(b)\le k+2\deg_{\rm ext}(a)\). Every nonisolated exterior vertex therefore has degree at least three there. It supplies a cycle of length at least four in the exterior of the longest cycle of \(K\). The checked Bondy order bound at index three gives \(n\ge4k-5\). This proves the original conjecture through \(n\le4k-6\), including every moderate order \(n\le3k+2\) at \(k\ge8\). ## The last moderate order at \(k=7\)

Here \(n=23\). Degree two is already settled, so \(\delta\ge3\). If deleting a low vertex \(z\) leaves a Hamiltonian path, a longest path through \(z\) omits at most four vertices by the deletion bound. Every omitted vertex has at least three omitted neighbors. Thus, unless this path spans, the exterior has exactly four vertices forming a clique. Each needs at least four path contacts. Four distinct matched contacts force \(n\ge5\cdot4+4=24\), including the four omissions and allowing for the one gap that contains \(z\). [Protected deletion](../lowblock/Graph189SevenProtected.lean)

Take an ordinary longest path \(P\). Suppose a high vertex is omitted. Every high omitted vertex has at least four omitted neighbors. If the exterior has a cycle on \(q\ge6\) vertices, take two disjoint links from it to \(P\). Each outer tail contains at least \(q\) vertices and the open middle interval at least \(\lceil q/2\rceil+1\), since one can route along the longer of the two cycle arcs. Counting the cycle and both marks gives \(n\ge3q+\lceil q/2\rceil+3\ge24\). [Menger links and cycle detours](../lowblock/Graph189TwoExteriorLinks.lean)

Otherwise every exterior cycle has at most five vertices. Choose a longest exterior path with a high initial endpoint. Its four or more neighbors occur within its first five vertices. Rotating at each neighbor produces four distinct endpoints whose exterior neighbors are all in the same set \(B\) of five vertices. At least three of these endpoints are high, so each is adjacent to all four other members of \(B\). The graph induced by \(B\) has minimum degree three and is Hamilton-connected by Ore closure. Each of the three high vertices needs three path contacts. Match them to three distinct marks. The two outer tails and two open inner gaps each have five or more vertices. Including \(B\) and the marks gives \(n\ge28\). It follows that \(P\) can omit at most the unique low vertex. The protected deletion theorem settles that possibility.
