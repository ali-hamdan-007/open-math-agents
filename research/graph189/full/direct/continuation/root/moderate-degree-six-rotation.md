# A longest-path proof at second-smallest degree six

This is a paper argument for the moderate parent at $k=6$, $n\le 20$. The kernel replay is outstanding. The general two-link and rotation arguments are also used in the [degree-seven formalization](Graph189ModerateSeven.lean).

Take a two-connected graph satisfying the original cap, a minimum-degree vertex $z$, and an ordinary longest path $P$. Write $J$ for the induced graph on the omitted vertices. Every vertex has at most six vertices at distance two. Except possibly $z$, every vertex is high, of degree at least six.

## Exterior degree

For minimum degree at least three, every omitted high vertex has at least three neighbors in $J$, by the checked exterior half-degree theorem. Suppose the minimum degree is two. The rooted layer bound at $z$ gives $n\le 19$. An isolated high omission would have at least six path contacts, whose predecessors and successors give at least seven distinct vertices at distance two. An omitted high vertex with a high omitted neighbor retains at least three omitted neighbors by the cross-contact inequality. It remains to exclude a high vertex $x$ with sole omitted neighbor $z$.

Such an $x$ has at least five marks. Every set of $t$ marks has at least $t+1$ distinct predecessor and successor positions. Consequently $t=5$, and all four internal spacings are two to reduce the ten shifted positions to six. If $x$ starts an exterior path on four vertices, its first and last marks have four-vertex tails. Then $|P|\ge 4+9+4=17$, and including the four exterior vertices gives $n\ge 21$. Thus the component is either the edge $xz$ or the two-leaf path $x,z,w$. In the latter case $w$ also has five marks at step two. Both rows have three-vertex tails at both ends and $|P|\le 16$, so their starting indices differ by at most one. For either offset, two distinct marks from the two rows are at most two apart. The three-vertex route between their leaves forces their distance to be at least four.

In the edge case $z$ has one path contact $j$. For every $x$-mark $i$, either $i=j$ or $|i-j|\ge 3$. Since the $x$-row has step two, $j$ lies at least three positions beyond its span. With two-vertex tails beyond the extreme marks, $|P|\ge 16$. At most one other vertex can be omitted and it would be an isolated high vertex, already excluded. For each pair of successive $x$-marks exchange $x$ for the vertex between them. Each such midpoint is high and it must have a neighbor off the new longest path. The only other omission is $z$. There are four distinct midpoints, contradicting degree two. This proves the exterior degree claim for every minimum degree.

## An ordinary longest path omits no high vertex

A cycle of $q\ge 5$ vertices in $J$ gives two disjoint links to $P$ by two-connectivity. Trim the links to their first path contacts. The two outer tails each contain at least $q$ vertices, and the open middle interval contains at least $\lceil q/2\rceil+1$ by the longer cycle arc. Including both marks and the omitted cycle gives $n\ge 3q+\lceil q/2\rceil+3\ge 21$.

Suppose a high vertex is omitted. Then $J$ has an edge. Choose a longest path of $J$ with its first endpoint high, reversing it if needed. That endpoint has at least three neighbors, each among its first four vertices because chords farther away give a cycle of length at least five. Rotation at each neighbor yields three distinct endpoints, all of whose $J$-neighbors lie in the same four-vertex set $B$. This is the endpoint form of the [rotation window](../lowblock/Graph189RotationExterior.lean). Every high rotated endpoint is adjacent to the other three members of $B$, so has at least three path contacts.

If three rotated endpoints are high, $B$ is a clique. Match those three vertices to three distinct marks of $P$. The two tails and two open intervals have at least four vertices each. Together with $B$ and the three marks this gives $n\ge 23$.

Otherwise two rotated endpoints $a,b$ are high, the third is $z$, and the fourth vertex $c$ of $B$ is high. If $c$ has no exterior neighbor outside $B$, its three required exterior neighbors make $B$ a clique and the same three-match applies to $a,b,c$. Otherwise let $w$ be such a neighbor of $c$. Only $c$ can meet vertices outside $B$ in $J$. The high vertex $w$ needs at least two additional neighbors outside $B$, so at least seven vertices are omitted. Choose distinct marks for $a,b$. Each can start a five-vertex exterior path ending at $w$, since one can traverse $B$ from $a$ or $b$ to $c$ using the other high endpoint and $z$. There is a three-vertex route between $a,b$. Thus $|P|\ge 5+5+3+2=15$, and $n\ge 22$.

## Inserting the omitted minimum vertex

If $P$ omits a vertex it now omits just $z$, and $G-z$ is traceable. Choose a longest path $Q$ through $z$. Its exterior $E$, if nonempty, has minimum internal degree at least three by the protected half-degree theorem. Write $m=|E|$. The checked deletion bound gives $1+\delta+(\delta+1)m\le n$. Thus $m=4$ if $\delta\ge 3$, and $m=4$ or $5$ if $\delta=2$.

For $m=5$, the exterior is Hamilton-connected by Ore closure, since $2\cdot 3=5+1$. All its vertices have at least two contacts. Three matched contacts would give $n\ge 4m+3=23$; the checked Hall strengthening therefore shows all contacts coincide in a single pair of marks. This conclusion also holds after a length-preserving exchange avoiding $z$. Here $n\le 19$, so $|Q|\le 14=2m+4$, and the original cap is $7=m+2$. The [two-mark exchange](../lowblock/Graph189TwoMarkExchange.lean) excludes this case.

For $m=4$, the exterior is a clique. Each vertex has at least three contacts, so three distinct contacts can be matched. Every gap has at least one vertex. Each gap avoiding $z$ has at least four vertices. Including the four omissions and three marks gives $n\ge 20$. This settles $\delta=2$. In the only remaining order $20$, three gaps have four vertices and the gap containing $z$ has one. Numbering positions of $Q$ from zero, $z$ is at position $0,5,10,$ or $15$. For each position the three matched marks are uniquely determined. Every individual contact extends to a three-row matching, so all four exterior vertices use exactly these same three marks.

Exchange a four-vertex gap avoiding $z$ for a spanning path of the exterior clique. All four newly omitted vertices are high, and the same three-mark conclusion applies to the new protected-longest path. A bounding mark adjacent to the original gap endpoint is therefore adjacent to every newly omitted vertex. Every newly omitted vertex has distance two from an original omission via that mark, since it was not a contact on the original path. Among the six shifts of the three original marks, at most one pair coincides (only the one-vertex gap has marks at distance two). At most two lie in the exchanged gap. Together these give four plus at least three distinct distance-two witnesses at an original omission, contradicting the cap of six.
