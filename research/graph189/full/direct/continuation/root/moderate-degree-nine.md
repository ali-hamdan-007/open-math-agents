# The moderate parent case for every second-smallest degree at least nine

Let G be two-connected and satisfy the original even-distance cap. If k is its second-smallest degree, k≥9 and n≤3k+2, then G has a Hamiltonian path. This direct paper proof deals with the full H1 order range at those degrees. It uses the paper path-gap lemma and standard graph theory. The arithmetic has a separate Lean file. The entire graph argument has received independent mathematical review and still needs formalization.

Choose a minimum-degree vertex z. Every vertex of H=G−z has degree at least k−1 in H and at least k in G. If G is at least three-connected, H is two-connected. If G has connectivity two, the minimum-cut argument in [moderate-minimum-cuts.md](moderate-minimum-cuts.md) shows that a counterexample has only the minimum separator N(z), where z is its unique low vertex. Deleting z cannot then leave a separating vertex: a cut vertex v of H would make {z,v} a minimum cut of G containing z. Again H is two-connected. The first source also handles the hypothetical connectivity-two counterexample without any low vertex.

Suppose G has no Hamiltonian path. Put h=ceil(k/2). Choose a longest path P in H. An exterior component D with an edge exists, and each of its vertices has D-degree at least h−1. Here is the input in detail. The path is also a path in G, so the established whole-graph gap shows that it misses at least h+2 vertices in G, thus at least h+1 in H. If all H exterior vertices were isolated, each would attach to at least k−1 path vertices. Those contacts are nonconsecutive and avoid the ends. Their predecessors and successors on P give at least k vertices at distance two in G: each shifted set has size at least k−1 and its minimum index differs from that of the other set. This uses up the cap. The vertex has exactly k−1 path contacts and must be adjacent in G to z, since it is high. Two such exterior vertices see each other at distance two through z, exceeding the cap. For an exterior edge xy, write a(x) for contacts on P and q(x) for exterior neighbors. The checked two-vertex shift inequality gives a(x)+deg_H(y)≤k+q(x). Since a(x)+q(x)≥k−1 and deg_H(y)≥k−1, we get q(x)≥h−1. Thus every vertex in D has the asserted degree.

Take M equal to D if D is two-connected, or a leaf block of D otherwise. A bridge leaf is impossible as its nonarticulation vertex would have degree one. Write m=|M|. All vertices of M except its one possible articulation have degree at least h−1 inside M, and m≥h.

**Large block.** If m≥k−2, Fan's circumference theorem gives a cycle Q in M of order q≥k−2. By two-connectivity of H, two vertex-disjoint paths join Q to P with distinct endpoints on each. Trim their interiors off P and Q. Mark their endpoints on P. Each outer tail beyond its mark has at least q vertices: it can be replaced by the link and a traversal of Q. The open inner gap has at least ceil(q/2)+1 vertices, by using both links and a longer arc of Q. With z and both marks included, this gives

    n ≥ 3q+ceil(q/2)+4 + |H minus (P union Q)|.

For k≥9 this rules out the moderate order except for equality with q=k−2 and H=P∪Q (the only borderline degrees are nine and ten). Every Q vertex now has at least two neighbors on P, since its H-degree is at least k−1 and it has only q−1 other Q vertices. Choose adjacent Q vertices with distinct direct contacts on P. A path spanning Q joins that pair along the other arc. Now both tails and the open gap each have at least q vertices. The result n≥4q+3 exceeds 3k+2.

**Short block.** Suppose h≤m≤k−3. Every nonarticulation vertex of M has at least k−m neighbors on P and has no neighbors elsewhere in H outside P∪M. The block admits spanning paths between all prescribed pairs of nonarticulation vertices except possibly a single pair. To see this, add a degree-two marker with the desired neighbors. Each nonarticulation vertex has degree at least h−1, with 2(h−1)≥m+1, so Hamilton-cycle closure in the marked graph completes the nonarticulation vertices to a clique. If the block has a distinguished articulation w, it has at least two neighbors in M. In the completed high clique one may thread w between two such neighbors while routing between the terminals, except possibly when the terminals are exactly both its only neighbors. More explicitly, contract the segment u,w,v to one item in the clique ordering. Unless {u,v} is exactly the terminal pair, the items can be ordered to give the prescribed ends. Closure transfers the corresponding Hamiltonian cycle of the marked graph back to the original, and removal of the marker gives the desired path. For the closing of an outer tail M has a spanning cycle by Fan, since m≤2(h−1).

Exclude the articulation and one member of the possible forbidden pair, leaving at least m−2 available terminals. If m≤k−4, at least four terminals remain, with at least four contacts each. The sole small exception in this count is m=5: every nonarticulation vertex then has four neighbors in M, so M is a clique and at least four vertices are available even in a leaf. Match four terminals greedily to four distinct contacts on P. Both outer tails and the three open inner gaps each contain at least m vertices by the possible spanning detours. Including the four marks, M and z gives n≥6m+5>3k+2. If m=k−3, use three matched contacts instead. They imply n≥5m+4>3k+2.

This rules out every moderate graph with k≥9. Together with the existing focused Lean theorem for k≤2, the remaining degree values for this direct moderate route are 3,4,5,6,7,8.
