# A triangle inside the omitted component at every high–high edge

Let P be a longest path in a hypothetical Graph 189 counterexample, let k≥3 be its second-smallest degree, and let C be a component outside P. Each edge xy of C with both endpoints of degree at least k belongs to a triangle inside C. More generally, if y is high, the number a_x of neighbors of x on P is at most one plus the number c of common neighbors of x and y inside C.

Write A,B for the positions of the neighbors of x,y on P. Neither set includes an endpoint. No two marks of A are consecutive, since x would extend P. A mark of A and one of B cannot differ by one or two. In the first case insert x,y between consecutive path vertices; in the second case substitute x,y for the intervening vertex. The three sets A−1, (A∩B)+1, and B∖A are disjoint. For the first two, a shared position would place a mark of A exactly two beyond a mark of B. For the last set disjointness follows from the cross-consecutive exclusion. All these positions are at distance two from x. Thus on P already x has at least |A|+|B| distance-two witnesses. Another d_C(y)−1−c witnesses come from the C-neighbors of y other than x and their common neighbors. The cap k gives

    |A|+d_G(y)−1−c ≤ k.

Suppose now x,y are high and c=0. Both attachment sets have order at most one. Every edge in a counterexample belongs to some triangle by the earlier [triangle-free edge bound](../../interval/global-triangle-free-edge.md) and the bounded-order theorem. The common neighbor is therefore on P, and A=B={p}. The two path neighbors of p, together with N_C(y) minus x, exhaust the positive even-distance budget at x: there are at least 2+(k−2)=k of them. The same holds with x,y reversed. In particular there are no vertices at distance four from x, so its eccentricity is at most three. Every C-neighbor of y has its P-neighbors confined to p: any other contact lies at distance two from y, hence must be a path neighbor of p, which the cross-consecutive insertion forbids.

Every vertex of P other than p and its two path neighbors is at distance three from x. Its penultimate vertex on a geodesic must be one of the two path neighbors of p, since the other possible penultimate vertices belong to N_C(y) and cannot reach that target. Also the target is not adjacent to p, since then it would be at distance two from x. All |P|−3 such vertices therefore lie at distance two from p, giving |P|≤k+3. A counterexample is 2-connected by the established cut-vertex case. Each distance-two pair has a member of degree at least k, so Fan's circumference theorem gives a cycle of length at least min(n,2k). A nonspanning cycle can be prolonged into a path with one more vertex in a connected graph, whereas a spanning cycle would finish the problem. Hence |P|≥2k+1>k+3 for k≥3. This proves the local triangle claim.

A consequence for any bridge in C follows at once: it must be incident with the unique low vertex. In particular a wholly high exterior component is bridgeless for every connectivity. If C has a leaf at a low vertex x and its unique neighbor is high, the contact bound gives a_x≤1, hence d_G(x)≤2; this is already a settled parent case. These conclusions use the original hypotheses with no half-degree connectivity restriction. The graph argument was independently reviewed; the accompanying Lean lemma checks the path-index count.
