# Exchanging an exact-size gap at one below half connectivity

Let k be the second-smallest degree, t=floor(k/2) and s=t−1 the vertex connectivity in a hypothetical counterexample to Graph 189. Suppose t≥5. By the unrestricted matching reduction in ../sep/k10/uniform-matching.md, every high-bearing component outside any longest path is in the near-Hamilton-connected case and its maximum contact matching has size s. For even k every such component has size m=t+2. For odd k its possible sizes are t+3 and t+4. We eliminate the smaller possibility m=k−s+1 in both parities. This proves traceability at the whole line s=k/2−1 for even k. For odd k, the t+4 alternative still requires a separate argument. The smaller degrees already follow from the connectivity-three theorem.

Fix a longest path P and a high-bearing exterior component C of size m=k−s+1. Each high C member needs at least s path neighbors. The size-s cover of C-to-P contacts must consist entirely of path marks S because there are more than s high members. They all touch every mark and every other C member to reach degree k, and C has no P contacts beyond S. Thus C is a clique with at most one low vertex. Distinct prescribed marks may be joined through all C by choosing distinct high neighbors at its ends. The marks in every realization on the same vertex set consequently delimit s+1 gaps, including both tails, each containing at least m unmarked vertices.

For clarity the reusable endpoint argument works at any guaranteed gap size L. The endpoint-growth theorem (../verify/local-excess-connectivity.md) gives at least k+1 attainable high endpoints; those touching S consume distinct distance-two slots at a fixed high c in C, so some high attainable endpoint e avoids S. Orient its realization from e. Write σ for the sum over the s+1 gaps of their excess above L. A neighbor of e in either the second or any later piece cannot be among the first L vertices following its preceding mark. If its index is p, rotation moves that preceding mark to an index at most L, impossible since the first mark must be at least L+1 on every realization. In the initial piece at most its length minus one can be neighbors. Therefore k≤d(e)≤L−1+σ. At L=m this gives σ≥s. Hence |P|+m≥(s+2)m+2s. The independent order bound n≤U−(k−δ), with δ minimum degree and U=t²+5t+1 for even k, U=t²+6t+4 for odd k, leaves at most one additional exterior vertex at even k, and at most three at odd k. Each high-bearing exterior component has ≥ceil(k/2)+2 vertices. Thus there can be only one additional low singleton, if any; δ≥k−3.

Such a singleton z cannot occur. Its path contacts have cardinality δ and avoid the endpoints and consecutive path positions. Partition their indices into r maximal runs with step two. Their two shifts give δ+r distinct distance-two vertices: no shifted position can be adjacent to z, and the overlaps are precisely the δ−r internal midpoints. Thus r≤k−δ≤3. At each of those δ−r internal midpoints replace the middle vertex by z. The new longest path omits the old middle vertex, which is high. If it has no neighbor in C, it is an omitted high singleton, contradicting the exterior degree bound. Each such midpoint is therefore one of the s marks. At odd k, δ−r≥2t−5>t−1=s. At even k the tighter order slack gives δ≥k−1 and r≤1, so δ−r≥2t−2>s. This finishes the exclusion. The whole complement of V(P) is now C.

Suppose some realization on V(P) has a gap J with exactly m vertices. Replace J with a spanning route through C, using its bordering mark or marks. The complement of the new longest path consists of J, which is connected. The same exterior classification applies at order m, so J is a clique, each high J vertex touches all of its s marks, and its marks contain every contact to the new path, including a retained original mark i bounding J. Fix an old high c in C. Since it touches i, at least m−1 vertices of J occur at distance two from c in the original graph. The 2s immediate neighbors of the old marks on the old path are distinct, are unmarked, and are all at distance two from c. At most two lie in J. Hence c has at least m−1+2s−2=k+s−2>k vertices at positive even distance. This excludes a gap of length exactly m in every realization on V(P).

Use the endpoint argument a second time, now with L=m+1. The surplus above this larger size is at least k−m=s−1. Thus
```
n ≥ m+s+(s+1)(m+1)+(s−1) = (s+2)m+3s.
```
At even k the right side equals t²+6t−1, exceeding U by t−2. At odd k it equals t²+7t, exceeding U by t−4. This proves the claimed exclusion. The proof uses the even-distance condition twice: to find an attainable endpoint avoiding the marks, and to forbid the equal-length exchange by counting additional second-layer vertices. No solver check is needed for this new step.
