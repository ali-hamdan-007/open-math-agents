# Formalization of the full Graph 189 paper argument

## Connectivity two at every order from degree eleven

Lean proves the original Hamiltonian-path conclusion for every graph of vertex connectivity two with second-smallest degree at least eleven, at every order. At degree eleven the stronger path gap covers balanced sides. The clean mixed case counts an opposite common port in both anchor columns, and the large shallow case uses the sharper mixed census from the parallel branch. This leaves degrees seven through ten at connectivity two. The strict circumference theorem remains the input for the separate three-connected assembly. ## Current separating-pair proof

At second-smallest degree eleven, [both sides without common contacts](bounded-order/Graph189PrivateEleven.lean) now give a Hamiltonian path throughout the residual order window. A clean side bounds the combined number of nonports, so the two path losses together with the unused separator vertex total at most nine. If neither side is clean, the existing defective-side bound puts both orders below twice the second-smallest degree. The [balanced common-free theorem](bounded-order/Graph189SmallPrivatePair.lean) applies in this latter case for every k≥7. The [independent Linux check](https://github.com/ali-hamdan-007/open-math-agents/actions/runs/46154963128) also verified the stronger path gap and the three-connected assembly with its explicit circumference hypothesis.

The [assembly for connectivity at least three](bounded-order/Graph189LongCycleAssembly.lean) now takes only the strict one-exception circumference theorem as its additional input. For separating pairs in the residual window, the [new path-gap theorem](bounded-order/Graph189NearDegreeWindow.lean) reduces the permitted loss to the explicit target of at most k−2 omitted vertices.

Lean now [excludes the final common-contact clique](bounded-order/Graph189DenseTerminal.lean) by a tight-gap exchange, completing the dense branch. The parallel [one-exception circumference module](bounded-order/Graph189FanCycle.lean) handles orders up to 2q. The strict larger-order circumference and the residual separating pairs are the outstanding inputs.

The [dense-component theorem](bounded-order/Graph189DenseClique.lean) now obtains the full clique, its common high contact rows, containment of the exceptional low row, and at most one other omitted vertex. The remaining terminal step for this component is to contradict its contact pattern.

The [two-connected component order argument](bounded-order/Graph189TwoConnectedCoreOrder.lean) excludes m≥2q once the circumference witness is available. In the complementary range the [prescribed routes](bounded-order/Graph189DenseComponentRoutes.lean) and [three-contact size bound](bounded-order/Graph189DenseMatching.lean) now give m≤k−2 in Lean, including a possible degree-two exceptional vertex inside the component.

The [short leaf block](bounded-order/Graph189ShortLeafOrder.lean) is excluded across the residual range. [Selecting an actual leaf](bounded-order/Graph189ExteriorLeaf.lean) and [extending its tail through the articulation](bounded-order/Graph189ShortLeafRoutes.lean) are proved. The other leaf alternative, of order at least twice its minimum interior degree minus one, uses the circumference bound for a two-connected graph with one exceptional low vertex.

The [single checked counterexample window](Graph189SmallOrderWindow.lean) is now 7≤k≤21 and 4k−5≤n≤5k−5. The [choice of exterior component](bounded-order/Graph189ChosenExterior.lean), [local graph-to-arithmetic inequalities](bounded-order/Graph189OptimalNeighborhood.lean), [Hamilton-connected high neighborhood](bounded-order/Graph189OptimalHighCore.lean), and [matching with tail extensions](bounded-order/Graph189OptimalPorts.lean) have pinned kernel proofs. They supply the first part of the five-times-order paper’s graph argument. The endblock, cycle, and tail arguments that force the final order remain.

The [terminal-order theorem](bounded-order/Graph189LastCliqueOrders.lean) eliminates all orders n≥5k−4 at 7≤k≤21. This supersedes the terminal type enumeration for the parent. The [midpoint lemma](bounded-order/Graph189OptimalMidpoint.lean) is the graph exchange at the beginning of the bounded-order paper. Its remaining geometric target is to force n≥5k−4 under connectivity at least three. Connectivity two remains separately at k=7,…,11.

The checked conditional theorem in parent/parent-assembly.md treats graphs with a cut vertex and all orders through 2k+2. Its two hypotheses concern two-connected graphs at larger orders. The moderate child is now proved in Lean. The larger-order child remains open. Here k is the second-smallest degree counting multiplicity. The full-target paper argument is in full-argument.md. The original upstream theorem still requires a single unconditional Lean term.

The surviving original inputs are now confined to 7≤k≤21 and 4k−5≤n≤5k−3. The maximum-root estimates give this upper bound even when the exceptional vertex is away from the separator. The exact all-orders conclusion holds for k≥22, for connectivity two at k≥12, connectivity four at k≥15, and connectivity at least five at k≥14. The remaining paper proof is the [bounded-order theorem](../global/cut3/five-times-order-small.md) together with the small separating-pair arguments it cites.

The [weighted removable-vertex theorem](../lowblock/Graph189WeightedCriticalOrder.lean) strengthens the structural order step. For a hypothetical counterexample of order n, connectivity s and minimum degree δ, if s≥2, 2s+5≤k, s<n−1, 3s<2(δ+1) and k+6≤2δ, it proves (s+1)k+δ<n+s(s+1). The successor set in the linked-set argument has distinct vertices. Including the exterior witness when needed preserves distinctness, so only one summand can fall below k.
