# Absorbing a minimum three-vertex separator into a small side

A fragment here is a connected component after the deletion of a separator of minimum cardinality s. Choose a smallest fragment A over all such separators, and let S witness it. Assume |A|>s. For any nonempty X⊆S, at least |X|+1 distinct vertices in A have a neighbor in X. If the union N_A(X) were at most |X|, the set (S∖X)∪N_A(X) would still disconnect the nonempty A∖N_A(X) from an old opposite component. Its size is ≤s, so it is a minimum cut and some resulting component inside A∖N_A(X) is strictly smaller. Here N_A(X) is nonempty since each member of a minimum cut touches every component. The contradiction proves the strict form of Hall's condition. The same arguments below apply to any specified side satisfying the indicated neighbor-set conditions.

Let s=3, and suppose A is wholly high, |A|≤2k−3. For **every** choice of joining anchor r, there is a path spanning A and all three anchors which ends at r. In particular a B path starting at any genuine contact attaches without leaving any unused separator vertex. The argument uses only the minimum-cut and degree assumptions.

Write S={r,i,j}. Add a leaf at r to the induced graph on A∪S. Its order is |A|+4 and every A vertex has its full original degree, at least k. Path closure allows adding a missing edge in A whenever the endpoint degree sum is at least the graph order minus one: this follows from the standard cycle closure theorem upon adjoining a universal vertex. Thus complete A to a clique, since 2k≥|A|+3. Choose two distinct contacts of i, and two of j, giving two distinct unordered pairs. This is possible since the two contact sets each have ≥2 members and their union has ≥3. Connect each pair via its anchor. The resulting two two-edge paths are either disjoint or meet at just one A vertex, forming a linear forest in the clique with at most one A vertex of degree two. Choose an r neighbor x of degree at most one in this forest (r has ≥2 A contacts). Orient the component containing x to end at x, treating isolated clique vertices as one-vertex components. Concatenate the components by clique edges and append r and its leaf. This spanning path in the closure yields one in the original augmented graph; delete its forced leaf endpoint.

If the single low vertex z belongs to A and |A|≤2k−6, fix any two distinct anchors r,j. There is a spanning path of (A−z)∪{r,j} with endpoints r,j. Each A contact set loses at most z, so the two remaining sets have distinct representatives by the strict Hall property. In the induced graph on (A−z)∪{r,j}, add a degree-two marker adjacent only to r,j. The graph has |A|+2 vertices. Every original high A vertex has degree ≥k−2, since only z and the third anchor were removed from its original neighborhood. Complete the high subgraph by cycle closure as |A|+2≤2k−4. Distinct representatives then allow a spanning cycle through the marker, r,j and the high clique; removing the marker after reversing closure proves the claim. Joining this side path to any B contact at r leaves at most the third anchor and z unused outside B.

Thus in the wholly high small-fragment case, **any B path meeting the port set** with q omitted vertices, t of them nonports, costs at most q+floor((N_B−t)/2) total omissions in G when taking the nearer terminal port; N_B denotes the total number of nonports. If A has a high nonport, N_B≤k−3+1_{low nonport in B}, independently of B's size. If the B path already has a port endpoint the cost is just q. This is a conditional path-length estimate; it does not ensure arbitrarily large B admits a path of the needed length. The smallest-fragment choice is only justified if all possible resulting fragment types are treated in the parent argument.
