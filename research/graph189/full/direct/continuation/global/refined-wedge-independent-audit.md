# Independent verification of the refined induced-wedge bound

I checked ../verify/wedge-order-refinement.md directly from the defined vertex sets and the prior high-pair diameter result. Let u,w,v be an induced path, let j=|N(u)∪N(w)∪N(v)|−d(u)−d(v), K be its number of admissible penultimate positions as below, and e(w) its number of positive-even targets. For k≥3 the resulting inequality is

    (k−1)j≤3k+2−n+(k−2)(1+d(w)+e(w)−d(u)−d(v)).

Indeed Q=N(w)−(N(u)∪N(v)) contributes one duplicate per vertex across the three closed even sets: each member is in the closed even sets of u and v (this includes the two endpoints themselves). If C denotes the common neighbors of u,v, then j=|Q|−|C|. A vertex outside all closed even sets has three odd distances, equal because successive roots are adjacent. Those of distance one form C₁=C∩N(w), where |C₁|≤|C|−1 because w∈C−C₁. Let T be the remaining common-odd vertices.

For a target t∈T of common distance three, any penultimate vertex a along a geodesic from w has distance two from w and is adjacent to neither endpoint (otherwise the target would be closer to it). Fix b on a w-to-a geodesic preceding a; b cannot equal u or v, by the preceding nonadjacency. Write c_a for the number of u,v adjacent to b. The other 2−c_a endpoints are at distance two from b via w. Every target assigned to a is also exactly two from b, using the difference of w-layers, and differs from both endpoints. Therefore at most k−2+c_a targets are assigned to a. The vertex a itself belongs to the even set of w and of the c_a endpoints adjacent to b; it contributes c_a additional duplicate incidences, distinct from Q since its w-distance is two. Summing c_a over the distinct used penultimates gives precisely the correction shared by the upper and lower counts. Targets at common distance at least five must be low because some root is high and any high pair is at distance at most four. There is at most one such target in all, so a later used penultimate receives at most one≤k−2.

Among second-layer penultimates the allowed ones are M=D₂(w)−(N(u)∪N(v)); all later ones count in the positive-even layers beyond two. The neighborhood union is B₂[w] minus M, giving K=|M|+e(w)−|D₂(w)|=1+d(w)+e(w)−d(u)−d(v)−j. Put c=Σc_a. The candidate count gives |T|≤(k−2)K+c. The even-union count, taking |C₁| off the complement, gives |T|≥n−3(k+1)+|Q|+c−|C₁|≥n−3k−2+j+c. The displayed inequality follows by cancellation and rearrangement.

In particular any positive j gives n≤3k+1+(k−2)(d(w)+e(w)−d(u)−d(v)). Under δ=k and Δ≤k+2 the final excess is at most two, so nontraceability implies n≤5k−3: above that order all induced wedges obey local Ore, hence the rooted local Ore theorem gives traceability (the standard local Ore Hamiltonicity theorem also applies). This audit is a paper derivation, independent of the original proof author.
