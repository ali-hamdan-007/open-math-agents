# Second degree eight and connectivity three

Assume \(k=8\), \(\kappa(G)=3\), and the exact Graph 189 cap. Then \(G\) is traceable. The arguments below are a paper proof. They use the checked order and small-cover bounds, the independently audited Hamiltonian-minimum-deletion elimination, and the exterior geodesic and block arguments from the accompanying degree-five and degree-six proofs.

In a counterexample the checked order bound gives \(n\le1+\delta+8+\lfloor9^2/4\rfloor=29+\delta\le37\). Choose a longest path \(P\); its support has at least nine vertices because a high endpoint has all neighbors on the path. The same shifted-contact proof as in degree five makes each high exterior vertex have a high exterior neighbor and internal exterior degree at least \(h=4\). Indeed the only possible low-neighbor exception would force the low neighbor to have degree one. For high \(x\) with \(a_x\le4\) path marks the internal bound is direct. If it has more, the shift and shared-contact count with a high exterior neighbor gives \(8\ge a_x+9-q_x\). The exterior cannot be just the low vertex by the Hamiltonian-minimum-deletion proof, so it has a component \(H\) containing a high vertex.

## No exterior cycle of length at least eight

For a cycle \(D\) of length \(d\ge8\) disjoint from \(P\), three trimmed disjoint connectors exist by connectivity. At their three marks the two outer tails each have length at least \(d\) and both inner gaps have length at least \(\lceil d/2\rceil+1\), by exterior detours. Thus \(n\ge3d+3+2(\lceil d/2\rceil+1)\). The sole possibility is \(d=8,n=37,|P|=29\). Here the entire exterior is \(D\), all vertices are high by the checked order bound, and the connectors are direct. A fourth disjoint contact would give \(n\ge43\) by the same count, so the maximum contact matching has size three.

Any marked position on \(P\) belongs to some matching of size three. If the mark is unused in a maximum matching, exchange the matched edge at one of its exterior neighbors for this new edge. Such a neighbor must be matched, or the matching could be enlarged. Every matching of three has the same marks, at the path positions \(9,15,21\) numbered from one. This follows from equality in the two eight-vertex tails and two five-vertex interior gaps. Each \(D\) vertex needs a mark because it is high and has only seven other exterior vertices. A \(D\) vertex attached to the middle mark cannot have a cyclic neighbor attached to an extreme mark. Their cyclic adjacency would give an eight-vertex detour fitting into a five-vertex gap. Every cyclic neighbor therefore uses only the middle mark. Propagating this along the cycle forces all marks to be the middle one, a contradiction.

## The exterior admits only two small cliques

Every exterior two-connected block with all but one vertex of internal degree at least four has order \(b\in\{5,6,7\}\) by Fan's circumference theorem, and has a Hamiltonian cycle. The prescribed-endpoint closure argument used at degree five gives a spanning path between any two ports, except possibly the unique pair formed by the two neighbors of the exceptional vertex when it has internal degree two. A longer cycle arc on that exceptional pair loses at most two vertices.

If \(H\) has a cutvertex, take a leaf block \(B\) whose noncut vertices are high. Let \(u\) be its articulation and \(R=H\setminus B\). If \(R\) contains a high vertex then \(|R|\ge4\), and a route through all of \(B\) ending at \(u\) can continue through at least three vertices of \(R\). Follow a shortest path from \(u\) to a high member of \(R\); the high induced graph of \(R\) has minimum degree two and a path from that entry visits at least three high members. Each high noncut member of \(B\) has at least \(9-b\) path marks. Consequently choose four, three, or two distinct noncut vertices matched to distinct marks when \(b\) is five, six, or seven respectively. The two tails each use at least \(b+3\) exterior vertices. Each intermediate gap uses \(b\) vertices except possibly one with loss at most two. At \(b=5\) the block is a clique and there is no exception. These give the lower orders
\[
(5+4)+4+2(5+3)+3\cdot5=44,\quad
(6+4)+3+2(6+3)+2\cdot6-2=41,\quad
(7+4)+2+2(7+3)+7-2=38.
\]
If \(R\) contains only low \(z\), its sole exterior neighbor is \(u\). The shifts about the marks of \(z\), the other exterior neighbors of \(u\), and the marks of \(u\) outside those of \(z\) force at least \(\deg(u)-1+\deg(z)-1\ge9\) even witnesses, exactly as in the leaf argument at degree five. Thus \(H\) cannot have a cutvertex.

For two-connected \(H\) of order seven, three disjoint attachments exist by connectivity and the matching–cover theorem. The two tails use seven vertices, and at most one gap loses up to two. Thus \(n\ge7+3+4\cdot7-2=36\), so \(\delta\ge7\). If every prescribed pair has a spanning path, the bound is 38. Otherwise the exception is the pair of neighbors of low \(z\), which has exactly two internal neighbors and at least five path marks. Choose a matching of three using \(z\) and two high vertices not adjacent to \(z\) in \(H\). Each of the two high vertices has at least two marks, so first match them to distinct marks and then choose a different mark for \(z\). This choice avoids the exceptional pair and again gives 38. Hence \(H\) has order five or six.

## Uniform marks and an exchange

If \(|H|=6\), four disjoint contacts would give \(n\ge6+4+5\cdot6-2=38\). Each of its at least five high vertices needs at least three marks. Thus all high supports are the same triple: a union of size at least four among the high supports supplies four matched contacts by Hall's criterion. Any low vertex touching outside the triple also gives a fourth contact. Each high vertex then has all five possible internal neighbors, so \(H=K_6\), and each high vertex has original degree eight. All of its distance-two vertices can be reached through the three marks, since its other neighbors belong to the clique whose only external contacts are those marks. The checked small-cover bound gives \(n\le1+8+8+3(9-3)=35\). The three marks leave four intervals each of length at least six, hence at least two are exactly six.

If \(|H|=5\), it is a clique. Every high vertex has at least four marks, so every mark belonging to any vertex of \(H\) can be included in a matching of four contacts. Such four marks need five intervals each at least five vertices. Since \(|P|\le32\), the four marks respectively lie in the bands \(6\)–\(9\), \(12\)–\(15\), \(18\)–\(21\), and \(24\)–\(27\), in indices from one. Two different marks in one band cannot have distinct clique contacts because the resulting clique route would lengthen \(P\). A band containing only marks with the same singleton support has at most two of them, as consecutive marks of one vertex are impossible. If all at least four high vertices own a private band, this exhausts the four bands and gives each at most two marks. Otherwise a high vertex with no private band needs a shared mark in every band and each band has only that mark. Every high vertex therefore touches precisely the same four marks, and any low vertex has no other mark. The five intervals at these positions have length at least five, with at least two exactly five given \(|P|\le32\).

In either clique case choose an interval \(J\) of exact length equal to \(|H|\) avoiding the possible low vertex of \(P\), and rotate it out of the path using a spanning route through \(H\). This leaves a new longest path and the all-high connected interval \(J\). The exterior volume apart from \(H\) before the exchange was at most three, by the preceding interval bounds, so no other component could contain a high vertex. Thus the new high-containing component includes \(J\) and at most one extra low vertex. Its order is five or six by the preceding exclusions, and its high vertices have uniform contact marks. In particular a retained old boundary mark of \(J\), which touches a high endpoint of \(J\), touches every vertex of \(J\). Every old high member of \(H\) consequently sees all of \(J\) at exact distance two, as these vertices were originally unmarked. In addition the old mark shifts are distinct and at most two of them lie in \(J\). This gives at least \(5+8-2\) or \(6+6-2\) even witnesses, contradicting the quota of eight.
