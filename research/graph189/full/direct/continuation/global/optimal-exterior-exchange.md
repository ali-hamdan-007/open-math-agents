# Equal-size exchanges between a path and its smallest exterior component

In a qualifying 2-connected graph with k≥3, choose among all longest paths P one maximizing the sum of the squares of the sizes of the components of G−V(P). All rotations on the same vertex set preserve this choice. Let C be a component of least size among the exterior components containing a high vertex, and write m=|C|. By the preceding results every other exterior component is high-bearing or the unique singleton low vertex.

First, for any longest path (without the optimization), let x∈C have two path neighbors two positions apart with middle vertex y. Then y has no C-neighbor. Otherwise the shortest path in the connected C from x to such a neighbor can be inserted between one of the two neighbors and y (if x itself also touched y, insert just x). Exchanging x and y produces another longest path, so if y is high it has at least ceil(k/2) neighbors in its new exterior component, by the previous internal-degree lemma. Since y has no C-neighbor, these all lie in old exterior components other than C. Thus every such high middle vertex contacts an old high-bearing component D≠C, even if a low exterior singleton is also present. This last step uses ceil(k/2)≥2.

If x is not a cut vertex of C, the above high middle is impossible for the chosen P. Indeed C−x stays connected and shrinks by one. The newly omitted y joins D of order M≥m. The increase in the square sum is at least

    (m−1)²+(M+1)²−m²−M²=2(M−m)+2>0;

merging any additional components touched by y only increases it further. Thus every noncut x∈C has all its attachments spaced at least three apart, with the only possible exception of two marks flanking the single globally low vertex on P. This holds on every path using the same longest vertex set, not just its initial ordering. If no other high-bearing component is omitted, the same one-vertex conclusion holds for every x∈C even without optimization or the noncut assumption.

There is also a two-vertex form at k≥4. Suppose xy is an edge of C with C−{x,y} connected. If x and y touch path positions three apart, replacing the two interior path vertices v,w by x,y yields an equal-size path. Neither v nor w sees C: each lies next to one of the two path attachment positions, so a contact into C would permit an immediate insertion on that edge, as above. The two newly omitted vertices are joined by their old path edge. At least one is high. If their only other old omitted neighbors belonged to a low singleton, their new exterior component would have at most three vertices, below ceil(k/2)+2. Hence they join another high component of size M≥m. Since C−{x,y} is connected (and m≥4), the resulting square sum increases by at least

    (m−2)²+(M+2)²−m²−M²=4(M−m)+8>0.

Therefore x and y cannot touch positions three apart. Positions one and two apart were already forbidden by the strict path-length extension. When C is the sole high exterior component, no connectivity assumption on C−{x,y} is needed for the distance-three exclusion. These exchanges add vertex-set changes to same-set rotations, but do not yet control all gaps of four or more positions.

## Uniform nonseparating-path version

Write h=ceil(k/2). Let a path H on q≤h vertices of C have its endpoints adjacent to two distinct vertices of P, and assume C−V(H) is connected. For q=1 allow the same single vertex to be both ends. Then these two attachment positions have at least q+1 intervening vertices whenever q≥2, or when q=1 and the intervening single vertex in the equality case is high. The ordinary longestness precludes fewer than q intervening vertices. For equality let W be the removed q-vertex path segment and insert H. Each endpoint of W has no neighbor in C, since it immediately adjoins a contact position; any such neighbor permits inserting a C-path on that edge. At least one endpoint w of W is high under the stated conditions. Its degree in the new exterior graph is at least h; it has at most q−1 neighbors in W and none in the residual C−V(H). Thus the new connected exterior set W attaches to at least one old exterior component other than C.

If any such component is high-bearing of order M≥m, the increase in the square sum is at least (m−q)²+(M+q)²−m²−M²=2q(M−m)+2q²>0, including when W also connects the residual C or an additional old component. Otherwise W attaches to only the unique low singleton outside C. Its new component cannot consist of W and this singleton alone, since it contains high w but has at most h+1 vertices. Therefore W also attaches to the connected C−V(H); the result is a component of order m+1 replacing the old C and low singleton. Its square exceeds m²+1. These contradictions prove the strict gap claim. They apply on every path with the same vertex set as P.
