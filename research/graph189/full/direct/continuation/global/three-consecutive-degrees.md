# Three consecutive degrees when the minimum is the second-smallest

Under Graph 189, suppose all degrees belong to {k,k+1,k+2} with k the second-smallest degree. For k≥65 there is a Hamiltonian path. The bounded-degree assumption is used only for n>5k+1 and in the final three-contact count.

The wedge theorem gives Hamiltonicity above order 5k+1. The known all-high separating-pair and cut-vertex theorems settle connectivity at most two. Assume κ≥3, n≤5k+1 and nontraceability. Choose the square-maximizing longest path P, smallest exterior component C, and good block B as in two-thirds-exterior-degree.md. All vertices are high. Put b=|B| and h=ceil(k/2). Then b≥k−2: if h+1≤b≤k−3, the existing contact bound n≥b(k−b+3)−1 gives a contradiction at both endpoints by concavity (at k−3 the lower bound is 6k−19; at h+1 it is (h+1)(floor(k/2)+2)−1).

Every noncut member of B has internal degree at least d=ceil((2k−8)/3). Indeed 3q≤2k−3 implies (q+1)(2k−3q+1)≤5k+2 and (5k+2)/(q+1)<10, giving 3q≥2k−8. The other branch is immediate. If b≥2d−1, Fan supplies a disjoint cycle of length L≥2d−1≥(4k−19)/3. Three Menger connectors give n≥4L+5≥(16k−61)/3>5k+1 for k≥65. Hence b≤2d−2.

If C has a cut vertex, let w be the unique C-cut vertex of its chosen endblock. The high vertices of B−w induce a Hamilton-connected graph, by their minimum degree d−1. There are two disjoint contact edges from B−w to P (otherwise a one-vertex König cover together with w separates G). Each matched C port can start a B-spanning path to w, by first traversing B−w to another neighbor of w. From w append a rooted path through ≥h vertices in the remainder R=C−B: start at a neighbor of w, and every member of its R-component has ≥h−1 R-neighbors by the previous ambient-independent exterior degree theorem. Each P-tail therefore has ≥b+h vertices, the inner gap ≥b−1 and |R|≥h. This gives n≥4b+3h+1>5k+1. Thus C itself is two-connected and Hamilton-connected; write m=|C|=b.

A matching of size t from C to P gives n≥(t+2)m+t by spanning routes in C between its matched ports. There are at least three disjoint contacts by κ≥3 and König. Therefore m≤k−1, and there cannot be four matched contacts. A cover of size three for the contact graph exists, and m=k−2 or k−1. Every C member has at least k−m+1 neighbors on P.

We use the following finite bound. Suppose all C–P contacts are contained on the P side in a set A' of at most six vertices. Suppose a vertex w outside C misses C completely, but sees at least k−2 members of C at distance two. Then |V(G)−C|≤3(k+2)+9. Besides its ≤k+2 neighbors, there are at most two positive-even vertices of G−C from w. Every remaining vertex of G−C other than w has distance three, by the high-pair diameter bound. A length-three geodesic whose penultimate vertex lies in C must terminate in A'; if the penultimate is outside C it lies in the two-member even set, each with ≤k+2 neighbors. Summing yields the claim. This contradicts |P|≥4m+3 throughout the range.

At m=k−2 the minimum three contacts per C vertex forces the three-vertex cover to consist of marks A on P only. Each mark sees all C. Any P-neighbor w immediately before or after such a mark misses C by the connected-exterior insertion argument, and sees all C at distance two. The finite bound applies. At m=k−1 every C member has ≥2 contacts, so a three-element contact cover includes at least two marks. If it has two, its sole exceptional C member y has ≤4 P-neighbors: each nonexceptional x must touch both marks, making it universal within C by degree≥k. Hence all of C's contacts lie in ≤6 marks, and a P-neighbor of either common mark sees ≥k−2 C vertices at distance two. This also contradicts the finite bound.

It remains that all contacts of C lie among a three-element set A of marks with m=k−1. Each C member has internal degree ≥m−2 and meets at least two of the marks. If a mark has ≤1 C-neighbor, a different mark meets all but at most one vertex of C and the finite bound applies. If two marks have no common neighbor in C, every C vertex meets the third mark, with the same conclusion. Assume therefore that marks each meet ≥2 C members and all mark pairs have a common C neighbor. Every C vertex is within two of every mark, since it misses at most one internal neighbor other than itself.

Partition V−(C∪A) into X,Y,Z according to distances 1,2,≥3 from A. Summing the degree plus positive-even budgets at the three marks gives total ≤6k+6. Each member of C counts once for each of the three marks, each of the six ordered distinct mark pairs counts, and every member of X or Y counts at least once. Thus |X|+|Y|≤6k−3m. At most two C vertices together touch all three marks. One of the two sees at least |X|/2 members of X at distance two, and all Z at distance exactly four, since each graph path leaving C uses A and all vertices are high. Therefore |Z|≤k−|X|/2. From 4m+3≤|V−C|=3+|X|+|Y|+|Z| we obtain |X|≤14(k−m)=14.

Each mark has ≥k neighbors, only ≤|X|+2 of which can lie outside C, hence misses at most |X|+1≤15 C vertices. As m>45, some x∈C meets all three marks. Now X and Z are disjoint even witnesses at x, giving |V−C|≤3+k+|Y|. It follows that |Y|≥4m−k=3k−4. Each missed mark–C incidence is at distance two and uses that mark's even budget in addition to Y. The three even budgets therefore leave at most four missed incidences in total. Some mark misses at most one C member and meets ≥k−2, so its immediate P-neighbor w gives the finite-bound contradiction once again. This proves the assertion.
