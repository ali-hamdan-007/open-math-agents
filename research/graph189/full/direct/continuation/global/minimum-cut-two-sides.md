# A small minimum cut has exactly two components in a counterexample

Let S be a cardinality-minimum vertex cut in a 2-connected qualifying graph of order n, write s=|S|=κ, and let k be the second-smallest degree. If k>6(s−1) and n>3k+2, then G−S has exactly two components. Together with the previous order≤3k+2 and cut-vertex results, this applies to hypothetical counterexamples, for every choice of a minimum cut. In particular, using `near-degree-exterior.md`, every minimum cut in every sufficiently large-degree counterexample has exactly two components.

Every component of G−S touches every member of S: missing one member would make its neighborhood in S a smaller cut. The previous three-component anchor inequality says that if a member u∈S has high neighbors in three different components, then k≤6(s−1). For completeness, if A_i=N(u)∩C_i, choose a high y_i∈A_i, i=1,2,3. From y_i the other two A_j are at distance two, hence 2Σ|A_i|≤3k. The closed internal neighborhood of y_i has size at least k+1−s and is contained in A_i plus the distance-two vertices from u in C_i. Summing gives 3(k+1−s)≤3k/2+k, as asserted. Consequently if there are at least three components, there are exactly three, and the third is the singleton low vertex z. Indeed three wholly high components suffice. If the low vertex belongs to a nonsingleton component, at least two vertices of that component touch S (a sole attachment vertex would cut G); choose u touching a high one, and it already has high neighbors in both other components. When the low vertex lies in S or is absent, every exterior component is wholly high.

We now exclude the remaining low singleton and prove the following slightly stronger statement. Suppose G−S consists of {z}, C₁, C₂ where z is low, and k>2s−2. Then n≤3k+1. Minimality gives N(z)=S. Let B_i be the vertices of C_i touching S, and D_i=C_i−B_i. Since z sees every vertex of B=B₁∪B₂ at distance two, |B|≤k. Every vertex of C_i is high and has at most s neighbors outside, so |C_i|≥k−s+1.

Both D_i are nonempty. Otherwise, say D₁=∅, hence |B₁|≥k−s+1 and |B₂|≤s−1. If D₂ were nonempty, removing B₂ would disconnect it from S, contrary to κ=s. If D₂ were empty, |B|≥2(k−s+1)>k, also impossible. Any pair from opposite D_i are at distance at least four, and exactly four by the registered fact that two high vertices have distance at most four. Thus |D_i|≤k; put d_i=k−|D_i|.

Let B_i* be the members of B_i neighboring D_i. Its removal disconnects D_i from S, whence |B_i*|≥s. For each b∈B_i* take x∈D_i adjacent to b. In x's even budget, all members of D_j are at distance four and all neighbors of b in S are at distance two; these sets are disjoint. So b has at most d_j neighbors in S. If either d_i≥s then d₁+d₂≥s immediately. Otherwise, counting missed incidences between S and the at least s vertices in each B_i* gives at least s(2s−d₁−d₂) missed pairs. Every u∈S is high and has at most s neighbors in S∪{z}, hence sees at least k−s members of B. As |B|≤k, it misses at most s. Thus total missed pairs are at most s² and d₁+d₂≥s. Finally

    n = s+1+|B|+2k−d₁−d₂ ≤ 3k+1.

This proves the assertion. It leaves the two-component minimum cuts themselves unresolved.
