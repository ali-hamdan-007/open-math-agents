# Every qualifying graph of vertex connectivity at most three has a spanning path

Let G be any finite connected graph satisfying the Graph 189 parity condition, and let k be the second-smallest degree. If κ(G)≤3, then G has a Hamiltonian path. The cases κ≤2 are the previously established cut-vertex and full separating-pair theorems. At κ=3 the k≥16 proof is in all-three-cuts.md; the argument below covers all smaller k.

By five-times-order-small.md, a graph with n≤5k−3 is traceable. The prior verified quadratic order theorem gives n≤1+2k+floor((k+1)²/4), so the k≤5 theorem and direct comparison at k=6,7,8 leave only 9≤k≤15. Suppose 5k−2≤n≤1+2k+floor((k+1)²/4). Choose a minimum three-cut S, a smallest connected component A of G−S, and let B be the union of **all** other components. This allows any number of components. Each anchor touches A and B by minimality of S; no A–B edge exists. A is connected and |A|≤|B|. None of the constraints below assumes B connected.

Both sides contain a high member. Otherwise some side is just the globally low z. It touches every anchor. All members on the other side are high, have anchor-depth at most four (all three anchors are high), and split between depths 1,3 whose vertices count in z's even budget, and depths 2,4 counted at one of the anchors. Hence n≤4k+4<5k−2. With a high member in each side every high vertex has anchor-depth at most three (opposite high-pair distance ≤4); the possible low vertex has depth ≤4 by the global diameter bound and the existence of opposite high vertices.

Use the same rational linear population variables and Boolean occupancy flags as all-three-cuts.md, with all high vectors up to depth three and low vectors up to depth four. There is **no restriction on port supports or singleton-nearest classes**, and neither side is required to contain a nonport. The hypotheses include the exact fixed integral value k, the stated upper and lower order bounds, |A|≤|B| and the choice of |B|>3k or ≤3k. The large-B double-decrease and internal-neighborhood disjointness constraints apply only in A. Every type, including each low location, has the same cross min-sum metric, even-budget bounds, boundary correction, degree lower bound and predecessor conditions as the audited nonshallow formulation. In particular distances in the ambient graph crossed through one of the three anchors remain exact for x∈A,y∈B even when B is a union.

Without distinguished missing anchor labels we may sort the three pair distances so D_01≤D_02≤D_12. Consider all such triangles obeying the high-pair maximum four and general maximum five, all six low locations, all seven degrees and both B-size branches. Each branch contains 826 queries. All **1,652** queries returned UNSAT with Z3 4.13.3.0 in exact real-linear arithmetic and Boolean combinations. Two hundred and one initial subsecond timeouts were resolved by the longer pass, with every remaining result UNSAT. Each graph would have supplied an integral, hence real, solution to its case. This excludes every surviving small-degree possibility.

The standalone verifier needs small_three_types.py, check_small_three_cases.py, and small-three-cases.jsonl. Run `python check_small_three_cases.py --replay`. The positive control is a qualifying k=10 graph on 21 vertices consisting of two 9-cliques and three independent anchors; in each clique exactly three vertices have each of the three two-anchor supports. Its actual BFS distance vectors, second-smallest degree and parity counts are checked; imposing those counts yields SAT after dropping only the high-order lower bound.
