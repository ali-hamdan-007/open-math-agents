# Three different sole-nearest anchors in a component of a three-cut

Let S={1,2,3} be a cardinality-minimum vertex cut in a qualifying graph, with G−S having two components A,B. Write k for its second-smallest degree. Assume that for each i there is a high nonport x_i of A whose distance to i is two and whose distances to the other anchors are at least three. When k≥16 the graph has a spanning path if B contains a high nonport or if every port in B is private (touches just one anchor). In both cases we show n≤5k−3, then apply ../five-times-order-unrestricted.md.

All distances here are ambient. Every component meets every anchor. For any i, every high B vertex not touching i is at distance at least four from x_i, since an opposite-side path must visit an anchor. The distance equals four by the high-pair bound. If there are any such vertices then x_i's distance-two layer separates it from them and has size at least three. Thus B contains ≤k−3 high vertices missing i. Each i has at most k−1 B contacts: otherwise a port in A touching i sees all k B contacts at distance two, and must be universal within A (any internal nonneighbor supplies a first distance-two vertex on an internal shortest path). Then all A nonports are at distance two from i, contradicting the specified x_j for j≠i. Thus |B|≤2k−4+ε_B, where ε_B=1 if B contains the possible low vertex, otherwise 0. Every high B nonport is at distance two from all three anchors: each of its three distances to x_i equals four and must be achieved through i.

Suppose B has both a high nonport and a port touching at least two anchors. The high nonport bounds the high A nonports by k−3, as all are at distance at least four and its second layer has size ≥3 if any exist. The multiport counts all opposite A ports touching its own anchors at distance two, so they number ≤k; ports at the remaining anchor contribute ≤k, by a B contact at that anchor. Therefore |A|≤3k−3+ε_A and n≤5k−4+ε_A+ε_B≤5k−3.

Now suppose every B port is private. Put p,d for the numbers of high B ports and high B nonports respectively. Every high private B port touching i is at distance two from each other anchor j: otherwise its internal closed neighborhood in B lies in B−N(j) and has order at least k, contrary to |B−N(j)|≤k−3+ε_B. For each anchor choose a private B port touching it, choosing a high one if possible. A low representative z, if necessary for i, is the only B i-port. For j≠i, a high private j-port v exists. If ij is an anchor edge then z has distance two to j; otherwise the length-two route from v to i must use z, hence z again has distance two to j. Thus every chosen representative has distance two from both other anchors.

For each high A nonport y let J_y be the set of anchors at distance two from y, possibly empty. Each chosen i-representative counts at distance two all A ports touching i. It counts at distance four any y with i∉J_y and J_y nonempty: all anchor routes cost at least four, and a j∈J_y gives a four-edge walk. Those y with J_y empty count at all high representatives by the high-pair bound, so at least twice. Let Q=Σ_y |J_y|. Summing the three even budgets, each high A port counts at least once. For a nonempty J of size j, the contribution available for |A_high| above the three quotas is 1−(3−j)=j−2≤j/3; for empty J it is at most −1≤0. Hence |A_high|≤3k+Q/3.

At the anchors themselves the high B ports spend two even incidences each, high B nonports three each, and high A nonports Q in total. So Q+2p+3d≤3k. Also 2p+3d≤3(k−3), by the three bounds on B vertices missing i. Counting the anchors and the possible low vertex outside S gives

    n≤4+|A_high|+p+d≤4+4k+p/3≤(9/2)k+5/2.

For k≥16 this is ≤5k−3. This covers the private-port B case regardless of whether B itself is shallow. A remaining complementary shape for this three-nearest configuration is a B side with no high nonport and at least one multiport.

## The opposite side can also consist entirely of high ports and a possible low vertex

This completes the three-sole-nearest theorem with no condition on B. If B contains no high vertex, it is a singleton low vertex adjacent to all three anchors. Its even quota pays all vertices of A at anchor-depth 1 or 3. Vertices of A at depth 2 or 4 count in at least one anchor's even budget, and depths are at most four as all anchors are high. Thus n≤4k+4.

Suppose every high B vertex is a port and there is at least one. Each anchor i has a high B port, since otherwise x_i bounds the total high B vertices by k−3. Then |B|≤k−2 and every high B vertex, missing i, has degree ≤|B|−1+2<k. Every high B port is at distance two from each anchor it misses. Otherwise its closed B-neighborhood of size ≥k−|T|+1≥k−1, where T is its anchor support and |T|≤2, would lie in B−N(i), of size ≤k−2 by x_i. Below put b_h=|B_high|, b_i=|N(i)∩B_high| and let Q_i count high A nonports at distance two from i. A high B port of support T sees every A port whose support intersects T, and at distance four every high A nonport whose nearest set J is disjoint from T (if J is empty use high-pair diameter; otherwise the missing anchor in J is at distance two from the B representative).

Suppose B has a high port meeting all three anchors. Its even budget counts all high A ports and all high A members of anchor-depth three. A high A member has depth at most three since there is a high B port on the other side, and all opposite high pairs have distance ≤4. The remaining high A members have depth two and count at least once in the three anchor even budgets. Let g and v count respectively the high B ports meeting all three anchors and touching just one. The high B ports use 3b_h−Σ_i b_i incidences in these same budgets, since every missing anchor is at two. If the anchors span e edges, their ordered nonedges supply 6−2e more, via the common B port. The three x_i also supply three distinct A ports private to the respective i, by taking a geodesic to the sole nearest anchor: an intermediate to another anchor would make a second nearest anchor. These three private A ports cost at least 2e extra even incidences in the anchor budgets, since the neighbors in S of their sole anchor are at distance two. **Count only their incidences in anchor budgets here**: the vertices themselves are counted with other A ports in the B root's quota, and no anchor A-port incidence was previously used in the depth-two sum. Summing gives n≤4+4k+(Σ_i b_i−2b_h)−6=4k−2+g−v. Also g≤k−1. If g=k any A port sees all those high B vertices at distance two and is internally universal in A, contrary to two distinct sole-nearest anchors on A. Thus n≤5k−3.

In the absence of a high three-anchor port, suppose there is a high double port of support U=12. Choose a high B port whose support V contains 3. If V={3} the two chosen even budgets pay every high A port, and all high A nonports except potentially those with nearest set meeting both U and V. Every such unpaid set has size ≥2, so |A_high|≤2k+(Q_1+Q_2+Q_3)/2. The anchor budgets give Σ_i Q_i≤3k−b_h, since each high B port has a missing anchor at distance two. Hence n≤4+(7/2)k+b_h/2≤(9/2)k+4, where b_h≤2k by the source x_i bounds.

If V is another double, relabel the anchors so U=12,V=23. An unpaid nearest set must contain 2 or contain both 1 and 3. Their number is ≤Q_2+(Q_1+Q_3)/2. The anchor quotas give Q_i≤k−(b_h−b_i). Hence n≤4+4k−b_h+b_2+(b_1+b_3)/2. With no high B triple port the final correction is ≤b_2/2: the only positive individual contributions are one-half at the double ports touching 2. Since b_2≤k by a contact of A, this is at most (9/2)k+4. Both double cases imply n≤5k−3 for k≥16. When all high B ports are private, the earlier private-representative proof uses a high representative at every anchor, whose existence here was just proved; a single additional low multiport does not change the high-port or high-nonport counts. This covers all possibilities for B.
