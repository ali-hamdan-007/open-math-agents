# A shallow side with a port incident to all three minimum-cut vertices

Let k≥16. Suppose a smallest separator S={1,2,3} leaves two components A,B, and every high vertex of A is a port (has a neighbor in S). If A contains a vertex e adjacent to all three anchors then a qualifying graph has a spanning path. The low vertex, if any, may be in S or either component. The proof invokes the unrestricted n≤5k−3 theorem.

If A has no high members it is the singleton e. Its quota counts B vertices at S-depth one or three, and the three anchor budgets count all B vertices at depth two or four; larger depth is impossible since the anchors are high. Thus n≤4k+4. Otherwise any high B vertex has S-depth ≤3 by its distance to a high port in A. The one even budget of e counts all high B ports and all high B depth-three vertices, with exact distances two and four. Throughout let a count the high vertices of A, and count at most one low vertex outside S separately.

If B has high nonports solely nearest to each respective anchor at distance two, apply three-nearest-anchors.md. Suppose at least one such high nonport is solely nearest to i but at least one other sole-nearest anchor class is missing. Choose two anchors K including i such that every nonempty nearest set of B high depth-two vertices meets K (discard an absent singleton class). The high B nonport solely nearest i sees at distance exactly four every high A vertex missing i and has a separate second layer of order at least three. Therefore |A−N(i)|≤k−2 even with a low vertex there. Every high A port missing i is at distance two from it: otherwise its closed internal neighborhood of ≥k−1 vertices lies in A−N(i). Thus the two anchor even budgets of K count all high B depth-two vertices and all high A members missing i. At most k further high A members touch i, by an opposite port. Together with e's one budget this yields n≤4k+4.

Assume therefore that each high B vertex at depth two has at least two nearest anchors and let q be their count. If a≤2k, then 2q≤3k and the preceding one-budget count directly gives n≤(9/2)k+4. A multiport in B implies a≤2k: one port touching two anchors counts the union of opposite A contacts there, and an opposite contact at the remaining anchor accounts for the others. Hence we may assume all B ports are private and a>2k. No high B vertex can have depth at least three, for it would have all a high A vertices at distance four. Let b_j be the count of high B private j-ports, b=Σb_j≤k as seen from e.

Write H=a−2k>0. For distinct i,j,l, let s_i be the count of high A private i-ports and a_j the count of high A j-ports (of any type). Each high A port belongs to the private i class or touches j or l, so s_i+a_j≥a−a_l≥a−k. If all high B j-ports are within two of i, their b_j incidences count at i. Otherwise fix a high private B j-port v farther than two from i. It sees the a_j high A contacts at distance two. It also sees, at distance exactly four, each high A private i-port farther than two from j: via i or j the length is at least four, and via l both endpoints need at least two steps. Thus at least s_i+a_j−k≥H high A private i-ports are at distance two from j. The ordered pair (i,j) in either case supplies at least min(b_j,H) anchor-even incidences from high ports. The charges for all six ordered pairs are disjoint: on either side a charged port is private, and its own and its charged anchor determine the ordered pair.

The three anchor budgets consequently give 2q+2Σ_j min(b_j,H)≤3k. Since a≤3k by opposite contacts at each anchor, this yields

    n ≤ 4+a+b+q ≤ 4+a+(3/2)k+b−min(b,H) ≤ (9/2)k+4.

For b≥H use a−H=2k and b≤k; for b<H use a≤3k. This upper bound is ≤5k−3 at k≥16. This completes the proof.
