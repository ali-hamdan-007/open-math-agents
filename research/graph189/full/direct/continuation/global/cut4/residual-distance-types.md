# Four-cut distance vectors after the small-side theorem

For k≥16, write S for a minimum four-cut with two components A,B, both containing a high nonport. Assume n≥6k−10, the complement of the proven connectivity-four moderate-order range. The paper order/four-cut-small-deep-side.md resolves a side of order ≤2k−4, or order ≤2k−3 when its opposite side is wholly high. The following simplifications hold in any surviving case.

* There is no high nonport with a single nearest anchor. Indeed all its opposite high vertices missing that anchor have distance at least four, hence exactly four, and a fourth layer requires at least four separate second-layer vertices. The opposite anchor's contacts number at most k by an edge into the first side at that anchor. Thus the opposite side has order ≤2k−4+ε, with ε=1 only if it contains the low vertex, in which event the first side is wholly high.
* There is no port touching all four anchors. It would see all opposite ports at distance two. A high nonport on the first side sees all opposite high nonports at distance four and has a separate second layer of size at least four. The same small-side cutoff applies.
* Every high nonport has S-depth exactly two, since the opposite side has a high nonport and high pairs have distance at most four. The low vertex can have depth at most three. For opposite high nonports the two nearest-anchor sets intersect, by the exact cross distance and the high-pair bound. The possible two-nearest sets form three pairs of complementary two-subsets of S. For each pair of complementary labels U,V, any allowed supports can be extended to one of four placements: U,V on A alone; U,V on B alone; U on both sides with V unused; V on both sides with U unused. Thus 4³ templates cover all two-nearest supports before relabelling anchors/sides.
* Suppose both sides have a high port whose distance to each anchor it misses is at least three. Any pair of opposite high ports with this property is at distance two (intersecting anchor supports) or four (disjoint supports). Let p be the total count of high ports, d the total high nonports, and E the number of positive-even anchor incidences at high ports. Each side has at most k opposite high ports with the property just stated. Every other high port consumes at least one unit of E. Therefore p≤2k+E. Since high nonports have at least two nearest anchors, E+2d≤4k. Consequently n≤6k+5−d, allowing four anchors and a possible low vertex. At n≥6k−10 this bounds d≤15 in the branch where both types of far high port exist.

The exact model four_deep_residual.py implements the four-anchor analog of the three-anchor conditions, with the two small-side consequences above, but without requiring particular two-nearest templates. Four anchors give 6 anchor distances and a boundary allowance of 4−t when a vertex touches t anchors. At |B|>4k the double-decrease constraint is valid from a four-root even-distance cover. Three exploratory all-high queries returned UNSAT: all six distances equal two with |B|≤4k or |B|>4k, and all six equal three with |B|≤4k. These isolated cases are not an exhaustion of vertex-connectivity four.
