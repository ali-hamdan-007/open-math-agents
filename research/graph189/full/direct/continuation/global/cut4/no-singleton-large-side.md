# A shallow side opposite multiply-nearest nonports

For every k≥16, let a minimum four-cut separate exactly two components A,B and every high member of A be a port. Suppose every high B nonport has at least two anchors at distance two. Then G is traceable, including the case of arbitrary unique low vertex. Assume n≥6k−10, beyond the moderate-order theorem. Write a,p for high ports in A,B, d for high B nonports, and E for all exact-distance-two incidences between anchors and high ports. Then E+2d≤4k and n≤5+a+p+d. Write v=min(a,p), w=max(a,p).

The support bounds of verify/four-cut-all-high-ports.md apply without requiring the nonports on either side to be absent. If w≥2k, every port on the side with v high ports touches at most two anchors. Indeed a universal would see all w ports at distance two. Thus each opposite contact class has at most k−1 members, by using any measuring port and its positive boundary reserve. A three-anchor port would now give w≤(k−1)+(k−1). Similarly if w≥3k−3 all ports on the smaller side are private: a two-anchor port would give w≤(k−2)+2(k−1). In this last case each class on the opposite side has at most k−3 members, so w≤4k−12. The boundary reserve used here is the number 4−r of own-side or anchor even vertices at a port with r contacts. Summing the resulting even-pair charges and covering odd pairs as in cross-port-pairs.md gives vw≤(k−s)v+kE when each measuring port has reserve at least s.

* If w≤2k then n≤5+2k+3v/2+w−vw/(2k). Using w≤2k, the coefficient 1−v/(2k)≥0, and v≤2k this is at most 5k+5.
* If v≥2k, both sides' ports have support at most two and every contact class on both sides has size at most k−2. The odd-pair coefficient therefore improves as well, giving vw≤(k−2)(v+E). Hence n≤5+2k+3v/2+w−vw/(2(k−2)). Using w≥v and maximizing the remaining concave quadratic gives n≤5+2k+(25/8)(k−2)<6k−10.
* In the remaining cases v≤2k<w. For w≤3k−4 the measuring ports have reserve two, so n≤5+2k+3v/2+w−v(w+2)/(2k). This is nondecreasing in w. At w=3k−4 it equals 5k+1+v/k≤5k+3.
* For w≥3k−3 the smaller side's ports are private, so vw≤(k−3)v+kE and w≤4k−12. If v=a, at least one A member is high (the singleton-low case has n≤5k+5). Each high A member has only its one anchor and at most a internal neighbors, including the possible low. Thus a≥k−1. The preceding bound with reserve three gives n≤6k−7−a(k−9)/(2k)<6k−10. The strict inequality is equivalent at a=k−1 to k²−16k+9>0 for all k≥16.
* Finally v=p and w=a≥3k−3. If d=0 the stronger bound E+3d≤4k is already valid. Otherwise a high B nonport with exactly two nearest anchors would force a≤2(k−3)+(k−4)=3k−10. Indeed each corresponding A contact class has size at most k−3 by privacy in B, and every other high A port has distance four from the nonport. Its second layer has at least four vertices when the fourth layer is nonempty. Hence E+3d≤4k in either case. The stronger inequality yields n≤5+4k/3+4p/3+a−p(a+3)/(3k). Substitute a≤4k−12 using p≤2k to obtain n≤16k/3−7+3p/k≤16k/3−1<6k−10.

Every case is below the minimum order 6k−10. At k≥16, the resulting reduction for one high-shallow side requires a high opposite nonport with a single nearest anchor. High depth three is already excluded by the all-high universal-side argument in shallow-large-degree.md. The two-sided deep and two-sided shallow alternatives are already covered by their separate theorems.
