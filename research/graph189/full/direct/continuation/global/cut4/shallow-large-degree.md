# Every minimum four-cut at large second-smallest degree

A qualifying graph of vertex-connectivity four and second-smallest degree k≥94 is traceable. For order n≤6k−11 this is the checked moderate-order theorem. At larger order, minimum-cut-two-sides.md gives exactly two components A,B of the complement of a minimum four-cut S. The case with high nonports on both sides is settled by cross-port-pairs.md, or for all k≥16 by verify/four-cut-deep-all-sizes.md. We may take A such that all its high members are ports. Write a for their number. A singleton low A touches all S. Its even sets pay the B vertices of depths one and three while the four anchor budgets pay depths two and four. The distance five bound ensures that no B vertex is deeper, giving n≤5k+5. Hence A has a high member, |A|≥k−3 and a≥k−4. By the opposite high-pair distance bound, every high B member has depth at most three. If B is only a low vertex, the preceding singleton bound applies.

## A high port touching all anchors

We first record a criterion used twice below. Suppose some high A port u touches all four anchors, |A|≤k+1, and for every i every high A vertex missing i is at distance two from i. Then n≤6k−11. The high port u pays for every B vertex of odd anchor-depth, which is exactly one nearer S than its distance from u. Each even-depth vertex is paid by a corresponding anchor. If some label i has no high depth-two B vertex solely nearest to it, three anchor budgets pay for all high B vertices at depth two, and a low B member of even depth can be added separately. No high B member can have depth four. This gives n≤4+|A|+4k+1≤5k+6.

Otherwise choose private B ports v_i from geodesics to all four singleton-nearest high classes. These ports are distinct, and each touches only i. All nonadjacent anchor pairs are at distance two via u. For adjacent anchors i,j, v_i is at distance two from j. Thus there are at least twelve even anchor incidences outside the even-depth B vertices, counting oriented pairs and their respective replacements. Additionally let E_A be all even anchor incidences in A. Every i has at most k−3 A neighbors, since v_i sees them at distance two and the boundary of its closed internal B-neighborhood gives ≥4−1=3 further second-layer vertices in B or S. Choose i freely if all A vertices are high, choose an incident i if the low vertex in A is a port, and otherwise choose i incident to an A-neighbor of that low vertex. Such a neighbor is high and a port by connectedness. For the chosen i every A vertex missing i is at distance two. Therefore E_A≥|A|−k+3. Adding the odd and even B depths gives n≤4+|A|+k+4k−12−E_A≤6k−11.

In particular, if some high B vertex has depth three, every high A port is at distance four from it; a disjoint second layer has size ≥4. Hence a≤k−4. The earlier lower bounds force a=k−4, |A|=k−3 and the low vertex lies in A. Every high A vertex now needs all available neighbors in A and S. The criterion applies. Henceforth every high B nonport has depth two.

## A label adjacent to all high A ports

In this case a≤k by any opposite contact at this label, and |A|≤k+1. Assuming n≥6k−10 gives |B|>4k. No first step from A shortens two anchor distances, since that step, at most two others chosen toward the remaining anchors, and the starting vertex parity-cover all of B. Each label has a high A contact. Indeed if j touches only a low A vertex z, the common neighbors of the edge jz all lie in S. The established edge-order lower bound gives at least ceil((n−3k−2)/(k+1))≥3 common neighbors, so j touches every other anchor. A high A vertex at any other label i must then touch j: otherwise its step to i would shorten both i and j. This is a contradiction.

Fix a high A port u with support T, the set of labels it touches. Its even quota pays for opposite ports touching T and for high B nonports whose nearest labels miss T (they are at least four away and the high-pair distance bound applies). One A representative at each label outside T covers the remaining B ports. The anchor budgets at T cover all remaining high B nonports. The total number of quotas used is five. For any i in T let e_i count its distance-two A vertices. The bound omits a possible low vertex from the high counts, so n≤5+a+5k−e_i. At n≥6k−10 this entails e_i≤15. Every label has a high contact and therefore satisfies this bound. Any high contact at i misses at most four other A vertices by internal degree ≥k−4 and |A|≤k+1. Hence at most 19 A vertices miss each label. Since a≥k−4>76, some high A port touches all four. Each label is adjacent to more than four members of A. Every high A vertex missing the label therefore has a neighbor incident to it. The criterion in the preceding section applies.

## No label adjacent to all high A ports

Write p for the number of high B ports, d_1 for the number of high B nonports with exactly one nearest anchor and d_2 for the other high B nonports (which have ≥2 nearest). Let E count the anchor/distance-two/high-port incidences on both sides. The anchor caps give E+d_1+2d_2≤4k.

First suppose d_1>0. Any one of those singleton-nearest vertices, at label i, sees all a−a_i high A ports missing i at distance four, where a_i≤k. Its second layer of size at least four is disjoint from these vertices whenever this fourth layer is nonempty. In the empty case a=a_i≤k. Thus a≤2k−4. Choose A representatives such that every label is touched, and at every used singleton-nearest label at least one chosen HIGH representative misses that label. This is feasible by the present case hypothesis. At most four representatives suffice. Indeed in an inclusion-minimal collection of at least five, each row has a coordinate where it alone witnesses either the required contact or the required high noncontact. A coordinate cannot witness both sorts of uniqueness: apart from those two rows, at least two additional rows are high and must either touch or miss that coordinate. Each row therefore needs a different one of the four coordinates, a contradiction. Chosen representatives cover every high B port at distance two and every singleton-nearest B nonport at distance four, so p+d_1≤4k.

Every odd high port pair is charged as in cross-port-pairs.md, giving at most kE such pairs. At A the even cross-port pairs and all pairs between singleton-nearest B nonports and A high ports missing the respective label share the ak capacity. Therefore

    ap + max(a−k,0)d_1 ≤ ka+kE,
    (a/k)(p+d_1)+2d_2 ≤ a+4k.

The second inequality follows by adding the anchor quotas; for a≤k one may weaken the coefficient of d_1 from 1 to a/k. With a≤2k the resulting upper bound on n is nondecreasing in p+d_1, so n≤5+2k+(3/2)a+(1−a/(2k))4k=5+6k−a/2≤7+11k/2≤6k−11.

Now suppose d_1=0. Put v=min(a,p), w=max(a,p). Equations (1) and (2) of cross-port-pairs.md give

    n ≤ 5+2k+v+w−v(w−k)/(2k).

When v≥32, taking w≤4k if v≤2k and w≥v if v≥2k yields n≤max(5+6k−16,5+41k/8)≤6k−11. When v<32, a≥k−4 forces p≤31. All high nonports then lie in B, and all have at least two nearest anchors, giving d_2≤2k. If a≤7k/2 this gives n≤5+7k/2+31+2k≤6k−11 at k≥94. If a>7k/2, the number of private high A ports at each label i is at least a−3k, because at most 3k ports touch any of the other three labels. A high B nonport missing two nearest labels would see more than 2(a−3k)>k private A ports at distance four. Thus every high B nonport has at least three nearest labels and d_2≤4k/3. This gives n≤5+4k+31+4k/3≤6k−11. All alternatives contradict the assumed order.
