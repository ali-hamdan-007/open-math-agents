# A complete four-anchor neighborhood on one side

Let G be qualifying with κ=4, k≥16, and a minimum four-cut S leaving exactly two components A,B. If every vertex in A touches every anchor, and G[S] has a vertex of degree at least two, then G has a Hamiltonian path. In particular this applies if G[S] is connected and |B|>4k, for then the four-root double-decrease argument below forces all of A to be complete to S.

From any B port all of A is at distance two, so |A|≤k. Every B vertex at anchor-depth 1 or 3 counts in a single A member's even budget, with exact distances two or four. Depth cannot exceed four by the global diameter-five bound. Every depth-two or depth-four B vertex counts at least once in the four anchor budgets. If an anchor has no B depth-two vertex solely nearest to it, the other three anchor budgets suffice for all depth-two vertices. A depth-four B vertex, if any, is the lone low vertex: a high A member is at distance five from it. If A itself is a singleton low, all anchors are high and may have several depth-four vertices, but n≤1+4+5k≤6k−11 directly using all four budgets. Otherwise allow one exceptional fourth-layer vertex when counting only three anchors. This gives n≤|A|+4+4k+1≤5k+5≤6k−11.

If all four depth-two singleton-nearest classes occur, choose an immediate B port on a geodesic to each corresponding nearest anchor. The chosen ports are private and distinct. For e edges in G[S], there are 12−2e ordered anchor nonedges at distance two via A, and at least 2e further even anchor incidences from these four private B ports via the anchor edges. Thus B depth-two and depth-four vertices total ≤4k−12. Moreover a private B i-port sees all of A and each of the d_S(i) adjacent anchors at distance two. Equality |A|+d_S(i)=k would imply this private port is universal within connected B (otherwise the first internal second-layer vertex exceeds the cap), which gives every B nonport distance two to i and contradicts a singleton-nearest class at another anchor. Choosing d_S(i)≥2 yields |A|≤k−3. Consequently n≤|A|+4+k+4k−12≤6k−11; the audited connectivity-four moderate-order theorem applies.

For |B|>4k no neighbor of y∈A can shorten two of the four anchor distances: choosing additional first-step neighbors towards the remaining anchors would cover B by at most four positive-even root sets. If ij is an anchor edge, each i-port then touches j, and no vertex touching neither can be adjacent to one touching both. Connectedness of A forces every A vertex to touch both. For connected G[S] this gives all four anchors, as asserted.
