# Traceability through order 5k−3 without a maximum-degree assumption

Let G satisfy the original finite connected Graph 189 hypotheses. If k≥16 and n≤5k−3, then G has a Hamiltonian path. The degree spectrum and vertex connectivity are unrestricted.

The all-order cut-vertex and separating-pair theorems reduce to κ≥3. Assume nontraceability. In max-degree-plus-two-exception.md, from the choice of the maximizing longest path P through the reduction a=3, m=|C|=k−2, n≥5k−4 and |P|≥4k−2, the only use of Δ≤k+2 was to obtain n≤5k−3 at the outset. The remainder of that reduction uses the order inequality alone and hence applies here. In particular C is a clique; all its high members have the same three marks A on P, and only a possible low member of C can miss some marks. These are all the edges from C to V−C.

Let X,Y,Z partition V−(A∪C) by distances 1,2,≥3 from A. For a high x∈C, every X vertex is at distance two and every high Z vertex is at distance four, by the bound on distances between high vertices. Thus |X|≤k, and |X|+|Z|≤k+ε, where ε=0 when z∈C or all vertices are high, and ε=1 is allowed when the low vertex is outside C. It follows that |Y|≥3k−5−ε. Any mark sees at most k members of Y at distance two. For i∈A let Y_i be the vertices of Y at distance two from i alone among the marks. The other two marks together see at most 2k vertices of Y, so |Y_i|≥k−5−ε. Among the Y vertices at distance two from i, at most 5+ε lie outside Y_i.

At least two marks touch all of C. Indeed, if a low z∈C missed two distinct marks i,j, then all of Y_i∪Y_j would have distance exactly four from z. A route through another clique member and one of i,j has length four; a shorter route leaving directly through z's own marks is excluded by privateness. Since ε=0 in this case, |Y_i|+|Y_j|≥2k−10>k. If C is wholly high every mark already touches it all.

For a full mark i choose its immediate neighbor w along P. The vertex w misses all of C and sees its k−2 members at distance two. Thus ≤2 other vertices are at positive even distance from w. Its fourth layer cannot separate a fifth layer since κ≥3. The distance-three targets outside C use only those two exterior penultimates, with ≤k targets per penultimate by looking back one layer, or else they are marks. One mark is already a neighbor of w. Hence

    4k−2≤|V−C|≤d(w)+2k+5,        d(w)≥2k−7.

Every neighbor of w belongs to A, X or Y. The Y-neighbors all lie at distance two from i and number at most k. There are initially at most three A-neighbors and at most k−1 X-neighbors; hence w has ≥k−9 Y-neighbors, ≥k−14−ε of them private to i. The latter is positive at k≥16, so w touches no other mark: any such mark would also be within two of every Y-neighbor. Repeating the count with just one A-neighbor shows that w has ≥k−7 Y-neighbors, ≥k−12−ε of them private to i, and at least k−8 X-neighbors.

Any v∈X adjacent to w that touches another mark j cannot touch those private Y_i-neighbors; otherwise they would be at distance two from j. It consequently sees all ≥k−12−ε of them at distance two through w. It also sees the C-neighbors of j at distance two, since v is outside the whole contact set. If z∈C there are ≥k−3 such neighbors and ε=0; otherwise there are k−2 and ε≤1. Either way there are at least 2k−15>k distance-two witnesses for k≥16, impossible. Thus w and each of its ≥k−8 X-neighbors touch i alone. The two full marks require disjoint sets of at least k−7 such vertices in X, whereas |X|≤k and 2(k−7)>k. This contradiction proves the theorem. It supplies the distance-budget step in place of the maximum-degree and rooted-wedge argument in the three-mark conclusion.
