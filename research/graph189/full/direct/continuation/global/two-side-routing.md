# Routing every separator vertex across two moderately sized sides

Let G be a finite 2-connected graph whose second-smallest degree is k. No distance bound is needed in this routing lemma. Suppose a minimum cut S of size s≥2 has exactly two components C₁,C₂, k≥5s, and both sides have order at most 2k−3s−1. Then G has a Hamiltonian path. Consequently for the small minimum cuts isolated by `minimum-cut-two-sides.md`, the larger side must have at least 2k−3s vertices in any counterexample (for k≥5s).

Both components contact every member of S, since otherwise a smaller cut would suffice. Call vertices of degree at least k high. If both sides contain only high vertices, choose the possible low vertex in S as the joining vertex r, or choose any r∈S when all are high. Take a joining contact c_i∈C_i adjacent to r in each side.

If the unique low vertex z lies in C₁ and C₁−z is nonempty, choose distinct x,c₁∈C₁−z with x adjacent to z and c₁ adjacent to some joining r∈S. Such distinct choices always exist. The C₁-neighbor set of z is nonempty by connectedness. The S-neighbor set in C₁−z is nonempty since otherwise z cuts off the rest of C₁. If both choices were forced to the same singleton, that vertex would cut off the remaining high vertices from S and z. The remaining set cannot be empty: if C₁ consists of just z and that vertex, its high vertex has degree at most s+1<k. Fix c₂∈C₂ adjacent to r. We will prescribe both endpoints x,c₁ of the high part of the first side. If C₁ is the singleton z, take as r any neighbor of z in S, choose c₂∈C₂ adjacent to r, and use just the second-side construction below, appending r,z at its open end.

Each a∈S−{r} is high and has at least k−s neighbors in the high parts of the sides: remove up to s−1 vertices in S and a possible low z. Assign a to a side containing at least ceil((k−s)/2)≥2s such neighbors. Greedily reserve two different neighbors there for each assigned a, with all its side's reserved terminals distinct and different from c_i and from x when prescribed. There are at most s−1 assigned vertices on a side; before choosing the last pair at most 2(s−2)+2=2s−2 vertices are excluded. In the case of a singleton low side all assigned vertices go to C₂.

In a high part of a side retain its original graph. For each assigned vertex retain just its two reserved edges, forming a degree-two marker. If x,c₁ were prescribed, add one further degree-two marker between them. The vertices of the high part have degree at least k−s−1 there (or k−s without deleting a low vertex). In the first side with deleted low, its augmented order is |C₁|−1+#assigned+1≤2k−2s−2. Without deleted low, and without the extra marker, it is |C_i|+#assigned≤2k−2s−2. Thus the sum of degrees of every nonadjacent pair of high-part vertices reaches the augmented order. Successive Bondy–Chvátal closures turn that part into a clique while preserving Hamiltonicity of the augmented graph. The reserved pairs are disjoint, so arranging each pair consecutively in a clique cycle and inserting its marker gives a spanning cycle in the closure, and hence in the original augmented graph. Each high side has more than two vertices by the degree inequality, and can accommodate all the selected terminals as shown above.

When two endpoints were prescribed, delete the extra marker to get a spanning path from x to c₁ and prepend z. In the other cases, open the spanning cycle at c_i to obtain a path ending there, preserving all assigned markers. Joining the side paths at r through c₁,r,c₂ gives the desired Hamiltonian path. The singleton case was described separately. This handles arbitrary assignment counts and every location of the possible low vertex.
