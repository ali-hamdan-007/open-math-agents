# Private contacts on the smaller side of a five-cut

Consider a qualifying graph and a minimum s-cut whose components are partitioned into A and B. Write X,Y for the numbers of high vertices and assume every A port has a single label and each label i has a high A representative u_i. Write P_A,P_B for numbers of high ports and d for the number of high B nonports. Then

    Y ≤ sk + P_A/s + 1.

Indeed u_i has at least k−1 high members in its own closed internal neighborhood (losing at most its single anchor and the possible low). Each of these is either a high A contact of i or a high A vertex at distance two from i. Thus the sum over all high B nonports of their numbers of labels at distance two is at most P_A+s, by summing all anchor quotas. The high u_i sees every B port on i and every high B nonport whose distance from i is greater than two, at even distance. For the latter this uses that a length-three path to a B nonport from the private u_i must use i on its first step. Therefore P_B+sd−(P_A+s)≤sk. Since P_B≤sk this gives the displayed bound. Depth three in B and the possible low are both allowed.

In a hypothetical sequence of connectivity-five counterexamples with k tending to infinity, the smaller high count X satisfies limsup X/k≤(17−sqrt(109))/6<1.1 and liminf Y/k>5.5. Specifically the optimized block bound q≥(2k−2sqrt(3k)−5)/3 with the five-connector cycle estimate gives liminf n/k≥20/3; the near-Hamilton-connected small block has even larger liminf n/k≥7. The product XY≤kX+5k², X≤Y, bounds X/k by (1+sqrt(21))/2, and its other root in x(17/3−x)≤5 is outside this range. This proves the stated constraints.

By k>6(s−1) there are exactly two components. At |B|>5k no first step in A can reduce two anchor distances, by the standard parity cover. Every label has a high A contact: otherwise its only A contact is the low z, and the common neighbors on this edge are all in S. At n/k>6.5 the edge-order theorem gives at least four common neighbors, hence this label is adjacent to every other one. A high A port on any label must also touch that label by the first-step restriction, contradiction. A high A port exists unless the connected side is the single low. In that case the root and five anchor budgets give n≤6k+6, impossible in this regime.

Suppose A has a high nonport x. All high B nonports are at distance four from it. If the nearest anchors of x are covered by at most four A ports, then those ports and x cover all of B's high vertices in their positive-even sets. Namely every uncovered B port has no contact on a nearest anchor of x and is then at distance four, while an opposite port touching any covered label meets a selected port at distance two. If x itself had depth three then it alone sees every high B vertex (which must be a port) at distance four; a greater depth is impossible. Thus x has depth two. The bound Y>5k now forces all five labels nearest and all A ports private, since a port touching at least two and three other representatives would cover the five. The private-side inequality would now give n/k≤5+1.2·1.1+o(1)<20/3, contradiction.

Consequently in any sufficiently large k connectivity-five counterexample, every high vertex of the smaller side is a port. The smaller side cannot have all its ports private. The same localization applies if private A ports cover every label with high representatives even though a small number of low contacts occur separately: the all-private bound was stated without needing this extension.

## Effective range and a further support restriction

All preceding five-cut exclusions hold for k≥6000. The large-block bound gives n≥10q+9>(20k−20sqrt(3k)−50)/3+9≥13k/2+6 in that range. The near-Hamilton-connected whole and terminal minima used in lowblock/geodesic-width-and-order.md exceed 13k/2+6 (their linear terms are 7k−24 and 6(k−3)+3ceil(k/2)−1, and either alternative quadratic term is larger). Thus X+Y≥13k/2 and the product forces X<6k/5: on the range 6/5≤X/k≤(1+sqrt(21))/2, x+1+5/x is bounded by its larger endpoint maximum, at most 191/30<13/2. In particular Y>5k and X+X/5+5k+7<13k/2+6. This supplies the finite thresholds used in the private-side argument.

Suppose also that there is no label common to every high member of A. Every label has a high contact as above. For any distinct high support sets T,U we have |U−T|≤1. In fact a high A port on T covers high B ports meeting T and high B nonports not at distance two from any member of T. The remaining nonports are covered by the anchor budgets in T; if U covers two missing labels, one A port on U and at most 3−|T| further ports cover the other B ports. This covers all Y high B members with only five quotas. The nonports allegedly covered by the T-port are indeed at distance four, since a three-step cross path to a nonport must start at an incident anchor at distance two from it.

An elementary set-system description follows. All high supports have size d or d+1, and if both sizes occur every d-set lies in every (d+1)-set. Two distinct d-sets then force the unique larger member to be their union. One d-set instead supplies a common label. For a uniform family of d-sets, pairwise exchange of one member forces a common (d−1)-set or containment in one (d+1)-set: fixing two members C∪{i},C∪{j}, any third member either contains C or contains both i and j and all but one of C. Once a latter member exists, a member containing C cannot use a third new element. Accordingly a family covering all five labels and having empty intersection consists of all singletons, or consists of all five complementary four-sets and optionally the full set.

The singleton family is excluded by the preceding private-side count even if the low member has several labels. Indeed sum only over high A contact counts in that proof, and choose the high singleton representative on each label. In the four-set family choose high A representatives for all five missing labels. Each high B port is seen at distance two by at least four representatives; thus P_B≤5k/4. Write F for the number of full high A ports and let A_i count high contacts at i. Every high A vertex has at most X−k+5 internal nonneighbors among the other high vertices, allowing one low vertex in A. If A_i>X−k+5, every high A noncontact to i has a neighbor at that label, contributing an even anchor incidence. At most one i can fail the inequality: each failure needs ≥k−5 A vertices missing i, and the missed labels of high A vertices are disjoint between labels. At a failing label one high representative still has all but at most X−k+5 other high vertices as neighbors. It follows that the number E_A of positive-even anchor incidences on high A vertices is at least X−F−max(0,X−k+5).

Every high B nonport has depth two. A high one of depth three would have every high A vertex at distance four, giving X≤k−5, and the degree lower bound on A would force |A|=k−4 with every high vertex complete to all five anchors. This contradicts the assumption of no common high label. Hence d≤5k−E_A. If F=0 we obtain n≤6+X+5k/4+5k−E_A≤(25/4)k+6+max(0,X−k+5)<13k/2+6 when k≥6000. If F>0 then P_B≤k, giving n≤6k+6+F+max(0,X−k+5). The lower order bound forces F≥3k/10−5. Thus a five-cut counterexample with no label common to its small-side high vertices requires at least 3k/10−5 high small-side ports each adjacent to all five labels. Only this case and the common-label case survive this paper reduction.
