# Exact credits just below the sk high-population bound

The order cutoff in three-common-labels.md may be written n>(s+1+3/(s−2))k+s+2. Its proof only needs H=X+Y>(s+1+3/(s−2))k+1 for the private alternative; the c=1,2 singleton gap automatically exceeds c+1 since k>6(s−1), and the separate codegree ceiling follows from n>(s+1)k+s.

Here is a finer sufficient test, used only when s≥8 and k>6(s−1). Write h=n−s−1, b=sk−s and d=s−3. Suppose

    h>(s+1)k,   (h−b)(b−k)>sk²,
    d(d−1)≥s+4+k/d,   s(s−1)≥s+1+k/(s−2).

Then G is traceable. These include all side/low placements. The first two tests give Y>b at every minimum cut: if Y≤b, put X≥h−Y. On h/2≤Y≤b the concave product (h−Y)(Y−k) has its minimum at an endpoint. At b it exceeds sk² by hypothesis; at h/2 it exceeds sk² from h>(s+1)k and s≥8, since (s²−1)/4>s. Likewise X<sk/(s−2) by the convex product argument of three-common-labels.md.

For q A ports covering all anchors, their q boundary reservations together with repeated B-port incidences across their roots save at least s(q−1) of the qk even slots. Indeed if their total support count is M, their boundaries reserve at least qs−M in A∪S. Choose s distinct B representatives matched bijectively to the s labels by s-connectivity; the one at each label is seen by every A representative touching that label, implying at least M−s units of duplication/extra low witnesses. If only a subset of labels needs to be covered, the same total credit remains valid by using one distinct B representative on every label touched and M−s as a lower bound on duplication.

A high A port with support T touching r labels and another A port (even low) with ≥2 labels outside T would give a cover with r anchor roots and q≤s−r A roots, q≥2, covering all Y high vertices as in three-common-labels.md. Choose the A roots to cover all S, including T's source and this extra port. The credit then gives Y≤sk−s. Thus the directed difference is at most one for every high A port against every A port. Likewise if a high A nonport has its nearest labels covered by at most s−1 A ports, then this root reserves at least s outside B and yields Y≤sk−s. High depth three is impossible as before. Consequently any high A nonport would force all A ports to be private.

Every label has a high A contact. Indeed if label j touches only the low vertex z of A, the ≥s−1 common neighbors on zj (from n>(s+1)k+s) force z to touch all labels and j to touch every other anchor. The high-nonport alternative has just forced all A ports private and so cannot apply. Each high A member touches some label other than j and therefore sees j at distance two. The full low A port z pays for all odd-depth B vertices; every high depth-two B member costs at one of the s anchors, one of which, j, has already spent X slots in A. High B vertices have depth at most three by the high-pair diameter (the singleton low side was already excluded by its root/anchor order). We get Y≤(s+1)k−X, violating h>(s+1)k.

In the all-private case the s high representatives have reserves s−1 each. Both the high B port count and the total port/nonport root count in private-five-cut.md therefore have capacity s(k−s+1) rather than sk. It follows that Y≤sk−s(s−1)+X/s+1≤sk−s. This also excludes the high A nonport alternative.

For a singleton residual-support family with c common labels and d'=s−c, the representative quotas and individual D-only B contact class cap both decrease by the reserve d'−1. Thus Y≤sk−d'(d'−1)+X/d'+c+1. If 1≤c≤3, X≤k and d'≥d, so the stated d test gives Y≤sk−s. In the co-singleton case the d' representatives each spend one own/anchor slot. The bound of five-cut-large-degree.md sharpens to 2Y≤2sk−d'−(d'−3)P, where P is the number of high B ports, for d'≥3. Here P≥s−1 by the s-matching on labels in B. For c≤3, s≥8 gives d'+(d'−3)(s−1)≥2s. Thus every minimum cut has c≥4.

If no common label had a high private B contact, the high full-root case gives Y≤k+(s+d'+1)k/2≤sk−k/2; the no-full case gives Y≤sk−(c−2)k/2+d'(c+1)/2. Both are at most sk−s by c≥4 and k>6(s−1). The high private B port at a common label sees k−s+1 A vertices except possibly when X=k−s with the low in A and all high A vertices full. In that exceptional case let T be the low A vertex's labels. If no B label in T has a private contact (all B members are high), every high B depth-two vertex meeting T in its nearest set has at least two nearest labels, while every depth-two vertex missing T is at distance four from the low A vertex (every label is at distance at most two from it). There are then at most (s+1)k/2 B depth-two vertices, contradicting Y>sk−s after the full port pays for odd-depth B. This works also with T empty. A suitable B private port is thus always available. Its neighborhood gives a second minimum cut as before; more than (c−1)k−2s>sk/(s−2) opposite high vertices remain, while the chosen high member has only one neighbor on its new cut, contradicting c≥4.

## Exact tolerance when Y may fall below sk−s

For a fixed (n,k,s) let Xcap be any upper bound on the smaller high population and eps any integer with Y≥sk−eps. Suppose eps<k and s≥8. The following improvements are implemented in finite_scalar.py, which uses the exact product inequality to obtain Xcap and eps. The first-step high-label requirement can be relaxed to n>sk+s−1.

First consider a prospective support-difference witness with a high first port on T and another A port, possibly low, touching ≥2 other labels. Select q≥2 A representatives covering all S (including those two), and root at the r labels of T to pay for high B nonports whose exact-two label sets meet T. The remaining high B vertices are covered by those A roots. Since Y>(s−1)k, necessarily q+r=s. At each i∈T put Z_i equal to the high B nonports at distance two from i and let H_i be its members exclusive to this one T label. The deficit rk−Σ|Z_i| plus the sum of repeated Z_i incidences is at most eps: even the union of all Z_i and all q A even quotas must cover Y. Since a member counted m≥2 times costs m≤2(m−1) exclusive failures, Σ_i(k−|H_i|)≤2eps. Call an i good if it has a B private port. If i is not good all H_i members must also be distance two from an outside-T label (a sole-nearest nonport would give a private predecessor). Distinct H_i are disjoint. Using all q outside labels' quotas, the number of nongood indices in T is at most q+floor(2eps/k). Thus at least M=max(0,s−2q−floor(2eps/k)) indices are good. Every ordered pair of distinct good labels inside T supplies an additional anchor credit beyond the Z_i: a nonedge between them is at distance two via the first A port, while on an edge the private B contact of the first label is at distance two from the second. These M(M−1) anchor credits are independent of A-root boundary/Hall credits s(q−1). Therefore a support-difference witness requires s(q−1)+M(M−1)≤eps for some 2≤q<s. If eps<0 it already fails the ordinary s-root bound.

A high A nonport whose nearest labels have a cover by at most s−1 A ports also gives a contradiction at eps<s(s−1): at most s−2 ports and the root use fewer than s quotas, whereas at exactly s−1 ports the Hall/own credits s(s−2) on the ports and s from the nonport suffice. Thus such a high nonport forces every A port to be private.

If a label only touches a low A member z, the codegree of its edge to z is at least s−2 by n>sk+s−1. In particular z touches at least s−1 labels. There can be no high A nonport, which would force even z private. The directional difference bound using z as the second representative now ensures every high A support contains supp(z) except for the label j that they all miss. Hence the high A members share at least s−2 labels. Further P_B≤2k (z and one representative at its possible omitted label cover all B ports); there are no high B depth-three vertices because X≥k−s+1 as every high A member misses j. Such a depth-three high vertex would see X high A and a disjoint second layer of ≥s. Without any B private port at a common label, singletons among B nonports can use just the ≤2 other labels; every other nonport has ≥2 nearest. Thus Y≤(s+6)k/2. Provided this lies below sk−eps, the required private B port exists, is high because the low is in A, and supplies the same second cut. In particular the absence of a high contact does not create a lower-order gap when applying the common-label conclusion uniformly.

For the other branches take d=s−3. The scalar test uses strict thresholds corresponding to the non-strict Y≥sk−eps. In the private case require s(s−1)>eps+Xcap/s+1. For the 1≤c≤3 singleton case require d(d−1)>eps+k/d+4, and for the c≤3 co-singleton case require d+(d−3)(s−1)>2eps. These suffice by the same bounds above. For c≥4, k>2eps and 2k>2eps+5(s−4) suffice to force a high private B common-label contact: the latter inequality implies (c−2)k>2eps+(s−c)(c+1) at every c≥4 because the difference increases with c when k>s. The possible X=k−s case is compensated as before whenever (s−3)k>2eps; the low-only-label branch uses the stricter (s−6)k>2eps. Finally 3k−eps−s>Xcap guarantees W is the smaller side by the count of high B nonports outside its boundary (the low-only-label branch is stronger). Thus all branches contradict the common-label statement at the second cut.

## Three common labels, seven anchors, and the last finite range

A good i in T supplies one distinct anchor credit at EVERY other j in T, as the receiving anchor does not need a B private contact. Thus the anchor debit strengthens to M(s−q−1). The low singleton alternative at any minimum cut is already excluded by root/nontrivial-cut-sides-and-exterior-cycle.md, independently of the order being tested.

For s≥7 it suffices to force c≥3. Once at least one common label is present, X≤k and the usable deficit becomes E=min(eps,(s+1)k−h). For c=1,2 separately use d=s−c and test d(d−1)>E+k/d+c+1 for singleton supports or d+(d−3)(s−1)>2E for co-singletons. At c=0 the co-singleton test instead uses d=s and eps. The private test also uses eps, as before.

Any high B nonport singly nearest a common i has a HIGH B private predecessor on i. More strongly it has at least s such private predecessors, before deleting the possible single low. Choose one private contact v of i adjacent to this nonport y, and any opposite high common-label contact x in A. The geodesic x,i,v,y has |N(x)∩N(v)|=1 since v is private and the sides are separated. The checked geodesic-width theorem gives |N(y)∩N(i)|≥s. Every member of this latter intersection is in B, touches i and no other label, since y is a nonport uniquely nearest i. Hence the one exceptional low-predecessor label previously allowed is unnecessary.

In particular for c≥3 and no high private B port at a common label, a full high A root gives Y≤k+(s+d)k/2≤sk−k/2. Without a full high root only the residual singleton family remains, unless a co-singleton family with d≥3 survived its own count at a positive E. In that latter case use its own stronger bound to exclude it or leave that case unresolved. In the s=7 terminal application E<0, so every d≥3 co-singleton family is already excluded. For general s and E≥0 one may instead directly apply the no-full co-singleton formula below. In the singleton case one can retain the previously conservative bound Y≤sk−(c−2)k/2+d(c+1)/2. At c≥3 it is strictly below sk−E when k>2E+4(s−3), since the inequality improves with c. With a full high root k>2E suffices by the strengthened singleton-predecessor count. For a no-full co-singleton family with d≥3, the d representative count gives 2(P_C+P_D+G)≤2dk−(d−2)P_C−(d−3)P_D; the H count of nonports with a common exact-two label satisfies 2H≤ck+Q_H. The residual-anchor budgets give Q_H≤dk−2G+G_1+2G_0, but a simpler bound suffices in our finite certificate: this family is excluded whenever d+(d−3)(s−1)>2E. If not, check it separately. Finally 2k−E−s>Xcap guarantees the orientation of the second cut at c≥3.

**Frozen terminal test.** On all remaining 33 integer (s,k) families from sep/cover/point_7_1.tsv, s=7, 71≤k≤113 and n≥9k−55. Thus h≥9k−63, E≤63−k≤−8. At the product upper Xcap the integer deficit eps=7k−h+Xcap is at most four (see terminal-seven.tsv for the individual certified Xcap). The nontrivial-difference test passes already from q≥2 and s(q−1)≥7>eps; high-nonport and all-private cases are excluded as above. The c=0 co-singleton family uses d=7 and has deficit at least (7+4·6)/2>4. For c=1,2 the singleton d≥5 bound gives Y≤7k−20+k/5+3<7k+k−63 for k≥71; any co-singleton with d≥3 gives Y≤7k. Thus every minimum cut has at least three common labels. Since Y≥7k+k−63>7k on these common cuts, the existing high-private common-label and second-minimum-cut proof of three-common-labels.md applies outright. A missing high A contact at a label gives ≥5 common high labels by the low-only lemma above; every numeric bound used in that branch holds on these rows. This excludes every one of the 33 terminal degree/order families, including all listed orders beyond the lower endpoint.
