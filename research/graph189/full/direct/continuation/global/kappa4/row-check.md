# The four-cut population row (31, 38, 124)

Consider a minimum four-cut in a qualifying graph. The second-smallest degree is 31 and the oriented high side populations are 38 and 124. This check treats nonsaturated contact counts on both sides, with at most 29 high contacts per label on the larger side. Write `a_i` for the four high contact counts on the smaller side.

For a high port record the subset of labels it touches. For a high nonport record the subset at distance two. The latter subset is nonempty, since otherwise it would see every opposite high vertex at exact distance four and exceed the even quota. At every active label of a nonport's mask, the middle vertex on a two-step path is a port on the same side, and its support lies in the nonport mask. The encoding allows one optional low port on either side. It leaves other locations of the possible low vertex unrestricted.

An opposite high port sharing a label with a high port has distance two. An opposite high nonport whose mask is disjoint from a high port has distance four, as does any opposite high nonport pair. The same disjoint-mask rule applies when the first vertex is the high nonport. These witnesses are distinct and spend the available opposite even quota `k−4+|T|`, where T is the first vertex's actual support. A low port is subject only to the distance-two witnesses in this rule.

Let `e_i` and `f_i` count high vertices at exact distance two from label i on each side. On a side with high population x, a high noncontact has at least k−3 internal neighbors. Whenever the high contact count is greater than `x−k+3`, it cannot avoid all high contacts, even allowing one extra low vertex on that side. Then all high noncontacts contribute to its exact distance-two count. Each label has `e_i+f_i≤k`. The encoded pair bound is

    xy ≤ (k−4)x + sum_i a_i + sum_i (a_i f_i + b_i e_i).

The first two terms are the total checked cross-even debit. Every odd high cross pair is at distance three, and a shortest path uses some anchor at distances one and two from the two endpoints. This yields the two product contributions per label. The products use finite integer coefficient bounds, so the formula is quantifier-free linear integer arithmetic.

The three enumerated cases have zero, one or two labels with `a_i=30`. Each formula returned `unsat` in the existing libz3 exact integer replay. At exactly three such labels, the opposite ports touching any of them each have at least three labels by the boundary debit. At most 29 high ports avoid all three. Summing the four contact counts gives at most `(116+2·29)/3=58` high ports on the larger side, so at least 66 high nonports remain. A nonport with just one label at distance two cannot use a label of count 30. Only the fourth label may supply up to 31 such nonports. This requires at least 101 incidences, while the eight small-side distance-two witnesses at each of the three labels leave only 100. At all four `a_i=30` the smaller side has a high port touching all four labels, since `120>3·38`. This bounds the large-side high ports by 31, while all its at least 93 high nonports must use at least two labels. That too exceeds the total quota.
