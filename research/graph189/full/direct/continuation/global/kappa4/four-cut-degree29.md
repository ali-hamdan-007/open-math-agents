# Four-cut degree 29

In a putative counterexample at connectivity four and $k=29$, the Hamiltonian minimum-deletion alternative is kernel-excluded. The low-on-cut paper bound rules out a low vertex on any minimum cut. The linked completion lower bound and the four-cut upper bound leave $155\le n\le163$, with all separator vertices high. At each oriented minimum separator, the one-common-label argument and strict support covers of four-cut-degree30.md apply at $y\ge4k-1$. The checked debited product then leaves nine population rows, representing seven distinct pairs $(x,y)$. They are the input to check_degree29_masks.py.

## Direct contact bounds for the seven distinct pairs

There is no saturated large-side label. The stronger saturation bound $y\le9k-4x-4$ fails in every row. A saturated label on each side would force universal opposite contacts, hence at most $k$ high ports on each side. With $x>k$, the high small-side nonport bounds the large-side nonports by $k-4$, contrary to $y>2k-4$. This justifies the extra four places in the saturation bound. There cannot be two saturated small-side labels either. Their large-side contact sets are identical and consist of universal contacts, so their high contact set is already contained in both remaining high contact sets. The large side then has at most $2(k-1)$ high ports and at most $k-4$ high nonports, below every listed $y$.

If a small-side label $i$ saturates, its universal large-side contacts imply a high small-side nonport and then at least $y-(k-4)$ high large-side ports. Each of the $b_i$ high contacts at $i$ counts on all four labels, so $y-(k-4)\le4(k-1)-3b_i$. Thus $b_i\le9$ throughout and $b_i\le7$ at $(x,y)=(36,114)$. Also $a_i^{(2)}=x-k$ by the small-side degree bound. The even debit and the other three labels already save at least $4x$ in the product. The additional loss $(k-b_i)(x-k)$ on the saturated label exceeds the raw slack in every row with $x\ge37$.

At $(x,y)=(36,114)$ the raw slack is 304. With saturation on the small side, the savings just proved are $4\cdot36+(29-7)\cdot7=298$. To fit within the raw slack, the sum of its four direct label counts must be at least 81. Indeed the estimate of the even debit already used the upper bound $3k=87$ on that sum. Hence the three nonsaturated counts sum to at least 52. No nonsaturated label $j$ can have $a_j=28$. Its large-side contacts would each carry at least three labels by boundary debit, so the at least 89 high large-side ports would give $89\le112-2b_j$ and $b_j\le11$. Its $a_j^{(2)}=8$ then loses another $(28-b_j)\cdot8\ge136$ in the odd charge. All three nonsaturated $a_j\le27$. Every corresponding $b_j$ must equal 28, since any smaller one would lose another $k>6$ in the odd charge. Some nonsaturated label then has $8\le a_j\le27$, because their sum is at least 52. At that label the extra loss beyond the baseline 29 is at least

$$(28-a_j)(k-a_j^{(2)})\ge (28-a_j)(a_j-7)\ge20,$$

using $a_j^{(2)}\le x-a_j$. This exceeds the slack of six and rules out all saturation.

In fact every $b_i\le27$. Suppose $b_i=28$. An opposite contact touches at least three labels. If exactly three, the actual whole contact sets of those labels are contained in the 28 high contacts of $i$ by the boundary debit at that port. The other label has at most 28 high contacts, so there are at most 56 high ports. All high nonports use the distance-two quota of either $i$ or the omitted label. Both quotas already pay at least eight high small-side distance-two vertices. To see this, every high small-side vertex is a port because at least $y-56>k-4$ high large-side nonports remain. If $a_j\ge x-k+4$, every missing high small-side vertex has an internal neighbor among the $a_j$ contacts and their number is at least $x-a_j\ge8$. If $0<a_j\le x-k+3$, a high contact at $j$ has at least $k-4-a_j\ge 2k-x-7\ge10$ high internal neighbors missing $j$. If $a_j=0$, the edge to the low-only small-side contact has at least three common neighbors on the separator by the edge-order inequality, so every high small-side port sees $j$ at distance two. Thus $y\le56+2k-16=98$, impossible.

If the opposite port is universal, let $P$ be the number of high large-side ports. Then $28\le P\le29$ and every high small-side vertex is a port. The universal port sees all $P$ high large-side ports at distance two. At most $k-P$ high small-side ports other than itself fail to be adjacent to it, since they share a label with it. Consequently each anchor has at least $x-a_j-(k-P)\ge x-(k-1)-(k-P)$ high small-side vertices at distance two. All high large-side nonports have at least one anchor at distance two, because otherwise its distances to the $x>k$ high opposite vertices would all be four. Summing the four quotas gives $y\le8k-4(x-k)-4-3P$. This already fails in all rows except possibly $(x,y,P)=(36,114,28)$.

In that last case there are 86 high large-side nonports and their total label incidence budget is at most 88. Thus at least $2\cdot86-88=84$ of them must have a single nearest label. This label $j$ has a private large-side port, so $a_j\le k-3=26$. Its small-side charge is at least $36-26-1=9$, leaving at most 20 places for high large-side vertices at two. At most 80 high nonports can have a single nearest label, a contradiction. This proves $a_i\le28$ and $b_i\le27$ in all seven pairs.

## Exact mask calculation

For each side, classify a high port by its nonempty subset of direct labels and a high nonport by its nonempty subset of labels at distance two. A high nonport cannot have an empty distance-two mask, because every high vertex on the opposite side would then be at distance four, and both high populations exceed $k$. There may be one low port among the two sides. The high counts at a label and the two possible low-contact flags pay the original degree of at least $k$, except for the three possible edges within the separator.

The model keeps the exact distance-two incidence counts at each label. They include every nonport whose mask contains the label and can otherwise contain only ports missing the label. A large direct contact count forces all high same-side noncontacts to be at distance two. A positive small direct contact count also forces at least $k-4-a_i$ such neighbors through a high representative on that side. A low-only contact forces the low port to be universal by the three-common-neighbor edge-order argument, yielding distance two to all high same-side ports. For an actual nonport, each label at distance two has a middle port whose support is contained in its nearest-label mask. The original degree $k$ requires at least $k$ counted high within-side possible neighbors when allowing the one low neighbor to offset counting the nonport itself.

Across the cut, ports sharing a label are at distance two. A port and a high nonport whose respective masks are disjoint are at distance four, and two opposite high nonports are always at distance four. For a high port the checked boundary debit reserves $4-$support size even places. For a high nonport, an actual distance-four partner forces at least four vertices in its second layer, by minimum connectivity. These give the corresponding port and nonport bounds. At every anchor both sides' exact-distance-two counts together are at most $k$. The checked Graph189DebitedCutProduct.with_columns yields the exact even-plus-odd rectangle inequality in both orientations. Each product of counts is encoded by a bounded integer multiplier selected through its exact integer value.

All 35 systems, split over the five possible numbers of maximum small-side labels for each of the seven population pairs, gave exact UNSAT responses in Z3 QF_LIA. The planted positive control uses two disjoint 26-cliques each adjacent to all four independent separator vertices. Its profile gives SAT. This is a computational paper result for degree 29.
