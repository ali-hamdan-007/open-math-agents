# Four-cut degree 31

Assume the original Graph 189 hypotheses, connectivity four and \(k=31\). The verified minimum-deletion bounds and original order cutoff bound every putative counterexample by \(167=6k-19\le n\le6k-11=175\). At an oriented minimum cut let \(x\le y\) be the high populations and \(a_i,b_i\) the two sides' high port counts at label \(i\). A high vertex has degree at least \(k\). This note uses the strengthened product and the saturation bounds from four-cut-strict-product.md.

## The population rows

The original two-common-label inequalities, the strict product and the plain even reserve leave nine integer rows. Two have \((x,y)=(42,120),(43,119)\). In every row \(y>9k-4x-4\), so large-side saturation is excluded by the stronger saturation bound. Here \(y>4k-7\) throughout, so the small side has at most one saturated label. Indeed two such labels make their large-side contact sets identical, bounding the high large-side port count by \(3(k-1)\) since no large-side label saturates. A universal large-side port forces a high small-side nonport, so there are at most \(k-4\) high large-side nonports. This would give \(y\le4k-7\). The strengthened product therefore saves at least \(4x\), ruling out \((42,120),(43,119)\).

The surviving ordered pairs are \((38,124),(39,123),(39,124),(40,122),(40,123)\). The largest raw product slacks at these three small-side sizes are respectively 310, 256 and 204.

## The equality row \(x=38,y=4k\)

A common label for all high small-side vertices is impossible because any opposite contact would see all \(x>k\) of them at exact distance two. We apply the existing support classification and improve its strict order thresholds at equality.

Every label has a high small-side contact. Suppose the only small-side contact of a label \(i\) is the low vertex \(z\). The known edge-order inequality, applied to \(zi\) with \(i\) high and \(n>5k+4\), gives at least three common neighbors. They all lie in the separator, so \(i\) is adjacent to every other label. There is a high small-side port \(x_0\) because deleting \(z\) preserves connectedness and the small side has another vertex. It touches a label \(j\ne i\), and its edge to \(j\) decreases the distances to both \(i\) and \(j\). The existing first-step separator cover bounds the large side using \(x_0\) and at most three first-step roots. But \(x_0\) is missing label \(i\), so its own quota on the large side is at most \(k-1\) by the boundary debit. Thus \(y\le4k-1\), contradictory.

Any two high small-side supports differ by at most one label in either direction. In the proof of Graph189PortDifference.one_new_label, if a support has two new labels relative to the support of \(x_0\), the four-root cover includes \(x_0\). Missing at least those two labels makes its own even large-side quota at most \(k-2\), so \(y\le4k-2\). This proves the required set-difference hypothesis. The support classification then gives either singleton supports on the residual labels, or co-singleton supports on the labels attained by high vertices. All four labels are attained and none is common. In the co-singleton case choose a representative missing each label. The existing double cover of the high large-side population uses four anchor quotas and four representative quotas, with every target covered at least twice. Each representative misses a label, cutting its budget by one. Hence \(2y\le8k-4\), contradictory. In the singleton case the checked private-port quotient gives \(4(x+y)\le4(4k-12+x+1)+x\), but here \(648>642\).

## The rows with \(x=39,40\)

There is no small-side saturation either. A saturated small-side label \(i\) yields a universal large-side contact and at least \(y-(k-4)\) high large-side ports. Its \(b_i\) high contacts are all universal, so \(y-(k-4)\le4(k-1)-3b_i\) forces \(b_i\le8\). The small side has order at most \(x+1\), so its high noncontacts at \(i\) all see \(i\) at distance two by their internal degree, giving \(a_i^{(2)}=x-k\ge8\). The three unsaturated labels together with the even debit already save at least \(4x\), and the saturated label saves another \((k-b_i)a_i^{(2)}\ge23\cdot8\). This exceeds either available slack.

We claim every \(b_i\le29\). If some \(b_i=30\), an opposite contact touches at least three labels. If it is universal, the earlier four-anchor counting argument gives \(y\le5k-4(x-k)-1\), below either remaining order. If it has exactly three labels, all large-side contacts of those labels lie in the high 30-element contact set of \(i\), because the boundary debit gives exactly 30 even slots. The high large-side nonports then use two anchor quotas, at \(i\) and the omitted label. Every such nonport sees some anchor at distance two, since otherwise it sees all \(x>k\) high small-side vertices at distance four. There are at most 60 high large-side ports, giving \(y\le122\). At equality \(x=40,y=122\), both chosen anchor quotas would be full of large-side nonports. There are at least 62 high large-side nonports, so every high small-side vertex is a port by the opposite nonport bound. In particular no high small-side vertex could have distance two from anchor \(i\). If \(i\) has a high small-side contact, the absence of small-side distance-two vertices forces every internal neighbor of that contact to touch \(i\) or be low. This requires \(a_i\ge k-4\), and every high small-side noncontact to \(i\) must then see it at distance two by the internal degree bound. If \(i\)'s only small-side contact is low, the edge-order argument from the equality row makes \(i\) adjacent to all other labels. Since all high small-side vertices are ports in this branch, they also see \(i\) at distance two if they miss it. This excludes equality and proves \(b_i\le29\).

Let \(j\) be the number of small-side labels with \(a_i=30\). The base saving is \(4x+4\). A label with \(a_i\le29\) saves at least another \(k\) in the odd charge. A label with \(a_i=30\) saves at least another \(x-30\), since \(b_i\le29\) and all \(x-30\) missing high small-side vertices see it at distance two. If \(x=40\) and \(j\le3\), these extras exceed slack. If \(j=4\), every high large-side port has at least three labels, so at most \(\lfloor116/3\rfloor=38\) are ports. All high nonports have two nearest labels because a sole nearest label would force \(a_i\le k-3=28\) by the private port along its length-two route. The four label quotas cannot cover these high nonports twice.

If \(x=39\), the extra saving closes \(j\le1\). The case \(j=4\) has the same proof as for \(x=40\). If \(j=3\), at most 29 high large-side ports avoid all three labels and the others have at least three labels. The total port count is at most \(29+(116-29)/3=58\). Thus at least 65 high large-side nonports require at least \(2\cdot65-31=99\) incidences, with at most one label possibly solely nearest. The three near-full small-side contact sets consume 27 anchor places, so the capacity is at most 97.

Finally take \(x=39,j=2\). At most 58 high large-side ports avoid the two near-full labels, and the others use at least three. Thus the total port count is at most \(58+\lfloor(116-58)/3\rfloor=77\), leaving at least 46 high nonports. If both other labels have \(a_i=29\), no high nonport can have a sole nearest label and the demand of at least 92 exceeds the capacity \(124-2\cdot9-2\cdot10=86\). Otherwise let a weak label be one with \(a_i\le28\). The even reserve is at least 39, the two near-full labels save at least 40 each in the odd charge, and the other two save at least 62 each. This spends at least 243 of the raw 256. A weak label must have \(b_i=29\), as a further \(k\) loss would be impossible. Its distance-two large-side incidences save at least one additional unit each, so there are at most 13 such incidences at weak labels. If both labels are weak, at least \(46-13=33\) high nonports avoid both and must use two incidences each, exceeding the combined capacity 44 of the two near-full labels. If exactly one label is weak, any large-side port touching the remaining \(a_i=29\) label has at least two labels and, if it avoids both near-full ones, must touch the weak label. Thus there are at most 29 high large-side ports avoiding both near-full labels, improving the total port bound to 58. There are then at least 65 high large-side nonports, at most 13 of which can have a sole nearest label. Their demand of at least \(2\cdot65-13=117\) exceeds the capacity 96 from the three nonweak small-side counts. This excludes the last row.

The equality row independently passes a separate exact integer mask relaxation. This note is a paper argument for the full degree-31 extension. The local Lean all-order theorem currently starts at degree 37.
