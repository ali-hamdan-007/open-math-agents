# Four-cut degree 30

Assume the original Graph 189 hypotheses, connectivity four and $k=30$. The kernel-checked minimum-deletion and four-cut bounds force $161\le n\le169$ in a counterexample. Use $x,y,a_i,b_i$ as in four-cut-strict-product.md. Write $a_i^{(2)},b_i^{(2)}$ for the high populations at exact distance two from anchor $i$ on the respective sides.

## Large-side populations at least 119

For $y\ge119$, the exact arithmetic replay places every row either in the original numerical range or in the enlarged range with $4(4k-11+x)+x<4(x+y)$ and $x+y\ge156$. The following two strictness arguments extend the four-cut geometry to the enlarged range.

**At most one common label.** The ordinary proof already excludes zero common labels here whenever the supports are either all singleton or all co-singleton on the residual labels, with the strict versions of the classification proved below. For exactly one common label and $y>4k$, the residual singleton quota is $3(x+y)\le3(5k-4)+x$. The common label implies $x\le k$ by any opposite contact, and $x+y\ge156$, so equality and $x=30,y=126$ are necessary.

Use the intermediate quantities of Graph189PrivateQuota.residual_singleton_quota. Let $P_C$ be the high large-side ports touching the common label, $P_D$ the remaining high large-side ports, $H_N$ the high large-side nonports at distance two from the common label, and $G_N$ the remaining high nonports. At each of the three residual labels $i$, write $Q_i\subseteq P_D$ for its direct contacts and $Z_i\subseteq G_N$ for its distance-two vertices. Write $r=k-2=28$. Equality in the quota forces $|P_D|=3r=84$ and $|H_N|=k=30$, since both estimates have positive coefficients in the final inequality. The bound $|P_D|\le\sum_i|Q_i|\le3r$ gives $|Q_i|=r$ for every residual label. Consequently $|P_C|+|G_N|=12$. The pointwise row inequalities $|P_C|+|Q_i|+|G_N|\le r+|Z_i|$ give $|Z_i|\ge12$, so $P_C$ is empty, $|G_N|=12$ and $Z_i=G_N$. On the other hand every large-side contact to the common label is universal. It sees all $x=k$ high small-side vertices at distance two, so boundary debit forces four direct anchor neighbors. Every two-step route from a member of $H_N$ to the common label has its middle vertex on the large side, hence via such a universal contact. Every residual anchor thus sees all 30 members of $H_N$ and all 12 members of $G_N$ at distance two, impossible.

**Equality and near equality of the four-root cover.** For $y=119$ or $120$, we have $x\ge36>k$ by $x+y\ge156$. A common label is already impossible. We show every label has a high small-side port. Suppose label $i$ has only a low small-side contact $z$. By the edge-order bound at order $n>5k+4$, the edge $zi$ has at least three common neighbors, all on the separator, so $i$ sees every other anchor. A high small-side port missing another label besides $i$ has a first step toward one of its labels that shortens both the route to that label and the route to $i$. The first-step cover uses at most four roots including that port. Its at least two missing labels spend two places outside the large side by boundary debit, giving $y\le4k-2$, contrary to $y\ge119$. If every high small-side port touches all labels other than $i$, there must be a high nonport because $x>k$ forbids a common label. A path in $G-z$ from that nonport to the separator has an edge from a high nonport to a high port in the small side, at the first port encountered. That edge shortens the distances to three labels at once. The first-step cover now uses at most three roots, giving $y\le3k$. This proves the high-contact claim.

If two high small-side supports differ by at least two labels in one direction, the existing four-root support-difference cover includes a root missing those two labels. It spends two places outside the large side, giving $y\le4k-2$. Thus the support classification applies at $y=119,120$. All four labels are attained and none is common, so the co-singleton branch has four representatives each missing one label. Its twofold cover pays at least twice for every high large-side vertex, and each of these four representatives has one even place spent outside the large side. Hence $2y\le8k-4$, again impossible. In the singleton branch, the usual private-port quota contradicts the strict numerical inequality in the enlarged range. Thus no row with $y=119,120$ can occur.

At $y>4k$, the original support classification and high-label lemma already apply. The strict private quota and the one-common argument above force two common labels. For the large-order cut proof, all these assertions hold for every oriented minimum cut. Therefore the original private-port recut completes that proof verbatim once the smaller population rows are excluded.

## The smaller large-side populations

After the strict product and the range just proved, the only remaining rows are $(x,y)=(38,118),(39,117),(40,116),(39,118)$ at $n=161$ and $(39,118)$ at $n=162$. Their largest raw product slacks at each $x=38,39,40$ are 256, 207 and 160.

There is no large-side saturation since $y>9k-4x-4$. Since $y>4k-7$, at most one small-side label saturates by the saturated-class intersection argument at degree 31. If a small-side label $i$ does saturate, all $b_i$ high large-side contacts of $i$ are universal. There are at least $y-(k-4)$ high large-side ports because a high small-side nonport exists. Thus $y-(k-4)\le4(k-1)-3b_i$, forcing $b_i\le8$. Also $a_i^{(2)}=x-k\ge8$ by the internal degree argument. There are at least $4x$ savings from the other three labels and the even debit. The further odd savings of $(k-b_i)a_i^{(2)}\ge176$ exclude each row.

With no saturation, suppose some $b_i=29$. An opposite contact touches at least three labels. If universal, the earlier universal-port argument gives $y\le5k-4(x-k)-1$, below every remaining row. If it touches exactly three labels, their actual large-side contacts all lie in the high 29-element contact set of $i$, by the boundary debit. There are at most 58 high large-side ports and the high nonports all see one of two anchors at distance two, namely $i$ or the omitted label. The latter follows because every high large-side nonport has some anchor at distance two and the three supported labels have contact sets contained in that of $i$. Since $y\ge116$, more than $k-4$ high large-side nonports remain, so every high small-side vertex is a port.

Each of these two anchor budgets has at least nine high small-side vertices at distance two. Indeed, for an anchor $j$ with $a_j\ge x-k+4$, every high small-side noncontact has a neighbor in its $a_j$ contacts, because the side has order at most $x+1$ and the noncontact has at least $k-3$ internal neighbors. Thus it has at least $x-a_j\ge9$ high small-side vertices at distance two. If $1\le a_j\le x-k+3$, a high contact of $j$ has at least $k-4-a_j\ge2k-x-7\ge13$ high internal neighbors missing $j$, all at distance two. If $a_j=0$, the unique low small-side contact makes $j$ adjacent to every other anchor by the edge-order argument, and all high small-side ports then see it at two. It follows that the two anchor quotas pay at most $2k-18$ large-side nonports, giving $y\le100$, impossible. Thus every $b_i\le28$.

The base saving is $4x+4$, with an extra $k$ in the odd charge for each label with $a_i\le28$, and at least $x-29$ for each label with $a_i=29$. For the latter, $a_i^{(2)}=x-29$ by internal degree. This immediately excludes $x=40$. For $x=39$ it excludes all configurations except when every $a_i=29$. In that case every high large-side port touches at least three labels, giving at most $\lfloor112/3\rfloor=37$ ports. No high nonport has a sole nearest label because the corresponding private port would force $a_i\le k-3=27$. The four anchor quotas cannot pay all these high nonports twice.

At $x=38$, let $j$ be the number of labels with $a_i=29$. If $j\le1$, the extra loss is at least $3k+(x-29)+3=102$ when $j=1$, including the three additional even places from $a_i\le28$. This exceeds the remaining slack of 100, and the case $j=0$ saves more. The case $j=4$ is excluded as above. If $j=3$, at most 28 high large-side ports avoid the three labels, while each other high port has at least three labels. Thus the total high port count is at most $28+(112-28)/3=56$, leaving at least 62 high nonports. At most 30 can have a sole nearest label, as there is only one possible such label. This demands at least 94 anchor incidences, but the three near-full small-side labels consume 27 places, leaving at most 93.

Finally let $x=38,j=2$. The high large-side ports avoiding both near-full labels number at most 56, while each other port has at least three labels. The total high port count is at most $56+\lfloor(112-56)/3\rfloor=74$, leaving at least 44 high nonports. If both other labels have $a_i=28$, no nonport can have a sole nearest label and at least 88 incidences are needed, whereas the capacity is at most $120-2\cdot9-2\cdot10=82$. Otherwise call a label weak if $a_i\le27$. The even reserve is at least 39, the two near-full labels save at least 39 each in the odd charge, and the other two save at least 60 each, totaling 237 of the raw 256. Every weak label must have $b_i=28$ because another loss of $k$ would be impossible. Its large-side distance-two incidences each save one more in the odd charge. Thus there are at most 19 incidences at weak labels. If both other labels are weak, at least $44-19=25$ high nonports avoid both and must use the two near-full labels, whose total capacity is 42. If only one label is weak, a large-side port at the other low-count label with $a_i=28$ must have at least two labels. If it avoids both near-full labels, it must then touch the weak one. Thus at most 28 high ports avoid the near-full labels, and the total high port count is at most 56. At least 62 high nonports remain, at most 19 of which can have a sole nearest label. They demand at least $124-19=105$ anchor incidences, beyond the capacity $120-(2\cdot9+10)=92$ of the four quotas once the three nonweak small-side counts are paid.
