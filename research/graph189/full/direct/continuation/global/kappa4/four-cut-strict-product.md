# Four-cut order cutoff at degrees 32 through 36

Let a minimum four-cut partition a qualifying graph into two sides whose high populations are $x\le y$. Write $k$ for the second-smallest degree. For label $i$ of the cut, let $a_i,b_i$ count the high contact vertices on the two sides. Minimum-cut contacts always exist on both sides, and $a_i,b_i\le k$ follows by taking the positive even-distance quota at any opposite contact. Call a label saturated on a side when its high contact count is $k$.

The ordinary cross-product proof bounds the even cross pairs by $kx$ and the odd cross pairs by $4k^2$. The even pair charge in Lean theorem Graph189BoundaryDebit.even_quota gives an improved bound of $kx-4x+\sum_i a_i$. At each label with $a_i,b_i<k$, its odd charge is at most $k(k-1)$, since the two exact-distance-two counts on both sides together are at most $k$.

Assume $x>k$, $y>4k-4$. Any contact opposite a saturated label has four labels by the same boundary debit. Saturation at two labels of the small side would therefore make their two large-side contact sets equal, and the large side would have at most $3k$ high ports. The universal large-side contact sees all high small-side ports at distance two, so the small side has at most $k$ high ports and therefore a high nonport. Each high large-side nonport is at distance four from it and there are at most $k-4$ such vertices by the compulsory second layer. This would give $y\le4k-4$, so at most one label is saturated on the small side.

If a large-side label saturates, some small-side contact $u$ is universal. The $k$ high contacts at that saturated label use up the even quota of $u$, so all large-side ports are these $k$. There are more than $k-4$ large-side high nonports, which precludes any high small-side nonport. Every high small-side port different from $u$ shares an anchor with it and thus must be adjacent to it, since the quota leaves no same-side distance-two vertices. Therefore each label $i$ has at least $x-a_i\ge x-k$ small-side high vertices at distance two. Every high nonport on the large side must have some anchor at distance two. Otherwise every high vertex on the small side is at exact distance four from it, by passage through the cut and the high-pair distance bound, and the compulsory second layer gives $x\le k-4$. Consequently the number of high large-side nonports is at most $4k-4(x-k)$. Including the $k$ ports gives $y\le9k-4x$.

Suppose now that $y>9k-4x$ as well, so the large side has no saturated label. If no small-side label saturates, all four labels save $k$ in the odd charge and $\sum_i a_i\le 4$k-1$$. The total saving over the ordinary bound is at least $4x+4$. If one small-side label saturates, every high small-side port has at most three labels. Indeed a universal large-side contact bounds the high small-side ports by $k$, so a high nonport exists there. A universal small-side port would also bound the large-side ports by $k$, contradicting $y>2k-4$. Hence $\sum_i a_i\le3k$, while three doubly unsaturated labels save $3k$ in the odd charge. The total saving is at least $4x$. Both cases give

```
xy + 4x <= kx + 4k^2.
```

For $k=34,35,36$ and $6k-19\le n\le6k-11$, the accompanying exhaustive integer replay lists every row for which the original two-common-label inequalities do not follow directly from the unsupplemented product bound. All listed rows satisfy $x>k$, $y>4k-4$, and $y>9k-4x$, and the strengthened product rejects them all. For $k=33$ it rejects all but the row $n=179,x=42,y=132$. The improved bound is valid for every oriented minimum cut, including the recut in the original proof. Therefore the existing recut argument and linked minimum-deletion lower bound give an all-order paper proof at connectivity four and $k\ge34$.

## The last row at degree 33

Here $x=42,y=132$, with raw product slack 198. The large side cannot have a saturated label by the preceding bound $y\le9k-4x=129$.

Suppose a small-side label $i$ is saturated. A large-side contact at that label is universal, bounding the small-side high port count by $k$. A high small-side nonport therefore bounds the number of large-side high nonports by $k-4$, so there are at least $3k+4=103$ high large-side ports. Every large-side contact of $i$ sees all four labels. The number of high large-side ports is accordingly at most $\sum_j b_j-3b_i\le128-3b_i$, forcing $b_i\le8$. Every high small-side vertex missing $i$ has a neighbor in the $a_i=33$ high contacts of $i$. It has at least $k-3$ neighbors in its own side of order at most $43$, and avoiding all 33 contacts would leave at most nine internal neighbors. Thus the small-side exact-distance-two count at $i$ is $42-33=9$. The odd pair charge at this label alone is at least $(33-8)\cdot9=225$ below its original allowance, exceeding the total available slack.

Now all $a_i,b_i\le32$, so the original product loses at least $4x+4=172$ by the no-saturation case above. A label with both counts at most 31 would save another $k=33$ in the odd charge, exceeding slack. Each label therefore has $a_i=32$ or $b_i=32$. Let $J$ be the labels with $a_i=32$, and let $I=S\setminus J$, whose labels have $a_i\le31,b_i=32$. At a label in $J$, every high small-side vertex missing it is at distance two by the same degree argument with 32 contacts, so the exact-distance-two counts are 10 on the small side and at most 23 on the large side. Each label with $b_i\le31$ is in $J$ and saves another 10 in the odd charge, beyond the $k$ already saved there. If at most one label has $b_i=32$ this saves at least 30, which suffices.

Otherwise choose a label with $b_i=32$. An opposite contact has at least three anchor neighbors by the boundary debit. The high ports on these labels count at most $k$ together in its even quota, and the fourth label contributes at most $k-1$. Thus there are at most 65 high large-side ports and at least 67 high large-side nonports. Every high large-side nonport sees an anchor at distance two. Otherwise it sees every high small-side vertex at exact distance four by passage through the cut and the high-pair distance bound, exceeding the cap because $x>k$. A high nonport with only one label at distance two has a private port on the corresponding length-two path, and that label's small-side high contact count is at most $k-3=30$ by the boundary debit. Thus any high nonport avoiding $I$ spends at least two incidences on $J$. If (I$ were empty, the total capacity (4\cdot23) of (J) could not pay all 67 twice. For nonempty (I), at least (67-\lfloor23|J|/2\rfloor\ge33) high nonports see some member of (I) at distance two. Each such incidence saves at least one more in the odd charge because its label has (a_i\le31). This exceeds the remaining slack.

## The four rows at degree 32

The initial strengthened product leaves only $n=173$, and $(x,y)=(40,128),(41,127),(44,124),(45,123)$. The raw slacks for these four rows are respectively 256, 201, 48 and 1. The even-only reserve of at least $4(x-k)$ rules out $(45,123)$. For $(44,124)$ equality would require every $a_i=k$. Any opposite contact would then be universal, so all $k$ high contacts at every small-side label are the same set and are universal too. Both sides then have at most $k$ high ports. As $x>k$, a high nonport on the small side would bound the large-side high nonports by $k-4$, contradicting $y>2k-4$.

For the remaining two rows there is no saturated label on the large side. In fact the argument giving $y\le9k-4x$ when a large-side label saturates has a further saving of four. There can be no saturated label on the small side in that case. Saturation on both sides would produce universal contacts of both sides and hence at most $k$ high ports per side, again impossible when $x>k$ and $y>2k-4$. Thus $a_i\le k-1$ and each label's small-side distance-two charge is at least $x-k+1$, giving $y\le9k-4x-4$. If the small side has a saturated label, the same proof as at degree 33 forces its opposite high contact count at most eight and its own exact-distance-two incidence count at least eight. The extra odd loss is at least $(32-8)\cdot8=192$, in addition to $4x$ already saved. This disposes of both rows with any saturation.

With no saturation, suppose some $b_i=31$. An opposite contact $u$ touches at least three labels. If it touches exactly three, all large-side contacts of these three labels together have cardinality at most 31 by the boundary debit at $u$. The large-side high contact set of $i$ already has cardinality 31, so all actual large-side contacts of the other two labels are in this set. The three respective distance-two layers on large-side nonports are therefore covered by the layer of $i$. Every high large-side nonport sees some label at distance two, since otherwise it would see all $x>k$ high small-side vertices at distance four. It follows that at most two label quotas cover all large-side high nonports. The large-side high ports number at most $31+31=62$, giving $y\le126$. If $u$ is universal, let $P$ be the high large-side port count, which is 31 or 32. Then the high large-side nonports number more than $k-4$, so all high small-side vertices are ports. At most $k-P$ of the high small-side ports can fail to be adjacent to $u$, apart from $u$ itself, since they share a label with $u$. A high small-side vertex missing label $j$ is at distance two from $j$ if it is adjacent to $u$. The four anchor budgets therefore pay at least $4(x-(k-1)-(k-P))$ for these small-side vertices, leaving $y\le5k-4(x-k)-1$. This again contradicts the two rows. We have shown $b_i\le30$ throughout.

For $x=41,y=127$, the no-saturation saving of $4x+4=168$ increases by at least 32 for every label with $a_i\le30$, and by at least 10 for every label with $a_i=31$. For the latter, $x-a_i=10$ high small-side noncontacts are at distance two by the internal degree argument, and the coefficient $b_i\le30$ saves an extra one for each. The minimum extra saving across four labels is 40, exceeding the raw 201.

For $x=40,y=128$, the base saving is 164. If at most one label has $a_i=31$, three labels give the extra saving $3\cdot32$, which is enough. If all four have $a_i=31$, their total incidence count 124 exceeds $3x=120$, so a high small-side contact is universal. This leaves at least 96 high large-side nonports, all of which are at distance two from at least two labels because their sole nearest label would have $a_i\le k-3=29$. The four label quotas cannot pay all of them twice.

If exactly three labels have $a_i=31$, any large-side port touching one of these labels has at least three labels by the boundary debit. At most 30 high large-side ports avoid all three, because they touch only the fourth. Summing the $b_i\le30$ gives at most 60 high large-side ports, hence at least 68 high nonports. Only the fourth label can be solely nearest to a high nonport, and it can pay at most 32 such vertices. At least $2\cdot68-32=104$ incidences are required, whereas the three near-full small-side contact sets cost $3\cdot9$ anchor places, leaving at most 101.

Finally let exactly two labels have $a_i=31$. At most 60 high large-side ports avoid both, and the others have at least three labels, so there are at most 80 high large-side ports and at least 48 high nonports. If the other two labels both have $a_i=30$, no high nonport has a sole nearest label. At least 96 incidences would be required, against a capacity of at most $128-2\cdot9-2\cdot10=90$. If at least one other label has $a_i\le29$, each such label must have $b_i=30$. A label with both counts at most 29 would exceed the remaining product slack. Indeed the even reserve is at least 39, the two near-full labels lose at least 41 each in the odd charge, and the other two lose at least 64 each. This totals 249 of the raw 256. Each actual distance-two large-side incidence at a label with $a_i\le29,b_i=30$ saves another unit, so at most seven such incidences are possible. If both remaining labels have $a_i\le29$, at least 41 high large-side nonports avoid them and must use both near-full labels, whose combined capacity is at most 46. If just one label has $a_i\le29$, a large-side port at the other remaining label, with $a_i=30$, must have at least two labels. Thus all large-side ports avoiding the two near-full labels touch the label with $a_i\le29$. At most 30 high ports avoid the near-full labels, so the earlier total port bound improves to 60. At least 68 high nonports would require at least $2\cdot68-7=129$ incidences, exceeding even the full four-anchor capacity.
