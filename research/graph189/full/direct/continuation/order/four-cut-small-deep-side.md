# An unrestricted large opposite side at a minimum four-cut

Let G be a qualifying Graph 189 graph with k≥16 and κ(G)=4. Suppose a minimum cut S leaves precisely two components A,B, each containing a high nonport. If |A|≤2k−4 then G is traceable, with arbitrary |B|, anchor geometry, maximum degree, and low-vertex placement. If B is wholly high the bound improves to |A|≤2k−3. In particular this covers the requested |A|≤2k−9 throughout. We use the established connectivity-four moderate-order theorem that every such instance of order n≤6k−11 is traceable.

Assume n≥6k−10. Opposite high nonports have distance four and hence every high nonport is at distance two from S. If three or fewer A ports together cover the nearest anchors of a high A nonport y, their positive-even sets cover opposite ports touching one of those nearest anchors. Every other high vertex of B has distance four from y, since a three-edge path would have its last edge from such an anchor. The second layer of y has at least four vertices since it separates y from the opposite high nonport; all these are outside B. An A port of support size r spends at least 4−r even slots outside B, since the external boundary of its closed A-neighborhood separates from B and every boundary member other than its own r anchors is at distance two from it in A∪S.

One or two ports in the cover immediately give |B_high|≤3k−4. For a minimal cover of size exactly three, each of its ports owns a distinct necessary anchor in the nearest set, which none of the other two ports touches. There is at most one other anchor, so each selected port has size one or two. If g of the three also touch the fourth anchor, the three ports together spend at least 9−g slots outside B. If g≥1, any B neighbor of that fourth anchor is counted g times by the three roots, so the distinct covered B vertices save at least g−1 further slots. This duplication count concerns all B vertices; if it involves a low vertex, either include it as covered or simply omit all its repeated incidences from the high-vertex bound. Therefore |B_high|≤4k−12. Since |B|=n−4−|A|≥4k−10 in the general size range, or ≥4k−11 when B is wholly high, this is impossible even allowing the single low in B. Thus every high A nonport has all four anchors nearest and every A port has singleton support, since a double or more together with at most two other ports covers all four. Every anchor touches each component by minimality.

Write a=|A|=k+ρ, d for the number of A nonports (including a possible low nonport), p_i for the private A contacts on anchor i and q_i for B contacts. The high A nonport has degree at least k entirely inside A, so ρ,d≥1. By the prior edge-order theorem, every edge has at least ceil((n−3k−2)/(k+1))≥3 common neighbors for k≥16, n≥6k−10. Applied to an A private port and its anchor, all the common neighbors belong to the same private A class. Hence each class has size at least four. In each select a high representative u_i; at most one vertex in the whole graph is low. Every high A vertex has internal degree ≥k−1 and any two nonadjacent high A vertices have a common internal neighbor since |A|≤2k−3.

Let t_i count other anchors at even distance two from u_i and T=Σt_i. All the q_i opposite i-ports have distance two from u_i. All high A nonneighbors of u_i apart from itself have distance two as well. If low z is in A, let L count anchors i for which z is not an i-port and d(u_i,z)>2; otherwise put L=0. At each anchor i, all A vertices outside its own contact set that neighbor u_i have distance two from the anchor. The preceding even budgets consequently deliver at least

    4a−Σp_i−4k+Σq_i+T−L

incidences from A in the four even anchor budgets. Each B nonport has a nearest anchor at even distance, except possibly a low vertex z at an odd depth. In that case an A representative on a nearest anchor has distance exactly that depth plus one from z by passage through S. This is an additional even slot at that representative beyond q_i, t_i and all A charges, paying for z. Using Σp_i=a−d, Σq_i≥the number of B ports and n=4+a+|B| now gives

    n≤8k+4−2a−d−T+L = 6k+4−2ρ−d−T+L.

It remains to prove 2ρ+d+T−L≥15. A crude L≤4 already gives 2ρ+d≤18 under the presumed lower bound on n, hence ρ≤8. When z∈A choose a high A-neighbor w of z (A is connected and contains a high nonport). Its internal closed neighborhood has size at least k and lies within distance two from z. At most ρ high A vertices are farther from z than two. In each anchor class with no z, all four or more members are high, so choosing the representatives close to z whenever possible gives L=0 at ρ≤3 and L≤1 at ρ≤7. A class containing z itself never contributes to L.

Every representative misses at most ρ other A vertices by its internal degree. For ρ≤3 it sees some vertex of each of the other three private classes, so T=12. Then 2ρ+d+T≥15. For 4≤ρ≤7 no representative can miss two entire other classes, so T≥8 and 2ρ+d+T−L≥16. For ρ=8 every representative sees at least one other class, so T≥4, and even L≤4 gives 2ρ+d+T−L≥17. In all cases the order is at most 6k−11 after all, proving the theorem.
