# A large side at a minimum four-cut

Let a qualifying graph have k≥16 and a minimum four-cut S leaving two components A,B, each with a high nonport. If b=|B|≥4k−10, it is traceable. When B is wholly high, b≥4k−11 suffices. The single low vertex can otherwise occupy any position. Together with four-cut-small-deep-side.md, a possible counterexample in this class needs each side strictly between 2k−4 and 4k−10 (and the sharper individual endpoints when the opposite or the named side is wholly high).

Write E(X,x) for vertices of X at positive even distance from x. Use the already established moderate-order result at n≤6k−11. Suppose n≥6k−10. The three-port cover argument in four-cut-small-deep-side.md shows that every A port is private (has a single anchor), and every high A nonport has all four anchors at distance two: otherwise |B_high|≤4k−12. Every private class in A contains a high vertex because at this order its edge to the anchor has at least three common neighbors, all other private ports in its own class.

There exists a high nonport y_B at distance two from all S. Otherwise choose a high A representative u_i of each private class i. The four representatives cover the B ports in their even sets, including the ports with several anchors. For each high B nonport x missing anchor i at distance two, a path from x to u_i of at most three edges would need to pass through i on its last edge (or from another anchor within two steps of x and another two from u_i), which is impossible. The high-pair diameter therefore gives distance exactly four. These representatives thus cover all high B vertices. Each spends ≥3 slots outside B by the private port boundary, so |B_high|≤4k−12, a contradiction. Fix any high nonport y_A and such a y_B. For each T=A,B put W_T=N_T[y_T]. Its order w_T is at least k+1. For each anchor i pick a port u_(T,i)∈W_T touching i. The same port may serve several distinct indices.

Every u=u_(A,i) sees at least two of the other three anchors at distance two. Each foreign anchor not seen at two has distance exactly three via y_A. If at least two are missed, take the first step i toward i, y_A toward all missed anchors, and, if needed, a neighbor v toward the sole foreign anchor j at distance two. These steps cover S: each lies on a shortest route to its specified anchors. The parity cover of root/fractional-separator-cover.md then covers B with the even sets at u and those neighbors. In the case of at most two distinct steps this gives b≤3k, impossible for k≥16. Otherwise v is not i and so is a private A port touching j, because u has i as its sole adjacent anchor. Outside B, u and v each spend at least three slots, y_A spends at least four, and i spends at least two, at y_A and v (both are at distance two via u). Hence b≤4k−12, again impossible.

For completeness, here is the window sum for four anchors. Write P_T for the number of ports in T, D_T for its nonports, D'_T for its nonports in W_T, p_(T,i) for the number of i-ports in T and p'_(T,i) for the number in W_T. Define the excess of a port to be its number of anchors minus one. All of W_T is within distance two from u_(T,i). The vertex u_(T,i) sees every one of the p_(T',i) opposite i-ports at distance two. Any further charges at that representative from an anchor or an opposite vertex not already counted may be included, with multiplicity across indices as usual. Denote these by c_(T,i). Among the w_T−p'_(T,i) window vertices not incident to i, all except at most k−p_(T',i)−c_(T,i) are neighbors of the representative, and so at distance two from i. Consequently the four anchor budgets count there at least
     
    3 w_T + D'_T − (excess of window ports in T) − 4k
        + P_T' + (excess of all ports in T') + Σ_i c_(T,i).

Each high nonport outside the windows has some anchor at distance two, since it has distance four to an opposite high nonport and both are nonports. Any outside low nonport at even depth from S similarly pays at a nearest anchor. For a low nonport z at odd depth, choose a nearest anchor i: u_(T',i) is then at even distance exactly dist(z,S)+1 across S. Include this particular representative charge to pay for the vertex. Adding the anchor quotas, using n=4+P_A+P_B+D_A+D_B, gives

    n ≤ 12k+4 − 3(w_A+w_B) − (excess of all outside ports)
            − (additional representative charges).

The optional charge paying for an odd-depth low nonport has already been spent on that vertex and is omitted from the additional charges. The baseline is n≤6k−2. The foreign anchors seen by the four A representatives provide at least eight more charges.

One more unit always exists. If B has a multiple port outside W_B, its excess pays. If a multiple port is inside W_B, use it as the B representative on any of its labels: it sees at least one private A port of another label at two, in addition to all opposite ports counted for its index. Otherwise B ports are private. If any A representative sees every foreign anchor the ninth unit already exists. In the remaining case fix any A representative u_i and a missed foreign anchor j. For the private B representative v_j on j, a length-two route from v_j to i gives an additional foreign-anchor charge. If dist(v_j,i)≥3, passage through S makes dist(u_i,v_j)≥4: at i or j one endpoint distance is at least three, and at every other anchor both are at least two. Also dist(u_i,j)=3 through y_A and so dist(u_i,v_j)≤4. This supplies a fresh opposite slot at the A representative, since v_j is not an i-port. The optional low-nonport payment is disjoint from all these additional charges. Thus n≤6k−11, proving the claim.
