# Both sides with only private ports and all three anchors nearest

Let a minimum three-cut S in a qualifying graph have two wholly high components A,B, each containing a nonport. Suppose every port has just one incident anchor and every nonport is at distance two from all three anchors. Without bounding either component order, n≤5k−3 for k≥6. In combination with the established order theorem for k≥16 this gives traceability. In particular the two sides of a clean nontraceable counterexample cannot both have order at least 3k−5: the sharpened two-port cover in no-small-clean-three-cut.md would force the stated structure in each of them.

Choose a nonport y_T in each side T=A,B. Write W_T=N_T[y_T] and w_T=|W_T|≥k+1. Write P_T for the total number of ports in T, d_T for its nonports, and d'_T for its nonports inside W_T. Let p_{T,i} count ports on anchor i in T and p'_{T,i} those inside W_T. Each y_T has a neighbor u_{T,i} with each one of the three possible anchor labels i. Choose one such representative per label. Every member of W_T is within distance two from each of the three representatives, through y_T if needed. The p_{T',i} opposite i-ports all have distance two from u_{T,i}, and any other anchors at distance two from it consume additional separate slots. Let t_{T,i} count these other anchors.

The number of nonneighbors of u_{T,i} inside W_T apart from itself is consequently at most k−p_{T',i}−t_{T,i}. Of the w_T−p'_{T,i} vertices not themselves adjacent to i in that window, all but at most this many have distance two from i via u_{T,i}. Summing those lower bounds over i produces at least

    2w_T + d'_T − 3k + P_T' + Σ_i t_{T,i}

anchor incidences in W_T, since the port classes partition all ports. Every nonport outside W_T contributes three further incidences by hypothesis. Let L count any further established incidences at ports outside the two windows (none are needed for the baseline). Adding all three anchor budgets yields

    n ≤ 9k+3 − 2(w_A+w_B+d_out) − Σ_{T,i} t_{T,i} − L,

where d_out=(d_A−d'_A)+(d_B−d'_B). Here n=3+P_A+P_B+d_A+d_B. As w_A+w_B≥2k+2, the baseline is n≤5k−1.

If two anchors i,j are adjacent, their chosen representatives in A each see the opposite anchor at distance two, giving Σt≥2 and proving the desired bound. Likewise, suppose two adjacent port vertices of distinct labels i,j exist in a common side. For each endpoint lying inside its W_T, choose that endpoint as its label's representative: it then sees the opposite anchor at distance two. Both choices can be made together since the labels are distinct. If an endpoint lies outside W_T, it instead supplies one of the extra L incidences, at the opposite anchor via its neighbor. These increments count different incidences, so again Σt+L≥2.

In the remaining case S is independent and no two ports of different labels are adjacent within either side. A port of label i is at distance at least three from any other anchor j (a length-two intermediate would have to be an adjacent anchor or a j-port). Opposite ports of different labels have distance at least four by passage through S and at most four since both are high. Opposite ports of the same label have distance two. Every port thus sees every opposite port at positive even distance, and P_A,P_B≤k. Every nonport is at distance two from each anchor, so d_A+d_B≤k as well. Thus n≤3k+3≤5k−3 for k≥3. This completes all cases.

## Only one all-nearest window center is needed on each side

Assume now all A ports are singletons, but allow arbitrary B support sizes and allow other nonports of either side to have fewer than three nearest anchors. It is enough that the two chosen high nonports y_A,y_B are each at distance two from all of S. All other high nonports still have at least one nearest anchor by the opposite high nonport. The same upper n≤5k−3 holds. To verify this in the window sum, let the port excess be the number of incident anchors minus one for each port. Then Σp_{T,i} exceeds P_T by the sum of all port excesses in T, while Σp'_{T,i} exceeds the number of window ports by the sum of window port excesses only. Thus the final sum gains the excess of every port outside the two windows. An outside nonport supplies its actual number h≥1 of anchor incidences, leaving a nonnegative credit h−1 after paying for the vertex itself. The resulting upper bound is 9k+3−2(w_A+w_B) minus outside port excesses, outside nonport credits and any additional t or L charges. The baseline is still at most 5k−1.

A B port v of multiple support inside W_B may be used as a representative for two of its own anchor labels. Each such use pays an extra slot beyond the baseline opposite i-contacts, since the other anchor has a nonempty, disjoint private A port class. This saves at least two. Two separate multiple ports outside W_B, or one triple outside, save at least two through the excess terms. If there is exactly one B double port v outside W_B and all others are singletons, its mandatory internal degree at least k−2 exceeds the number (≤k−3) of B nonports. So it neighbors a singleton port u in B. Port u sees one of v's two anchors different from its own i at distance two. Select u for its own representative if inside W_B; otherwise count the extra outside-window even incidence of this singleton u at that anchor. This saves the second slot beyond v's own excess. If all B ports are singletons the edge cases from the preceding proof apply unchanged, except that in the final no-crossing-edges case we use d_A,d_B≤k−3, whence n≤4k−3. This proves the strengthened bound.
