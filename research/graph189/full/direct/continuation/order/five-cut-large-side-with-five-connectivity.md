# Routing a five-cut through a large internally five-connected side

For k≥300, let a minimum five-cut S of a qualifying graph of connectivity five leave two components A,B, chosen so A contains at most as many high vertices as B. Delete the sole low vertex of B if it exists and call the result B'. If B' is five-connected, the whole graph is traceable. In particular no order bound on B is needed; the conclusion includes the case that A is shallow and that arbitrarily many high B nonports have only one nearest anchor. The hypothesis on B' is automatic at internal connectivity at least five if B is wholly high and at least six if B contains the low. All cuts have two components in the relevant order at k≥300, by global/minimum-cut-two-sides.md.

## A path of one side can be cut open between two different anchors

Here is the gluing fact for any minimum s-cut and any B path P of order at least s. There are s mutually disjoint paths from the s anchors to distinct vertices of P, with interiors in B outside P. Indeed use vertex Menger in G between S and V(P): a separator with fewer than s vertices cannot remove either set entirely or disconnect the remaining sets. Stop each path at the first P contact and start it at the last member of S. The s disjoint paths therefore use every anchor once; their interiors lie in B. Mark the s distinct positions with their source labels. Mark every additional port of P by one of its actual incident anchors (direct connection). Connectors at any two positions of distinct labels are disjoint, since the only nondirect connectors are from the original matching, and lie off P until their distinct ends.

The ordered list of marks contains every label. There are at least s−1 occurrences of a change between consecutive labels, since each new label after the first needs such a change. The respective unmarked intervals, together with the two terminal unmarked intervals, are disjoint. Their total length is at most the number D(P) of nonports on P, because every port was marked. At least one has length at most floor(D(P)/(s+1)). For a terminal interval discard it and join its first retained mark through its connector to A. For an interval at a change of labels, replace the interval between the two marks by their connectors, two distinct anchors, and a path through A. Thus the same B path works even with both endpoints far from all anchors.

## Side sizes and the small-side paths

Write a≤b for the respective counts of high vertices. If one side is the singleton low, all opposite odd-depth vertices are at even distance from it, and even-depth vertices are paid by the five anchors. This alone gives n≤6k+6 and does not imply the desired moderate-order cutoff. The final paragraph handles this case without the small-core argument.

For now assume A has a high vertex. Then a≥k−5 by its degree. In a counterexample n≥6k−10 by the existing moderate-order theorem, so a+b≥6k−16. The all-high pair inequality in root/minimum-cut-populations.md gives ab≤ka+5k², whence a<2.8k. In fact a<1.5k: otherwise a(5k−16−a)≤5k² fails at both endpoints 1.5k and 2.8k and hence throughout by concavity for k≥300. Thus b≤6k+26 via b≤k+5k²/(k−5). The function a+k+5k²/a decreases throughout [k−5,1.5k], so n≤7k+27.

The induced high A core has minimum degree ≥k−6 on a<1.5k vertices and is Hamilton-connected by degree-sum closure. For two distinct required anchor labels, two disjoint connectors to distinct high A vertices exist, directly or with the sole low as intermediate. Indeed a vertex cut of size at most one for this two-label linkage, together with the other three anchors, would separate remaining high A vertices from B by fewer than five vertices: any entrance to the high core has to pass directly from a retained anchor or through the low. An analogous one-label connection is immediate. Thus using the preceding gluing fact loses at most one low A vertex and at most four unused separator vertices.

## An ordinary long B path from a universal marker

Adjoin a single universal marker g to B', making a six-connected graph H with minimum degree ≥k−5, order ≤6k+27. For any longest cycle Z the complement contains no cycle of length six or more: apply Corollary A of Nikoghosyan, On Some Versions of Conjectures of Bondy and Jung, Mathematical Problems of Computer Science 45 (2016), pp. 20–21, with λ=6. Its order condition |H|+2≤7(δ(H)−4) holds since 6k+29≤7(k−9). Source: https://noad.sci.am/Content/136032/03%20%281%29.pdf.

The universal g belongs to Z, since otherwise it could be inserted in any cycle edge. Suppose two exterior vertices x,y are adjacent. Write T_x,T_y for their respective Z contact sets, a_v=|T_v|, q_v=d_(H−Z)(v). For a fixed cycle orientation let r count successors of T_x that are not predecessors. Maximality forbids consecutive T_x contacts; T_y is disjoint from both shifts, since their intervening edge would admit x,y in its place. Every common contact i∈T_x∩T_y has i+2 outside T_x (otherwise the two-edge segment could be replaced through y,x); hence |T_x∩T_y|≤r. Exactly as in root/longest-cycle-exterior.md, the two shifts of T_x give a_x+r even witnesses, T_y outside T_x at least a_y−r, and exterior neighbors of y missing from N(x) at least q_y−q_x. The latter two collections are genuine B' distance-two pairs (g belongs to both contact sets). Of the first collection only the two shifts of g can be false, since witnesses through any actual B' contact have the same distance two in G. The three collections are disjoint. Consequently k≥a_x+a_y+q_y−q_x−2, so q_x≥a_x−7. Thus every member of a nontrivial exterior component has internal degree ≥ceil((k−12)/2), and the end of a longest path in it gives a cycle of at least that degree plus one. This contradicts the published theorem. Hence the exterior is independent.

Let x be any omitted vertex of B'. Every actual B' neighbor is a Z contact, and all their distinct successors are real B' points at G-distance two. None can be g since g also contacts x and consecutive contacts are impossible. In addition for a port of support size r there are at least r distinct opposite A contacts at distance two, by the minimum-cut matching to A (a≥5). Write l_B for the number (zero or one) of removed low B vertices. The real-successor and opposite-contact counts leave at most l_B further even slots for each omitted port. As omitted B' vertices are independent, any one anchor occurs on at most 1+l_B omitted supports. Thus there are ≤5(1+l_B) omitted ports.

An omitted high nonport has ≥k−l_B real successors, and some outside even witness. Indeed depth two gives an even anchor; at larger depth its distance to any opposite high A vertex is at least and at most four, and hence four. Such omissions are impossible if B is wholly high. If l_B=1, an omitted nonport already exhausts its budget and cannot share a real B' neighbor with another omitted nonport. Their disjoint sets of at least k−1 neighbors on Z imply at most b/(k−1)<7 such omissions. Removing g therefore leaves an ordinary B path P missing at most 5 when B is wholly high, and at most 17 altogether from B otherwise (including the removed low).

## Completing the path

If A contains a high nonport, every high B nonport is at distance four from it and there are at most k−5 of them. If A is high-shallow and all high B nonports have depth at most two, each contributes to a nearest anchor's even quota, giving at most 5k such vertices. In either case the gluing fact constructs a global path missing at most 22+floor(5k/6), at most k−5 for k≥300. For a graph of n≤7k+27 this suffices: otherwise the known exterior-component bound gives m∈[ceil(k/2)+2,k−5] with m(k−m+3)≤n+1. The concave quadratic is at least (k/2+2)(k/2) at the first endpoint and is 8(k−5) at the second, both exceeding n+1.

It remains that all high members of A are ports and a high B vertex has depth at least three. Every high A vertex is then at distance four from it; its disjoint second layer has at least five vertices. Hence a=k−5. The degree requirement forces the low vertex to lie in A and every high A vertex to see all five anchors and every other A vertex. In particular A is a clique of order k−4, and B is wholly high. All its vertices have depth at most three by the high-pair diameter. An omitted B port would have all a high A vertices at distance two, far more than its support size, in addition to its required real successors. Thus P spans B. Also A can absorb every anchor in a path between any two distinct prescribed anchors, or with one prescribed anchor endpoint: intersperse the other anchors between distinct high A clique vertices and put the low between two high clique vertices.

Let p be the B port count. A high A universal port sees every B port and every B depth-three vertex at even distance, so the depth-three count is at most k−p. If one label lacks a B vertex of depth two solely nearest to it, the depth-two count is ≤4k, hence D(P)≤5k. Otherwise choose one private B representative at each of the five labels from such a geodesic. The five anchor quotas pay at least 20 distinct additional incidences outside the depth-two class: for each ordered pair i,j of distinct labels, use j itself at i if ij is a nonedge (distance two via high A), and the private j-port at i if ij is an edge. Hence D(P)≤5k−20+k−p≤6k−25, since p≥5. The gluing fact with all anchors and all A absorbed has deficit at most floor((6k−25)/6)≤k−5, as required.


Finally suppose A is the singleton low z. Each anchor touches z and has degree at least k; it has at least k−5 B neighbors. In particular the B port count satisfies p≥k−5. All B vertices have depth at most four since their distance to z is one more than the depth and the global diameter is at most five. Odd-depth B vertices cost a slot at z; even-depth B vertices cost one at their nearest anchor. Thus b≤6k, n≤6k+6, and there are at most 6k−p≤5k+5 nonports. The universal-marker longest-cycle proof works with B wholly high exactly as above up to the independent exterior. An omitted B nonport has at least k real successors plus an outside even witness (a nearest anchor at even depth, z at odd depth), so none occurs. An omitted port on r≤5 labels has its ≥k−r real successors, leaving at most r slots for other witnesses; one is z, so at most four other omitted vertices can share its anchor. There are at most five such omitted ports per label and at most 25 in all. Use the same gluing fact: z joins any two distinct anchors as i,z,j, or extends any one at a terminal gap. The global path omits at most 25+4+floor((5k+5)/6)≤k−5 for k≥300. The preceding exterior contradiction applies.
