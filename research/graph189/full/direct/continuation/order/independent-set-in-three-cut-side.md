# Independent sets in a large side at a deep minimum three-cut

Let G be a qualifying graph of connectivity three and second-smallest degree k≥24. Let a three-cut S have two components A,B, each with a high nonport. If |B|≤3k−8, the set of degree-at-least-k vertices in B induces a graph with independence number at most k−2. The possible below-k vertex may lie anywhere. In particular α(B)≤k−2 if B is wholly high. The interval worker independently supplied the final two-cut contradiction below and the application using a universal marker.

Assume I is an independent set of exactly k−1 high vertices in B. Any two of I have a common neighbor in B: if their internal neighborhoods were disjoint they and I would give |B|≥2(k−3)+(k−1)=3k−7. Hence each I vertex already sees k−2 others from I at distance two.

Write p_T(a)=|N(a)∩T| for a∈S and T∈{A,B}, and i_a=|I∩N(a)|. We have p_B(a)≤k since any A neighbor of a sees all of its B neighbors at distance two. If i_a>0 then p_A(a)≤2 for the same reason applied to that member of I after counting its k−2 I peers. In fact i_a≤p_A(a)+5≤7. To see this, choose b∈I adjacent to a. It has ≥k−3 B-neighbors, of which at most p_B(a)−i_a can be adjacent to a, since I is independent. Thus a sees at least k−3−p_B(a)+i_a distance-two vertices in B.

We claim it sees at least p_B(a)−2−p_A(a) additional distance-two vertices in A, even if a's sole A neighbor is the low vertex. Choose any A neighbor v of a. If v is high its closed internal neighborhood has size ≥k−2≥p_B(a)−2. If v is low, its A-neighbor w exists because A is connected and contains a high nonport. That w is high and |N_A[w]|≥k−2. These vertices are all within distance two from v, while p_B(a) distinct vertices in B already lie at distance two from v. Hence |N_A[v]|≥(k−2)−(k−p_B(a))=p_B(a)−2. Subtracting a's p_A(a) contacts proves the claim. Adding the two disjoint counts under the cap gives i_a≤p_A(a)+5. Consequently I has at most 21 ports.

There is therefore a high nonport x∈I. Its k−2 peers in I are at distance two. At least one high nonport of A lies at distance four from x, and at least one anchor lies at distance two. The cap forces there to be exactly one high A nonport, and exactly one anchor a at distance two from x. Every high port in A must touch a: if not, it cannot be at distance three from x (such a path would have to pass through a at its second step). The high-pair distance bound would make it another distance-four vertex. The unique high A nonport requires |A|≥k+1 since all its neighbors are internal. Allowing the possible one other low A vertex, this proves p_A(a)≥k−1.

Choose a B port u on a two-edge x-to-a path. Its only anchor is a, or x would have two nearest anchors. Its p_A(a) opposite even2 vertices leave at most one other positive-even slot. If u neighbors every other B vertex, then both other anchors are at distance two (B has a contact on each), impossible. Otherwise there is a member w of B−N_B[u] with a B-neighbor in N_B[u], and all such w have ambient distance two from u. There is exactly one. No neighbor of u in B touches an anchor other than a, since that would give a further even witness distinct from w. All neighbors of N_B[u] outside it therefore lie in {a,w}. Deleting these two vertices disconnects N_B[u] from A, contrary to connectivity three. This contradiction proves the claim.

For wholly high B, the interval worker's universal-marker argument now supplies a spanning ordinary path in B throughout |B|≤3k−9, regardless of the location of the possible low in A or S. Add a universal vertex to B. The result is 2-connected because B is connected, has unchanged independence number, and has minimum degree at least k−2. For m=|B|≤3k−9 the augmented order m+1 has (m+3)/3≤k−2; the Nash–Williams Hamiltonicity theorem applies. Delete the universal vertex. This proof of traceability inside B leaves the first potential growing-side exception at m=3k−8. If B contains the low, the proven independence conclusion concerns B with that vertex removed. The universal-marker degree threshold for the entire side then requires a further argument.
