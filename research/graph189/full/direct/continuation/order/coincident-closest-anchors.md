# Bounding an entire opposite component by two even-distance budgets

Suppose S is a cardinality-minimum cut of size s in a qualifying Graph 189 graph, and G−S has two components C,D, each with a high nonport. For a high nonport y in D let A(y) be the nonempty set of anchors at distance two from y. If one D port b is adjacent to every member of A(y), then C has at most 2k−s high vertices. Consequently if such a pair y,b exists in each component, n≤4k−s+1, including a possible lone low vertex anywhere. If both components are wholly high, n≤4k−s. These inequalities do not require adjacency among the separator vertices, small separator degrees, or a size restriction on the components.

Indeed, opposite high nonports are at distance four, so every high nonport y is at distance two from S. Every high x∈C without an edge to A(y) is at distance four from y. The distance cannot be ≤2; a path of length three would have to first reach S in two steps from y and then use a single last edge to x. The high-pair bound gives the upper bound of four. These x lie outside y's second layer, which has at least s vertices because it separates y from the opposite high nonport. Hence there are at most k−s such high x. Every remaining x∈C touches A(y), so is at exact distance two from b via an anchor. There are at most k of them. This proves the bound.

In particular the side condition holds whenever some high nonport has just one nearest anchor, and whenever the side contains a universal port. In a three-cut residual of order >4k+1 with wholly high sides, at least one side has no universal port, every nonport there has at least two nearest anchors, and no port there is adjacent to all nearest anchors of any nonport. Whenever a nonport has exactly two nearest anchors in such a side, both singleton-support port types on those anchors occur as its neighbors (a shortest path to either anchor must use a port, whose entire support is necessarily contained in the nearest set).

## An anchor far from all the other anchors

Suppose instead that S contains an anchor a at ambient distance at least four from every other anchor, and each component supplies a high neighbor of a. Then n≤4k−s+1 again. Fix such a port u in one component. Its only anchor neighbor is a. Every opposite high port x has distance two from u if it touches a. Otherwise a path of length at most three from u to x would have to cross S. On a three-edge path some crossing anchor is adjacent to u or to x. If adjacent to u, this is a, and the final two edges reach x; following those by x's incident edge to another anchor contradicts distance at least four between a and that anchor. If the crossing anchor is adjacent to x, reaching it within two steps of u gives the same contradiction via a,u. A shorter path would require a common incident anchor. Thus the distance to x is exactly four by the high-pair bound. All opposite high ports are even from u, so each side has at most k high ports. Each side also has at most k−s high nonports, using an opposite high nonport's second and fourth layers. Adding the anchor set and the possible sole low vertex proves the inequality. In a high-order residual with three anchors and wholly high sides, the graph on the anchors that joins pairs at ambient distance at most three must therefore be connected.
