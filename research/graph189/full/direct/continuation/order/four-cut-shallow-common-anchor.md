# A four-cut with one anchor common to its shallow side, for all k≥16

Suppose a qualifying graph has connectivity four, second-smallest degree k≥16, and a minimum four-cut S leaving sides A,B. All high vertices of A touch an anchor i in common. Then G is traceable, for arbitrary B, the exceptional degree and the graph on S. Use the moderate-order theorem, so it is enough to exclude n≥6k−10 in a nontraceable instance. The global/cut4/shallow-large-degree.md universal-vertex criterion and the pair/anchor coverage argument there are used below. The common-anchor condition simplifies its universal-vertex criterion by removing any density prerequisite.

If A is the singleton low, its sole vertex touches all four anchors, so its even set pays all odd B depths and the anchors pay the even depths. Hence n≤5k+5. Otherwise write a=k−d for the number of high A vertices, l≤1 for its low count; a≤k by any B contact on i, and d≥0. Each high A vertex with r anchor neighbors has at most r−1−d+l high internal nonneighbors other than itself: its internal degree is at least k−r on a+l vertices. In particular d≤4. Write b_h for the number of high B vertices. We have b_h≥5k−15+d>4k.

No first step from an A vertex can shorten the ambient distance to two anchors: such a neighbor and at most two others cover all four anchor geodesics, and they and the original root cover all B by the even parity principle (root/fractional-separator-cover.md). Every anchor j has a high A contact, as observed in global/cut4/shallow-large-degree.md. Otherwise its only A contact z is the low vertex, and the ≥3 common neighbors of the edge jz all lie in S. Thus j touches every other anchor, and the step from a high A vertex via i, which it touches and j touches, would shorten both i and the missing j.

If some high B vertex has depth at least three from S, every high A vertex is at distance four from it. Its own disjoint second layer has at least four vertices. Thus a≤k−4, forcing d=4,l=1 and every high A vertex adjacent to all four anchors and every other A vertex. High B vertices cannot have depth more than three by the high-pair bound. For the following arguments involving a nonuniversal high A support we may consequently assume that every high B nonport has depth two.

If some high A vertex y touches all S, its even quota pays all odd-depth B vertices, while the four anchor budgets pay all even-depth vertices. If an anchor has no high depth-two B vertex solely nearest to it, three anchor budgets suffice for high even-depth B vertices; at most one low B vertex needs separate payment. This gives n≤|A|+5+4k≤5k+6≤6k−11 at k≥17. At k=16 the same branch gives n≤5k+5=6k−11: if the extra low B payment is needed, then A is wholly high and |A|≤k, otherwise the extra payment is unnecessary.

Otherwise select a private B port at each of the four labels on the route from a vertex solely nearest to that label. Each unordered nonedge of S gives two ordered anchor-to-anchor incidences at distance two via y. Each ordered anchor edge jh gives the incidence of its private B j-port at distance two from h. These are twelve distinct additional savings in the four anchor budgets. At the private B i-port, all its A contacts via i and three own-side/anchor boundary vertices have even distance, hence A has at most k−3 i-contacts. If the low A vertex exists and misses i, it is at distance two from i via an internal neighbor (all other A vertices are high and touch i), saving one more anchor-budget unit. Hence |A| minus this extra saving is at most k−3. The order bound is n≤4+(k−3)+k+4k−12=6k−11.

For a contradiction assume now that no high A vertex touches all labels. For any high A port u with support T⊆S form a collection of roots consisting of u, the anchors in T, and enough high A ports to cover the labels outside T. They cover all high B vertices by positive-even sets. For B ports, use a port covering any incident label. For B nonports, their nonempty nearest-anchor set is at depth two. If it meets T, use that anchor, and if disjoint, distance to u is four: a three-step path would require an anchor of T as its first step and nearest to the B vertex. In particular, if another high A port touches two labels outside T, at most four roots suffice, contrary to b_h>4k.

After removing the common i, each high support is a subset of {p,q,r} of size at most two. Every ordered pair of occurring residual supports differs by at most one extra label, and all three labels occur. This leaves precisely three alternatives: all supports have size at most one; exactly two distinct pairs occur, say pq and pr, with p the only possible singleton; or all three pairs occur and there are no smaller residual supports. For example if only pq were a pair, coverage of r by a singleton would violate the ordered condition. An empty support cannot coexist with a pair.

Let t_j count high A vertices missing j and e_j all A vertices at distance two from j. From any chosen high A contact of j having r anchors, we get t_j≤e_j+r−1−d+l. In a five-root cover as above, the total slack for outside-B spending and repetitions is at most 5k−b_h≤15−d. Each A port of support r costs at least 4−r even slots on its own side or S (the boundary of its closed internal neighborhood separates from the other side and its only potentially non-even boundary elements are the r incident anchors). Any repeated port-root contact counts as another loss when estimating the number of high B vertices. Distinct labels have distinct matched B contacts by Hall on a minimum separator (four-cut-window-credits.md). A repeated low B contact can equivalently have every occurrence discarded.

1. With all residual supports of size at most one, pick one representative per foreign label. For a fixed j take that representative as root and the other two as the complementary representatives. Each of the three A roots costs ≥2 outside-B slots, and a common B i-contact saves ≥2 duplicate slots. The anchor j is among the remaining two roots, so e_j≤7−d. Thus t_j≤9−2d. Each high A vertex misses at least two foreign labels, giving 2(k−d)≤27−6d, impossible.

2. With precisely pq,pr, high vertices all touch i,p and none touches both q,r. Pick high roots u on q and v on r. To cover opposite high vertices, use also the three anchors i,p,q; swapping q and r works as well. The two A roots pay at least one slot each and share distinct B contacts via i,p, gaining at least two repetition savings. Each of q,r is nonadjacent in S to each of i,p, since a high A vertex touching the common label but missing q (respectively r) would otherwise have one step towards two anchors. Each relevant nonedge has distance two via a high A contact. In the three budgets i,p,q this counts six ordered incidences: i and p each see q and r, and q sees i and p. Hence e_q≤5−d, and t_q≤8−2d. Similarly t_r≤8−2d. The two classes of q and r contacts are disjoint, so t_q+t_r≥k−d and k≤16−3d. The only remaining case allowed by k≥16 has d=0. Then any B i-contact x has already exhausted its k even slots in A. Connectedness of B forces x adjacent to every B vertex, or an internal second-layer vertex would violate the cap. In B all pairs are within two, so δ(B)≥|B|−1−k≥(|B|+1)/2 as |B|>4k. The high A subgraph also has minimum degree ≥k−5≥(a+1)/2. By degree-sum closure both graphs are Hamilton-connected. Start the two spanning paths at x and at any high A contact respectively, and join via i. This misses at most the other three anchors and the possible low A vertex, within the established almost-spanning threshold.

3. When all three pairs occur, each high A vertex misses exactly one foreign label. The high-contact sets of any two distinct anchors differ, precluding an edge between them by the same two-anchor first-step argument. Every pair of anchors has a common high A neighbor. The three anchor roots in any cover therefore pay nine ordered incidences in S. For a high root missing foreign j, use one high A port on j as the complementary root. These two triple supports spend at least two own-side slots and share at least two distinct matched opposite contacts. The sum of the e counts of the two foreign anchor roots is thus at most 2−d. The sum of their two t counts is at most 2−d+2(2−d+l)≤8−3d. Summing over the three possible missing labels j gives 2(k−d)≤24−9d, impossible.

This exhausts every high support assignment.
