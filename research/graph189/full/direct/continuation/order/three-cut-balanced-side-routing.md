# A prescribed-pair deficit bound throughout the balanced three-cut range

Let κ(G)=3, k≥24, and let a minimum cut S have two wholly high sides A,B, each with a nonport. If both side orders are in [2k,3k−6], G has a spanning path, with no assumption on the edges in S. More generally, whenever both sides have order ≥2k and one of them, C, has m≤3k−6, then for every prescribed pair R⊂S there is a path in C beginning at a genuine R-contact which misses at most floor((k−2)/3) members of C. The proof below works under n>5k−3, enough by the proved order theorem. The single-side path lower bound in this form is asserted under that order assumption.

Both sides have at most k−3 nonports, by the second/fourth-layer count from the opposite high nonport. Neither side can contain a universal port: such a port covers all opposite ports at exact distance two, and the opposite nonports number at most k−3, contradicting the opposite order ≥2k. Every vertex on a wholly high side thus has internal degree at least k−2.

**Private occupancies.** For each anchor a at most k−2 C ports have singleton support {a}. Otherwise take an opposite a-port u. A port touching r anchors spends at least 3−r positive-even slots in its own side or S, since the boundary of its closed internal neighborhood is a separator and every boundary vertex other than its incident anchors is two steps from it. If u has singleton support, its ≥k−1 opposite private a-ports and its two own slots exceed k. If it touches a second anchor b, it sees in addition a C contact of b distinct from every private a-port, and it still spends at least one own slot. The universal alternative was already excluded. This proves the occupancy bound.

For a prescribed anchor pair R add a marker h to C adjacent precisely to the R-contacts. This is the coordinator's 2-connected restricted-pair augmentation of Ali's apex construction: deleting the remaining anchor and any one C vertex leaves G connected, so each component of C after such a deletion has an R-contact. The marker degree p satisfies

    p ≥ m−(k−3)−(k−2) = m−2k+5,

since the only C vertices unseen by h are nonports and private contacts of the excluded anchor. Every other vertex in this augmentation H has degree ≥k−1: a retained port gains h, a missed port has at most one neighbor in S, and a nonport has all its ≥k neighbors inside C.

Moreover α(C)≤k−2. Apply the independent-set proof in independent-set-in-three-cut-side.md, now using the improved internal minimum degree k−2 in its opening step. An independent (k−1)-set has each pair at ambient distance two, as otherwise their internal neighborhoods and that independent set require m≥(k−1)+2(k−2)=3k−5. The light-anchor bound, unique-nearest saturation and two-vertex separator contradiction are unchanged. Therefore α(H)≤k−1 even if h joins the independent set.

The degree sum σ of each independent triple in H is bounded below by min(3k−3,p+2k−2), which is ≥m+3=|H|+2 by m≤3k−6 and the private-occupancy bound. If H has no independent triple, the ordinary Chvátal–Erdős theorem gives a Hamilton cycle. Otherwise apply Theorem 5 of Bauer, Broersma, Veldman and Rao, JCTB 47 (1989), 237–243 (https://fileserver-az.core.ac.uk/download/572157558.pdf). Its cycle length is at least min(|H|,|H|+σ/3−α(H)). The cycle omits at most max(0,α(H)−ceil(σ/3)) vertices. This is at most floor((k−2)/3), since the three-original-vertex case costs zero, while p≥1 and 3(k−1)−(p+2k−2)=k−1−p≤k−2. A cycle containing h opens to a C path with a genuine R-contact endpoint. A cycle missing h can be reached from a genuine R-contact by a shortest path inside C and opened there; the path then covers at least the cycle vertices. In either case at most the displayed number of C vertices is lost.

When both sides meet the size range, each pair of labels contains a label able to start a path with that deficit. Thus each side has at least two feasible labels. Use a shared label to join the resulting two paths via its anchor. This loses at most 2+2 floor((k−2)/3) vertices in G. But here n≤6k−9, and any nontraceable Graph 189 instance of that order with k≥24 omits at least k−3 vertices on every path. Indeed otherwise a high-containing omitted component would have order m₀ in [ceil(k/2)+2,k−4] with m₀(k−m₀+3)≤n+1 by the established almost-spanning/exterior bound. At the lower endpoint this concave quadratic is ≥(k/2+2)(k/2)>6k−8. At the upper endpoint it is 7(k−4)>6k−8 for k≥24. The contradiction follows since 2+2 floor((k−2)/3)<k−3. This proves the theorem.
