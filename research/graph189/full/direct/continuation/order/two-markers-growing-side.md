# Two anchored path pieces suffice beyond the single-cycle cutoff

Suppose k≥24, κ(G)=3, n>4k+1 and a minimum three-cut has two wholly high components A,B each containing a nonport. Assume |A|≤2k−5. Then the first order of B left by the following argument is |B|=3k−3. At this order, if G is nontraceable, B contains three independent vertices of original degree exactly k, each adjacent to exactly two members of the separator. All sizes |B|≤3k−4 give traceability when combined with the preceding side bounds. Any sole below-k vertex may lie in the cut. The two-piece covering argument itself is specific to the range 3k−5≤|B|≤3k−3; the other sizes use the existing results described below.

A universal port u (one touching all s anchors) sees every port of the opposite side at distance two. Every port in its own side is u, one of its internal neighbors, or at distance two from u through S. The total number of ports is therefore at most k+1+d_G(u)−s. Each component contains at most k−s high nonports, since from an opposite high nonport all are at distance four and the second layer contains at least s vertices. Including the one possible low vertex, this proves n≤3k+d_G(u)−2s+2. At s=3 and n>4k+1, every universal port has degree at least k+6. Every vertex of either wholly high side therefore has internal degree at least k−2. The small A is Hamilton-connected by the ordinary degree-sum closure with an endpoint marker. Every pair of distinct anchors r,s has distinct respective contacts in A; otherwise their sole common choice z would disconnect A−{z} from B after deleting z and the third anchor, contrary to κ=3.

For |B|≤3k−6 an ordinary spanning path of B follows by the universal-marker Nash–Williams argument at orders at least 3k−9, with the previously treated smaller orders. In fact α(B)≤k−1 when |B|≤3k−5. If an independent k-set I exists, all its members share pairwise internal neighbors since |I|+2(k−2)>|B|. Its k−1 distance-two peers preclude a nonport: such a vertex sees an additional nearest anchor and opposite high nonport. A port in I on anchor a forces |P_A(a)|≤1 by the k−1 peers and opposite contacts. The light-anchor bound in independent-set-in-three-cut-side.md holds at any size of I and gives |I∩P_B(a)|≤|P_A(a)|+5≤6, so all three anchors together cannot cover k≥24 independent ports. The single-marker improvement similarly works with a genuine-port marker, whose degree in the boundary band ≥3k−9 is at least 2k−6, since B has ≤k−3 nonports. Any independent set containing that marker has ≤k−2 vertices. A spanning cycle in its torso opens into a spanning B path with port endpoints, so one can join it through an anchor to A while losing at most the other two anchors.

For the growing sizes use two markers h,g, not adjacent to each other. Vertex h is adjacent to every B port (and only these), and g is adjacent to every B vertex. Deleting either marker leaves a connected graph, and deleting any B vertex leaves both markers connected through remaining B ports; each surviving B vertex has g as neighbor. Thus the augmentation H is 2-connected. In the band 3k−5≤|B|≤3k−3, the degree of h is at least |B|−(k−3)≥2k−2, and g's is |B|. The minimum degree is at least k: a nonport has internal degree ≥k and gains g, and every port has internal degree ≥k−2 and gains both markers.

For all these orders α(H)≤k. An independent (k+1)-set I in B has every two members at distance two in G. Indeed, without a common internal neighbor their internal degrees and I would give |B|≥(k+1)+2(k−2)=3k−3. At equality both internal degrees equal k−2, so the two vertices are degree-k double ports and share an anchor because S has size three. Each member of I thus has k even peers and at least one more positive-even vertex outside B (an opposite port via an incident anchor, or a nearest anchor for a nonport). This excludes I. An independent set containing g has size at most two; one containing h and no g has at most 1+(k−3) vertices.

When |B|≤3k−4, Nash–Williams' theorem makes H Hamiltonian since |H|=|B|+2≤3k−2 and δ(H)≥max(α(H),(|H|+2)/3). When |B|=3k−3, apply Theorem 4 of Bauer, Broersma, Veldman and Rao, JCTB 47 (1989), 237–243 (https://fileserver-az.core.ac.uk/download/572157558.pdf): a 2-connected graph with σ₃≥max(n+2,3α) is Hamiltonian. Here |H|+2=3k+1. The only way the condition fails is an independent triple all of degree k in H, and these can only be original degree-k double ports. For the remaining construction assume a Hamilton cycle in H.

Deleting the two nonadjacent markers gives two nonempty disjoint paths spanning B, each with at least one genuine port endpoint (neighbor of h). If two such endpoints from different paths touch distinct anchors, join them through those two anchors and a spanning path of A with the two distinct contact endpoints. This leaves only the third anchor. Otherwise the two available endpoint supports are both the same singleton {r}. Call a B vertex good if it touches an anchor other than r, and bad otherwise. There are at most 2k−3 bad vertices (at most k−3 nonports and at most k r-only ports, using an A contact of r). At least one vertex is good because each anchor touches B. If each path contains a good vertex, its bad prefixes from either end up to a first good vertex are disjoint (even for a single good position); the four prefixes together have size at most 2k−3. Trimming the shortest prefix yields a good endpoint and one may use the original r endpoint in the other path, spanning A in between as before. The total loss is at most 1+floor((2k−3)/4).

Otherwise write q for the order of the entirely bad path. Discarding that path gives a loss of at most q+2 by joining the remaining path's original r endpoint through r to A. Keeping it and trimming the smaller bad prefix of the other path gives a loss of at most 1+floor((2k−3−q)/2). Their minimum is less than k−2 for k≥24: if q≤k−5 use q+2, and otherwise the other estimate is at most 1+floor((k+1)/2). The same strict bound holds when both paths had good vertices. But n≤(2k−5)+(3k−3)+3<5k+1 and at that order a hypothetical nontraceable graph misses at least k−2 vertices on every path (paths-through-two-sides.md). This contradiction completes the construction.

If A contains the low vertex z but still contains a high nonport, the same conclusions hold at |A|≤2k−6. Delete z to form A′; its vertices have internal degree at least k−3 and its order is at most 2k−7, so A′ is Hamilton-connected. Any two distinct prescribed anchors r,s have vertex-disjoint paths to distinct A′ vertices, either direct or with the sole internal vertex z. Indeed consider the auxiliary graph retaining just edges from {r,s} to A and from z to A′. A separator of size at most one between {r,s} and A′ in this graph, together with the third anchor, would separate the remaining high vertices of A from B in G: every entry into A′ would have to start at r or s and either be direct or pass through z. Vertex Menger gives the disjoint paths, which use z at most once. A single prescribed anchor likewise has an A′ contact, possibly through z. Joining the contacts via A′ loses at most z. The two-piece route above tolerates this one extra omission. In its final choice, use discarding when q≤k−6, losing ≤q+3<k−2, and otherwise trim, losing ≤2+floor((k+2)/2)<k−2. The independent-set exclusion used only that each side contains a high nonport and allowed a low A contact. The B path-cover proof is therefore unchanged. This extension stops short of |A|=2k−5, where the high part can have only half its order as minimum degree.
