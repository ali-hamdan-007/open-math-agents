# Replacing a dense block by at most two representatives per boundary vertex

Let U be a proper, nonempty vertex set of a finite simple graph G, with outside neighborhood S, |S|=b, |U|=m. Suppose each vertex of U misses at most d vertices of U when counting itself as a miss; equivalently its degree inside U is at least m−d. Assume
$$
 m\ge 2d+3(b+1)+1.
$$
There is a graph G' on at most |V(G)−U|+2b+1 vertices which is traceable if and only if G is traceable. A spanning path in G' can be lifted while preserving the sequence of exterior vertices and the boundary contacts at its retained representatives.

**Construction.** Make two copies of every member of S on the left of a bipartite graph, each with the original neighbors of that member in U on the right. Find a maximum matching M and let R be its matched right vertices. In G' retain R as individual vertices with their original exterior adjacencies, discard the other vertices of U, and introduce one new vertex with no exterior neighbor. Make R together with this new vertex a clique. The exterior and its edges are unchanged. Certainly |R|≤2b.

**Routing a given list of intervals.** A spanning path, or any simple path considered here, meets U in runs of consecutive vertices. It has at most b+1 such runs, since between consecutive runs it uses a distinct boundary vertex. Denote this number by r. Singleton runs with exterior neighbors on both sides use the same terminal once. Runs with exterior neighbors on both sides and at least two vertices have distinct terminals. A run at an endpoint of the whole path has just one prescribed terminal. Assume that the terminals are distinct across different runs, and that at least one run can receive extra vertices: this means it has two distinct terminals or has at most one prescribed terminal.

In a dense U as above, fix that run as the last one to route. Reserve all terminals (at most 2r). For each other two-distinct-terminal run, join its endpoints by an edge if possible, otherwise by a two-edge path through a fresh common neighbor in U. Two vertices have at least m−2d common neighbors in U. Fewer than 3r vertices are excluded by terminals and previously chosen common neighbors. Leave singleton and singly prescribed runs as single vertices. The reserved paths for all these other runs occupy at most 3r vertices. The remaining graph on m'≥m−3r≥2d+1 vertices has minimum degree at least m'−d≥(m'+1)/2. Hence it is Hamilton-connected, by the usual Ore/Bondy–Chvátal Hamilton-connectedness criterion. Alternatively add a degree-two marker joined only to the two prescribed endpoints, then close the remaining graph to a clique in cycle closure since every nonedge has degree sum at least m'+1. Choose arbitrary missing endpoints if the last run has fewer than two prescribed terminals. This routes all of U.

**Lifting.** In a spanning path of G', use the same outside order and the same representative terminals for its clique intervals. The new vertex has no exterior neighbor, so its interval either has two distinct prescribed terminals or meets an endpoint of the entire path. The preceding construction routes these intervals in U and gives a spanning path of G.

**Projecting.** From right vertices unmatched by M, traverse alternating paths, using unmatched edges from right to left and matched edges from left to right. Write K for the reachable right vertices and T for the reachable left vertices. Every neighbor on the left of a member of K belongs to T: if the edge in question is the matched edge, the right vertex was itself reached from that matched left vertex, and any other edge can be traversed. All of T are matched, since reaching an unmatched left vertex would give an augmenting path. Their matched representatives belong to K∩R. Both copies of any member of S neighboring K lie in T. All vertices of U outside K belong to R.

Now take a spanning path of G. Keep every terminal outside K unchanged, including a shared singleton terminal with two exterior neighbors. For each exterior incidence at a terminal in K, assign the representative matched to one copy of the corresponding boundary vertex. The path uses at most two such incidences at each boundary vertex, so distinct copies and distinct representatives can be chosen. All these representatives are in K, disjoint from the terminals preserved outside K. A shared singleton in K may now use two distinct representatives; their clique edge works. A run with two originally distinct terminals still has two distinct terminals. Since G's path covered m>b+1 vertices in U, some original run has at least two vertices. This run can contain the new clique vertex and every still unused representative, in arbitrary internal order. Traversing the resulting intervals gives a spanning path of G'. The case of no contact and a nonempty exterior makes both graphs disconnected. ∎

The stronger routing assertion is also valid directly in G for a longest path: if it uses any internal U edge, the exterior order can be retained and all of U included. The same holds if an endpoint of the path lies in U. Thus a longest path omitting a vertex of U can only meet it in isolated interior singletons, at most b+1 in total.
