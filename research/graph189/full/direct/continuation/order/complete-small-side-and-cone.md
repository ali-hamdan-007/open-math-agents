# Completing the small side restores the minimum degree before one cone

In any Graph 189 counterexample of connectivity s≥5 and second-smallest degree k≥25s,

    n ≥ (s+2)(k−s+1)−1.

This holds also when the unique below-k vertex is in a minimum cut. The resulting explicit five-connectivity theorem covers k≥125. For large s it gives traceability whenever k≥25s and (1−3/(s−2))k≥s²+3s+2. The coefficient of the leading quadratic threshold is one.

If a minimum-degree vertex belongs to no minimum cut, the displayed lower bound is root/universal-minimum-order.md. Otherwise choose such a vertex z on a minimum cut S. The two components A,B outside S are wholly high because any unique below-k vertex must then be z. The two-component theorem applies since k>6(s−1). Take a=|A|≤b=|B|. Every anchor touches both components. We have a≥k−s+1. Also δ(G)≥k−s+1: if δ<k, full closed neighborhoods of one z-neighbor in each component contain ≥2k+2 vertices counted with multiplicity, all in B₂[z] and with intersection contained in S. Thus 1+δ+k≥2k+2−s. For δ=k the bound is immediate.

The unconditional previously proved lower n≥(s+1)(k−s+2)−1 gives a+b≥(s+1)k−s²+1. The cross-pair inequality ab≤ka+sk² gives x=a/k≤(1+sqrt(1+4s))/2:=r and (a+b)/k≤1+x+s/x. In fact x<9/5: on [9/5,r] the convex upper expression takes its maximum at an endpoint, at most max(14/5+5s/9,1+sqrt(1+4s)). Both are less than 1+24s/25 for s≥5, whereas the lower bound gives (a+b)/k>1+24s/25 since k≥25s. We also use b≤k+sk²/(k−s+1)≤(1+25s/24)k, so b/(k−1)≤2s.

Complete A∪S into a clique to obtain F. Its minimum degree is at least k, because the only vertex needing repair was z and a+s−1≥k. By the critical-connectivity theorem of Chartrand–Kaugars–Lick and Mader there exists a vertex w of G for which G−w remains s-connected: the minimum degree of G exceeds 3s/2−1. Thus F−w is s-connected. Make w universal in F to obtain K, with κ(K)≥s+1 and δ(K)≥k. Every longest cycle of K uses w (otherwise insert it), and upon deleting w becomes a longest path P of H=F−w: any longer H path would close through w.

Suppose n+2≤(s+2)(k−s+1). Corollary A of Nikoghosyan at λ=s+1 implies that the exterior of P in H has no cycle of length ≥s+1. In particular, at most s vertices of the clique A∪S are in this exterior. The following argument returns to actual even-distance witnesses of G throughout.

Suppose exterior B vertices x,y are adjacent. Their adjacency and all their incident H edges are actual G edges. Write T_x,T_y for their contact positions on P, a_v=|T_v| and q_v=d_(H−P)(v). No contact lies at a path endpoint or is consecutive to a contact of the same exterior vertex. Set R to be the number of successors of T_x that are not predecessors of T_x. The two shifts provide a_x+R distinct witnesses in H. At most 2s of them may fail to be genuine distance-two witnesses in G: a path edge may be artificial only inside A∪S, so when a pivot is an x-neighbor the only possibly artificial shifts start at an S contact, of which x has at most s. Each remaining witness has actual distance two, as H-nonadjacency implies G-nonadjacency. The y-contacts avoid both shifts of T_x, since the intervening edge could be replaced using x,y. Each shared contact i has i+2 outside T_x: otherwise insert y,x in place of that two-edge segment. Hence |T_y∩T_x|≤R, so its ≥a_y−R remaining genuine contacts are at distance two from x via y. Finally at least q_y−q_x exterior neighbors of y are distinct from x and nonadjacent to x, and all of them lie at genuine distance two. The three sets of witnesses are disjoint. Therefore

    k ≥ a_x+a_y+q_y−q_x−2s ≥ a_x+k−1−q_x−2s.

Together with a_x+q_x≥k−1 this gives q_x≥ceil((k−2s−2)/2). Only ≤s of its exterior neighbors can be outside B, since x has no A neighbor. Thus every nontrivial component of the exterior induced on B has minimum degree ≥ceil((k−4s−2)/2)≥s. The beginning of a longest path in that component sends an edge to its furthest neighbor, producing a cycle of length at least s+1. This is impossible by Corollary A, so the exterior B vertices are independent.

An exterior B port x with r anchor neighbors has at least k−r−1 actual B neighbors on P, since only the removed w may be missing and no exterior B neighbor exists. Their distinct path successors are actual B or S vertices at genuine distance two from x: a path edge out of B was never added. The s anchors match to distinct A contacts by the minimum-cut Hall condition, since a≥s. The r corresponding A contacts contribute another r distinct genuine even witnesses. Hence at most one further witness is possible. In particular each label occurs on at most two omitted B ports, since such ports are pairwise nonadjacent and all see one another at distance two. At a nonport x at least k−1 genuine B successors suffice for the same one-slot conclusion. Thus at most two omitted nonports may share any particular B neighbor on P, and their count is at most 2b/(k−1)≤4s. Altogether there are at most 6s B vertices in H outside P, and possibly w in B.

It remains to lift the path in the completed graph. Keep its B vertices in order and their true B edges. Each run of vertices in A∪S bounded by B has genuine distinct anchor endpoints if its length is at least two. A singleton such run must be an anchor with two genuine B edges and is kept intact. A run at one endpoint of P has at most one prescribed anchor. Delete interior anchors in all other runs, freeing all A for replacement. Every prescribed anchor for these non-singleton runs occurs only once on P and has exactly one A-linkage obligation. Assign it a distinct actual A neighbor, simultaneously over all obligations, using the same matching from S into A. A singleton endpoint anchor may be retained and extended into A if needed. At least one run contains A, since at most s A vertices are exterior in H plus possibly w, and a≥k−s+1. At least one B vertex lies on P as b≥a>6s+1. Thus at most s of the new A runs require routing; each has two distinct A ends or a free end, and one can absorb every remaining A vertex.

To verify this last statement put d=a−δ(G[A]); each high vertex loses at most s neighbors on restriction to A. Thus 2δ(G[A])−a≥2(k−s)−a>k/5−2s≥3s. Equivalently a≥2d+3s+1. Reserve the assigned A terminals; for all but the last run use the actual edge between its ends or a fresh common neighbor if the ends are nonadjacent. Each pair has at least a−2d common neighbors, and fewer than 3s points are occupied by terminals and previous intermediates. A run with a free end may be shortened to its assigned vertex. The remaining induced A graph has order a'≥a−3s≥2d+1 and minimum degree ≥a'−d≥(a'+1)/2, hence is Hamilton-connected by the usual degree-sum closure. Fill the last run, choosing a different free endpoint when required. The resulting path is genuine in G even if w was in A (it may now be used), and all original B edges and B-to-anchor incidences were retained.

At most s anchors and 6s+1 B vertices are omitted. This is at most ceil(k/2)+1 at k≥25s. The known near-spanning path theorem contradicts nontraceability. Consequently n+2>(s+2)(k−s+1), proving the claim.

Combining with global/arity/arbitrary-cut-high-order.md gives traceability whenever k≥25s and k≥2s²+6s+2, in particular for s=5 and k≥125. Combining with global/arity/three-common-labels.md gives the displayed sharper large-s condition. The external inputs are the critical-connectivity theorem cited in global/arity/critical-removable-vertex.md and Zh.G. Nikoghosyan, On Some Versions of Conjectures of Bondy and Jung, Corollary A (Mathematical Problems of Computer Science 45 (2016), 20–21), https://noad.sci.am/Content/136032/03%20%281%29.pdf.

## A new marker and the sharper finite range

The completed-side argument on a cut containing z actually proves n≥(s+2)(k−s+2)−2 for k≥15s. No critical-connectivity theorem or removable vertex is required for this version. Complete A∪S as above to F, then simply adjoin a new universal marker g. Since F is s-connected and has minimum degree ≥k, the augmentation is (s+1)-connected and has minimum degree ≥k+1. Its order is n+1. A longest cycle always contains g, and after deleting g is a longest path of F. Under n+3≤(s+2)(k−s+2), Corollary A precludes a cycle of length s+1 in the exterior, as required. All B-neighbor successor shifts are now available (no original w was removed). In the nontrivial-exterior count degrees ≥k replace ≥k−1 and the improved real B exterior lower (k−4s)/2 is still ≥s. If the B exterior is independent then each exterior port with r incident labels has at least k−r real successors and r distinct matched A contacts, leaving no spare slots. Thus at most one omitted port touches each label. Each omitted nonport has at least k real successors and cannot share a B neighbor with any other omitted nonport. This gives at most s+b/k≤3s omitted B vertices.

The small-side stability condition a≤2k−5s−1 already holds at k≥15s. Otherwise x=a/k≥5/3, and the convex upper 1+x+s/x is at most max(8/3+3s/5,1+sqrt(1+4s))≤1+14s/15 for every s≥5. The earlier (a+b)/k>1+s−s²/k≥1+14s/15 is contradictory. Also b/k≤1+15s/14≤2s. The earlier Hall-based routing now applies verbatim as 2δ(G[A])−a≥2(k−s)−a≥3s+1. At most 4s vertices are missed, within the global near-spanning threshold. The two-component condition k>6(s−1) is automatic.

If a minimum-degree vertex avoids every minimum cut, one instead has the slightly weaker lower n≥(s+2)(k−s+1)−1 by root/universal-minimum-order.md, requiring only k≥25. Combining these branches gives the unconditional five-cut result k≥82 and six-cut result k≥110: use k≥15s and k≥2s²+6s+2 with the elementary high-order upper. In larger arity use k≥15s and (1−3/(s−2))k≥s²+3s+2. On the branch with a minimum-degree vertex on the cut the latter sufficient condition improves to (1−3/(s−2))k≥s²+2s+1.

## Sharper contact accounting for finite degrees

The new-marker proof applies already at k≥8s when s≥7, and k≥9s at s=5,6. In the A-routing lemma, after retaining the genuine singleton anchors there are h two-ended runs and f≤2 runs with a free path endpoint, where 2h+f≤s: each prescribed end uses a distinct actual anchor. If h≥1 save one double-ended run for expansion. The remaining h−1 pairs use at most three A vertices each, and the f one-ended short runs use one each. For a nonadjacent pair its own terminals are not common neighbors, and there are at most 2h+f−2 other terminals and h−2 earlier intermediate points to avoid. Thus the final Hamilton-connectedness condition 2δ(A)−a≥3h+f−2 suffices for both common-neighbor routing and the final residual. If h=0 at most two one-ended runs occur and 2δ(A)−a≥2 suffices. Maximizing over 2h+f≤s gives floor(3s/2)−2, including odd s by choosing f=1. Therefore a≤2k−floor(7s/2)+2 is enough.

At s≥7 and k≥8s, failure of this bound implies x≥25/16. The convex upper for (a+b)/k on the interval up to (1+sqrt(1+4s))/2 is then at most max(41/16+16s/25,1+sqrt(1+4s)), less than 1+7s/8 throughout s≥7. This contradicts the lower bound (a+b)/k>1+s−s²/k. For s=6 at k≥9s use x≥29/18; both endpoints similarly give less than 1+8s/9. At s=5 the existing moderate-order theorem gives a+b≥6k−15. Failure of the density cutoff implies a≥2k−14, while ab≤ka+5k² and a≤b give a≤2.8k. Throughout this interval the concave a(5k−15−a) exceeds 5k² at k≥45, as follows at its endpoints from (2k−14)(3k−1)>5k² and (2.8k)(2.2k−15)>5k². This contradicts the two side bounds. The exterior-degree and cycle thresholds need only k≥6s; the B omissions and unused anchors number at most 2s+b/k≤2s+1+8s/7≤ceil(k/2)+1 at k≥8s. This completes the sharper claim.
