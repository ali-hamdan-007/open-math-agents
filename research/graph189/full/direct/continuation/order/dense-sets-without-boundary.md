# A path through at most s+1 dense sets, without boundary assumptions

Let G be a finite s-connected graph, s≥2. There are t disjoint nonempty sets U_1,…,U_t, 1≤t≤s+1. Write m_j=|U_j| and assume δ(G[U_j])≥m_j−d_j. If m_j≥s and m_j≥2d_j+3t+1 for each j, then a path in G contains the entire union of the sets. If t≤s there is such a cycle, except for the harmless t=1 case where the same conclusion follows directly inside U_1. Additional specified vertices outside the sets are permitted, counting as one apiece toward t in both the number-of-objects limit and the displayed threshold. No bound on the vertex boundary of any set is required.

Complete each U_j to a clique, then add a distinct dummy w_j adjacent precisely to its U_j. The augmented graph remains s-connected: each addition of edges preserves connectivity and adding a vertex with at least s neighbors to an s-connected graph retains s-connectivity. The terminal list consists of the dummies and any separately prescribed vertices. By Dirac's cyclability theorem every list of at most s vertices lies on a cycle. To find a path for a list of at most s+1, add one further universal vertex to the augmented graph. Its connectivity is at least s+1, so a cycle through the whole list exists. Remove the universal vertex if present or open the cycle otherwise.

Choose a shortest simple path in the augmented graph containing the list; its two endpoints are terminals if the list has at least two members. For the cycle version choose a shortest simple cycle containing the list. Between successive terminals in the traversal, every U_j occurs in at most one contiguous run: if positions of that clique were separated by a point outside it then a clique edge skips that nonempty intervening segment without skipping a terminal. Here successive bounding terminals are excluded from the open gap; separate prescribed original vertices lie outside every U_j. There are at most t−1 such gaps for the path and t for the cycle. A cycle-shortcut cannot create a cycle with fewer than three vertices when there are at least two terminals outside the clique in question; these are the t≥2 cases (also its own dummy is outside). The t=1 conclusion follows directly since δ≥(m+1)/2 implies Hamiltonicity. The same internal argument proves the t=1 path statement.

Delete the dummies from the chosen traversal and treat each pair of former neighbors of the same deleted dummy as joined provisionally inside its own clique. If there is just one neighbor on a path, that vertex becomes an endpoint. This operation only merges existing runs of the dummy's clique, so each U_j is now met in at most t runs. External adjacencies into every clique are original edges, and no artificial edge connects two different sets. Each U_j has at least one run which can receive extra vertices: its dummy had either two distinct clique neighbors on the traversal, hence they now lie in a run with distinct vertices, or the dummy was a path endpoint and the resulting run has a free endpoint. If a path endpoint run consists of one vertex with an outside adjacency, it has only that one prescribed terminal; a different free endpoint can be chosen when expanding the run. In the case of only U_j and no exterior portion, an internal spanning path or cycle suffices.

For completeness take any U=U_j with r≤t runs, and reserve the original end vertices of every run where it has exterior incidences. There are at most 2r distinct prescribed terminals; singleton runs between two exterior contacts have just one. Terminals of different runs are distinct by simplicity. Designate one expandable run to be routed last. For all other runs, use one vertex for singleton runs and runs with at most one prescribed end. For a pair of distinct prescribed ends use their edge if present, otherwise a path of length two through a fresh common neighbor. There are at least m−2d common neighbors, and fewer than 3r are occupied by reserved terminals and previously used intermediates, so this can always be done. At most 3(r−1) vertices were used in these other runs. The leftover induced graph has order m'≥m−3r≥2d+1 and minimum degree ≥m'−d≥(m'+1)/2, so it is Hamilton-connected (the ordinary Ore degree-sum closure with two prescribed endpoints). Choose two distinct ends consistent with all prescriptions of the designated run; there are at most two, and in the doubly prescribed case the ends are distinct. The resulting spanning route of U supplies every run, without modifying vertices exterior to U or any edge between U and its exterior. This applies independently to all sets, so after expanding them all the resulting traversal is a genuine simple path or cycle in G.

A prescribed initial terminal is also allowed for a path through t≤s+1 objects, including a designated root dummy. In an s-connected graph a path from any one specified vertex x through any other s specified vertices exists. To prove this by extension, start with a rooted path covering as many specified vertices as possible, prolonged to have at least s vertices if necessary (or take a maximal rooted path through a maximum number of marks; its endpoint has degree ≥s and no neighbor off the path). To insert a missing mark y take an s-fan from y to distinct vertices on the path, whose interiors avoid the path. If a fan end is at or after the last present mark, append its reverse after retaining the prefix through that end. Otherwise the fan ends lie strictly before the last present mark. They belong to at most s−1 intervals between consecutive present marks, assigned half-open intervals from a mark inclusive to the next exclusive; at most s marks including x were present. Two ends in one interval give a detour through y between them without losing any mark. Both alternatives contradict maximality. Now choose a shortest path through the list starting at the prescribed terminal and apply the same clique shortcut. If its root is a dummy w_j and t≥2, the terminal count of the runs in U_j, AFTER removing the dummy, is at most 1+2(t−2)=2t−3: one exterior terminal for the initial run and at most two for a run in each later gap. In this count a singleton with two incidences counts as one terminal.
