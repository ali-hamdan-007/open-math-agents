# A small deep side at a minimum three-cut, with arbitrary low vertex

Let k≥24 be the second-smallest degree of a Graph 189 graph and let S be a cardinality-minimum cut of size three. Suppose G−S consists of A,B, where A has a nonport and a=|A|≤2k−5. The component B may consist entirely of ports. Then G has a Hamiltonian path, without restrictions on the other side's size, the anchors' degrees, or the location and degree of the unique low vertex. This extends the wholly-high-component proof in ../order/no-small-clean-three-cut.md. We use the previously proved unrestricted n≤5k−3 theorem, the high-pair distance bound of four and the fact that every edge in a counterexample belongs to a triangle.

Assume n≥5k−2; then m=|B|≥3k. Each anchor touches both sides by minimality. A port touches S and a nonport does not. First suppose A has a high nonport y. Let J consist of its anchors at distance two. If J is empty, every high B member is at least four from y and hence exactly four. Together with three second-layer witnesses by connectivity this is already impossible. If at most two A ports covered J with their anchor contacts, all B vertices touching J would be seen at even distance by those ports, and every high B member missing J would be at distance four from y. At least one high B member missing J exists, because the two ports' quotas cover at most 2k vertices whereas B has at least 3k−1 high vertices. The second layer of y has at least three vertices. Thus B would have at most 3k−3 high members, impossible. Therefore J is the full three anchors, and every A port is private; indeed a multiport and a contact at the third anchor would cover J. This works for every high A nonport. Every high B nonport is at distance two from some anchor, by its distance four to y. It is harmless if B's only nonport is low. Since a private port and its anchor are in a triangle, every A port class has at least two vertices and thus a high representative, even if the low vertex is itself a port.

Let d,e be the numbers of A,B nonports, and let p_i,q_i be the respective port counts at anchor i. Then Σp_i=a−d, Σq_i≥m−e. Put ρ=a−k≥1 because a high A nonport has at least k internal neighbors. Select a high A i-port u_i in each class and let t_i count its other anchors at distance two. Set T=Σt_i. Every pair of high A vertices is adjacent or at ambient distance two: their internal degrees are at least k−1 since all ports are private, and |A|≤2k−5. Each u_i misses at most ρ vertices of A other than itself by its internal degree. Let f_i count its A nonneighbors at ambient distance exactly two. Its quota gives f_i≤k−q_i−t_i.

Let L_B be the number of B nonports not at distance two from any anchor, hence at most one and possible only if the low vertex is in B. For each i charge every A vertex missing i at distance two from i through u_i when it is adjacent to u_i. The high nonneighbors lie in f_i. When there is a low A vertex z, it also causes no loss if it touches i, is at distance two from i, or is at distance at most two from u_i: in the last case it is a neighbor or is counted in f_i. Let L_A count the remaining possible exceptional anchor incidences of z, and set L=L_A+L_B. Thus the summed anchor caps give
```
3k ≥ Σ(a−p_i−f_i) + e − L
   ≥ 2a + d + m − 3k + T − L.
```
Equivalently n≤5k+3−ρ−d−T+L. It suffices to show ρ+d+T−L≥6. Initially ρ+d≤5+L≤8.

**Low absent from A.** Here L≤1 and ρ+d≤6. The largest class contains more than ρ A vertices, since its size is at least (k+ρ−d)/3 and k≥24. Choose representatives of the other two classes, each seeing a member of this largest class, then choose a largest-class representative adjacent to one of these two. This ensures T≥3. For ρ+d≥4 the desired inequality follows. At ρ+d≤3 the largest class has more than 2ρ members, so we may choose its representative adjacent to both selected representatives, giving T≥4. This suffices if ρ+d=3. At ρ=d=1 every representative misses at most one A vertex. Since each anchor class has at least two ports, every representative sees both other anchors at distance two. Then T=6.

**Low in A, with a high A nonport.** Put F={high v in A : d(v,z)>2}. The low z has a high internal neighbor because A is connected; the closed internal neighborhood of this high neighbor misses at most ρ A vertices. Hence |F|≤ρ. The largest class has more than ρ high members even after allowing one low, by k≥24 and ρ+d≤8. The previous three-incidence choice always gives T≥3, and L≤3. This suffices when ρ+d≥6.

If ρ+d=5 and z is a port, its own anchor causes no loss, so L≤2 suffices. If z is a nonport then d≥2 and ρ≤3. Choose high representatives on the two smaller classes. Choose a high representative on the largest class outside F and adjacent to the first smaller representative: the high membership of that class exceeds 2ρ. This gives T≥3 and L≤2.

At ρ+d=4 choose a high representative outside F in each smaller class whenever one is available. Choose the largest representative outside F adjacent to one of the selected smaller representatives; its high class size again exceeds 2ρ. Thus T≥3. If both smaller classes caused a loss then neither is the low vertex's own class and each has at least two high members, all in F. This contradicts |F|≤ρ≤3. Hence L≤1.

At ρ+d≤3 the largest class has more than 3ρ high members. Select the smaller representatives outside F when possible and then a large representative outside F adjacent to both, so T≥4. As above at most one loss occurs if ρ=2; if ρ=1, no class can cause a loss since each class other than the possible low vertex's own has at least two high members. These estimates prove ρ+d+T−L≥6 in all cases.

**The only nonport of A is its low vertex z.** Every neighbor of z is a high A port. For such a neighbor u let M(u) consist of the anchors i for which d(u,i)=d(z,i)−1. These sets cover S, since each shortest path from z to an anchor starts with a neighbor in A. If at most two such sets cover S, z and the selected neighbors see every B vertex at even distance between them. Indeed take an anchor i on a shortest z-to-v path for v in B, and choose u attaining the one-step improvement towards i. The triangle inequalities through z and i give d(u,v)=d(z,v)−1. Since z already sees an anchor at two via any neighbor, this implies m≤3k−1, impossible. Therefore each M(u) is a singleton: any set of size at least two could be completed by one further set. Every anchor touched by u is in M(u), so each neighbor is private, and all three anchors are at distance two from z because the singleton sets cover S.

Every high B nonport has an anchor at distance two. Otherwise all a−1 high A ports would be at distance four from it, in addition to at least three second-layer vertices. This would give a≤k−2. The degree of any high A port forces a≥k−2, and equality would make every such port internally universal and adjacent to all three anchors. That contradicts the private neighbors of z. It follows that z sees all B nonports at four (B is wholly high), and all three anchors at two; hence B has at least m−(k−3)>2k ports. A multiport in A and one contact at its possibly missing anchor would see every B port at even distance, impossible. All A ports are therefore private.

Now ρ=a−k≥0 by any high private A port's degree, d=1, and L=0 in the anchor count: z is at distance two from all three anchors, and every B nonport is charged. Thus ρ+1≤5. The largest class has more than 2ρ members since (k+ρ−1)/3>2ρ for ρ≤4 and k≥24. The representatives can give T≥4 as before, enough when ρ≥1. At ρ=0 every high private A port is universal within A, and all three representatives see both other anchors, so T=6. This rules out the final configuration.

## Anchor distances on a shallow side

The parity argument yields a restriction on the remaining shallow-side case. Assume only m≥3k and let y be any vertex of A. Moving to any one of its neighbors u cannot strictly decrease y's distances to two distinct anchors. Otherwise select u and a first step v of a shortest path from y to the remaining anchor. These two first steps suffice for all anchor distances. Thus every B vertex has an even distance from at least one of y,u,v, as in the preceding shortest-path argument. At least one of the two decreased anchor coordinates belongs to an anchor different from u. The distances from y and u to that anchor are consecutive and positive, so one of the three roots also sees a positive even witness outside B. This forces m≤3k−1.

In particular for any two vertices y,u in A, the sum over the three anchors i of max(0,d(y,i)−d(u,i)) is at most their internal A-distance, by adding the edge inequalities on an internal shortest path. For two high vertices this sum is at most two when |A|≤2k−5: each has internal degree at least k−3 even without private-port assumptions, so nonadjacent high vertices have an internal common neighbor. The same inequality holds with y,u exchanged. On a shallow A an edge ij inside S also forces all A ports to touch either both i,j or neither, by the forbidden double decrease when moving from an i-only contact to i. A port touching neither cannot neighbor a port touching both. Connectedness of A and the existence of an i-contact then force all A vertices to touch both i and j.
