# A small shallow component with exclusively private ports at a three-cut

Let k≥24 in Graph 189 and let S be a minimum three-vertex cut with two components A,B. If |A|≤2k−5 and each A vertex touches exactly one anchor of S, G has a spanning path, for any degree spectrum and order. There is no depth hypothesis on B. This settles the private-port alternative in the small-side three-cut classification.

Assume n≥5k−2 by the existing all-graph order theorem. Put a=|A|, m=|B|, so m≥3k. Each anchor touches A and B by minimality. Every edge in a counterexample lies in a triangle, so each private A port has another A port at the same anchor. Every anchor class therefore has at least two members and one is high. Every high A port has ≥k−1 internal neighbors, hence a=k+ρ for ρ≥0 and it misses at most ρ vertices of A apart from itself. Every two high A vertices are adjacent or have an internal common neighbor by a≤2k−5.

Every high B nonport is at distance two from at least one anchor. Otherwise it sees all ≥a−1≥k−1 high A vertices at distance four by the high-pair theorem, and its second layer has at least three members by 3-connectivity, a contradiction. Write p_i,q_i for A,B port counts touching i, e for the number of B nonports, and choose a high representative u_i in each A class. Let t_i count its other anchors at distance two and T=Σt_i. Let f_i count A nonneighbors of u_i at ambient distance two, excluding u_i itself. Then f_i≤k−q_i−t_i. At anchor i the adjacent representatives' neighbors which miss i are at distance two via u_i. All high A nonneighbors of u_i belong to f_i. When z∈A is low, a missing anchor incidence can occur only if z does not touch i, is not itself at distance two from i, and is at distance greater than two from u_i. Let L count these possible losses. There is never any loss at the anchor to which z belongs, so L≤2.

There is no net loss from a possible low B nonport z. If z is at distance two from an anchor it is already charged. Otherwise every neighbor of z is a high B nonport (adjacency to a port would put z at distance two from S). It follows that the distance from z to S is exactly three. At a closest anchor i the chosen representative u_i sees z at distance four, through i and at least four by any anchor route. This is distinct from u_i's other even charges, so f_i≤k−q_i−t_i−1 and this extra slot repays the one uncharged B nonport.

Since Σp_i=a and Σq_i≥m−e, the three anchor quotas therefore give
```
3k ≥ 2a + m − 3k + T − L,
n ≤ 5k + 3 − ρ − T + L.
```
In particular ρ≤5+L≤7. It suffices to choose representatives with ρ+T−L≥6.

If ρ≤1, every high representative sees both other anchors at two since both other classes have ≥2 members and each representative misses at most one A vertex. Then T=6. If a low z belongs to A, it has at least two internal neighbors since its ambient degree is at least three and it touches one anchor. A high vertex farther than two from z misses both these neighbors and z inside A. Thus no such vertex exists at ρ≤2, and L=0 there.

The largest class has at least ceil((k+ρ)/3) members. Even allowing for one low, its number of high members exceeds ρ throughout ρ≤7, and exceeds 2ρ for 2≤ρ≤4. Choose high representatives of both smaller classes. Each sees the largest anchor at distance two. Choosing a high representative in the largest class adjacent to one of them gives T≥3, and choosing it adjacent to both whenever the high membership exceeds 2ρ gives T≥4. For ρ≥5 this suffices even at L=2. At ρ=4 we have ρ+4−2=6. At ρ=2 we have L=0 and T≥4. At ρ=3 with no low A vertex the result likewise follows.

Finally let ρ=3 with low z∈A. Its internal neighborhood contains a high member, whose closed internal neighborhood misses at most three A vertices. Thus at most three high A vertices are farther than two from z; call this set F. Select smaller-class representatives outside F whenever possible. If the largest class is z's own then it never incurs a loss, and the two smaller classes cannot both be contained in F since each has at least two high members. Choose the common large representative to obtain T≥4 and L≤1. If the largest is not z's own class, only one of the smaller classes besides z's own can incur a loss. If it offers a near representative, L≤1 regardless of the large choice. Otherwise it consumes at least two of the three elements of F. The largest class is wholly high of order at least nine, so the two smaller representatives have at least three common neighbors there; at least one is outside the remaining F. This again gives T≥4 and L≤1. All cases give ρ+T−L≥6, contradicting n≥5k−2.
