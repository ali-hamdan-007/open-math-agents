# A three-cut with a wholly high component of order 3k−9

Suppose G satisfies Graph 189, k≥24, and deleting a minimum cut S={p,q,r} of size three leaves components A,B with |A|≤2k−5 and |B|=3k−9. If B is wholly high, G has a spanning path. The low vertex may belong to A or the cut, and the anchors' degrees and incidences are unrestricted. Use the previously established through-4k+1 order theorem at connectivity three, the high-pair distance bound of four, and the almost-spanning theorem. Each anchor touches each side since S is a minimum cut. A port is a vertex adjacent to S. For anchor a write p_X(a) for its number of contacts in X. Each p_X(a)≤k by an opposite a-contact's even quota.

**Independent sets in B.** In any independent set I in B of size at least k−1, every pair shares a common internal neighbor. Otherwise two internal neighborhoods of size at least k−3 would be disjoint within B−I, requiring |B|≥3k−7. An I port spends at least k−2 even places on peers. Thus each anchor a it touches has at most two A contacts. Write i_a for its number of incident I vertices, and take an I port adjacent to a. Its ≥k−3 internal neighbors include at most p_B(a)−i_a other a-contacts by independence. Thus a sees at least k−3−p_B(a)+i_a B vertices at distance two.

If a has a high A contact, the internal closed neighborhood of that contact has size ≥k−2, giving ≥k−2−p_A(a) vertices at distance two from a in A. When its only A contact z is low, take a high A neighbor v of z; in the applications A contains a high vertex and is connected. The set N_A[v] has ≥k−2 members within two steps from z. At least p_B(a) of z's even places are already spent in B, hence |N_A[z]|≥p_B(a)−2. Thus a sees at least p_B(a)−2−p_A(a) A members at distance two. The latter lower bound holds also in the high-contact case since p_B(a)≤k. The quota at a therefore gives i_a≤5+p_A(a)≤7. Counting the three incidences shows I has at most 21 ports. In particular it has a nonport for k≥24.

**A has a high nonport.** Every B nonport is then exactly four from it, and it has at least three distance-two witnesses, a separating layer. Thus B has ≤k−3 nonports. Suppose I has size k−1 and take a nonport x in I. The high nonport in A is four from x; any shortest such route goes through an anchor two steps from each. Along with its k−2 independent peers x has used all its even places. It sees precisely one anchor a at two and precisely one high A nonport at four. Every high A port missing a would be at least four from x through any anchor and exactly four by the high-pair bound. Thus all high A ports touch a. Since A's high nonport has all k or more neighbors in A, |A|≥k+1, and at least k−1 A vertices touch a (allowing the one possible low).

Let u be an a-contact adjacent to x along its two-step route. It touches no other anchor since x sees only a at two. Its ≥k−1 A witnesses leave ≤1 other even place. If u were internally universal in B, it would see the other two anchors at distance two through their B contacts. Otherwise an internal shortest path to a nonneighbor supplies a B vertex w at distance two, exhausting the remaining quota. No member of N_B[u] can touch either other anchor, and its external neighborhood in B consists only of w. Removing a,w separates N_B[u] from A, contradicting connectivity three. Thus α(B)≤k−2.

Form H from B and a marker adjacent to all B ports. Identifying the connected outside A∪S to this marker preserves 2-connectivity: after deleting a B vertex, project ambient paths, and deleting the marker leaves B connected. Every high B vertex retains degree ≥k−2, gaining the marker if any anchor was lost. The marker has ≥|B|−(k−3)=2k−6 neighbors. Independent sets containing the marker have size ≤1+(k−3), and α(B)≤k−2. Therefore δ(H)≥max(α(H),(|H|+2)/3), and the classical Nash–Williams theorem gives a Hamilton cycle. Removing the marker yields a spanning B path whose endpoints are ports.

**A has no high nonport.** At n≤4k+1 the moderate-order theorem applies. Otherwise |A|≥k+8, with at least k+7 high ports. No B port can touch all three anchors since it would see all these A ports at two. Hence δ(B)≥k−2. Every B nonport x has an anchor two steps away: otherwise it sees all those high A ports at four. Suppose an independent set I of size k exists, and take its nonport x by the preceding count. Its k−1 peers and one anchor consume the full quota; the unique nearest anchor would have to touch every high A port by the same four-distance argument, contrary to p_A(a)≤k. Thus α(B)≤k−1.

If B is 2-connected then δ(B)≥(|B|+2)/3, so Nash–Williams' dominating-cycle theorem makes every longest B cycle dominating. If a vertex x were outside such a cycle, all its neighbors would lie on the cycle. The set consisting of x and all the successors of its cycle neighbors is independent: consecutive neighbors of x extend the cycle, and a chord between two successors permits reversing the intervening arc and adding x between their predecessors. Hence d_B(x)≤k−2. Because x is high and there are no triple ports, it has exactly two anchor neighbors and exactly k−2 internal neighbors. Its k−2 independent peers are all at internal distance two by the independent-set count. Thus only two high A ports in total can touch its anchor pair. All the remaining ≥k+5 high A ports touch the third anchor, impossible. Therefore B is Hamiltonian and has a spanning path from any port. The dominating-cycle theorem is stated in [Theorem 9 of Bauer et al., Toughness in Graphs – A Survey](https://cfc.nankai.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/c6/e1/a2c52bf04b1896f59003b5993582/446d9b25-e3ac-4a8e-a763-04a2cb68565c.pdf), alongside the Hamiltonicity criterion as Theorem 10.

If B has a cut vertex, each endblock has ≥k−2 noncuts and one articulation by minimum degree. Its size 3k−9 allows exactly two leaves and excludes an intermediate block with any noncut (such a block would need ≥k−1 vertices separate from both leaf interiors). Each endblock has a noncut port by connectivity of G, and its size is at most 2k−7. Its high noncut core has minimum degree ≥k−3 and order at most 2k−8, hence is Hamilton-connected. From the leaf articulation and any designated noncut, disjoint paths to distinct core landing points followed by its Hamilton path give a spanning leaf route. Joining these through the articulation chain gives a spanning B path beginning at a port.

**Joining A.** Choose an endpoint port of the B path and an adjacent anchor u. In A together with u and a leaf attached to u, every high A vertex retains degree ≥k−2 because only two anchors were discarded. The traceability-closure threshold is |A|+1≤2k−4, so the high A vertices can be completed to a clique. If there is no low vertex in A, this gives a spanning path beginning at the leaf and u. If the only u-contact is low z, proceed through z and an internal neighbor into the high clique. If u has a high contact e and z has a high internal neighbor y≠e, choose these as ends of the high clique route and finish at z. If z is itself a u-contact, one can also start u,z and immediately enter the high clique. The only remaining case has z not adjacent to u and sets N_A(u)=N_A(z)={e}. Delete z; every other high vertex except e keeps its previous degree, so the remaining vertices other than e,u and the leaf complete to a clique by closure. The vertex e has a high internal neighbor (its ambient degree is ≥k); start at the leaf,u,e and that high neighbor, then finish in the clique. Reversing closures supplies the original path in each case, missing at most z. Joining at u to the B path misses at most the other two anchors and z, within the almost-spanning bound. This proves the assertion.

The independent-set light-anchor estimate and its low-contact version are due to the parallel global-metric analysis. The endpoint-marker argument, two-vertex-cut contradiction, and shallow-A dominating-cycle case complete the boundary.
