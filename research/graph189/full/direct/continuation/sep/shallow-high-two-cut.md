# All separating pairs whose two components have high vertices only

Write p,q for a nonadjacent separating pair of a finite 2-connected graph under the Graph 189 hypotheses, k for the second-smallest degree, and C,D for the two components after deleting p,q. A port is adjacent to at least one of p,q. Call it common if adjacent to both, and private otherwise. Each side meets both anchors by 2-connectivity. If every component vertex has degree at least k, there is a spanning path. This completes the all-high-component case by adding the following arguments to the previously established deep-on-both-sides and shallow-private theorems. The proof uses the previously reviewed bound through order 3k+2 and the high-pair distance bound of four. Small k≤3 in the low-order situations below is also covered directly by the stated inequalities.

**A shallow D opposite a common C port.** Suppose every vertex of D is a port and C contains a common port h. The argument allows the possible low vertex in either component, provided D contains a high vertex. Write d=|D|≥k−1 by the degree of that vertex. From h all d vertices of D are at distance two, so d≤k. The torso on C,p,q formed by adding pq is 2-connected: after deleting any vertex its connectivity follows by compressing excursions into D on paths in the ambient graph. Its distances from h agree with ambient distances, as paths using an anchor can start directly from h at their last anchor. The torso consequently has at most k−d≤1 vertices at distance two from h. A nonempty third layer would make the preceding layer a cut of size at most one. Hence at most k−d vertices of C are outside the closed neighborhood of h. Every C vertex missing p outside these exceptions is at distance two from p, and q is another such witness. A p-contact in D sees all C p-contacts at distance two. Thus |C|≤k+(k−1)+(k−d), proving |G|≤3k+1. If D has no high vertex, it is a degree-two singleton and the established minimum-degree-two theorem applies.

It remains to handle a shallow D with a common port, opposite a common-free C, with all vertices outside the anchors high. Otherwise the previously established deep or shallow-private cases apply. Put P for the C ports, R=C−P, and r=|R|. A common D port sees P, so |P|≤k. Each anchor meets at most k−1 vertices of D. For example, if p met k then a C p-port would already see all these vertices at distance two. It would be universal within C, as otherwise a shortest internal path to a nonneighbor gives another distance-two witness. Its adjacency to a C q-port would then give q itself as another witness.

**Both kinds of private ports in D.** Let a,b,t be the numbers of p-only, q-only and common D ports. Here a,b,t≥1 and d=a+b+t≤2k−2−t. Each private D port has internal degree at least k−1. It sees the opposite anchor at distance two: otherwise all of its internal neighbors would be from its own private class, which has at most k−1 members. Each common port has internal degree at least k−2. If t≥2 then 2(k−2)≥d. If t=1, the sum of the internal degrees of any two distinct vertices is at least 2k−3≥d. Thus Ore's theorem gives a Hamiltonian cycle in D (d≥3). Some consecutive pair on that cycle can be assigned opposite anchors: for example, around a common vertex any neighboring vertex allows a distinct assignment. Cutting the cycle there gives a spanning p,D,q path.

Every C nonport is at distance two from an anchor. Otherwise its distance to every high D vertex is at least four, hence exactly four, and the at least k−1 vertices of D, together with at least two vertices in its second layer (a separator before its nonempty third layer), exceed its cap. Write W for the nonports at distance two from both anchors, and R_p,R_q for those with unique nearest anchor p or q. A p-only D port sees p-contacts of C and q itself at distance two, and all R_q at distance four. For the latter, entry through p has length at least 1+3 and through q at least 2+2. Apply the symmetric count at a q-only D port. Since C has only private ports this gives |C|≤2k−2+|W|.

If r≤k, form C together with a marker m adjacent to P, and a leaf at m. The traceability-closure threshold (order minus one) is |C|+1. The marker has degree |P|+1 and any nonport has degree at least k, allowing the addition of all marker–nonport edges by path closure. All C vertices have degree at least k after this (the private ports retain their degrees upon substituting m for their anchor). If |C|≤2k−1 they can now complete to a clique by closure. At the only larger possibility |C|=2k we have |P|=r=k. The k nonports now have degree at least k+1 and can become universal within C by closure; one can alternate the k nonports with the k remaining vertices. We have a spanning path in the completed graph starting at the leaf. Reversing the closures preserves traceability, and the leaf forces the next vertex to be m followed by an original port. Hence C has an original spanning path starting at a port.

Suppose r≥k+1. The positive-even budgets of p and q together count r+|W|, all a+b opposite D private contacts, and the two anchor-to-anchor incidences via the common D port. Therefore |W|≤k−a−b−3≤k−5 and |C|≤3k−7. Every C vertex sees at least two positive-even vertices outside C. For a private port take a common D port and a D private port meeting the same anchor; for a doubly reached nonport take both anchors; for a singly reached nonport take its nearest anchor and a D private port assigned to the other anchor. The last of these is at distance four by the preceding argument. We have δ(C)≥k−1. If k vertices of C were independent, every pair would have a common neighbor in C: otherwise their neighborhoods and the independent set would total at least 3k−2 vertices. Each of them would then see the other k−1 plus the two external witnesses, a contradiction. Thus α(C)≤k−1. If C is 2-connected, Nash-Williams' condition δ(C)≥max((|C|+2)/3,α(C)) yields a Hamiltonian cycle, which can be opened at a port.

If C is not 2-connected, each leaf block has at least k−1 noncut vertices, since every such vertex has at least k−1 neighbors inside its block. There are exactly two leaf blocks, by |C|≤3k−7. An internal block with a noncut vertex would have at least k vertices, disjoint from the noncut interiors of the two extremal blocks, which is impossible. Consequently any internal block is a bridge, and two consecutive such bridges would give a degree-two vertex, contrary to δ(C)≥k−1; the bound |W|≤k−5 ensures k≥5. In each leaf block B choose an interior port u, which must exist since its articulation v cannot be a cut vertex of G. We have |B|≤|C|−(k−1)≤2k−6. Append a marker adjacent to u,v in B. The noncut vertices have degree at least k−1, so Hamiltonian closure completes them to a clique since 2k−2≥|B|+1. The articulation has at least two neighbors in B. A cycle goes from the marker to u, through the clique ending with a neighbor of v other than u, through v, and back to the marker. Closure reversal gives a spanning u,v-path in B. Join the two block paths at the common articulation or across the sole intervening bridge. This gives the required port-rooted spanning path of C. Concatenating it with the spanning p,D,q segment in the matching orientation spans G.

**When D has a common port and lacks one private type.** One anchor then meets all of D, whence d≤k−1 by the contact bound. High D vertices need d≥k−1. At d=k−1 a private vertex cannot be high, so D is K_{k−1} complete to the two anchors. The following order argument in fact requires no high-degree condition on C. Every C port sees all k−1 vertices of D at distance two. If a p-port e has a neighbor that is a q-port, then q takes its last even place. The vertex e must therefore be universal in C by the internal shortest-path argument. Since |P|≤k and C has both private types, C has at most k−1 p-contacts. The other C vertices are counted by p at distance two through e, giving |C|≤2k−1.

It remains that the two C port types are anticomplete. Fix a p-port e. A q-port is an internal nonneighbor, so connectedness of C yields a vertex w of C at distance two from e along C. It is the sole ambient distance-two vertex outside D and the even cap is exhausted. In particular there is no fourth layer from e. Put U=N_C[e] and V=C−U−{w}. Every v∈V is at distance three from e and must be adjacent to w: its penultimate on a shortest path cannot belong to D. There is no U–V edge. If V is empty, p counts all C vertices missing p except possibly w, and q through D, giving |C|≤2k. If V is nonempty it contains a port, since otherwise w would be a cut vertex of G. Such a port must be a q-port f, as a p-port would be at distance two from e via p.

There is at least one U neighbor of w. All such neighbors are at distance two from f and f already sees the k−1 vertices of D at distance two. Thus w has just one U neighbor w'. All vertices of U−{w'} are at distance three from f and are adjacent to w', as D and w' exhaust the second layer and vertices in D have no neighbor in C. The vertex w' sees all V at distance two (via w), and w sees all U−{w'} (via w'). Hence |V|≤k and |U|−1≤k. If |C|>2k+1 then both bounds are equalities. Now w' has exhausted its even-distance budget on V and must be adjacent to p, which it reaches in at most two steps via e. Similarly w must be adjacent to q via f. But w'w is an edge, contradicting anticompleteness between C private types. Thus |C|≤2k+1 and |G|≤3k+2, proving traceability.
