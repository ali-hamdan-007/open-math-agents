# Traceability at second-smallest degrees seventeen and eighteen

Every Graph 189 graph with second-smallest degree seventeen or eighteen has a spanning path. The following estimates use only connectivity s≥4 and hence supplement the previously solved lower connectivities. The geodesic upper orders U are 107 and 115. All block notation is as in k14-k15.md. In particular h=ceil(k/2)=9 for both degrees.

In the near-Hamilton-connected exterior, the refined result ../../verify/near-hc-exterior-size.md gives 3j<k and m≥k−floor(j/2)+2 for the contact matching j≥4. Thus j=4 or 5, and j=5 would require m≥k and n≥7k+4>U. At j=4 the matched bound n≥6m+3 forces m=k. For clarity, in the refined size proof the eligible high vertex c has z≥j−r+1 missed real marks and at least one interior nonneighbor, where r=m−1+j−k. Indeed each high interior nonneighbor of c needs at least k−m+2=j−r+1 real marks, having lost c from its possible internal neighbors. Otherwise all such nonneighbors share one of c's marks and the j-slot rooted cover contradicts the matched lower bound. Now r=3, so z≥2 and 1≤b≤2r−j−u−1≤1, with u the number of covered C vertices. Hence u=0, b=1, z=2 and d(c)=k. At most j+b=5 neighbors cover its second layer, and n≤1+2k+5(k−4), which is 100 or 107, less than 6k+3.

For a small endblock, n≥(J+2)b+26, J≥max(3,k−b+1), b≥10. Only b=k−2 or k−1 with J=3 survives. The four-element boundary argument of k14-k15.md gives a high noncut root with d≤k+1 and a second-layer cover of size at most four. Then n≤1+k+(k+1)+4(k−3), which is 92 or 98. The small-block lower is at least 101 or 106 respectively.

For large endblocks and whole components, the preliminary cycle bound is n≥9q+7 using only m≥2q in the whole case; the endblock has a further h−3. As before r≤2q−k+1, r≥2, and I outside H=N_C[x], R and the possible exceptional block vertex must be nonempty whenever b≥2q−1 and q−r−3>0. This holds throughout the range left by the preliminary cycle bound (q≤11 or 12 respectively). Therefore b≥2q+2 by an interior vertex's q block neighbors outside H. The whole-component cycle count improves to n≥9q+9. Write l=1 when the low vertex lies in H, otherwise l=0. The smaller core H* of high H members is Hamilton-connected for each remaining row, and also for the preliminary q-values not excluded before using this core bound: after the improved cycle count q≤10 at k=17, q≤11 at k=18, and 2(k−q−l)≥q+2−l. The H*-matching J obeys J≥k−q−r+1. Applying the bound

    n ≥ m₀ + J + (J+1)(q+1−l) + 2(q−r+1+d)

from k14-k15.md, with m₀=2q+2 in a whole or 2q+2+h in an endblock, gives the complete table below. As in the earlier proof, d=1 if l=1 or in a whole with the maximum r=2q−k+1, and zero otherwise. The low can be in H only at r≤2q−k.

In any minimum H*-contact cover the high vertex x is covered, since it needs at least k−q=7 path neighbors in the table and J≤5. If the number of actual marks equals k−q−r, every uncovered H* member, written collectively as W, must touch those marks and every other member of H∪R. Enlarge H* to K=H*∪R, contained in B since its R vertices then have several B neighbors. In all rows concerned W is a universal majority large enough that 2|W|≥|K|+1. Thus K is Hamilton-connected.

At k=17 and r=3 only four real marks are possible and this is their mandatory number. We have |K|=13, J=5 and m≥22, giving n≥22+5+6·13+2·8=121 from tails reaching I, above U. At r=4 there are necessarily three marks; |K|=15,J=4 and each tail gains at least six beyond K, giving n≥22+4+5·15+12=113. This covers the endblock too. At k=18,r=3 the same expansion yields |K|=14, J=5, hence n≥24+5+6·14=113 before the two nontrivial tail gains, each at least nine. At r=4 with three real marks, |K|≥15,J≥4 and each tail gains at least q−r=7, giving n≥24+4+5·15+14=117>U.

The case k=18,r=4 with four real marks must have J=5,l=1. All ten W=H*−x members miss at most one among H∪R and these marks. Since there are at most ten missing W-to-R edges, the best two of the four R vertices each have at least seven neighbors in W. Adjoin them to H*, getting K of order thirteen and minimum degree at least seven. It is Hamilton-connected by its degree sum. The five direct H* contacts and two tails extending into I yield n≥24+5+6·13+2·8=123.

At k=18,r=5 the low is on P and C is wholly high. With two actual marks, the above universal-majority case gives K=H∪R of order 17. Four disjoint connectors from K to P give a base of m+4+5·17≥113. The two pieces adjoining each connector both receive the extra length of its interior. If these connector interiors have total size at least two, the gain is at least four. If exactly one connector has a single internal vertex, at least one of the outer two connectors is direct. Its tail can traverse all K and continue through I in B−v, adding at least one to the two already gained. If all connectors are direct, both outer tails can continue through I, whose minimum degree is at least q−r−1=5. They together gain at least twelve. In every case n≥116>U.

The final case k=18,r=5 with three actual marks has J=4 and W=H−x of order eleven. Each W member misses at most one edge among H∪R and the three marks. The best three of the five R vertices have at least eight W neighbors apiece since four or more missing at the third best would cost at least twelve missing W-to-R edges. With these three added to H, K has order fifteen and minimum degree at least eight. Its four direct H matches in a whole therefore yield n≥24+4+5·15+2·7=117. In an endblock there are nine additional exterior vertices and the tail terminals can lose only one apiece. This completes both degrees.
