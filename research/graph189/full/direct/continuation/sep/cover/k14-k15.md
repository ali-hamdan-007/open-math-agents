# Traceability at second-smallest degree fourteen and fifteen

Every connected finite graph satisfying the Graph 189 even-distance bound at second-smallest degree fourteen or fifteen has a Hamiltonian path. Connectivity at most three is covered by the earlier results. Connectivity at least five is covered by two-below-final.md and the preceding higher-connectivity theorems. This proof treats connectivity four. The quadratic upper bound U is 85 at k=14 and 95 at k=15, and δ≥k+n−U. No finite solver is used.

Take a longest path maximizing the sum of squared exterior component orders and choose its smallest high-bearing exterior component C. Put h=ceil(k/2). Use the previously established exterior layer, terminal, and matching results in ../k10/uniform-matching.md, ../../global/max-degree-plus-two-exception.md and local-cover-contacts.md.

## Small endblocks

If C has a cut vertex choose an endblock B of order b, with articulation w and all its noncut vertices high. Let q≥h be their minimum B-degree. The complement of B in C contains a high vertex and has at least h vertices. At b≤2q−2 the matching J between the noncuts and the path is at least max(3,k−b+1), with

    n ≥ (J+2)b+3h−1.

For k=14 and b≥8 the only admissible pairs (b,J) are (12,3) and (13,3). For k=15 and b≥9 they are (12,4), (13,3) and (14,3). In the k=14,b=13 and k=15,b=12 cases the lower bound is U, hence all vertices are high, improving the two tail lengths by one each. In the b=k−2,J=3 cases each noncut requires three marks and all other B vertices. The minimum cover wholly consists of these three marks, since there are more than three noncuts. The noncuts form a clique complete to the four-element set S consisting of the marks and w. These are their only outside neighbors. Every noncut c has degree k and its distance-two layer is covered by S. Its local order bound is 1+2k+4(k−3), which is 73 or 79 and contradicts the respective lower bounds 80 or 88.

Only k=15,b=14,J=3 remains. Each noncut needs at least two marks. A mixed minimum cover consists of two real marks and one noncut u. The other twelve noncuts must meet both marks and all thirteen other B vertices. They again form a clique complete to a four-set (the marks, u, w), with degree 15 and upper order 79. In the pure case the thirteen noncuts are separated from the rest by S consisting of the three marks and w. Each misses at most one neighbor among the other twelve noncuts and S. If any misses an S member, it is universal among noncuts and has degree 15. Its other three S neighbors cover its second layer except possibly that missed S member, which when at distance two can be covered by one chosen first step. If every noncut meets all S, every internal nonneighbor is reached through S and the noncut degree is at most 16. In both cases the root has a second-layer cover of size at most four, so n≤80<93.

## Large blocks and whole components

The argument below treats either a large endblock with b≥2q−1, or a 2-connected whole C=B with b≥2q. In the whole case q is its least internal degree among high members. In an endblock take a minimizing noncut high x; in the whole choose any minimizing high x. The packing bounds n≥9q+4+h for an endblock and n≥9q+7 for the whole leave q≤8 at k=14 and q≤9 at k=15. Write H=N_C[x] and R for all ambient distance-two vertices of x in C, with r=|R|≤2q−k+1; equality in this upper bound needs the possible low on P. Every member of H is in B. The block second layer from x is contained in R, so r≤1 would give b≤q+2 by 2-connectivity, inconsistent with either size hypothesis. Consequently q=7 at k=14 is impossible. In the remaining cases r≥2.

The set H* consists of the high vertices of H and has order q+1−l, where l=1 if the low lies in H and is zero otherwise. The optimized local-neighborhood inequality gives minimum degree at least k−q−l in H* (the original proof uses distinct path witnesses for adjacent high vertices). This makes H* Hamilton-connected in all cases here by 2(k−q−l)≥|H*|+1. Its members have at most q+r neighbors in C because all C neighbors of H lie in H∪R. Thus every H* vertex has at least k−q−r path contacts and x has at least k−q. Its contact matching J is at least k−q−r+1: a cover of size k−q−r must consist of marks since H* has strictly more vertices, and then x has too many contacts.

For an endblock let its exceptional vertex be w. For a whole C take the low C vertex, if present, as the sole exception. Let I be B outside H, R and any exceptional vertex. There are at least q−r−3>0 such vertices. Every I vertex has at least q neighbors within B, none in H, implying b≥(q+1)+(q+1)=2q+2. Further, the minimum degree in I is at least q−r−1, improved by one when there is no exception outside H∪R. For each matched endpoint v, connectivity of B−v supplies a route from H*−v to I, trimmed from its last H* vertex to its first I vertex. It has an intermediary since there are no H–I edges. After traversing all H* to the initial point of that route, extend through it and a path rooted inside I. Each tail therefore gains at least q−r+1+d vertices beyond H*, where d can be taken as one when l=1 or when B=C and r=2q−k+1, and zero otherwise. Indeed if l=1 the exception is in H, including for an endblock because all noncuts are high. In the second condition C is all high. Put m₀=2q+2 for a whole, or 2q+2+h for an endblock. We obtain

    n ≥ m₀ + J + (J+1)(q+1−l) + 2(q−r+1+d).

At q≤8 or 9 as above, 2≤r≤2q−k+1, and l possible only when r≤2q−k, this leaves the following cases. In the row where the displayed bound equals U at the next J and l=1, there is a low vertex, so equality at U is excluded.

| k | q | B | r | l | Remaining J |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | 8 | whole | 3 | 0 | 4 |
| 15 | 9 | whole | 3 | 0 or 1 | 4 |
| 15 | 9 | endblock | 3 | 1 | 4 |
| 15 | 9 | whole or endblock | 4 | 0 | 3 or 4 |

For r=3 the cover of size four has exactly three path marks and x, since all H* members need three contacts and x needs six. All the other high H* members therefore touch all these marks and every other vertex in H∪R to get degree k=q+r+3. Thus K=H*∪R lies in B and is Hamilton-connected: the at least eight members of H*−x (also eight at k=14) are universal in K, of order at most thirteen. Every R member lies in B as it has at least two neighbors of the endblock, if that case applies. The four original matched contacts persist. Each outer tail from a matched v can traverse all K and continue through I because B−v is connected. The extra length beyond K is at least q−r+d (an initial intermediary is no longer required). In the k=14 row this gives n≥18+4+5·12+2·6=94. At k=15, l=0 gives n≥20+4+5·13+2·6=101, and l=1 gives n≥20+4+5·12+2·7=98. Endblocks only increase m₀.

There remains k=15,q=9,r=4,l=0. Here C is wholly high, |H|=10, and every H vertex needs at least two marks. Since x needs six, it belongs to every size-three or size-four cover. If the cover contains just two real marks then every uncovered H member touches those two marks and all other members of H∪R to reach degree 15. At least eight such uncovered members are universal in K=H∪R of order fourteen, so K lies in B and is Hamilton-connected. Four disjoint connectors from K to P exist by four-connectivity. Their four marks and five pieces cost at least 4+5·14 path vertices. If one connector is not an edge, its internal vertices increase both adjacent piece lengths, including when one is an outer tail, giving at least two extra vertices. If all four are edges, the block B has vertices outside K, and B−v is connected for each matched core v. Each of the two tails can therefore append at least one vertex to its traversal of K. Thus n≥20+4+5·14+2=96>U in either case.

The only other cover has three marks and x, with J=4. Each of the nine W=H−x misses at most one member among all other H and R vertices and the three marks, by its required degree. Among the four members of R choose the two with most W neighbors. Both have at least six, and at least one has seven or more. Indeed at most nine W-to-R edges are missing, hence the second smallest missing count is at most three and the smallest at most two. These two R vertices belong to B. Add them to H to obtain K of order twelve. Every member of H has degree in K at least seven: x is adjacent to the other nine of H, and every W member misses at most one of its other nine H vertices. The added R vertices have degrees at least seven and six respectively. Thus K is 2-connected (its minimum degree is at least half the order), and the terminal-marker closure lemma, with the sole exception of degree at least six, makes K Hamilton-connected. The four direct H matches remain. In a whole C, I has minimum degree at least 9−4=5, so each K tail gains at least six vertices. In an endblock each gains at least five even when its articulation is outside H∪R. Consequently n≥20+4+5·12+12=96 for a whole, or n≥28+4+5·12+10=102 for an endblock. All the large cases have been excluded.

## The near-Hamilton-connected exterior

We have C 2-connected with m≤2q−1. By local-cover-contacts.md, 3j<k and m≥k−j+3 for its maximum contact matching j. Connectivity four forces j=4 at both k values. Set r=m−1+4−k in this paragraph only. The count n≥6m+3 leaves k=14,m=13,r=2, or k=15,m=14,15,r=2,3. In those cases δ≥10,7,13 respectively.

Use the cover notation from local-cover-contacts.md. There are u covered C vertices Y, 4−u real marks M and a high c∈C−Y. Let b count its internal nonneighbors and z its missed real marks. Then b+z≤r−u and the available second-layer cover size is at most 4+b. Here 4+b≤floor((k+1)/2), so retaining the exact quadratic term in the order bound gives

    n_lower − n_upper ≥ 1+4(r−ε)+b(8−k+b).

Here ε is zero if C is Hamilton-connected and one if there is a terminal obstruction. The latter can arise only from a low C vertex of internal degree two. If such a low lies outside Y, its ambient degree is at most 2+4=6, inconsistent with the stated lower bounds. Thus ε=1 implies u≥1 and every member of C−Y is high.

At ε=0 the displayed difference is positive through all allowed b except possibly k=15,r=2,b=2. If some uncovered high c has smaller b, use it instead. Otherwise b=2 everywhere among these high vertices, forcing u=z=0. All high members then touch all four marks. Choose a high member adjacent to the low C vertex if one exists. Its internal nonneighbors are high and share a mark with it, so four neighbors cover the entire second layer. This replaces f(4+b) by f(4) in the local bound, making the difference positive.

At ε=1, the displayed difference is positive for b≤r−2 in all cases, as substitution shows. With u≥1, any remaining possibility has u=1 and b=r−1 for every uncovered high vertex, so all these vertices touch the three marks of M. In this case four neighbors again cover the whole second layer of c. Its internal nonneighbors outside Y share these marks, and the single member of Y can either be selected directly if it is adjacent or be covered by one neighbor if at distance two. The latter case requires no first step through Y. With p≤4 the difference is at least 1+4(r−ε)+b>0. This eliminates the final possibility and establishes both degree theorems.
