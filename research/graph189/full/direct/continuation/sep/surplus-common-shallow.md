# Small surplus at a separating pair with a common port or shallow component

Suppose a Graph 189 graph has a separating pair p,q of degrees at most k+1. It is traceable if a component is shallow or has a common port. This statement imposes no bound on other high degrees or the unique low degree. We use the reductions and terminology of exact-degree-two-cut.md, including 2-connectivity, exactly two components, nonadjacent high anchors, and at least two same-side contacts per anchor. Put ε_u=d(u)−k and E=ε_p+ε_q≤2. The case with only high vertices outside the anchors and the case with the low vertex in a shallow component were previously settled. We may therefore take both anchors to be high. Write U_u(S) for the vertices of side S at distances one or two from u. In a 2-connected graph any vertex having a nonempty third layer has at least two vertices in its second layer.

## A shallow side

The remaining configuration has shallow all-high D with a low vertex in deep C. If C has a common port, use shallow-high-two-cut.md. Otherwise C has private contacts only. Write d=|D|, t for the common D ports, r for the C nonports, and w for the high C nonports at distance two from both anchors. Each anchor has at most k−1 contacts in D, by the C-private-port even cap (see exact-degree-two-cut.md). If D lacks a private type, it is the all-common clique K_{k−1}, covered in shallow-high-two-cut.md. Otherwise both private types occur, d≥k, and every D private port sees its opposite anchor at distance two since its own private class has at most k−1 members. Every high C nonport has distance two from some anchor, since otherwise it has all d high D vertices at distance four and at least two second-layer vertices.

We have n=2+2k+E−t+r. If n≥3k+3 then r≥k+1+t−E. Together the even caps at p,q give
r−1+w+d−t+2[t>0] ≤ 2k.
Also a D p-private port sees q itself, all C p-contacts, and all high C nonports singly nearest to q (at distance four via q, and at least four via either anchor). Adding the symmetric count and allowing a single missed low nonport yields |C|≤2k−1+w. If t>0 the first inequality gives d+w≤k+E−2, so n≤3k+1. Otherwise d+w≤k+E, giving n≤3k+E+1≤3k+3. Equality at n=3k+3 requires E=2 and equality in every anchor charge. In particular the sole low vertex z is a C nonport, uncharged at both anchors, and every anchor has all k even places used by the displayed vertices at distance two. Each anchor has eccentricity at most three. Thus z is at distance three from each, whence at distance four from every D vertex. Together with two second-layer witnesses this exceeds its cap since d≥k.

## Deep sides, common ports

If both sides have a common port, use exact-degree-two-cut.md, section “Both sides are deep and a common port exists.” Its argument for both sides having common ports needs no degree bound on p,q. It also covers a side whose only nonport is the low z whenever the opposite side has a common port. We can assume only D has common ports, C has a high nonport, and C has only private ports. There is a high private u-contact in C for each anchor u. Its internal closed neighborhood of size at least k lies in U_u(C). Since the other anchor is at distance two, this gives |U_u(D)|≤k−1+ε_u.

**D has a high common port h.** Both sets U_p(D),U_q(D) contain the internal closed neighborhood of h, of size ≥k−1. Hence their union has size at most k−1+E. Every high D nonport is in this union by the presence of the opposite high nonport (their mutual distance is at most four). If D has a high nonport, there are at most k−2 high C nonports: an opposite high nonport sees all these at distance four and some anchor at distance two; equality at k−1 would require internal universality and put both anchors at distance two. There are at most k C ports since h sees them all. Thus C has at most 2k−2 high vertices, D has at most k−1+E high vertices, and the possible single low gives n≤3k+E≤3k+2.

Suppose instead D's only nonport is the low vertex z. All its neighbors are ports, so it belongs to U_p(D)∪U_q(D) and d=|D|≤k−1+E≤k+1. If z is at distance two from both anchors, its distances to C are exactly one more than those from h, since each distance is obtained by the minimum over the two anchors. The opposite side has at most 2k−2 vertices (z already sees both anchors at distance two), so n≤3k+1. Otherwise name the singly closest anchor p. Every neighbor of z is p-private, and such a high port implies d≥k. Every C nonport has distance two from some anchor, since otherwise it sees the d−1≥k−1 high D ports at distance four as well as two distance-two witnesses.

Write t for the number of common ports in D, r for the C nonports and w for those near both anchors (at distance two). When a D q-private port exists, its internal closed neighborhood has size ≥k, and |U_q(D)|≤k, so equality holds. As z is outside, d=k+1 and each such port has internal closed neighborhood D−{z}. All D private ports thus see the opposite anchor at distance two, using a q-private port or high common port. When there is no D q-private port, p meets all d−1 D ports and at least two C vertices. Thus d=k, and every D p-private is internally universal, again giving opposite-anchor distance two. The anchor charge in either case is
r+w+(d−1−t)+1+2 ≤ 2k
(the last entries count z and the opposite anchor pair). Together with n=3+2k+E−t+r, this gives n≤3k+2 at d=k+1. If d=k the sole outstanding case is E=2 and n=3k+3 with equality in all counts. In particular w=0 and C has no edge between opposite port types, since the resulting anchor witnesses are uncharged. Exactly two C vertices are p-ports since p has degree k+1 and d−1=k−1 contacts in D. A high C p-port needs k−1 internal neighbors among the other p-port and its singly p-nearest nonports. There are consequently at least k−2 of these nonports. They are all distance four from z, via p (entry via q costs at least 3+3). The vertex z also sees p at distance two and every common D port at distance two through a universal p-private neighbor. Thus t≤1. Yet q has at least two contacts in D and all are common, a contradiction.

**D has only a low common port z.** All nonports on both sides are high. Since each anchor has at least two D contacts, high private contacts exist for each anchor on each side. Their two internal closed neighborhoods of size ≥k, together with the opposite anchor at distance two, force ε_p=ε_q=1 and equality. For each u∈{p,q} and each S∈{C,D}, U_u(S) has size k and equals the internal closed neighborhood of each high u-contact. Every nonport is at distance two from at least one anchor by the opposite high nonport. In D take high p-, q-private ports e,f. Both are adjacent to z, so are two steps from their opposite anchors. The same holds for every high D private port.

A D nonport y singly near q has k internal neighbors, hence one outside the k-element set U_q(D) containing y. This neighbor belongs to U_p(D) since every vertex of D lies in the union. Therefore it neighbors e or is e. The latter is impossible since y is not near p. Thus d(e,y)=2. For singly near p, exchange e,f. A C nonport singly near q is at distance four from e, as passage via p or q always costs at least four and via q realizes four. The symmetric claim holds for f. Count positive-even incidences with roots p,q,e,f. D private ports are charged at their opposite anchors and C ports at e or f. Only common z is uncharged among the 2k+1 distinct ports. Each nonport is charged twice, either by both anchors or by one anchor and the indicated private representative. The p–q incidences and the incidences of each private representative to its opposite anchor give four extra slots. Consequently 4k≥2k+4+2r_total and n=2+2k+1+r_total≤3k+1.

Combining the cases proves the assertion.
