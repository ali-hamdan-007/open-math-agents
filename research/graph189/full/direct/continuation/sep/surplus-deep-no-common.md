# Traceability across a separating pair with degrees at most k+1

Under the Graph 189 hypotheses, suppose G has a separating pair p,q with d(p),d(q)≤k+1. A spanning path exists when |G|≤4k+1 or k≥9. Other high degrees and the unique low degree are unrestricted. In particular every qualifying graph with connectivity two and global maximum degree at most k+1 is traceable: the separate rooted local Ore theorem covers |G|>4k+1 for that degree class.

Use the cut-vertex, adjacent-cut, at-least-three-components, minimum-degree-two and half-connectivity theorems, and surplus-common-shallow.md. We need only k≥6 (for k≤5 connectivity two is at least floor(k/2)), in a hypothetical 2-connected counterexample with minimum degree at least three. The anchors are high and nonadjacent and their deletion leaves exactly two deep components C,D, both without common ports. At most one of these components contains a vertex of degree below k. Every edge in such a counterexample belongs to a triangle, so each anchor has at least two contacts in each side, at least one high. Each port is adjacent to just one anchor. Write U_u(S) for the set of vertices of side S at distance one or two from anchor u. Write ε_u=d(u)−k∈{0,1}.

**Two-ball bound.** Every high u-port has at least k vertices in its closed internal neighborhood, all in U_u(S). Since u's first and second layers together have size at most 2k+ε_u, each U_u(S) has size between k and k+ε_u. If p,q are at distance four, each one's first, second and fourth layers already contain at least 2k+1 vertices. Thus both ε equal one and there are 2k+2 ports in all. Given a high p-port in either side, opposite-side p-ports are at distance two, and all high opposite-side q-ports are at distance four. For the latter, a route of length ≤3 would force a p-to-q path of length ≤3 through an edge between opposite-type ports on one side; distance at most four follows from the high-pair bound. Choosing a high p-port in each side gives two even caps covering at least 2k+1 distinct ports, impossible. The high-pair bound and absence of common ports now give d(p,q)=3; some side contains an edge between opposite-type ports, called a crossing edge.

Every high nonport is in U_p(S)∪U_q(S). If the opposite side has a high nonport, the two must have distance exactly four through a common nearest anchor two steps from each. Otherwise the opposite side's only nonport is low; it contains a high private port and has order at least k, with at least k−1 high ports. A high nonport at distance ≥3 from both anchors would see all those high ports at distance four. Its second layer has at least two vertices by 2-connectivity and the existence of a fourth layer, exceeding its even cap. Thus t_S=|S outside U_p(S)∪U_q(S)| is at most one, and t_C+t_D≤1.

Let w be the number of nonports of S at distance two from both anchors. If S contains a crossing edge, both its ends are in U_p(S)∩U_q(S), which gives |S|≤2k+t_S. Otherwise choose a crossing edge e,f on the other side, with e a p-port and f a q-port. The even cap at e charges q, all S p-ports and all S nonports at distance two only from q, the last at exact distance four by the two anchor routes. The symmetric count at f charges p, all S q-ports and the singly p-nearest nonports. Thus |S|≤2k−2+w+t_S. The two-ball sizes independently give |S|≤2k+2−w+t_S. Averaging yields in either case

    |S|≤2k+t_S.

**Paths inside each side.** A 2-connected side S has a cycle of length at least min(|S|,2k−2). Indeed every vertex except possibly one has internal degree at least k−1, so Fan's circumference theorem applies. From any specified port, a path to the first point of that cycle followed by the opened cycle covers at least that many vertices.

Now let S have a cut vertex. Every leaf block has a high noncut vertex. Otherwise its only noncut vertex would be low, have at most one internal neighbor, and have ambient degree at most two. A high noncut has at least k−1 neighbors in that leaf block, so each leaf has at least k−1 noncuts. Since |S|≤2k+1 and k≥6, there are exactly two leaf blocks, and the block-cut tree is a path. There are no high noncuts in intermediate blocks, since a block with such a vertex would have ≥k vertices, disjoint from the ≥2k−2 noncuts of the two leaves. Every leaf block has a port among its noncuts by 2-connectivity of G. Each leaf B has order at most k+2 and is 2-connected.

For a leaf B with articulation b, let H consist of its high noncut vertices. If there is no low noncut in B, then |H|≤k+1 and δ(H)≥k−2. If there is one, |H|≤k and δ(H)≥k−3. In either case 2δ(H)≥|H|+1 for k≥7, and also for k=6 except potentially when |H|=6 and a low noncut is present. Thus H is Hamilton-connected by the endpoint form of Ore's degree-sum criterion. For any designated noncut port e in B, take vertex-disjoint paths from e and b to distinct members of H, allowing a zero-length path when e is already in H, and truncate at the first members of H. They exist by 2-connectivity of B and the vertex-set form of Menger's theorem. Join those two points through H. The same construction works for any designated noncut endpoint in the other leaf. Join the two b-to-endpoint paths through consecutive articulations inside the intermediate blocks. The resulting side path starts at the prescribed leaf port e and contains every high vertex of S. All intermediate noncuts, if any, are low.

If S is wholly high, there is an available leaf port at either prescribed anchor u. Otherwise both leaf interiors would have only ports of the other anchor v. Their high ports have internal closed neighborhoods of size ≥k in different leaf blocks and intersect in at most one articulation, contradicting containment in the same U_v(S) of size ≤k+1. For a side containing the low vertex it suffices to prescribe a port in whichever leaf contains one. Consequently the two sides have paths whose start ports meet the same anchor: choose any available anchor on the low side first, then use that anchor on the wholly high side. Join the paths through that anchor.

At the exceptional k=6 leaf, |B|=8. The other leaf has ≥5 noncuts, so |S|=13. The high core H has order six and minimum degree at least three; it is 2-connected by Dirac's cycle theorem. For any two prescribed distinct vertices x,y, it has an x,y-path with at least four vertices. Indeed each component of H−{x,y} has a contact to each of x,y by 2-connectivity. If no component has distinct contacts to these vertices, every component must be a singleton, since a unique joint contact would otherwise be a cut vertex. Then these singletons have degree at most two, impossible. Using the internal path between two distinct contacts gives the required four-vertex path. The preceding construction in this leaf thus misses at most two high vertices in addition to the possible low.

**Total missed.** At |G|≤4k+1, the sizes of the two sides sum to at most 4k−1. If both are 2-connected, their two cycle deficits sum to at most three: if both exceed 2k−2 use the sum bound, and otherwise use |S|≤2k+t_S. If both are cut-vertex sides their losses sum to at most one, apart from the k=6 exception. If just one is a cut-vertex side, its loss is at most the number of low vertices in it; the 2-connected side's loss is at most two plus its t_S. Together these total at most three. The unused anchor costs one further vertex. In the k=6 exception one side has order 13 and the opposite side at most ten, is wholly high, and spans either by its cycle or its block-chain path. The total loss then is at most two high vertices, one low and one unused anchor, again four. The prior almost-spanning theorem gives traceability since four is at most ceil(k/2)+1 for k≥6.

Without any order restriction, the two side deficits total at most five from |S|≤2k+t_S, including the mixed block-chain cases when k≥9. One unused anchor makes at most six missing, which is within ceil(k/2)+1 for those k. This proves the result.
