# Odd second degree and connectivity two below half

A qualifying Graph 189 graph with k=2t+1 and connectivity s=t−2 has a spanning path whenever t≥11. Thus the first odd degree in this uniform result is 23. Write h=t+1 and U=t²+6t+4. As before, n≤U−(k−δ). Use the optimized path P and smallest high-bearing exterior component C from even-two-below.md, and the same exterior bounds and terminal-routing lemma. Let m=|C|≥h+2=t+3.

## Exterior reduction

If C has a cut vertex, choose an all-high endblock interior with order b for the whole block and least noncut degree q≥h. The b≥2q−1 bound n≥(s+5)q+2s+h−4 gives n≥t²+7t−4>U. The b≤2q−2 bound is 3h−1+min((h+1)(k−h+2),(s+1)(k−s+2)), namely t²+7t−3>U. Thus C is 2-connected.

Let q be the minimum internal degree among high C vertices. Suppose m≥2q. The Fan-cycle bound n≥(s+5)q+2s−1 implies q=t+1. For a high minimizing x, its set R of ambient distance-two vertices in C has size r≤2q−k+1=2, and r≤1 unless the unique low vertex is on P. The closed C-neighborhood H of x has size q+1. If r≤1, C has at most q+2 vertices by the 2-connected one-gateway argument. Thus r=2 and all C vertices are high. Let I=C−(H∪R), which is nonempty since m≥2q. No edge joins H and I, and every neighbor within C of a vertex of H lies in H∪R. Thus H has minimum internal degree at least q−2, which ensures Hamilton-connectedness since 2(q−2)≥|H|+1. Every member of H has at least k−q−2=t−2=s contacts to P. The central vertex x has at least k−q=t such contacts. A size-s vertex cover of H-to-P edges must consist entirely of marks, because with fewer marks there is an uncovered H vertex missing its required s contacts. But s marks cannot cover x's contacts. We can therefore match s+1 distinct H vertices to marks on P.

The resulting inner gaps each contain at least q+1 vertices. A tail at any prescribed matched v can use all H and at least q additional vertices of C. Indeed C−v is connected. Take a path from H−v to I there, trimming to the last H point w before the first I point y. The part from w to y has at least one intervening R vertex. Traverse H from v to w and then follow this part to y. All I vertices have internal degree at least q−r=q−2, so a path rooted at y within I has at least q−1 vertices and is disjoint from the already used H and R. Thus at least q vertices are gained. Counting m≥2q exterior vertices, the s+1 marks, the s+2 gaps of base size q+1, and the two supplementary tail lengths gives

    n ≥ 2q + (s+1) + (s+2)(q+1) + 2q
      = (s+6)q+2s+3 = t²+7t+3 > U.

We conclude m≤2q−1. Let j be the maximum C-to-P contact matching size. Then j≥s, and near-Hamilton-connected routing gives n≥(j+2)m+j−1. At j=t this gives n≥U+1 already from m≥t+3. Therefore j=s or s+1. At j=s+1=t−1 the degree demand of any uncovered high vertex in a minimum cover implies m≥k−j+1=t+3, and the routing bound gives m≤t+4. At j=s=t−2 the same reasoning gives t+4≤m≤t+5 for t≥8.

## Excluding the one-mark deficit

For either allowed j, consider m=k−j+2. Its value is t+4 for j=t−1 and t+5 for j=t−2. These exclusions only require the near-Hamilton-connected routing conclusions above and hence also apply to any exterior of this order for which these conclusions can be checked without optimization. A minimum cover has at least j−1 marks. If all j are marks, any high C vertex c has at least j−1 neighbors among them. Even the weaker near-spanning routing bound forces n≥U−7 for either j, so δ≥k−7. A low in C then has internal degree at least δ−j≥t−5>2. There is no exceptional terminal pair, and C is Hamilton-connected. If there are j−1 marks M and one C vertex u in the cover, every high C−u member touches all M and all other members of C. At most one C edge can be missing. Match u to a contact outside M (one exists since the matching size is j) and match M to distinct high vertices of C−u. This is a j-matching in the Hamilton-connected C, and any high c outside u is adjacent to at least j−1 of its chosen marks.

As in even-two-below.md, the j chosen marks bound j+1 gaps of at least m vertices in every ordering of the same path vertex set. Their total excess σ is at most U−((j+2)m+j), which is 1 at j=t−1 and 6 at j=t−2. Each attainable high endpoint has at least k−m+1−σ neighbors among the fixed marks, hence at least t−3 in the first case and t−9 in the second. These numbers are at least two. All k+1 or more attainable high endpoints then lie at distance two from c, contradicting the even-distance limit.

## Exchanging a gap in the clique case

It remains to consider m=k−j+1 for each j. The chosen C is a clique with all high vertices complete to the j covering marks. The endpoint argument gives |P|+m≥(j+2)m+2j, equal to U−3 at j=t−1 and U−8 at j=t−2. Hence the only possible further omitted component is a low singleton z. If such z exists, n≥U−7 in either case and δ≥k−7. Its path contacts split into r runs with step two, and their immediate neighbors use δ+r distinct positive even witnesses of z. Replacing the midpoint of a consecutive pair of contacts in a run with z proves that every one of the δ−r such midpoints touches C, as otherwise it would be an exterior high singleton. Thus each midpoint belongs to the j covering marks. These marks are separated on P by at least m intervening vertices. Their bracket pairs therefore use 2(δ−r) distinct neighbors of z. It follows that 2(δ−r)≤δ and δ+r≤k, hence 3δ≤2k. At k≥23 this is incompatible with δ≥k−7. There is no additional singleton.

Suppose a gap or tail contains exactly m vertices. As in even-two-below.md, exchanging them with C leaves a connected exterior J of size m≤t+4. To apply the contact reduction to J, the same cut argument as in the even-degree paper shows J is 2-connected: each high vertex has at least h internal neighbors, a cut with high vertices on both ends needs order ≥2h+1, and a sole low leaf otherwise has ambient degree at most two. As m≤2h−1, the near-Hamilton-connected terminal and matching bounds apply on this unoptimized path. The matching must have size at least j=k−m+1. A matching of size t costs more than U at either m. At j=t−2, the additional possibility of a (t−1)-matching is precisely the one-mark-deficit case m=t+4, already excluded on any such near-Hamilton-connected small exterior. Thus J also has a pure j-mark cover and all its high vertices touch every one of its marks. The retained old bounding mark supplies m−1 even witnesses in J to a fixed old high c∈C. At least 2j−2 old brackets outside J supply distinct further witnesses, with total m+2j−3=k+j−2>k. The exchange is impossible.

All gaps now have length at least m+1. An attainable high endpoint avoiding marks, guaranteed by the quota at an old high member of C, forces excess at least j−1 above this larger length. Thus n≥(j+2)m+3j, equal to t²+7t for j=t−1 and t²+7t−6 for j=t−2. Both exceed U at t≥11, completing the proof.
