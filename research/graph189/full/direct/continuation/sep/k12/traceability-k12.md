# Traceability at second-smallest degree twelve

Let G be a connected finite simple graph with second-smallest degree 12 and at most 12 other vertices at positive even distance from each vertex. Then G has a Hamiltonian path. The four-anchor case below uses 1,095 exact rational linear-real arithmetic tests with Boolean type indicators.

The connectivity-three theorem and its smaller-connectivity predecessors, and the one-below-half theorem, cover every connectivity other than four. At connectivity four the moderate-order theorem covers n≤61. The quadratic bound gives n≤67 and δ≥n−55. The argument in even-two-below.md at t=6 reduces any hypothetical exception to a high-bearing, 2-connected component C outside an optimized longest path P. Every high C vertex has at least six C neighbors. The alternatives in its matching reduction are an eight-vertex clique with a five-mark cover, or |C|=9,10 with a four-member matching and cover. The eight-clique alternative is excluded there without computation by exchanging an exact eight-vertex path gap and counting second neighbors.

When |C|=9, any high vertex needs four P neighbors. The cover consists entirely of marks, making C a clique with all high vertices adjacent to all four marks. We take A=C and S equal to those marks. When |C|=10 and the cover includes one vertex u of C, its remaining three elements are marks. Each high vertex in C−u needs all three marks and all nine C neighbors, so A=C−u is a nine-clique whose high vertices are adjacent to all four S vertices, where S is the cover. In either nine-vertex case a low A vertex has exactly eight internal neighbors, so the number of incident anchors is between δ−8 and three. Each pair of anchors has distance at most two via a high A vertex. For every subset J of S the complementary site B=V(G)−(A∪S) has at least |J| contacts to J by four-connectivity: otherwise replacing J with these neighbors disconnects the vertices of B outside the replacement from A. At least |J|−1 of the anchors are actual marks. Their distinct brackets on the path supply at least 2(|J|−1) contacts in B. We use the maximum of the two bounds.

If |C|=10 and all four cover vertices are marks, put A=C. Every high vertex of A touches three or four marks. A high vertex missing a mark is universal in A because it needs all nine C neighbors to reach its degree. Its distance to that missed mark equals two. Any two anchors have distance at most three. Indeed, if their A-neighborhoods are disjoint, at least one has a high A contact, which must miss the other anchor and so is adjacent within A to any of its contacts. The four-contact matching bound n≥6·10+3=63 implies δ≥8. If a low vertex is in A, it has at least δ−4≥4 internal neighbors, so no exceptional degree-two terminal pair remains. Now C is Hamilton-connected, n≥64, and the two brackets of every mark give at least 2|J| distinct contacts in B to any J⊆S. The side A may have one low vertex of arbitrary qualifying degree. Every anchor still has an A contact and a B contact.

For completeness, every considered case satisfies |B|≥49>4k. If an edge from any A vertex x to a neighbor shortens the distances to two anchors, then that neighbor and at most two other neighbors of x account for all first steps on some x-geodesic to each anchor. Every vertex in B has an x-geodesic through an anchor. Thus B is covered by the even-distance sets of x and those at most three neighbors, contradicting |B|>4k. The two-coordinate decrease restriction is consequently valid throughout, even if B is disconnected.

The exact procedure uses the previously audited four-anchor distance and population constraints from ../../verify/k11/traceability.md with k=12, the population ranges above, and the following row from ../../root/edge-order.md. For any edge between anchor i and a vertex x of side T, write p_T(i) for the number of T contacts of i and c_S for the number of other anchors adjacent to both i and x. Their common-neighbor count c is at most p_T(i)−1+c_S. The edge theorem gives n≤2k+2+c+k(k+1+c−d(i)). Therefore

    12 d(i) ≤ 169 + 13(p_T(i)+c_S) − n.

The row is conditional on the vector of x being present, and it is imposed for every occupied type on both sides and every incident anchor. Anchor degrees are determined by the populations and anchor adjacency. Even a low anchor is covered by this row because the edge theorem does not assume high degrees.

The generator k12.py enumerates every permissible anchor metric up to permutation, every no-low, low-in-A, low-in-B and low-at-an-anchor orbit, and orders 62 to 67 at |A|=9 or 64 to 67 at |A|=10. At order 67 the degree bound forces all vertices high. The counts at |A|=9 are 53 for each n=62,…,66 and 11 for n=67. At |A|=10 there are 257 for each n=64,65,66 and 48 for n=67. All 1,095 were UNSAT, including the three cases exceeding the initial seven-second timeout. Original and retry outputs are first.jsonl and retry.jsonl. The sorted final results are in cases.jsonl. The command python run.py --timeout 100000 --workers 3 regenerates the results with the system libz3.so.4.

The program controls.py fixes all type populations to measured BFS vectors in six explicit qualifying small graphs. These graphs have second degree twelve, cover both A sizes and all four kinds of low location, and produce SAT in every case using their actual B order. The lower bound on the low degree is retained at eleven. The profiles exclude every necessary remaining geometry. This calculation and the cited graph-theoretic arguments establish the theorem.
