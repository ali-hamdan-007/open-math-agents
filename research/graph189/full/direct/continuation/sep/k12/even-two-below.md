# Connectivity two below half the second-smallest degree

A finite connected Graph 189 graph with second-smallest degree k=2t and connectivity s=t−2 has a spanning path if t≥8. The proof allows an arbitrary degree at the sole vertex below k. The first reduction below also holds at t=6,7. Write U=t²+5t+1. The established order bound is n≤U−(k−δ).

Choose a longest path maximizing the sum of squared exterior component orders, and a smallest high-bearing exterior component C. Here high means degree at least k. Every such graph without a spanning path has a high-bearing exterior component, and for every longest path each high exterior vertex has at least t internal neighbors, in a component of order at least t+2. On the optimized path, every noncut high x of C with internal degree q has at most 2q−k+1 ambient distance-two vertices within C. Only the latter observation requires optimization. The underlying path-exchange proof is in ../../global/max-degree-plus-two-exception.md, and the contact and block bounds used below are in ../k10/uniform-matching.md.

If C has a cut vertex take an endblock of order b with all noncut vertices high and minimum noncut degree q≥t. When b≥2q−1, the block count gives n≥(s+5)q+2s+t−4. At q≥t+1 this exceeds U. If q=t, a minimizing noncut vertex within this 2-connected block has at most one ambient distance-two vertex in C. Its closed neighborhood within the block has t+1 vertices. Every vertex at internal block distance two from x has ambient distance two as well. Thus the block has at most t+2 vertices: a more distant vertex would be separated from x inside the block by its only second-layer vertex. This contradicts b≥2t−1. When b≤2q−2 the endblock matching estimate gives n≥t²+6t−5, by taking the weaker of its two displayed bounds. This exceeds U for t≥7. At t=6, equality is possible only at n=U, hence δ=k. With every vertex high, both tails extend from the articulation for t vertices (instead of t−1), increasing the count by two. Thus C is 2-connected for all t≥6.

Let q now be the least internal degree among high C vertices and m=|C|. If m≥2q the cycle count gives n≥(s+5)q+2s−1. Again q≥t+1 exceeds U, while q=t implies m≤t+2 by the one-gateway argument just given. Thus m≤2q−1. The terminal-routing lemma provides a spanning path for each prescribed pair except possibly the two neighbors of a low C vertex with internal degree two. This pair still admits a path on m−1 vertices. If j is the maximum C-to-P matching size then j≥s and n≥(j+2)m+j−1. The last bound improves by one when all C vertices are high.

We have j≤t−1 because j=t would already cost at least (t+2)(t+2)+t−1=U+2. At j=t−1, m≥t+2 and m=t+3 already costs U, at which there is no exceptional low pair and the bound improves. Thus m=t+2. At j=s there must be at least k−m+1 marks in any size-s cover, by the degree demand on its uncovered high vertices. Now t+3≤m≤t+4, the upper bound following from n≥tm+t−3. Consequently only the following alternatives remain.

## Clique of order k−j+1

These are the alternatives (j,m)=(t−1,t+2) and (t−2,t+3). All j cover elements must be marks. All high C members are complete to these marks and to the other C vertices, making C a clique. The original gap lower bound m and endpoint rotation give |P|+m≥(j+2)m+2j, as in ../k10/uniform-matching.md. This is U−1 in the first alternative and U−5 in the second. Therefore the exterior of P besides C can only be empty or a low singleton z. If such z occurs, the bound on n improves by one. In the first alternative δ=k. In the second δ≥k−4. The low singleton is therefore impossible in the first alternative, and in the second whenever t≥7. Indeed at least 2δ−k≥2t−8>j distinct midpoints lie between pairs of its path contacts at positions two apart, as in ../../root/one-below-half-clique.md. Replacing any such middle vertex with z shows the middle vertex must touch C, because otherwise it is an omitted high singleton after the exchange. But only the j marks touch C. At t=6 the first alternative still has no singleton; no exclusion of a singleton in the second is needed for the separate four-anchor calculation.

Assume there is no exterior singleton, as just proved in the ranges at issue. An exact m-vertex gap or tail J in any realization on V(P) can now be exchanged with C, retaining its original bordering mark or marks. The new longest path has precisely J as complement. The arbitrary-longest-path internal degree bound applies to J. A cut in J is impossible: an all-high endblock interior forces order at least t+1 in its block, and its complementary remainder contains a high vertex and hence has at least t vertices. If the complementary remainder consists only of the low vertex, that low leaf has ambient degree at most two by the exterior edge contact inequality, contradicting s≥4. Thus J is 2-connected of order m≤t+3≤2t−1. The same near-Hamilton-connected terminal bound therefore applies without optimization. Each high J vertex requires at least k−m+1=j path contacts. A matching of j+1 contradicts the order bound with that m (in the second alternative equality n=U again improves because there is no low). Hence its minimum cover is entirely j marks and the high J vertices touch all of them. A retained original bordering mark i touches J and therefore belongs to this cover. Any old high c∈C has at least m−1 vertices of J at distance two via i, and also sees the 2j old immediate brackets of the original marks at distance two. At most two of these brackets lie in J. This gives m−1+2j−2=k+j−2>k distinct even witnesses.

Thus all j+1 gaps, including both tails, have at least m+1 vertices. For an attainable high endpoint e avoiding the marks, let σ be their total excess over m+1. Rotation at a neighbor in the first m+1 vertices of any gap following a mark would give a realization with a smaller initial tail. Consequently d(e)≤m+σ and σ≥k−m=j−1. Such e exists because there are at least k+1 attainable high endpoints, and every endpoint touching any mark lies at distance two from a fixed high C member. We get n≥(j+2)m+3j. In the first alternative this exceeds U for t≥6. In the second it is t²+6t−6, exceeding U for t≥8. The first alternative is therefore excluded also at k=12.

## Exterior of order t+4

There are exactly s matched contacts. The cover is either s marks, or s−1 marks M and a single covered C vertex u, because uncovered high vertices need at least k−m+1=s−1 path neighbors. Even the near-Hamilton-connected lower bound already gives n≥U−4, hence δ≥k−4. With only marks in the cover, the low C vertex, if present, has internal degree at least δ−s≥t−2>2, so C is Hamilton-connected by the terminal-routing lemma. Fix any maximum matching. Every high C vertex touches at least s−1 of the chosen s marks.

With a mixed cover, each high vertex of C−u meets all M and is internally universal: it requires k neighbors and at most s−1 can lie on P. All but at most one pair within C are therefore edges (the possibly missing edge joins the low vertex to u). In particular C is Hamilton-connected. The matching number s guarantees that u has a contact x outside M. Match u to x and all marks of M to distinct high vertices of C−u. Fix this matching, and choose any high c∈C−u. It touches every chosen mark except possibly x.

In either case the fixed s matching marks cut every longest-path realization on V(P) into s+1 gaps of length at least m. The external matching and the terminal routes persist under every reordering. The sum σ of the excess gap sizes is at most n−m−s−(s+1)m≤3. Each attainable high endpoint has at most m−1+σ neighbors outside the chosen marks by the same rotation argument, whether or not C has contacts on other marks. It therefore touches at least k−m+1−σ≥t−6 chosen marks. For t≥8 this is at least two. The fixed c misses at most one of the chosen marks and cannot be adjacent to any attainable endpoint. All k+1 or more high attainable endpoints are consequently at distance two from c, contrary to its even quota. This completes the theorem.
