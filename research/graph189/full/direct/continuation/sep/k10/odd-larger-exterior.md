# The larger odd exterior is impossible one below half connectivity

Let k=2t+1 with t≥5, s=κ=t−1, and use the preceding arbitrary-connectivity matching reduction in uniform-matching.md. It leaves a 2-connected exterior component C of order m=t+3 or m=t+4 behind a size-s contact cover on a longest path P, with at least s−1 of its members on P in the larger case. We exclude m=t+4 here, without a maximum-degree assumption. At t=4 the full k=9 theorem already applies.

Suppose m=t+4=s+5. The order bound is n≤U=s²+8s+11=(s+2)m+s+1, while the matching estimate gives n≥U−2 and hence δ≥k−2. Every high C vertex requires at least s−1 P-neighbors.

**A cover containing an exterior vertex gives an all-marks cover.** Let a size-s minimum cover consist of s−1 marks M and an exterior u∈C. Every high C vertex other than u has all its P-neighbors in M; to attain degree k it must touch all of M and every other member of C. Therefore C is a clique except possibly the edge from u to the unique low vertex (if different). In particular C is Hamilton-connected. Write X=N_P(u) outside M; it is nonempty since the matching size exceeds |M|.

For any x∈X match u to x and choose distinct high members of C−u for the s−1 marks of M. These s distinct path marks M∪{x} separate P into s+1 gaps and tails each with at least m unmarked vertices by the Hamilton-connected routes through C. At most one extra unmarked vertex is available because |P|≤n−m≤(s+1)m+s+1. Number positions of P from one. The jth chosen mark must occupy position j(m+1) or j(m+1)+1, for each j=1,...,s. These s two-position sets are pairwise disjoint and independent of the choice of x. The s−1 fixed marks of M occupy distinct such sets, so every x∈X belongs to the one remaining set. If two existed, they would be consecutive P neighbors of u and permit inserting u in P. Thus |X|=1 and M∪X is an all-marks minimum cover.

**The all-marks alternative contradicts rotations.** Let S be such a cover, so every high C member touches at least s−1 of its s marks. There exists an s-matching onto S. If z∈C is low then its internal degree is at least δ−s≥k−2−s=t, so the exceptional terminal pair (possible only at internal degree two) does not occur. Thus C admits a spanning path between any two distinct prescribed vertices. In every realization of a longest path on V(P), the same matching ensures that all s+1 mark-separated pieces have at least m vertices. Their total excess σ above (s+1)m is at most one.

Orient any such realization from a high endpoint e. Its exterior neighbors are absent by longestness. In the first piece it can neighbor at most the other vertices of that piece. It cannot neighbor any of the first m positions in a piece after a mark: rotating the initial endpoint at such a neighbor would give a realization with fewer than m vertices in its first tail before that mark, contradicting the matching bound. Hence e has at most m−1+σ≤m neighbors outside S, whether or not it meets any marks. It must therefore touch at least k−m=t−3≥2 marks. Fix a high c∈C. It touches at least s−1 marks, so every such e shares an incident mark with c and lies at distance exactly two from c. The exterior endpoint theorem supplies at least k+2 attainable endpoints on V(P), at most one low. This exceeds c's permitted k even witnesses. Thus m=t+4 is impossible and only the full-mark clique m=t+3 from uniform-matching.md remains.
