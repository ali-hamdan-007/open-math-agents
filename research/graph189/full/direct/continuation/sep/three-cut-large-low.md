# The low vertex may belong to the order-3k−9 side

Under the hypotheses of three-cut-large-boundary.md the graph is traceable without any restriction on the location of its unique low vertex. It remains to treat z low in B. Write B'=B−z, b'=3k−10. Every vertex of A and every other B vertex is high. We can assume n>4k+1 by the prior moderate-order result, so |A|≥k+8. An A nonport is high. An A port means an original S contact, and likewise for B and B'; deletion of z does not change which B' vertices are ports.

**Degrees and independence.** Every high B vertex has degree at least k−2 internally in B. Only a vertex u seeing all three anchors could otherwise have lower internal degree. It sees all A ports and all its internal nonneighbors among B ports at distance two. Consequently d_B(u)≥|P_A|+|P_B|−1−k. If A has no nonport, |P_A|≥k+8 rules out u. If B has no high nonport, |P_B|≥3k−10 already proves the degree bound. Otherwise both sides have a high nonport, seeing all opposite high nonports at four with at least three second-layer witnesses. Thus at most 2k−5 members of A∪B are nonports even allowing z. There are at least n−3−(2k−5)≥2k+4 ports, which again proves the bound. It follows that δ(B')≥k−3. A B' nonport has d_{B'}≥k−1.

For a set of independent B' vertices, mutual common neighbors in B (possibly z) suffice for the even charges in the companion proof. The same light-anchor argument, now with A wholly high, proves its bound of 21 ports in any independent set of size at least k−1. If A has a nonport, the argument at an I nonport x and its singly-nearest anchor a carries over verbatim, even when the intermediate a-contact u is z: that part uses only u's quota and the two-cut contradiction, not u's degree. Hence α(B')≤k−2 and B' has at most k−3 nonports. When A has no nonport, the argument at an independent k-set gives α(B')≤k−1.

**Disconnected B'.** Its components have order ≥k−2. Since b'=3k−10, if disconnected, B' has exactly two components and each has order at most 2k−8. Each is Hamilton-connected by its internal minimum degree ≥k−3. Both have z-contacts by connectivity of B. Each has at least two vertices touching S, since otherwise removing z and its sole port disconnects G. In each one take distinct endpoints consisting of a z-contact and a port. Join the two spanning paths through z and start at an actual port of B.

**Connected B'.** Form H by adding a marker to B' adjacent to all its ports. It is 2-connected, since deleting the marker leaves connected B', and paths after a B'-vertex deletion lift from the 2-connected G−z by projecting excursions through the connected A∪S onto the marker. Every original H vertex has degree ≥k−2: a port gains the marker on top of its B' degree, and a nonport already has ≥k−1. Whenever there are ≥k−2 B' ports the marker also has degree ≥k−2. At |H|=3k−9 this implies σ₃(H)≥3k−6≥|H|+2. Here σ₃ denotes the minimum degree sum of an independent triple, with the usual infinite convention when no such triple exists.

When A has a nonport, there are at least b'−(k−3)=2k−7 B' ports. The marker-independent sets have size at most 1+(k−3), so α(H)≤k−2. Nash–Williams gives a Hamilton cycle, and deleting the marker gives a spanning B' path from a port. When A has no nonport but there are ≥k−2 B' ports, α(H)≤1+α(B')≤k. By the Bauer et al. circumference theorem, a 2-connected graph with σ₃≥|H|+2 has a cycle of length at least min(|H|,|H|+σ₃/3−α(H)). Thus at most two H vertices are missed. If the marker is on the cycle, remove it to get a port-rooted B' path. If it is off, take a path from a port to the cycle and open the cycle at its first contact. Either way at most two B' vertices are missed.

**Fewer than k−2 ports in B' and no A nonport.** Suppose B' is 2-connected. Since σ₃(B')≥3k−9>b', Theorem 7 of Bauer, Schmeichel and Veldman (1996) says every longest cycle is dominating unless B' is a spanning subgraph of a member of one of four classes. The four possibilities are excluded as follows. The join of graphs adds all edges between the indicated sides.

1. In the join of K₂ to three disjoint cliques, each clique has order at least k−4 since vertices in it have degree at most its order plus one and δ(B')≥k−3. Their total order b'−2=3k−12 forces each order to equal k−4. All 3k−12 clique vertices would have degree at most k−3 and hence be ports, impossible.
2. The join of K₃ to four components each of order two or three has order at most fifteen.
3. The join of K_s to s disjoint edges and a triangle has order 3s+3, unlike b' which is two modulo three.
4. In the join of K_s to s+1 disjoint edges, matching b' forces s=k−4. The 2(k−3) vertices on the edges have degrees at most k−3, too many ports.

Let Z be a longest cycle of B'. If nonspanning, choose a vertex x outside. The dominating-cycle successor lemma for σ₃>b' says that all exterior vertices together with the successors of x's neighbors along an orientation of Z form an independent set. Since Z is dominating, all x-neighbors are on Z. Hence the number of missed vertices is at most α(B')−d_{B'}(x)≤2. A path from any actual port to Z followed by the opened cycle gives the desired route.

Finally, if B' has a cut vertex then each leaf has at least k−3 noncuts. There are exactly two leaves by b'=3k−10, and an intermediate block cannot have a noncut of internal degree ≥k−3 because its order ≥k−2 would be disjoint from the ≥2k−6 leaf interiors. Every leaf has a noncut port, since deleting its articulation and z would otherwise disconnect G. A leaf block has order at most 2k−7. If it attained that number, there would be only k−3 vertices outside it. Hence the opposite leaf has exactly k−3 noncuts, each of degree at most k−3 and therefore a port. Adding the first leaf's port contradicts the fewer-than-k−2 assumption. Every leaf now has order at most 2k−8, and its noncut core has minimum degree ≥k−4 on at most 2k−9 vertices, so it is Hamilton-connected. The two leaf routes join through the articulation chain to span B' from a genuine port.

In all cases a port-rooted B path misses at most z and two further vertices. Joining A through the corresponding anchor as in the companion paper misses at most the other two anchors, since all A vertices are high. Five is within the almost-spanning threshold at k≥24.

The four classes appear in [Bauer, Schmeichel and Veldman, A note on dominating cycles in 2-connected graphs, Theorem 7](https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/7044339/Bauer96note.pdf). The circumference result and its independent-set lemma appear as Theorem 13 and Lemma 1 in [Bauer et al., Toughness in Graphs – A Survey](https://cfc.nankai.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/c6/e1/a2c52bf04b1896f59003b5993582/446d9b25-e3ac-4a8e-a763-04a2cb68565c.pdf).
