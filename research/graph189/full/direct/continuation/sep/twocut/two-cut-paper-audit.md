# Independent audit of the high-degree separating-pair proof

The audited paper proof is `../../sep/twocut/high-degree-two-cut.md`. It applies at all orders when k ≥ 26 and vertex connectivity is exactly two. It uses the established longest-path gap, the published Fan circumference theorem, and the linked-set lower order theorem. The local Lean modules do not yet assemble the entire proof.

The chosen cut minimizes the order of one component among all components of all two-cuts. The minimum degree at least three ensures every component contains a high vertex. A component containing the sole low vertex cannot use that same vertex as its only contact with the entire cut. Consequently, in the three-component contradiction one can choose an anchor with high contacts in three components. The count at that anchor and the three high contacts is valid even when the low vertex lies on the cut. The original edge-order inequality gives the asserted triangle condition by orienting any edge toward its high endpoint.

In the balanced case the general apex has at most one artificial edge. An apex cycle yields a path whose initial contact is genuine, and an apex-free cycle is reached inside the original apex graph. When the anchors are nonadjacent, the contact edge's triangle on the wholly high side remains within that side and the selected anchor, giving at least two contacts. If the side is internally 2-connected, an apex adjacent to just those contacts is genuinely 2-connected. Each side vertex loses at most the other anchor from its original degree. This verifies the prescribed-anchor Fan application.

For a side with an articulation, each leaf block has at least k−2 noncut vertices. As the side order is at most 2k−1, there are just two leaves. Any intermediate block containing a noncut vertex would have at least k−1 vertices disjoint from both leaf interiors, so intermediate blocks on the block-cut path must be bridges. Consecutive bridges are forbidden by the internal degree. The leaf interiors have order at most k and minimum degree at least k−3, yielding Hamilton-connectedness by Ore's criterion. If a label misses both interiors, both cores have exactly k−1 vertices, one articulation is shared, and all 2k−2 core vertices touch the other label. This violates an opposite contact's even budget. The prescribed route is therefore available in both block cases. The two side paths miss at most four vertices in their sides and one separator vertex.

When the larger side has at least 2k vertices, the metric bound forces any high nonport on the smaller side to be at distance two from both labels and rules out a common port on that side. The remaining cases are no common port, a common port with both private types, and a common port missing one private type. If a private type consists only of the low vertex, its interior neighbor is high and touches the missing anchor in the common-port case. Thus the row count works for either low placement. In the no-common-port case, the smaller side loses at most one anchor neighbor at each high vertex, so deleting the low vertex preserves the required Dirac degree. If the chosen anchor touches only the low vertex, its interior neighbor gives entry to the cycle after that deletion.

In the last case, relabel so no small-side private port touches q. At the final recut, U is the closed B-neighborhood of a high p-private contact y, w is its sole internal distance-two vertex, and W is the remainder. The saturated even row rules out a p-contact in W. The absence of edges between opposite private types rules out q-contacts in U. Deleting {q,w} separates W from A, so W contains a component of size at least the original minimum a. Every high W vertex has distance three from y and its geodesic must use w as penultimate vertex, since every other distance-two witness is in A. Thus w touches every high vertex of W.

For `U₀ = U ∩ N(w)`, each member sees all high W at distance two. It cannot touch p, which would add the k−1 small-side p-contacts, so it instead sees p itself at distance two. This yields `|W_high|≤k−1`. A high private A vertex would force a≥k and the low vertex to be in A, making W wholly high and contradicting that bound. All high A vertices are consequently common. A sole low q-contact in W would have its q-edge triangle through w. Each U₀ vertex would then see p, q, at least k−2 high W vertices, and that extra low vertex, exceeding its budget. Hence there is a high q-contact f in W.

The row at f counts the high A vertices and U₀, leaving at most `2−|U₀|` distance-four witnesses. For high targets in U minus U₀, distance two is unavailable. Any length-three path to such a target must start via w. Starting in W or at q cannot reach a neighbor of the target in one further step, by the other absences and the missing pq edge. The w-row therefore counts the distance-three targets at distance two. Adding its quota k, the U₀ count, and the distance-four allowance gives `|U_high|≤k+2`. Together with `|W_high|≤k−1`, w and at most one low vertex, this gives `|B|≤2k+3`, and hence n≤3k+6, contradicting n≥4k−9 at k≥26.

## Sharpening to degree eight

The refinement `../../sep/twocut/two-cut-degree-eight.md` was independently checked. With the exact tight-cut theorem, a low minimum vertex lies on no minimum two-cut of a counterexample. A tied minimum has degree k, so even an arbitrary completion preserves the required degree. At linked index three the minimum-deletion construction requires k≥8, and the tied critical construction likewise uses 8≤k and k+6≤2k. Its preceding path gap excludes an independent exterior using the bound of two omitted vertices. Hence the lower order n≥4k−5 applies, implying the triangle condition for k≥8.

Both early recut exceptions at k=8 are exact equalities, with exactly k opposite p-contacts. Every A p-contact uses its entire even quota on those contacts, and connectedness makes it internally universal. It must touch q since an A q-contact would otherwise provide an additional distance-two witness. All high A vertices are common. The low A nonport is at distance two from both anchors and sees each B vertex at the opposite parity from a high common A vertex. Its two anchor witnesses therefore reduce the joint budget to b≤2k−2, eliminating both equalities.

At the final recut, if w touches q the U₀ row spends a place on q, giving `|W_high|≤k−2`. If w misses q, the high q-contact f gives w a distance-two witness at q, spending one place in w's charge to U. Either way `|U_high|+|W_high|≤3k−m−2`, where m is the number of high A vertices. Including w and the possible low vertex gives b≤3k−m−1+l_B and n≤3k+2. The sharpened routing inequalities and the k=8, t=0 low-A Dirac case also pass. All conclusions here are paper-level consequences, separate from the specific checked Lean modules they use.
