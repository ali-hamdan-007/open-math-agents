# All separating pairs for k=6,7,8,9

Together with unrestricted-two-cuts.md and the previously established half-connectivity result for k≤5, this proves traceability for every Graph 189 graph with connectivity two. All degree spectra and all orders are allowed. We use the notation, elementary size counts and one-anchor joining rule of unrestricted-two-cuts.md. Write h=ceil(k/2); at most h vertices may be missed in the two sides if a joining path uses just one anchor, or h+1 if both anchors are included.

## Short routes at the borderline core sizes

For a leaf block with specified articulation and noncut endpoint, the disjoint-entry and exit construction of the main paper needs a path between two specified vertices of its high noncut core H. If 2δ(H)≥|H|+1 the core is Hamilton-connected. If 2δ(H)≥|H|, Dirac gives a Hamilton cycle; its longer arc contains at least ceil(|H|/2)+1 vertices between any two distinct specified landings. In two further small cases we use the following bounds.

- A 7-vertex graph of minimum degree three has a path on at least four vertices between any two specified ends. It is connected. If 2-connected, Fan supplies a cycle of length ≥6. Two disjoint paths from the specified ends to the cycle and its longer arc cover ≥4 vertices. Otherwise any cut vertex separates two 3-vertex components, each complete to the cut vertex and a triangle, by the minimum degree. The two K4s have the claimed routing.
- A 5-vertex graph of minimum degree two has a path on at least three vertices between any two specified ends. It is connected. If 2-connected, every edge is on a cycle and nonadjacent ends already require a three-vertex path. Otherwise the two triangles at a cut vertex again give the bound.

The one-anchor construction for a common-free low side C of order c≤2k−1 can always cover every C vertex, with some port as its start. Add a marker adjacent to all its ports and a leaf at the marker. Every high C vertex has degree ≥k since private ports exchange their original anchor for the marker. The traceability-closure threshold is c+1≤2k, so the high vertices complete to a clique. The low vertex z has at least two high internal neighbors: it loses at most one anchor from its ambient degree at least three. Start at the leaf and marker, choose a high port, traverse the whole high clique ending at a neighbor of z different from the starting port, and finish at z. Reversing the closures gives the result because the leaf forces a port after its marker. The wholly high opposite side can match either anchor by the original leaf-accessibility or cycle argument.

We will use the earlier chain construction also below its stated degree ranges, with explicit core checks. If every ordinary leaf has at least a noncuts and |S|<3a, there are at most two ordinary leaves. Two such leaves exclude a high noncut of internal degree at least a in an intermediate block when |S|<3a+1. The stated small sizes satisfy these inequalities. A singleton low common leaf is stripped as in the main proof. In the remaining side its neighbor b may be treated as exceptional; a leaf has at least a noncuts since either it has an unaffected high noncut, or its only noncut is b, inconsistent with the high internal degree of b after losing one neighbor. The unaffected leaf(s) supply the required noncut port. Only one leaf can realize any of the borderline large cores in the cases below, since the noncut interiors are disjoint and at least one articulation remains outside them.

## Shallow side

The previous proof gives |C|≤2k−2+h, except in the even case D=K_k and |C|≤2k−1+h where the D traversal uses both anchors. A 2-connected C meets the required budget immediately. Leaf cores are as in the main paper. At k=9 the ordinary bound already supplies Hamilton-connected cores and there is no even exception. At k=8 only the even exception can exceed the usual range: B has at most 12 vertices, and the only failing core has a low noncut in B, |H|=10, δ(H)≥5. The longer Hamilton-cycle arc uses ≥6, losing at most four high vertices plus the low. All other leaves have Hamilton-connected cores. Both anchors are used, so the allowed loss is five.

At k=7 the bound is |C|≤16, leaf order ≤10. Without a low noncut |H|≤9 and δ(H)≥5 suffice. With the low noncut |H|≤8, δ(H)≥4, so at the sole borderline one can lose three high and the low, at most h=4.

At k=6 ordinarily |C|≤13, leaf order ≤8. Without a low noncut |H|≤7, δ(H)≥4. With one, |H|≤6 and δ(H)≥3, so the six-vertex Hamilton cycle permits losing at most two high plus the low, at most h=3. In the even exception |C|≤14, leaf order ≤9. Without a low noncut the sole borderline is |H|=8, δ(H)≥4, permitting three high losses. With a low noncut |H|≤7 and δ(H)≥3; the six- or seven-vertex bounds permit at most three high losses. Two losses from different large cores cannot combine: to lose a high vertex requires at least eight noncuts in an all-high borderline leaf or at least seven noncuts in a leaf containing the low, more than |C|−1 together. Thus at most three high plus the low are missed, within the two-anchor budget of four.

## Deep sides with no common ports

The unrestricted proof already covers k≥8. At k=7 all sides have order ≤16; a wholly high side's leaf cores are Hamilton-connected as for shallow k=7. A leaf with a low noncut can fail the same criterion only at side size 16. This cannot occur. If both sides fail cleanliness, both have at most 13 vertices. If D is clean, its earlier budget gives d≤15. If the opposite side C contains the low then D is wholly high, d≥8 and b≥1, giving 2w≤14−d−b≤5 and w+t≤3; hence |C|≤15. Thus every cut side has the same at-most-one loss as in the large-degree proof; its final totals require only h≥4.

At k=6 both dirty sides have size ≤11 and cause no difficulty. With clean D, all side orders are ≤13 by the previous inequalities. Only a side of order 13 with a low noncut can have a non-Hamilton-connected leaf core, the six-vertex case above with at most two high losses. This side cannot be clean D: in that case C is wholly high and deep, t=0,r_C≥1, giving d≤12. If it is the opposite C then D is wholly high, d≥7, b≥1. Its size of 13 needs w+t≥3, while 2w≤12−d−b≤4. Hence w=2,t=1,d=7 and D can span by its cycle (a high cut side would have order at least eleven). The three losses in C suffice.

Away from that exception every cut side loses at most its low. For two cycle routes the previous combined deficit is ≤max(h,3)=3. If C is cut and contains the low then D is wholly high with b≥1, so d≤11 and its cycle loses at most one. If C is cut and wholly high it spans, and the D deficit is at most three. If D is cut, d≥11 forces w≤1, so w+t and the possible low loss in D total at most three. Thus the one-anchor construction always misses at most four vertices overall.

## Only the deep side D has common ports

The prior reductions give |C|≤2k−1. If D has a high nonport and C is wholly high we have |C|≤2k−2. If D's only nonport is low then |D|≤2k−2: it has one nonport, ≥1 common port, and at most k−1 contacts of each anchor by an opposite private port. In the other cases with C dirty one has |D|≤2k−1. In the remaining clean C case its representatives give |D|≤2k−2+w+t−T with T≥1, and the anchor count gives 2w≤k−4+l_C. If l_C=1 then t=0, otherwise t≤1. In particular in the clean case |D|≤2k−4+floor(k/2). A common-free C is either covered by the marker lemma above if it contains the low, or has an available cycle route with loss (|C|−(2k−2))_+ and block route with no loss. For these C sizes and k≥6 its wholly high leaf core has |H|≤k−1 and δ(H)≥k−2, hence is Hamilton-connected. Each anchor can be used in a wholly high C.

At k=9 the improved D bound is 18 (the dirty bound is 17). Write a=k−2=7. An ordinary leaf core has |H|≤10,δ(H)≥6 if no low noncut and |H|≤9,δ(H)≥5 otherwise, so all are Hamilton-connected. The same bounds apply after stripping a low singleton, where the earlier two-loss allowance is ample. The main paper's quota estimates cover the two-cycle and mixed cases (h=5).

At k=8 we have |D|≤16 and a=6. An ordinary leaf B has at most 10 vertices, and its only failing core has low noncut, |H|=8,δ(H)≥4. The Hamilton-cycle arc loses at most three high plus the low, within h=4. At this size D contains a high nonport by the lone-low-nonport bound; hence C is wholly high, has size ≤14 and spans. When singleton low z is removed, D' has size ≤15, restoring Hamilton-connectivity of even the core excluding its exceptional b. All other cases use the main totals, valid for h=4.

At k=7 we have |D|≤13, a=5. Its cycle route loses at most three and C at most one. A D leaf has at most eight vertices. Its all-high core has |H|≤7,δ(H)≥4 and is Hamilton-connected. Its possible low-containing leaf has |H|≤6,δ(H)≥3, so at worst its cycle arc misses two high plus the low. When a singleton low leaf is stripped, |D'|≤12; these smaller cores, including the core excluding b, are Hamilton-connected. Including the stripped vertex gives at most three losses also for the D' cycle route. All combinations total at most h=4 before joining.

At k=6 we have |D|≤11, a=4. If D has a high nonport, C spans from an available port whether it contains the low or is wholly high, by the early marker lemma or |C|≤10. Hence D may lose three vertices. If D's only nonport is low, D has at most ten vertices and may lose two (C is wholly high and loses at most one). Its Fan cycle route meets these two allowances since its baseline is 2k−4=8. For block routes, each ordinary leaf has at least four noncuts and order at most seven. A leaf without the low has |H|≤6,δ(H)≥3, so loses at most two high. A leaf containing the low has |H|≤5,δ(H)≥2; at order five the three-vertex route may miss two high, at order four the Hamilton-cycle arc misses at most one high, and at order three the core is a clique. A substantial loss cannot occur in both leaves, as the all-high borderline has six noncuts, and a further leaf with a nontrivial loss would supply at least five more noncuts plus an articulation, exceeding 11. Thus D loses at most three inclusive of the low. If |D|≤10, a six-noncut all-high leaf cannot coexist with any other ordinary leaf (≥4 noncuts and an articulation). The low-containing leaf can have at most four high noncuts. Thus at most two vertices are missed in that case. If a low singleton common leaf was stripped, D has a high nonport and C spans. Now |D'|≤10, the same core argument treats b as exceptional and misses at most two in D', including b, and hence at most three in D. This covers every configuration and proves the claim.
