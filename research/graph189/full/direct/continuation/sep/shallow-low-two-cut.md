# A shallow component containing the unique low vertex

Use the notation of shallow-high-two-cut.md. Let D be a component consisting entirely of ports and containing the unique vertex z of degree less than k. The opposite component C is all high. Suppose also that p,q are nonadjacent, that there are exactly two components, and that G is 2-connected. Then G has a spanning path. We use the established minimum-degree-two theorem to assume δ(G)≥3, hence k≥4, and the order theorem to assume n≥3k+3 whenever convenient.

The opposite-common-port case and the singleton D case are already handled in the companion proof, with no degree restriction on C,D beyond the existence of a high D vertex for the former bound. Thus take C common-free. Every anchor meets at most k−1 D vertices, since saturation at an opposite private C port makes it universal within C and gives the opposite anchor as one more even witness.

**Both private types occur in D.** Number the two private types a,b and let t be the common count; t can now be zero. At least one private vertex is high and has at most one neighbor outside D, so d=|D|≥k. Every high private D vertex is at distance two from the opposite anchor by its degree and the bound on its own private class. Each private type has a representative at distance two from the opposite anchor. If z alone represents its type, the opposite anchor meets all d−1 other D vertices, whence d=k. The opposite type then has a high private vertex, universal in D by its degree, so z is also at distance two from the opposite anchor. Thus at most one of the a+b private contacts fails to see its opposite anchor at distance two.

A spanning p,D,q path exists. Every high private D vertex has internal degree at least k−1; high common vertices have internal degree at least k−2. The capacity bound on the two anchors gives d≤2k−2−t. If t≥2, any two high internal degrees sum to at least d. If t=1 there is at most one high common and their sums are at least 2k−3≥d. At t=0 the sum is at least 2k−2≥d. Hamiltonian closure can therefore complete the high vertices of D. Provided deg_D(z)≥2, there is now a spanning cycle, which reverses to one in D. Break a suitable edge for the two anchor contacts, using a common port when available, or an edge between the two private classes otherwise. If deg_D(z)=1, then z is common (its ambient degree is at least three). Remove z and add a leaf at its sole D neighbor to restore the degree of that neighbor. Complete the high subgraph by traceability closure, whose threshold is d−1. Removing the leaf from the resulting original spanning path gives a spanning path of D−z beginning at that neighbor. Prepend z. Because z is common, the opposite endpoint's anchor can always be paired with it.

Every nonport in C is at distance two from some anchor. At greater depth it would see at least d−1≥k−1 high D vertices at distance four, plus two second-layer vertices by 2-connectivity. The sizes r=|R|, |W| retain their companion definitions for the C nonports. The near representative of each D private class gives |C|≤2k−2+|W|, with no need for that representative to be high. For a p-private representative that is near q, every C nonport only near q has exact distance four from it: the two possible entry lower bounds are 1+3 and 2+2, with the latter realized as a walk.

If t>0, then |P|≤k. At r≤k the marker-plus-leaf closure in the companion gives a spanning C path starting at a port. At r≥k+1 we have r+|W|+(a+b−1)+2≤2k from the two anchor budgets. Hence |W|≤k−a−b−2≤k−4 and |C|≤3k−6. Every C vertex has two external even witnesses by the same near representative argument: private C ports see a common D contact and their own private D class. Both the Nash–Williams proof and the leaf-block proof from the companion apply unchanged with |C|≤3k−6 (still |C|≤3k−5, still |C|<3k−2 for the common-neighbor count, and three interiors of size k−1 cannot fit). In the block case k=4 could give degree at least three, sufficient to exclude consecutive bridges.

If t=0, let H=C plus a marker m adjacent to all C ports P. Projecting paths shows H is 2-connected: deletion of m leaves C connected, and upon deleting a C vertex all outside excursions can be replaced at m. Each vertex of C retains its degree since there are no common C ports. The anchor bound now reads r+|W|+d−1≤2k. Hence m has degree |P|=n−2−r−d≥k at n≥3k+3. Also r≤k: an equality r=k+1 in the preliminary r≤k+1 bound would require d=k and a private port not near the opposite anchor. At d=k all high private vertices are universal inside D, including an opposite-type vertex for the low vertex, so every private port is near. Now |W|≤floor((2k−d+1)/2)≤floor((k+1)/2), and |C|≤2k−2+|W|≤3k−4 for k≥4.

The independence number of H is at most k. For an independent (k+1)-set in C, two disjoint internal neighborhoods would force |C|≥(k+1)+2(k−1)=3k−1. Hence the independent peers already exhaust every member's even-distance budget, although each C port has an external witness sharing its anchor and each nonport has a nearest anchor at distance two. A (k+1)-set containing m would require r=k and all nonports independent. Each then needs at least k neighbors among C ports. At most k−1 C contacts belong to either anchor by counting from a near private D representative (the opposite anchor also lies at distance two). Each of the k independent nonports would belong to W, contrary to |W|≤1 when r=k. Thus Nash–Williams applies with δ(H)≥k, |H|≤3k−3, α(H)≤k. Remove m from its spanning cycle. In all these mixed and private cases, append the p,D,q segment to the resulting port-rooted C path.

**At most one private class occurs.** An anchor meets all d vertices of D, so d=k−1. Every high vertex is necessarily common and universal within D. If all D vertices are common, the last order proof of the companion applies (only the number of D vertices and their being common enters it). Otherwise z is the only private vertex, say p-only. A p-port e in C still sees all k−1 vertices of D, and every q-port f sees at least k−2. Opposite types in C cannot touch at n≥3k+3, as their edge would make the p-port see q and exhaust its even budget, forcing it to be universal within C and giving the small order bound from the companion.

Use the same construction with a p-port e in C. The set U=N_C[e] and the unique internal second-layer vertex w separate V=C−U−{w} from U. All of V is adjacent to w. The case V empty gives small order from p as before. Otherwise pick a q-port f in V; 2-connectivity ensures one exists. Put A=N_U(w). The vertex f sees A and at least k−2 vertices of D at distance two, so 1≤|A|≤2.

At most 2−|A| vertices of U−A can fail to have a neighbor in A. Indeed f has eccentricity at most four, as after the k−2+|A| second-layer witnesses its fourth layer has at most one member and cannot separate a nonempty fifth layer. A length-three path from f to u∈U−A must have its penultimate in A. Within U only A can be reached from f in two steps: there are no U–V edges; f's only possible intermediary outside C is q, which misses U; and from V the sole route to U in two steps is via w. Outside U, the only vertices that could be adjacent to u and be penultimate are w and p (no U–V edges and no q-contact in U); w misses u, and f has distance at least three from p, by anticompleteness of the private types and pq being a nonedge. Hence any exceptions have distance four and number at most 2−|A|. Thus w sees at least |U|−2 vertices of U at distance two. Each a∈A also sees all V at distance two.

If |V|≤k−2 we have |C|≤(k+2)+1+(k−2)=2k+1. If |V|=k, every a∈A must be adjacent to p, which otherwise lies at distance two via e (w is at distance two from e, so a≠e). The vertex w meets neither anchor: adjacency to p would make wf an edge between private types, and adjacency to q would do the same through any a. Both anchors are therefore distance-two witnesses at w, giving |U|≤k. If |V|=k−1 and w were a q-port, each a would miss p by opposite-type anticompleteness while seeing p via e and q via w, impossible in addition to V. Hence w sees q at distance two via f, giving |U|≤k+1. In all cases |C|≤2k+1 and d=k−1, so n≤3k+2. This finishes the shallow-low case.
