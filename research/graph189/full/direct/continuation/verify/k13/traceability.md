# All qualifying graphs at second-smallest degree thirteen

Every connected graph satisfying the Graph 189 condition with second-smallest degree 13 has a spanning path. This proof uses no new finite calculation. Connectivity at most three and connectivity at least floor(13/2)−1=5 are already settled. At connectivity four the moderate-order result settles n≤67. The quadratic order bound gives n≤63+δ≤76.

Take a longest path maximizing the sum of squared exterior component orders, and then a smallest exterior component C containing a high vertex. Such a component exists in a counterexample by the almost-spanning result. Every high exterior vertex has at least seven neighbors in its component and that component has at least nine vertices. For a high noncut x∈C of C-degree q, the optimized exchange bounds the number r of ambient distance-two C vertices by 2q−13+1; without the low vertex on the path the bound improves by one. The remaining estimates use sep/k10/uniform-matching.md and sep/cover/local-cover-contacts.md.

## Large omitted components

We first show that C is 2-connected. Otherwise choose an endblock B of C whose noncut vertices are all high, write w for its articulation, b for its order, and q≥7 for its least noncut degree. The exterior-edge lemma excludes a low singleton as the entire remainder C−B, since such a leaf would have ambient degree at most two. Thus |C−B|≥7.

If b≥2q−1, the endblock count gives n≥9q+11, impossible at q≥8. If q=7 choose a minimizing noncut x and suppose r≤1. Every vertex at exact block distance two from x is also at exact ambient distance two, as x and that vertex are nonadjacent and the two-step block route exists. A singleton such layer cannot separate further vertices in a 2-connected block, so b≤9. This contradicts b≥13. Thus r=2 and the possible low vertex is on P; all members of C are high.

Put H=N_C[x] and R=N₂ᴳ(x)∩C. The eight vertices of H lie in B. All C-neighbors of members of H lie in H∪R, giving each H vertex at least five internal neighbors and at least four path contacts; x needs at least six contacts. Hence H is Hamilton-connected by its degree sum, and a matching of five H-to-P contacts exists. Indeed a four-cover would have to consist solely of marks, since otherwise an uncovered H vertex would have at most three covered marks, but four marks cannot cover x.

Set I=B−(H∪R∪{w}). This set has at least two members and has minimum internal degree at least four, since its members are noncut, have block degree at least seven, have no H neighbor and can lose at most the two members of R and w. For each matched H member v, the block B−v is connected, and a path from H−v to I can be trimmed to the segment from its last H vertex to its first I vertex. The segment has at least one intermediary because H and I are anticomplete. From v traverse H to the initial vertex of the segment, follow it, then take a path of at least five vertices in I from its end. Each tail thus gains at least six further vertices beyond H, while every inner gap uses eight. Counting C, five marks, six pieces and the two tail bonuses gives n≥20+5+6·8+2·6=85.

If b≤2q−2, use j≥max(3,14−b) matched noncut terminals. The endblock count is n≥(j+2)b+20. For b=8,9,10 it is 84,83,80, and for b≥12 it is at least 80. Only b=11 remains, forced into j=3, with n≥75. The near-Hamilton-connected terminal-routing lemma also applies to B itself, treating w as its only exceptional vertex since all noncuts have block degree at least seven. For three matched noncut terminals at most one of their two inner gaps uses the exceptional unordered pair; the other traverses all eleven vertices of B, gaining one over the ten noncuts already counted. Thus n≥76. This forces δ=13 and all vertices high. In each tail the route through B to w can now continue through at least seven members of C−B, as starting at a remainder neighbor of w its induced remainder degree is at least six. The original bound used only six such vertices per tail, so even that original bound of 75 increases to at least 77. This contradiction excludes every cut in C.

Write m=|C| and q for the minimum C-degree of its high vertices. If m≥2q, the four-connector cycle bound is n≥9q+7, impossible at q≥8. At q=7 the same one-vertex separator argument again shows r=2 and that all C members are high. Use the same eight-vertex H with five matched path contacts. Now I=C−(H∪R) is nonempty, has minimum degree at least five, and C−v is connected for every matched v. Connecting H to I and continuing through at least six I vertices gains at least seven vertices on both tails. Thus n≥14+5+6·8+2·7=81.

## The near-Hamilton-connected case

We have m≤2q−1. Let j be the maximum contact matching. The unrestricted local-cover theorem gives 3j<13 and m≥13−j+3. Together with four-connectivity these imply j=4 and m≥12. The matched-path count, allowing one exceptional terminal pair, is n≥(j+2)m+j−1=6m+3, so m=12 and n≥75.

Choose a minimum vertex cover of the full contact bipartite graph. It has size four by König’s theorem. It has u vertices in C and 4−u path marks. Choose a high c∈C outside the cover. Let b be its number of internal nonneighbors other than itself and let z count the real cover marks missed by c. Thus d(c)=15−u−b−z and b+z≤2−u. The local cover of its second layer uses at most 4+b neighbors. For any inclusion-minimal such cover of size p the bound is n≤1+d(c)+13+p(14−p). Here p≤4+b≤6, in the increasing part of this quadratic. At b=0,1,2 the respective bounds are 69,73,75, before allowing for any u or z. Since n≥75, necessarily n=75, b=2, u=z=0. Thus all cover vertices are path marks. The global order bound gives δ≥12. Every C member loses at most four neighbors to P and has internal degree at least eight. The entire twelve-vertex C is now Hamilton-connected. Its four matched contacts require n≥6·12+4=76, contradicting n=75. This proves the result.

Equivalently, the final numerical table can be replaced by verify/near-hc-exterior-size.md: j=4, m=12 would give r=m−1+j−13=2, in conflict with 2r≥j+1. The table above keeps an independent route to that last contradiction.
