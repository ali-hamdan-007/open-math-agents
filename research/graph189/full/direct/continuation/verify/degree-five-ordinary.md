# Longest paths at second-smallest degree five

Write z for a minimum-degree vertex, δ for its degree, P for a longest path and E for the vertices it omits. The layer bound gives n≤15+δ and the registered small-order and cut-vertex cases allow two-connectivity. Every vertex has at most five vertices at distance two. Each nontrivial exterior component W has two distinct vertices attached to distinct marks i<j on P. If the two vertices start paths on u and v vertices in W, and are joined in W by a path on d vertices, the two tails and the interval between the marks give |P|≥u+v+d+2.

## Tied degree five

This case passed the pinned kernel. The theorem [Graph189FiveTiedOriginal.traceable_tied_five](Graph189FiveTiedOriginal.lean) proves the original conclusion at every order from connectedness, the original even-distance bound and the two degree equalities. It uses only the usual three Lean axioms.

Every omitted vertex retains at least three omitted neighbors. A component on four or five vertices is Hamilton-connected by prescribed-endpoint Ore closure. For a component on five vertices the two-port bound gives |P|≥17 and hence n≥22. A component on four vertices gives |P|≥14 and at most six omissions. A second component would require another four vertices, so exactly four vertices are omitted.

In a larger component any root starts a four-vertex path. If it fails to start a five-vertex path, each initial edge belongs to a tetrahedron whose other three vertices have all their neighbors inside it. Consequently the root is universal, every other vertex has degree three, and a component larger than a tetrahedron has at least seven vertices. At most one of its two matched roots can fail to start a five-vertex path, since a second universal vertex would have degree three. This classification is [Graph189FiveExteriorRoots.deficient](Graph189FiveExteriorRoots.lean).

On at most seven vertices of minimum degree three, any two vertices are joined by at least three vertices. After deleting their possible shared edge, choose another neighbor on each side. Their two closed neighborhoods of size at least four intersect. If both matched roots start five vertices, their connecting path of at least two gives |P|≥14 and hence |W|≤6. The improved connecting path gives |P|≥15, contradicting |W|≥6. If only one root starts five, W has at least seven vertices. The first bound gives |P|≥13 and hence |W|≤7. The three-vertex connecting path now gives |P|≥14 and n≥21. This proves the [four-omission classification](Graph189FiveTiedExterior.lean).

More generally an ordinary longest path in a graph of minimum degree at least m+1 with at most m+1 distance-two vertices per vertex cannot omit exactly m vertices when m≥3 and n≤4m+4. The half-degree theorem gives Hamilton-connectedness of the omitted graph by prescribed Ore closure. Every omitted vertex has at least two path contacts. Three distinct matched contacts would give n≥5m+3. The Hall sharpening therefore makes all contact sets the same two marks. Of the three gaps the smallest has exactly m vertices. Exchanging it for the omitted vertices gives m+2 distance-two witnesses, including two shifts outside the gap. The general theorem is [Graph189OrdinaryTwoMarks.exclude](Graph189OrdinaryTwoMarkGap.lean). Taking m=4 finishes the tied case.

## A vertex of degree three or four

Here n≤19. Every omitted vertex distinct from z retains three neighbors, by the exterior half-degree theorem. If z lies in a nontrivial exterior component it has an exterior neighbor of degree at least five and retains at least two neighbors. Thus every nontrivial component has minimum degree two and at most one vertex below degree three. Each of its roots starts a four-vertex path by the rooted one-low-vertex bound. The two-port inequality gives |P|≥12 and |E|≤7.

The three-vertex connecting argument still applies. At each given endpoint, another neighbor of degree at least three can be chosen. A high endpoint has three choices before the exclusion, and the only low endpoint has exclusively high neighbors. Thus |P|≥13 and |E|≤6. A graph on five or six vertices with minimum degree two and at most one degree below three starts a five-vertex path at every root. To see this, a putative deficient root u starts a four-vertex path u,a,b,c with both a and b high. The terminal c has no neighbor outside those four. It must be adjacent to a, because otherwise it must be adjacent to u and the rotation u,c,b,a forces a adjacent to c. Rotate to u,a,c,b. The high terminal b must see u. Finally u,b,c,a makes a terminal. None of a,b,c now has any neighbor outside the four vertices. Every exterior vertex can meet only u in the four. Two such vertices are needed to allow degree two and one is high, requiring at least three exterior vertices. This is impossible on at most six vertices. Thus a component of five or six supplies |P|≥15 and n≥20. The remaining component has four vertices. Each high vertex is adjacent to the other three, so it is a tetrahedron. The two-port inequality gives |P|≥14. The only possible additional omission is the isolated low vertex.

An isolated omitted z can be brought onto a longest path. All δ neighbors are marked. Unless two marks are two positions apart, the predecessor and successor positions give 2δ>5 distinct distance-two witnesses. Otherwise exchange the intervening position for z.

If a tetrahedron is omitted, its high vertices each have at least two contacts. Three distinct matched contacts would give n≥23. Apply the Hall sharpening to the at least three high vertices and route the matching through the entire tetrahedron. Exactly two marks meet each high vertex. The two tails and middle interval each have at least four vertices. The path has at most fifteen vertices, hence at least two of the three gaps have exactly four. If z was omitted, exchange a tight tail for a spanning tetrahedron path from z to a high endpoint. On a longest path now containing z, choose a tight gap avoiding z. Exchanging it keeps z on the new longest path, whose omitted vertices are all high and again have the same two contacts. The old bounding mark sees the entire exchanged four-vertex gap. Together with two outside shifts it gives six vertices at distance two from an originally omitted vertex. The Lean entry point for this case is Graph189FiveHighMinimum.traceable; its transitive replay is in progress.

## A vertex of degree two

The full degree-five assembly uses @carl2sheriff's short-order theorem for this branch. At a degree-two vertex the checked layer estimate gives n≤1+2+5+2(6−2)=16. The registered small-order result covers n≤12 and the cut-vertex theorem handles a separating vertex. In the remaining two-connected case, the bounds 13≤n≤16 are exactly the interval from 2k+3 to 2k+6 at k=5. [Upstream proof and credits](https://github.com/lefttosolve2/open-math-agents/pull/22587) · [Degree-five application](Graph189FiveShortOrder.lean)

The following independent ordinary-path argument gives the same conclusion on paper.

The sharper rooted layer bound gives n≤16. The ordinary leaf and midpoint argument below has an independent mathematical audit. Its full Lean replay is pending.

An isolated omitted high vertex is impossible by the single-row shift. A high omitted x with a high omitted neighbor retains at least three omitted neighbors by the half-degree theorem. The only remaining exceptional possibility is that x has the sole omitted neighbor z. Then x needs at least four marks. Any four marks must be consecutive at spacing two: out of their eight predecessor and successor indices at most three overlaps are possible and exactly three are required. If an omitted path from x uses four vertices, all x-marks have four vertices on each side; their width six is impossible because n≤16 and there are already four omissions. Consequently an omitted component with xz is either the edge xz or the three-vertex path x,z,w, since z has degree two. In the latter case w is a high leaf with four marks of its own. Both rows need width six and start and end at least three vertices from the path ends. At n≤16 their window has width at most six, so the two rows are the same. Distinct-row marks two apart are incompatible with the three-vertex route x,z,w.

For the two-vertex component, z has one contact j and x has four spanning six positions. Inserting xz requires j to equal each x-mark or differ from it by at least three. The only possible j is at least three beyond their span, requiring overall width nine and hence |P|≥14. Since n≤16, x and z are the only omissions. Swapping x for the midpoint of any pair of consecutive x-marks leaves an omitted high vertex. It needs an omitted neighbor and hence is adjacent to z. The three distinct midpoints force three neighbors at z, contradicting its degree two.

All high ordinary omissions thus retain three exterior neighbors. A component containing a high vertex has at least four vertices. Every root in that component starts a four-vertex path by the one-low bound, giving |P|≥12 and at most four omissions at n≤16. Consequently a nonempty high component is the whole omitted tetrahedron. Two distinct marked contacts at two high endpoints give |P|≥14, incompatible with n≤16. A nonspanning longest path therefore omits just z.

After deleting z the graph is traceable. On a longest path Q through z the omitted vertices have internal minimum degree two. Their number m satisfies 3+3m≤n≤16; nonempty cases are m=3 or 4. For m=4, every degree-two vertex of the exterior has a clique of neighbors by the protected contact inequality. If at least two such vertices exist, choose two and a third exterior vertex. The two low rows each have at least three contacts, the third has at least two, so a three-port matching exists. Each selected pair includes a simplicial endpoint and is joined by a spanning exterior path. The protected three-port bound gives n≥19. Otherwise at most one vertex has internal degree two, so the four-vertex exterior is complete. The protected Hall and two-mark exchange used for k≥7 apply directly and give six distance-two witnesses.

For m=3 the omitted graph is a triangle and each row has at least three contacts. A matching of three different marks produces four positive gaps. All gaps avoiding z contain at least three vertices. Since |Q|≤13, equality is forced. The three safe gaps have size three, the gap containing z has size one, and |Q|=13. With positions numbered from zero, z is at 0,4,8 or 12 and its position determines the matched triple uniquely. Any contact in any row can be extended to a three-row matching. All rows therefore coincide with the unique triple. Exchange a size-three gap avoiding z. The new path again has the same uniform three-contact property. Among predecessors and successors of the three original marks there are at least five distinct indices and no more than two lie inside the exchanged gap. Its three vertices and at least three shifts outside then give six distance-two witnesses.
