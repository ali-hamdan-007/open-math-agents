# Independent audit of the half-degree connectivity theorem

The proof in ../lowblock/high-connectivity-two-endblocks.md closes the remaining case of the previous connectivity manuscript. The strict bound is κ>k/2, where k is the second-smallest degree counting multiplicity. For k≥4 set t=floor(k/2), h=ceil(k/2).

I rechecked the dependencies. The earlier exterior-degree bound gives internal degree at least h to each high vertex with a high exterior neighbor. The earlier half-size exclusion places any component containing a high vertex at order at least h+2. The quadratic order bound is t²+5t+1 when k is even and t²+6t+4 when k is odd. A cycle of length at least 2h disjoint from a longest path contradicts these bounds by the t disjoint Menger connectors and longest-path gap inequalities. Fan's circumference theorem on a 2-connected block with all but at most one vertex of degree at least h consequently gives a Hamilton cycle and order at most 2h−1. The earlier two-endblock argument permits shared cut vertices and marks. Mixed 2-connected exterior components are excluded by endpoint-preserving closure in a graph with one degree-two marker. The sole-low-singleton exterior is excluded by the 2δ path shifts. These reductions leave one mixed exterior component C with just two endblocks, with the unique low vertex z a noncut vertex of one of them.

For the other endblock B with cut vertex w and order b, every other vertex has internal degree at least h, so h+1≤b≤2h−1. A vertex cover of fewer than t edges' ends for its noncut-to-P contact bipartite graph would disconnect a surviving vertex of each set after also removing w, using at most t vertices. Thus it supplies t matched ports at distinct marks. Adding a degree-two marker at any prescribed pair yields order b+1≤2h, so Bondy–Chvátal filling makes B−w a clique while preserving the existence of a spanning path between the pair. Since B is 2-connected, w has at least two block-neighbors. There is at most one exceptional unordered pair: exactly those neighbors when it has precisely two. Both tails can end the full B path at w and then append a neighbor in R=C−B. Distinct contacts allow at most one exceptional intervening gap, where a longer B-cycle arc loses at most floor(b/2)−1 vertices. For b=h+1 the block is already a clique and loses zero.

If R contains a high vertex, C−z is connected since z is noncut. A simple path from w to a high vertex in R avoiding z must start with a high R neighbor of w, since one cannot pass from B−w into R. That neighbor witnesses r=|R|≥h by its internal degree. Counting the t marked vertices, t+1 intervals and all C gives at least t+(t+2)b+r+2−s vertices. For b=h+1 the lower bounds are t²+5t+4 and t²+6t+7, respectively. For b≥h+2, use s≤h−2 to get at least t+(t+2)(h+2)+4. All exceed the quadratic upper bounds.

Thus R consists only of low z. It is adjacent within C only to w. Write q=d_C(w)≥h and A_x for the P-attachments of x. There are q−1 distinct C vertices at distance two from z. The |A_z|≥t marks give |A_z|+u distinct distance-two path shifts, where u≥1 is the number of maximal runs at step two. Consecutive cross marks of z,w would lengthen P. Thus the marks A_w−A_z are disjoint from the shifts, and all lie at distance two from z. The three sets give k≥q−1+|A_z|+u+|A_w−A_z|≥h+t=k. Hence q=h, |A_z|=t≥2, one run, and A_w⊆A_z. Every member of the run has another mark at distance two; if w had any contact the detour through z,w between that contact and the other mark would strictly lengthen P. Then d_G(w)=q=h<k, contrary to w being high.

Every qualifying graph with κ>k/2 has a spanning path. The k≤3 cases follow from the separately established small-degree results. No assertion covering all κ follows from this threshold.
