# Three or more components at a minimum four-cut

At every k≥16 a qualifying graph is traceable if a minimum cut of four vertices leaves at least three components. This fills the numerical boundary k=16,17,18 in the previous k>18 component-count theorem for four-cuts. Suppose n≥6k−10 by the four-connectivity moderate-order theorem.

Fix an anchor and choose high neighbors in three distinct components. Let t_i be the number of anchor neighbors of the chosen vertex in component i and p_i the number of neighbors of the fixed anchor in that component. The three closed internal neighborhoods and the anchor's even budget give Σp_i≥2k+3−Σt_i. Each of the three roots spends at least 4−t_i even slots within its own component or the anchors by its closed-neighborhood boundary, and sees all selected-anchor neighbor sets of the other two components at distance two. Summation gives 2Σp_i≤3k−12+Σt_i. Hence 3Σt_i≥k+18≥34. Since t_i≤4, all three roots touch every anchor.

In particular there are at least two wholly high components all of whose ports are universal to the cut. If there are at least three wholly high components, choose any port in one and two contacts on the same anchor in the other two. Otherwise the exceptional low belongs to a nonsingleton third component or is a singleton third component. In the nonsingleton case that component has a high port since its only attachment cannot be a single low vertex at connectivity four. Pick such a port and two wholly high representatives at one of its anchors. All three are universal, so that high port now supplies a representative at every anchor to prove universality of every port in the two wholly high components. A low singleton with just two other components was already excluded by the separate k>2s−2 part of global/minimum-cut-two-sides.md, which gives n≤3k+1. If a low singleton has at least three other components they are wholly high, already considered.

At least one of the two wholly high components with all its ports universal, say X, has a nonport. Otherwise both consist of at least k−3 universal ports, more than k in total, all seen at distance two by any third component's port. A port of X sees every outside port at exact distance two. A high nonport of X sees every outside high nonport at distance four, with a second layer of at least four whenever such an outside nonport exists. The components outside X therefore total at most k+(k−4)+1=2k−3 vertices. It follows that |X|≥4k−11. At most k of them are ports, as seen by any outside port. Thus X has at least 3k−11>k−4 high nonports, precluding every outside high nonport by the same fourth-layer budget. At least two outside components have high vertices, except for the already excluded three-component low-singleton case. Each has order ≥k−3 and at least k−4 ports. These exceed the bound k on all outside ports, a contradiction.
