# Connectivity at least three halves of a local Ore excess

In the full Graph 189 problem let r≥3 be an integer bounding d(w)+e(w)−d(u)−d(v) on every induced wedge with u,v distinct from some fixed root. Then

    κ(G)≥ceil(3r/2) implies traceability for every k.

In particular r may be Δ−k, independently of the one possible below-k degree. Write s=ceil(3r/2), h=ceil(k/2). By the half-connectivity theorem assume k≥2s+2. Then r≤floor((k−1)/2), so the [matching wedge theorem](wedge-penultimate-matching.md) lets us work at n≤N=(r+3)k+1−r(r+1).

Use the optimized exterior component, good endblock B (or whole 2-connected component), b=|B| and least internal degree q at its high/nonexceptional vertices, as in [the large-k proof](connectivity-three-halves.md). Write the small side in the proof below for b≤2q−1, and the large side for b≥2q.

In the large case a cycle of length at least 2q=2 floor((2q)/2) exists by Fan. With s disjoint connectors to P (h≥s+1) the cycle-tail and longer-arc count gives n≥(s+5)q+2s−1. Thus q∈[k/2,u] for u=(N−2s+1)/(s+5). The optimized external-neighborhood bound says either 3q>2k−3 or f(q)=(q+1)(2k−3q+1)−N−1≤0.

In the small case B is Hamiltonian and every prescribed pair admits a spanning route except possibly the two neighbors of its exceptional low vertex/articulation of internal degree two. Even for this pair a path uses b−1 vertices: replace that exceptional vertex by a marker on the same two neighbors, preserving the high degrees ≥q, then close the high clique since 2q≥b+1. The whole component therefore gives n≥(s+2)b+s−1. For a terminal block, s−1 noncut ports match to P, each outer tail can span B to its cut vertex (never a bad pair) and continues through ≥h−1 vertices outside it. The outside remainder has ≥h vertices. Relative to a whole-component (s−1)-contact count, this gains ≥3h−2. It follows for b≤k−1 that n≥(s+2)b+s−1 also, because 3h−2≥b+1. If b≥k, the terminal bound ≥(s+1)b+s−2+3h−2 already exceeds N since s+1≥r+3, and so does the whole-component bound.

Thus consider b≤k−1 and set a=k−b+1, F(b)=(a+2)b+a. The usual matching excluding one member of a possible exceptional pair gives n≥F(b) for a whole component, and n≥F(b)+3h−2 for a terminal block. The detailed port count is in local-excess-connectivity.md, including the case b=h+1 of a clique endblock. Put v=(N−s+1)/(s+2). We must have b∈[k/2+1,v] and g(b)=F(b)−N≤0.

The finite arithmetic certificate is connectivity-certificate.py. For r≥18 it takes the weaker s₀=3r/2 in u and v, increasing their upper bounds. Under r=18+y, k=3r+2+x, with x,y≥0, each of the numerators of (2k−3)/3−u, f(k/2), f(u), g(k/2+1), g(v) has positive constant term and nonnegative coefficients. The denominators are positive. Concavity excludes both cases. For 3≤r≤17 the earlier large-k proof covers k≥(4r+12)(s+5); enumerating the remaining scalar intervals leaves only the following five tuples.

In the large case (r,k,q)=(4,20,10), |C|≥b≥20. For an optimizing high noncut vertex x of degree q, X=N_C[x] after deleting any low has ≥10 vertices of minimum degree ≥9, hence is Hamilton-connected. The optimized shift inequality gives ≤1 exterior second-layer vertex in C, so every high X member has ≥9 P-neighbors. Nine matched contacts give n≥20+9+10·10=129>N=121.

The small tuples (r,k,b) are (3,12,8),(4,14,8),(4,14,9),(4,15,10). All terminal blocks are excluded by F(b)+3h−2>N. The whole-component choice b=8 when k=14 fails b≥h+2. In the other three cases, respectively, a=5,6,6 and N−F(b)=0,1,0. A contact matching of size a+1 would give n≥(a+3)b+a>N even allowing the one missed vertex on the exceptional pair. Each high C vertex needs ≥a P-neighbors and there are >a such vertices. Therefore a matching has size exactly a, and a size-a cover must consist entirely of marks A. Every high C member touches every mark and, to reach degree k=(b−1)+a, every other C member. Thus C is a clique, and all C contacts to P lie at those marks. Each mark touches every high member, so all a+1 gaps/tails on any rotated path with the same vertex set have ≥b unmarked vertices. Their total excess σ satisfies σ≤N−F(b)≤1. The ≥k+2 attainable endpoints include at least k+1 high endpoints; at most k can touch A by the even cap at a high member of C. From an attainable high endpoint e avoiding A, rotation at any vertex among the first b after a mark would create a new tail of length <b. Hence d(e)≤b−1+σ<k, as a>1. This excludes the last tuples and proves the theorem.

## Rounding down for odd r at least five

In fact κ≥floor(3r/2) suffices for every r≥4. Only odd r≥5 require an improvement. Set s=(3r−1)/2 and use the same N,u,v. In the large case b≥2q the optimized neighborhood packing gives a stronger obstruction than f(q)≤0 when 3q≤2k−3. Indeed the high part of the closed neighborhood of a minimizing x is Hamilton-connected, has ≥q vertices and has a₀=2k−3q−1 disjoint contacts, as proved in two-thirds-exterior-degree.md. Here the exterior component has order at least 2q, rather than the q+1 used in that paper's coarse bound. Counting it in full gives n≥2q+a₀+(a₀+1)q. Thus take f(q)=(2k−3q+2)q+2k−3q−1−N. All other concavity and case steps are identical, and s+1≥r+3 holds for r≥5.

The supplementary certificate connectivity-odd-certificate.py checks the five numerator polynomials at r=20+y, k=3r+1+x, and at each odd r in [5,19] for k=200+x. All have strictly positive constant and nonnegative coefficients. The finite complement leaves no large case and only (r,k,b)=(5,16,9),(5,16,10),(5,17,11),(5,18,12). The terminal-block bonus excludes all four. A whole component with k=16,b=9 again fails b≥h+2. For the other three a=7 and the possible σ=N−F(b) is 2,1,0 respectively. As before an (a+1)-matching is forbidden even with one bad gap, forcing the uniform high-contact clique, and σ<a contradicts the reachable high endpoint bound. Thus the rounding-down improvement holds.

## Four-connectivity for r=3

Finally κ≥4 suffices also when r=3. This gives κ≥floor(3r/2) uniformly for all r≥3. We may take k≥10 by the half-connectivity theorem, and n≤N=6k−11 by the matching wedge theorem. Keep the same optimized P, C, B, b, q and h.

If b≥2q, four connectors to a Fan cycle of length ≥2q give n≥9q+7, hence 3q≤2k−6. Put A=2k−3q≥6 and let x be a minimizing high noncut vertex of B. Set X=N_C[x]⊆B and R=N₂(x)∩C. Write ε=1 if the low vertex lies on P, zero otherwise. The optimized exchange gives |R|≤2q−k+ε and every high member of X has internal X-degree at least k−q and at least A−ε P-contacts. Delete any low member from X to form H. Its order is at least q (q+1 if ε=1), and it is Hamilton-connected by 3q≤2k−6. The A−ε required distinct ports match since A≤q.

Inside B−(X∪R) delete the possible exceptional vertex of B when present there, namely a low vertex in the whole component or the articulation of a terminal B. The resulting I has at least b−(q+1)−|R|−1≥k−q−2−ε≥1 vertices. Its minimum degree is at least q−|R|−1 since there are no X–I edges. For every matched H-port v, B−v is connected. Take a path from H−v to I in B−v, keeping the part from its last H vertex to its first I vertex. Its interior contains at least one vertex because there are no X–I edges. Traverse H from v to the entry of that part, follow it, then append a path in I from its end through at least q−|R| vertices, by maximal rooted path degree. Thus each outer tail uses all H plus at least q−|R|+1 further vertices.

At ε=0 this gives n≥(A+1)q+A+2k+2. For A≥7 use q≥k/2 to exceed 6k−11, and at A=6 use q=(2k−6)/3 for the same strict inequality. At ε=1 put a=A−1≥5, use |H|=q+1 and q−|R|+1≥k−q. The result is n≥(a+1)q+2a+2k+1. For a≥7 use q≥k/2, for a=5,6 substitute q=(2k−a−1)/3; all give n>6k−11.

It remains b≤2q−1. In the terminal case the three matched contacts and the outside remainder/tails give n≥5b+3h, as before. This already beats N when b≥k−3. For h+1≤b≤k−3 the concave bound F(b)+3h−2 beats N at both endpoints. At b=k−3 its excess is 3h−5, and at b=h+1 it is t²−5t+11 for k=2t and t²−4t+10 for k=2t+1.

For the whole component the four contacts give n≥6m+3, so m≤k−3 and a=k−m+1≥4. Also k≥2a+2 as m≥h+2. Any (a+1)-matching would yield n≥(a+3)m+a, exceeding 6k−11 by (a−3)k−a²−a+14≥a²−5a+8>0. Yet the high vertices yield an a-matching. Thus a minimum cover of size a consists of marks only, giving a clique C with all highs sharing its whole contact set. The earlier rotated endpoint bound forces slack σ≥a, so n≥(a+2)m+2a. The excess above 6k−11 is (a−4)k−a²+a+13, which is one at a=4 and is at least a²−5a+5>0 for a≥5. This closes the final case.
