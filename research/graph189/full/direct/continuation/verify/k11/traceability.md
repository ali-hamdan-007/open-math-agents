# Traceability at second-smallest degree eleven

Every finite connected qualifying graph with second-smallest degree 11 has a spanning path. This uses the previously checked theorems for connectivity at most three, connectivity at least floor(k/2), unrestricted moderate order n≤6k−11 at connectivity four, and the quadratic order bound. Together these leave κ=4, 56≤n≤59 and minimum degree δ≥n−48. The remaining calculation has 475 exact Boolean and linear-real cases. Its proof backend is Z3.

## Reduction to four anchors

Take a longest path P with a high-bearing omitted component C. The near-spanning theorem guarantees such a component in a counterexample, since at most one graph vertex is low. Write m=|C|. The exterior-degree and equality exclusions give m≥8 and internal degree at least h=6 at every high C vertex. A low leaf with a single C neighbor, when that neighbor is high, has ambient degree at most two by the earlier path attachment inequality.

Suppose C has a cut vertex. Choose a leaf block H whose noncut vertices are all high. Let b=|H| and let q≥6 be the least block degree of a noncut vertex. The remainder C−H contains a high vertex, since otherwise it would be the excluded single low leaf. It therefore has at least six vertices: at most the articulation is a C neighbor beyond the remainder of any such high vertex. If b≥2q−1, Fan's theorem supplies a cycle of order at least 11 in H. Four disjoint connectors to P give two outer tails with at least eleven unmarked vertices each and three inner gaps with at least seven each. Adding four marks and |C|≥17 gives n≥64. If b≤2q−2, removing the articulation leaves a Hamilton-connected block interior. There are at least t=max(3,12−b) matched contacts to the path from this interior. Its size is at least six, every member needs at least 12−b path contacts, and a cover of size at most two together with the articulation contradicts four-connectivity. The prior endblock matching bound gives n≥(t+2)b+3h−1. Each of the t−1 inner gaps uses b−1 vertices. The outer tails can traverse the block to the articulation and continue into the remainder for at least h−1 additional vertices apiece; the component itself contributes at least b+h. At b=7,8 this lower bound is 66,65 respectively, and at b≥9 it is at least 62. Thus C is 2-connected.

Let q now be the minimum degree in C over its high vertices. If m≥2q then Fan gives a cycle of order at least 12, and the four-connector count gives n≥3·12+4+3(6+1)=61. Thus m≤2q−1. By the earlier prescribed-endpoint degree-two-marker lemma, C is Hamiltonian and supports any prescribed pair of distinct terminals as the ends of a spanning path, with at most one exceptional unordered pair, which still admits a path on m−1 vertices. A matching with t distinct C-to-P contacts therefore gives n≥(t+2)m+t−1. Connectivity supplies four contacts, so m≤9. Five already give n≥7·8+4=60, and the maximum matching size is exactly four. Choose a minimum bipartite vertex cover of four contacts.

* If m=8, every high C vertex needs four P neighbors. A cover using any C vertex leaves an uncovered high C vertex with at most three covered marks, an impossibility. Thus the cover consists of four marks. Each high vertex touches all four and all seven other C vertices. Put A=C and S equal to these marks. The side A is an eight-clique, all high members are complete to S, and any low A member touches at least δ−7 and at most three anchors.
* If m=9, every uncovered high C vertex needs at least three marks. The cover therefore consists of four marks, or three marks and u∈C. In the latter case put A=C−u and S equal to the cover. Every high A vertex has three P neighbors and all eight of its C neighbors, including u. Thus the same eight-clique case applies. Only the three true P marks supply their two path brackets here.
* In the remaining case put A=C and let S be the four marks. The basic four-contact bound already gives n≥57, hence δ≥9. Every A vertex has at least δ−4≥5 internal neighbors. By the usual Hamilton-connected degree-sum criterion, all prescribed distinct terminal pairs work in this nine-vertex graph, so the four-contact bound improves to n≥6·9+4=58. Every high A vertex touches at least three marks. Whenever it omits a mark, its three contacts force all eight internal neighbors. Its distance to any missed mark is therefore two. Distinct anchors have distance at most three. Indeed, if two have no common A contact, at least one has a high contact. This contact misses the other anchor and is universal within A, hence is adjacent to any A contact of the other anchor.

In each case the rest B=V(G)−(A∪S) can have several components. The four anchors separate A from B, so each touches both and each anchor subset J has at least |J| neighbors in B by the separator form of Hall's theorem. A nine-vertex A gives at least 2|J| such neighbors from the disjoint immediate brackets on P. For size eight at most one member of S lies off P, giving at least 2(|J|−1) distinct brackets in addition to the ordinary Hall lower bound. The proof uses the stronger of these last two bounds, without assuming that an off-path anchor has two contacts in B.

## Distance and population calculation

Here |B|=n−4−|A|≥44=4k. An edge from x∈A to any neighbor cannot lower two distances to S. If it did, choose that neighbor for the two improved anchors and at most one neighbor for each of the remaining two anchors along shortest paths. Every B vertex has an x-geodesic through S, and one of the chosen steps reduces its distance by one. The even sets of x and at most three chosen neighbors therefore cover B. Of the two originally improved anchors choose one distinct from that first neighbor. Its distances from x and that neighbor are positive and differ by one, so it supplies at least one additional even witness outside B. This would give |B|≤4k−1.

For each side, possible low location, and four-anchor distance vector v, the program assigns a Boolean flag and a real population. Positive populations are at least one and the unique low type has population one. The total side sizes and lower bound on δ above are imposed. Same-side prospective neighbors must have coordinate differences at most one, and inside A respect the preceding first-step restriction in both directions. The same restriction is applied to steps from A to an adjacent anchor. Counts of eligible neighbors include the root itself. Every vector needs an actual first-step candidate toward each anchor not achievable by stepping first to an incident anchor.

Opposite distances are exactly the least coordinate sum through a shared anchor. Thus forced even types debit the root's quota. On one side, a coordinate difference lower-bounds the distance while a route through the anchors and the pairwise diameter bound give upper bounds. Their equality at a positive even distance supplies further certain witnesses. High pairs have distance at most four and arbitrary pairs at most five. A high A vertex supplies the upper bound of three on high B depth and four on low B depth even when it itself touches only three marks: a path to the opposite high vertex must first leave A. Every positive fourth layer requires a second layer of at least four. The boundary of each root’s closed internal neighborhood has size at least four and all its members except direct anchor contacts are at distance two in that side or S. Forced opposite-side even vertices plus forced same-side and anchor fourth-layer vertices must respect these reserved places.

The exact population tests are in k11.py. Up to relabelling of anchors we enumerate integral anchor-distance matrices with entries one or two when |A|=8 and one through three when |A|=9, imposing the triangle inequalities, together with the allowed order and the no-low, low-on-either-side, or low-anchor automorphism orbits. At n=59 the quadratic bound forces δ=11. There are precisely 475 cases. For |A|=8 the counts for n=56,57,58,59 are 53,53,53,11. For |A|=9 they are 257,48 at n=58,59. Every case is UNSAT. Five short-timeout cases were rerun to completion. The results exclude the required separator geometry and establish traceability.

Run `python run.py --timeout 80000 --workers 3` using Python and system libz3.so.4, or `python controls.py` to test six explicit qualifying graphs on their measured BFS populations. The controls have second degree eleven, use both candidate side sizes and cover all categories of low location. Only the B-population target is changed to its true control value. The complete mask and distance constraints are retained.
