# A directly checkable two-side path certificate

Given a specific finite connected graph G and a supplied vertex set S whose deletion leaves two components A,B, choose r∈S. Assign any of S−{r} to either side, disjointly, with the option to omit some. On each resulting vertex set D take the induced graph, requiring it connected and with at least one actual r-neighbor. Form an augmented graph H_D by adding a marker adjacent to all those genuine r-neighbors and to at most one extra vertex of D. Any lower bound L on the order of a cycle in H_D yields a path in D on at least L−1 vertices starting at a genuine r-neighbor. When the marker is on the cycle, delete it; at least one endpoint is genuine as only one incident edge can be artificial. Otherwise connect any genuine r-neighbor to the cycle by a shortest path in D, meeting it for the first time at its endpoint, then traverse the opened cycle. A single genuine r-neighbor always gives a length-one route.

Consequently an independently checkable left/right pair of cycle bounds yields a path on at least 1+max(1,L_A−1)+max(1,L_B−1) vertices in G, through r. The separator need not have minimum cardinality, the sides may contain the unique low vertex or their own cut vertices, and the exceptional additional marker edge may be chosen separately per side. This directly tests actual side structures. When the lower bound reaches |G| it proves traceability without the Graph 189 hypothesis. On a qualifying Graph 189 graph, a lower bound of |G|−ceil(k/2)−1 suffices by the previously proved omitted-component and near-spanning theorems. Unsuccessful tests are inconclusive.

The standard-library Python implementation torso-certificate.py explicitly verifies the original graph's degree and positive-even caps, then tries all joining anchors and assignments. It checks marker choices in turn, retaining the largest witnessed guarantee per side. The output lists its labels and the exact test used. In a reported clique-cover certificate, vertex numbers index the accompanying side_vertices list, with the next number assigned to the marker. Every listed clique can be verified edge by edge. The tool stops once it has a sufficient certificate, so the reported lengths need not be best possible.

* Bondy–Chvátal cycle closure certifies a spanning cycle if the closure obtained from nonedges with degree sum at least the augmented order N becomes complete.
* On a 2-connected augmentation, Fan gives a cycle of order at least min(N,2f), where f is the minimum max(d(x),d(y)) over its distance-two pairs.
* If the augmentation has no independent triple, its independence number is at most two, and Chvátal–Erdős gives a spanning cycle from 2-connectivity. Otherwise explicitly minimize d(x)+d(y)+d(z) over independent triples to obtain σ₃. A partition into c cliques certifies α≤c. If σ₃≥N+2, the circumference theorem gives L≥min(N,N+ceil(σ₃/3)−c). This includes the σ₃≥max(N+2,3c) Hamiltonicity condition. Bauer, Broersma, Veldman and Rao, JCTB 47 (1989), Theorems 4 and 5, https://fileserver-az.core.ac.uk/download/572157558.pdf.

Input is JSON with integer n, undirected edges as unordered pairs on labels 0 through n−1 and separator as a list of labels. Run python torso-certificate.py INPUT.json. For fixed separator order the search is polynomial in n, with 3^(|S|−1) assignments per r. Clique covers are constructive upper bounds; a particular independent set or its exact maximum size is not required. The engine reverses the added edges of a complete Bondy–Chvátal closure, recovering an explicit cycle and side path. When both routes are explicit it also returns the combined vertex list. Other tests return a theorem-based lower bound. An optional ordinary side route uses a marker adjacent to every side vertex and trims the shorter terminal run of genuine noncontacts. This requires a certified path longer than the number of noncontacts; the loss is at most half that count rounded down. No Hamilton-path oracle is used.

A fallback tests collective anchor compatibility without assigning additional S vertices to either side. For a subset R⊆S, the side is tested using genuine contacts to any member of R, yielding c_A(R) and c_B(R). For required side lengths p,q it is sufficient that every subset L⊆S satisfies c_A(S−L)≥p or c_B(L)≥q. Indeed let L consist of the anchors which can start an A route of the required length. The first disjunct is false for this L, so an anchor in L can start the required B route and the two paths join. The partition result includes the complete table of witnessing tests, with bitmasks indexing anchor_labels; for row L, a 0 refers to A on the complement and a 1 to B on L. This extends the degree-independent all-but-one anchor intersection argument to arbitrary per-graph compatibility data. It need not identify a particular common anchor.

The separate verifier torso-certificate-check.py checks every connected labelled side of orders one through five and every nonempty genuine contact set against exact exhaustive rooted paths. It also exercises the full input-to-certificate procedure on the qualifying 19-vertex prescribed-anchor obstruction and on a nonqualifying nontraceable star.

A connected side with cut vertices has an additional certificate even if none of its single-marker augmentations has a sufficiently long cycle. Compute its blocks and the tree of their vertex incidences. In a block Q with prescribed distinct entry x and exit y, put a degree-two marker on x,y; a spanning cycle of this augmentation certifies a spanning x-to-y route. Otherwise a cycle of order c in a 2-connected Q gives a route on at least ceil(c/2)+1 vertices by two vertex-disjoint x,y-to-cycle links and the longer arc. A bridge contributes its two vertices. Along each branch from a genuine port to a terminal vertex, sum these lengths and subtract one per shared articulation. The block interiors are disjoint, so all local routes concatenate. The certificate records the blocks, entry and exit vertices, and bound used at each. When the local cycles are reconstructed by closure, it returns the actual combined path.

## Constructive rooted extension

The first side candidate is an explicit path built from its genuine-contact marker. More generally start at any vertex z of a connected graph and keep a current path P starting there. For an exterior u touching P, extend if the free endpoint touches u, insert u at consecutive contacts, or insert u and an exterior common neighbor between a contact and its successor. For two contacts in order whose successors have a chord, follow P to the first contact, visit u and the second contact, traverse the old segment backwards to the first successor, and follow the chord to the second successor and the remaining suffix. Every move preserves the root and all old vertices and adds one or more vertices. If all moves fail at this u, its contact successors are independent, miss u, and all their common neighbors with u are contacts. The rooted Ore incidence count in ../root/rooted-local-ore.md therefore produces a wedge of positive local defect with both endpoints distinct from z. Under the rooted local Ore condition an extension exists at every step until P spans. This makes the criterion constructive in polynomial time. With no degree-sum hypothesis every intermediate route remains a checkable path, including the route returned if extensions stall.

The standalone rooted-ore-construction-check.py enumerates all connected labelled graphs through order six, all starting roots and all wedge defects, checking both the explicit returned paths and spanning in every rooted-L0 instance. torso-certificate-check.py additionally checks a branching block-tree route and compares 80 end-to-end solver results on orders eight through ten with exact Hamilton-path dynamic programming.

A further Hamiltonicity certificate uses Chvátal–Erdős with an explicit lower bound on vertex connectivity. If a partition into c cliques is available, test each nonedge xy. Its common neighbors give disjoint two-edge paths. A matching of edges between its two exclusive neighbor sets gives additional three-edge paths whose interiors are disjoint from all those common neighbors and one another. If each pair has c paths in total, any separator disconnecting the augmentation must meet c such paths for a separated nonadjacent pair. Thus κ≥c≥α and a spanning cycle exists. The matching tests are polynomial and reproducible from the local vertex order and the supplied clique cover.
