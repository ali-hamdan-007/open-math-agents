# A prescribed anchor at a small side of a minimum cut

Let S be a minimum cut of size s≥3, with components A and B and d=k−s≥3. Suppose |A|≤2d−1 and each vertex of A except possibly one has ambient degree at least k. Thus all high vertices have internal degree at least d. If A is 2-connected, a spanning path can start at any prescribed vertex x of A. Fix any free terminal other than x, choosing it to avoid the two neighbors of the low vertex as the terminal pair if the latter has internal degree two. In the graph obtained by attaching a marker to both terminals, closure completes the high clique since the degree sums are at least 2d≥|A|+1. The resulting graph admits a Hamilton cycle through the marker by inserting the low vertex into the high clique (it has at least two internal neighbors). Reversing closure and erasing the marker gives the desired path.

If A has a cut vertex, every ordinary terminal block with a high noncut vertex has at least d noncut vertices. Two such endblocks exceed |A|. There is therefore a singleton low leaf z, with sole internal neighbor w, and these are the only two endblocks. A−z is 2-connected: only w can have internal degree below d, and it still has degree at least d−1≥2, so every leaf of a non-2-connected A−z would have a noncut vertex other than w with internal degree at least d. Two such leaves cannot fit. The graph A−z is Hamilton-connected, by the degree sum ≥2d−1≥|A−z|+1 at all nonedges. Now any port other than w can start a spanning path through A, including z. Only an anchor r whose sole A-contact is w can be bad. Since z has only one internal neighbor and ambient degree ≥s by connectivity, it touches at least s−1 anchors, so at most one anchor can be bad. When d=2, |A|≤3 forces a triangle if A contains any high vertex (two leaves in a three-vertex path could not both reach degree d internally); the same adaptive conclusion holds.

Here is an explicit Graph 189 example where an individual prescribed anchor fails. Make S a clique of order seven with a distinguished r, and B a clique of order five. Form A from a clique of order six with a distinguished w and a leaf z attached only to w inside A. Make the six vertices of S other than r universal, and join r to all of B and just w in A. The order is 19. The articulation-free whole graph has connectivity exactly seven: deleting at most six leaves a universal vertex except if all six universal vertices were deleted, in which case r,w still connect the graph. Deleting S disconnects. The degrees include d(z)=7, five B members and five ordinary A-clique members of degree 11, d(r)=12, d(w)=13, and six degree-18 vertices. Thus k=11. All distances are at most two and the maximal positive-even count is 18−7=11. Here d=4 and |A|=7=2d−1, yet the only r-contact is w, which cannot start a spanning path of A because its pendant z would require immediate termination.

For the two-side joining argument, use the prescribed-pair form of Ali's apex lemma. Given any two acceptable anchors, augment B by an apex joined to all their B contacts and to the low B vertex if one exists. The genuine augmentation is 2-connected: deleting any v∈B cannot strand a component of B−v with no chosen contact as that would be separated in G by S minus the two anchors and v. Every high vertex has augmented degree ≥d+1, and the apex is adjacent to the only possible exceptional low. Fan's theorem yields a cycle on ≥min(|B|+1,2d+2) vertices. Remove the apex if used, starting at the other, genuine contact if an artificial edge was used; otherwise join the cycle by a path from a genuine contact. This gives a B-path using ≥min(|B|,2d+1) vertices whose start meets one of the two good anchors. Join to the spanning A-path there. Hence |B|≤2d+ceil(k/2)+3−s suffices by the almost-spanning theorem for a possible low in either side.
