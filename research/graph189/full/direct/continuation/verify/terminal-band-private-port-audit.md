# Private contacts in the terminal separator argument

Let A,B,S be disjoint cut sides, let s=|S|, and call a vertex high when its degree is at least the second-smallest degree k. Write X,Y for the respective high populations of A and B. The following checks concern the terminal-band hypotheses Y>sk, k>6(s−1), and the previously established support classification at every minimum cut. The three-contact conclusion must apply to the smaller high side of **every** minimum cut for the final change of cut.

**A high private predecessor always exists.** Suppose a B nonport y has i as its sole S vertex at distance two. Any middle vertex b in i–b–y belongs to B. Every label touching b is at distance two from y, so i is b's only label. If b is low then i and y are high, and the two-ball count at b forces another common neighbor of i and y. Indeed their neighborhoods, together with i and y, lie in the closed two-ball of b. A unique common neighbor would force d(b)≥k. The second common neighbor is high and private at i.

**A full high port forces such a contact at each prescribed label i.** Use the s roots consisting of that A port a and the labels other than i. Since Y>sk there is a high B vertex y at odd distance from every root. Its distance from a is three by the high-pair diameter bound and the absence of A–B edges. Hence y is a nonport. A shortest a–y walk meets S. Its encountered label j is adjacent to a, so d(j,y)=2. All labels except i are at odd distance from y. Thus i is the sole distance-two label, and the preceding paragraph applies.

**Residual singleton supports.** Suppose the c≥3 common labels have no high private B contact, and no high A port is full. The co-singleton family with at least three residual labels already gives Y≤sk. In the remaining case there are d=s−c≥2 residual labels and an A representative on each common core plus one residual label. Let H count high B nonports having a common distance-two label, and let G count the others. Let P_C and P_D count high B ports meeting and avoiding the core. Let Q_H,Q_G count the residual distance-two incidences of H and G. The high-predecessor proof implies that every vertex counted by H uses at least two separator distance-two incidences. Thus 2H≤ck+Q_H. The d representative quotas give dP_C+P_D+dG−Q_G≤dk. Also P_D≤dk by the same representatives. Hence P_C+P_D+G≤dk+Q_G/d. At every residual label j the closed high internal neighborhood of its representative contains at least k−c−1 vertices. Each such vertex missing j uses a slot at j, disjoint from Q_H and Q_G. Each high A vertex touches at most one residual label, giving Q_H+Q_G≤X+d(c+1). An opposite port on a common label gives X≤k. As d≥2, addition yields Y≤dk+(c+1)k/2+d(c+1)/2<sk. The strict inequality follows from (c−1)k>d(c+1). This contradicts the band input.

**A shorter count with the same conclusion.** Count two copies of each residual representative's even quota, one copy of each common label's distance-two quota, and the residual labels' distance-two incidences on high B nonports. Every high B port receives two charges from a representative. A high B nonport having at least two distance-two labels receives two label charges. Every other high B nonport has no common label at distance two by the private-predecessor argument. It also misses some residual i since d≥2. The representative with support C∪{i} sees it at even distance: a length-three path to this nonport would have to start through a label at distance two, and two high vertices cannot be at distance five. It thus receives two representative charges. This gives 2Y≤(2d+c)k+X+d(c+1)≤2sk for c≥3 and k≥2d. The terminal bands satisfy these inequalities.

**The lower endpoint X=k−s.** A high vertex in A has at most |A|−1+s neighbors, so A contains the only low vertex z, and every other A member is complete to the rest of A and all labels. If z has no label, use a high A neighbor a. Every label has distance two from z and one from a. Every path from z to B passes through S, so every B vertex belongs to the positive even set of z or a. This gives |B|≤2k, impossible. Thus some label i touches z. Every A member touches i, and the full-port argument gives a high B vertex b private at i. Therefore k+1=s+|A∩N(i)|. When X≥k−s+1 the common-label contact already gives this inequality. These arguments require X>0, which follows in the band from X≥k−s and k>s.

**Reorientation.** The set W=N_B[b] has at most s boundary vertices. Only i is adjacent to b across W, and all other boundary members use even slots at b disjoint from the at least k−s+1 A witnesses. A uniform lower bound of two labels for high vertices on the smaller high side of every minimum cut rules out this private b once W is shown smaller. In fact |W|≤2k whenever i has any neighbor a in A. Each w in W touching i has distance two from a. Every other w differs from b and has distance two from i via b. Thus a and i even-cover W. Since Y>sk and s≥5, k>s, we get 2|W|+s+1≤n, uniformly establishing the smaller-side orientation. The already compiled recut module and terminal order inequalities provide a separate kernel-checked orientation using Δ≤3k.

The local mathematical deductions above require no additional girth, regularity, restriction on the low vertex's location, or connectivity within the grouped opposite side. The earlier classification of the support family and its application to every minimum cut are inputs. The residual singleton incidence count is a paper verification.
