# Independent verification of the one-below-half connectivity theorem

The four manuscripts sep/k10/uniform-matching.md, root/one-below-half-clique.md, root/marks-and-endpoints.md and sep/k10/odd-larger-exterior.md together show traceability when κ≥floor(k/2)−1. The proof for the new equality line is analytic. Its earlier inputs include the quadratic bound, distance-two cap at each individual root, universal lower bound on high degrees within any omitted component of a longest path, omitted-component equality exclusions, the near-spanning theorem, and growth of the set of endpoints attainable by longest-path rotations.

For s=floor(k/2)−1 and t=floor(k/2), the Fan-cycle packing excludes the large-component alternatives. The small endblock alternative gives 3h−1 plus the lesser of (h+1)(k−h+2) and (s+1)(k−s+2), h=ceil(k/2), using concavity on the matching transition. Direct substitution gives the strict comparisons with the quadratic bound stated in uniform-matching.md. The remaining whole component is 2-connected, almost Hamilton-connected with at most one bad terminal pair costing one vertex, and has precisely s matched contacts. These arguments hold for every longest path to which the omitted component belongs, including those created by a length-preserving exchange. At even k only m=t+2 remains; equality at the first forbidden m=t+3 would force δ=k, restoring the one vertex. At odd k the candidate sizes are t+3 and t+4.

For m=k−s+1 all marks are common to its high vertices and the component is a clique. Since more than k high longest-path endpoints are attainable, a fixed high clique vertex leaves some attainable high endpoint without any mark neighbor. Rotating from that endpoint forbids pivots in the first L unmarked positions after each mark whenever all mark gaps, including tails, have length at least L. Thus its degree is at most L−1 plus the total surplus over those gap minima. With L=m there is room for at most one other exterior vertex in even degree and at most three in odd degree. All are ruled out except a possible single low singleton by the omitted high-component size bound. For that singleton the step-two runs give δ+r even witnesses and δ−r different middle vertices whose exchange with the singleton leaves a high vertex outside. Every middle vertex must touch C and hence is one of the s original marks, contradicting the stated bounds. This reasoning applies to fencepost cases t=5 as well.

An exact-size gap of m now allows exchange of its consecutive vertex set J for C, so J is the entire omitted connected component of the new longest path. Applying the same matching result to J gives all of its high vertices adjacent to the retained original mark bounding the gap. The old high c∈C therefore sees at least m−1 vertices of J at distance two in the original graph. It already sees the 2s distinct immediate original mark neighbors at distance two; at most two of them are in J. The resulting m−1+2s−2=k+s−2 bound exceeds k. Thus every gap in every realization has size at least m+1. Applying the endpoint degree argument again gives n≥(s+2)m+3s, exceeding the quadratic bound in both parities.

For odd m=t+4 the alternative mixed minimum cover contains s−1 path marks and one exterior member u. All other highs are universal inside C and attached to all s−1 common marks, so C is a clique with at most one missing edge. Each extra u-contact x can be matched with u and the common marks matched with separate high members. The entire path has surplus at most one, so the jth chosen mark occupies one of two adjacent positions, j(m+1) or j(m+1)+1. The s−1 original common marks leave just one such band free. Two extra u-contacts would be consecutive, contradicting longestness. Thus there is an all-marks cover. In it δ≥k−2 gives enough internal neighbors even at a unique low vertex to make every prescribed pair routable. The surplus is at most one, forcing each high attainable endpoint to touch at least k−m=t−3≥2 marks. Any fixed high C vertex misses at most one mark, so it sees every such endpoint at distance two. There are more than k. This excludes the last case. Smaller t is covered by the previously established cases with connectivity at most three.

The general marks-and-endpoints inequality remains valid when a chosen high omitted member misses r anchors. If each attainable high endpoint exceeded r mark contacts, it would share at least one with that member, which again cannot have more than k such endpoints at distance two. Combining with the rotation degree bound gives |P|≥sL+s+k+1−r.
