# The remaining low-minimum cases at second-smallest degree four

The verified proof uses the finite-graph detours from the [tied-minimum case](degree-four-tied.md). Write z for the unique low vertex, δ for its degree and n for order. The checked layer-cover bound gives n≤δ+11. The registered cut-vertex and short-order cases allow two-connectivity and δ≥2. Every other vertex has degree at least four, and each vertex has at most four distance-two witnesses.

A port is a position of an ordinary longest path P adjacent to an omitted vertex. Each port is internal. Distinct ports on one row are nonconsecutive. For a component W of at least two omitted vertices there are two disjoint contact edges to P, by two-connectivity. If every possible endpoint of those edges starts a t-vertex path in W, and their two endpoints can be joined by a d-vertex path in W, then P has at least 2t+d+2 vertices. This counts two tails and one interior interval.

## Minimum degree three

Here n≤14. Every high omitted vertex retains at least two omitted neighbors by the half-degree theorem. A nonisolated low vertex also retains two, since it has a high exterior neighbor and ambient degree three. Consider a nontrivial omitted component W. Every vertex of W starts a three-vertex path. Taking t=3 and d=2 gives |P|≥10, so there are at most four omissions. For W of size at most four, the two matched endpoints can be joined by at least three vertices by the closed-neighborhood intersection argument in the tied proof. Thus |P|≥11, leaving at most three omissions. The component W has at least three vertices, so all omissions are precisely W, an omitted triangle.

Two omitted vertices x and y are high and each needs at least two distinct P contacts. Each port has three P vertices on either side and |P|≤11. The allowable positions are among 3,4,5,6,7. A contact of x and a contact of y at different positions must differ by at least four, since x and y are joined by a spanning path of the triangle. Each of the two rows is therefore exactly {3,7} and |P|=11. The third row has at least one contact, all in {3,7}. This same classification holds for any longest path with three omissions, and when all three omissions are high every row is exactly {3,7}.

Both outer tails have exactly three vertices. Choose a tail avoiding z and replace it by a spanning path of the omitted triangle whose terminal endpoint is high and touches the retained bounding mark. The new longest path omits three high vertices, so their contact rows coincide. The retained bounding mark is adjacent to at least one and hence all old tail vertices. Fix the originally omitted high vertex x. The three old tail vertices and the two shifts of the far mark are five distinct vertices at distance two from x.

If every omitted vertex is isolated, there can only be the low vertex z. Its three contacts each have an immediate predecessor and successor at distance two. If two contacts are two positions apart, exchanging their midpoint for z produces an isolated omitted high vertex, impossible by the strict single-vertex shift. Otherwise the six shifts are distinct, again impossible.

## Minimum degree two

Here n≤13. An isolated omitted high vertex is impossible. If an omitted high vertex x has just one omitted neighbor then that neighbor must be z by the exterior half-degree estimate applied along a high-high edge. The vertex x needs at least three distinct contacts.

If its exterior component has order at least three, the path x,z,w for a third omitted vertex bounds both tails at every contact of x by three vertices. Since |P|≤10, possible contacts lie among 3,4,5,6. These do not admit three nonconsecutive positions.

If instead the component is the edge xz, every contact of either endpoint has two P vertices on either side. Since |P|≤11 all lie among 2 through 8, a window of width six. Choose any three nonconsecutive contacts of x. They must be i,i+2,i+4. Indeed their predecessor/successor pairs all give distinct distance-two witnesses except when successive contacts are two apart. The cap four permits just these two coincidences. The low endpoint z has one P contact j. For every contact t of x one has j=t or |j−t|≥3, by inserting the edge xz. None of the three positions themselves works, nor a position between them. Outside their span the distance from the nearest must be at least three, requiring total width at least seven. This eliminates the two-vertex component.

Hence every omitted high vertex has at least two omitted neighbors. In any nontrivial component containing a high vertex every root starts a three-vertex path. A high root can choose another high neighbor because at most one vertex is low. A low root can start by a high neighbor. Taking t=3 and d=2 gives |P|≥10, so at most three vertices are omitted. If there is any high omission, exactly three are omitted and they form a triangle: at least two are high, and each high vertex must see both others. Each of the two high vertices again needs at least two contacts. Distinct-row marks require spacing four, but |P|≤10 and both tails are of length at least three, giving a window of width at most three. This is impossible. Any nonspanning longest path thus omits just z.

## Protected path through the low vertex of degree two

The ordinary path spans G−z. Take a longest path Q through z, and let m be the number omitted. If it does not span then every omitted vertex is high. The checked protected exterior-degree bound gives minimum internal degree at least two and hence m≥3. The two marks of z on the ordinary path give three gaps each of size at least m, by replacing any gap by z. Thus n≥3+3m, so n≤13 implies m=3. The omitted vertices form a triangle, and each needs at least two distinct Q-contacts.

If their union contained a third position, the Hall sharpening would give three distinct matched ports. The protected matching theorem would imply n≥(3+1)3+3=15. Therefore there are precisely two common marks and every omitted vertex is adjacent to both. This holds for every such longest Q. The marks are internal, nonconsecutive and distinct from z, which has degree two and cannot be adjacent to the omitted triangle. At least two of the three gaps between the marks and the path ends avoid z. Those gaps have at least three vertices by detouring through the triangle. All gaps have at least one vertex. Since |Q|≤10 the three gaps total at most eight. At least one of the gaps avoiding z consequently has length exactly three.

Exchange this gap for the omitted triangle. The new Q is still longest through z. A retained bounding mark touches every old gap vertex by the new common marks result. Thus from an originally omitted vertex x all three old gap vertices are at distance two. If it was a tail, the two shifts adjacent to the far mark give two more witnesses outside it. If it was the middle gap, the predecessor of the first mark and successor of the second give two more. This contradicts the four-witness cap.

Its hypotheses are the original connectedness and maximum-even bound together with second-smallest degree four. For minimum degree three, the Lean proof also uses a shorter conclusion when the only omitted vertex is z. The ordinary path then spans G−z. A longest path through z with m omissions would satisfy 4+4m≤n≤14, while every omitted vertex has at least two neighbors in the omitted set. Consequently m is either zero or at least three, and the upper bound forces zero.
