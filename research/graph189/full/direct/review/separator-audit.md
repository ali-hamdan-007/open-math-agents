# Separator proofs and the cocomparability subclass

For a violating separator S chosen inclusion-minimal among violating subsets, at most |T| components can have all separator neighbors in any proper subset T. All these components survive deletion of T and there is also a component containing some vertex of S outside T. This proves the Hall-type component count even for T empty. Adding one separator vertex u back merges its t incident components into one, yielding t≥3. These claims use inclusion-minimality of the violation, not minimality as an ordinary vertex cut. The 2-connectivity assumptions on component attachments and exceptional singleton claims in both proofs are valid.

In clique-separators.md, under the contrary bound that all boundary-to-separator distances are at most two, each boundary vertex x spends at least c−1 distance-two slots on missed separator vertices and representatives of other components hit by its anchor neighborhood. All additional neighbors of its selected anchor u in those other components are further witnesses. Summation gives A≤t+t(h−1)/(t−1). Boundary vertices missed by u and second-layer interior vertices are disjoint, giving the second count. At least one anchor is incident with three entirely high components, and the possible additional component meeting it only at the exceptional vertex raises t by at most one. Summing these bounds produces the claimed contradiction at h≥2, since s≥2. At h=1 all anchors attain equality throughout, giving exactly one neighbor per component at each anchor, exactly one singleton, and c−1 components of order two. Absence of interior second layers at every anchor implies there are no uncounted interior vertices. Both vertices of an order-two component then need all s anchors, contradicting the one-neighbor rule. Every subset of a clique is a clique, so any clique violating the count supplies a minimal one contradicting this argument.

In independent-separators.md the choice of a high separator anchor with a high representative in every component it meets is valid including a nonsingleton component containing the exceptional vertex: choose an anchor adjacent to another boundary vertex of that component. If the exceptional vertex is a singleton adjacent to every anchor, its exact second layer contains the rest of the boundary, giving h≥s+2. At most floor((d+1)/2) nonsingleton components can be missed by an anchor of separator degree d. The radius-two count excludes this for s≥3; evaluating s=3 gives 5>3, while s≥4 satisfies s²−3s−2>0. When s=2 every component touches both anchors, and the sharper elementary inequality contradicts h≥4. For the selected anchor, combining the bounds before summing gives X≥t+(t−2)(A−t)≥A. Then the separator second layer ℓ satisfies ℓ≤d, and k≤ℓ+t d/(t−2)≤4d. Passing to a minimal violating subset cannot increase induced maximum degree and thus transfers the claimed maximum-degree condition to nonminimal separators.

The independent anchor-cut.md estimate uses no minimality. Each of three high neighbors of one anchor witnesses every other selected component's first-layer vertices at distance two. The three closed internal neighborhoods respectively lie in the first and second layers and have the claimed lower bounds. Dropping the possible exceptional component still leaves at least s+1 components, each touching at least two anchors. Their 2(s+1) incidences force a common anchor with three suitable neighbors. The sum of their anchor counts is at least 3+ceil(k/2), and at most 3s, giving s≥1+ceil(k/6).

In ordered-component-separators.md an anchor complete to two high-containing components would force contradictory strict inequalities between their sizes by choosing in turn a high neighbor on each side. Poset incomparability components left by deleting S have uniform orientation across each pair: the direction cannot flip along an incomparability edge, and connected paths on both sides establish the general fact. Uniform orientation and transitivity then order the components. An anchor spanning an intervening component is adjacent to it in full. Every interior component must be strictly spanned by some anchor by the Hall bound on the left- and right-anchor proper subsets. The selected anchors for high-containing interior components are distinct. At most one selected anchor can touch either extreme: a representative after the first high interior that touches the left extreme would also be complete to the first interior, and the reversed argument handles the right. These constraints leave exactly the stated case s=2,c=4 with one low singleton and one high interior. Both anchors then see both interiors entirely, and the singleton's second layer contains the high interior plus a representative from both extremes, contradicting the high degree upper bound there.

Adjoining a poset element incomparable with every original element adds a universal vertex to the incomparability graph. A disconnecting set of this cone must contain that vertex, so the proved inequality is exactly the 1-toughness condition. The original Deogun–Kratsch–Steiner publisher abstract confirms that every 1-tough cocomparability graph is Hamiltonian. Deleting the cone vertex gives a spanning path in the original graph. The distance-two-only result assumes 2-connectivity; the full-hypothesis result for connected graphs additionally uses the prior cut-vertex traceability result.
