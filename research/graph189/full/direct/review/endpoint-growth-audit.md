# Rotation-component cardinalities and two distant ends

The following checks concern one connected component of the graph of longest-path realizations on a fixed vertex set, using endpoint rotations and reversals. Its set of attained endpoints is R. All rotations in the paper arguments stay within that component. A compatible pair means the endpoints of a realization in the component. Compatible endpoints are nonadjacent because otherwise they close a cycle on the longest-path vertex set, which can be opened to add an omitted neighbor by connectedness.

A fixed endpoint has a high compatible mate: rotate a possible low opposite endpoint at some neighbor besides its successor. Rotating this high mate while fixing the original endpoint yields ≥k distinct compatible partners. Consequently the graph of compatibility has minimum degree ≥k. If |R|=k+1 it must be complete and R is independent. For each high e and any other r, choose a compatible third endpoint f distinct from both. In a path e to f, e's pivot predecessor map is onto R minus f, so r is at distance two from e. The cap is exhausted by all the other elements of R. Across any edge outside R the neighborhood in R's high vertices must therefore stay constant: crossing from an e-neighbor to an e-nonneighbor forces a further distance-two witness. An omitted vertex has no neighbors in R, so its component of G−R can touch R only at the unique possible low vertex. This contradicts 2-connectivity. Thus |R|≥k+2. In particular every omitted x is at odd distance from a high attained endpoint; it is exactly distance three whenever x is high by the established diameter and distance-five results. The unconditional cardinality bound uses only the distance-two cap.

Write |R|=k+1+t and q_e=|N(e)∩R|. Compatibility implies q_e≤t. The pivot predecessors for any high e include e once and at least d(e)−1−q_e distance-two points in R. Among its R nonneighbors at most t−q_e are incompatible. When k≥t+2 this gives a *compatible* distance-two endpoint f. Orienting a path from e to this f adds f itself to the preliminary count, since f never appears among the pivot predecessors. Therefore the whole positive-even set outside R, F_e, has cardinality ≤k−d(e)+q_e. It is nonempty: for each omitted x, either x has even distance from e or its preceding vertex along an odd-length geodesic has positive even distance and is outside R because it touches x. This fact applies with no high-degree assumption on x.

On any fixed realization, the consecutive blocks of vertices outside R are preserved as undirected path blocks by every rotation; neither an inserted nor a removed edge can have both endpoints outside R. The number h of R-R edges in the path is also fixed, because both swapped edges have their other endpoint equal to the pivot. There are |R|−1−h blocks, of which m are nonsingletons. The common singleton set has order j=k+t−h−m. From any high endpoint e, its non-R neighbors are entries in distinct blocks because every pivot predecessor belongs to R. A nonsingleton block adjacent to e spends its second vertex in F_e: a direct e-edge to that vertex is impossible since its predecessor is outside R. Thus e has at least k−q_e−min(q_e,m) singleton neighbors. This argument uses the same set of blocks for all high endpoints in this rotation component.

A compatible high pair u,v at odd distance must exist. Otherwise every high endpoint's compatible high partners have positive even distance. Since F_e is nonempty, ≥k compatible mates force a unique low endpoint z in R, compatible with every high and at odd distance from every high. In particular z is adjacent to none, and has no common neighbor with any high endpoint. Begin a realization at z and rotate at any pivot neighbor. Each exposed predecessor lies in R, and a high predecessor would be within two steps from z. Consequently every pivot must have z as its predecessor, leaving z degree one, impossible in a 2-connected graph. This argument assuming k≥t+2 suffices for the inequality below.

Orient the compatible odd pair as u to v. The R-R edges whose right end is a pivot adjacent to u and the R-R edges whose left end is a pivot adjacent to v are disjoint: sharing one such edge would form a spanning cycle within the longest-path set by the standard two-chord reversal. Thus h≥q_u+q_v. Their singleton neighbor rows are disjoint because they have distance at least three. The row lower bounds summed against j imply
k ≤ t−h−m+q_u+q_v+min(q_u,m)+min(q_v,m) ≤ t+min(q_u,q_v) ≤ 2t.
If k>2t, the compatible distance-two mate condition k≥t+2 holds as t≥1, proving the inequality for every k. This gives |R|≥k+1+ceil(k/2).

More generally, any clique in the graph O of compatible odd-distance high pairs has at most two vertices contacting any given fixed block, since only its two terminal vertices can receive endpoint neighbors and O-neighbors have no common graph neighbor. Every high e has at least k−q_e adjacent blocks. An O-clique of size four would therefore give 4k−Σq≤2(k+t−h), and 2h≥Σq by pairing its four vertices. Hence k≤t in that case. A triangle gives k≤2t−max q_i, and since each vertex in an O-edge must share at least k−t nonsingleton blocks with its partner, q_i≥k−t. Thus a triangle gives 2k≤3t. These two conclusions are conditional on clique existence. A theorem guaranteeing such cliques under the general hypotheses has not been proved.

## Exclusion of equality k=2t, t≥3

Independent review verifies the equality geometry. For an odd compatible high pair attaining the previous bound, q_u=q_v=t, h=2t and m=t; the latter is forced by 2min(t,m)−m=t. Consequently j=0. Every high endpoint must have exactly 2t neighbors, exactly t in R and precisely one terminal neighbor in each of the t nonsingleton blocks. Its entire outside-R even budget is filled by the t distinct second vertices of these blocks. Encode the chosen terminal of each block as one of two symbols; each high endpoint has a binary word. Every other high endpoint nonadjacent and at odd distance uses the complementary word, since no graph neighbor can be shared. There are at least t high copies of that complementary word in the absence of a low endpoint, or t−1 copies when the low endpoint belongs to R.

If only two complementary words occur, a graph edge between two opposite-word high vertices would put every opposite tip at distance two from one endpoint. Its t allocated outside-R even points are already the second vertices of these same t blocks, so each block must have length two. Then every vertex on the path is either an attainable endpoint (which misses the exterior) or a block tip touched by a high endpoint; an omitted neighbor of this tip would add a fresh outside-R even witness for that endpoint. This would disconnect the graph. Hence no such cross-word edge exists. On a realization joining opposite-word high endpoints every block is entered at the first word's tip and exited at the other. Each intermediate run of R must contain the low vertex: without it the run begins with the exit type, ends with the entry type, and crosses a forbidden edge. There are t−1 disjoint runs, impossible for t≥3.

If R has no low endpoint, every occupied word and its complement each have ≥t high copies. At |R|=3t+1 only one complementary pair can occur for t≥2. When the low endpoint z occurs, there are exactly 3t high members. Multiple occupied complementary pairs need ≥4(t−1) such vertices. Thus only t=3 or 4 remain among t≥3. At t=4 there are four classes of 3; at t=3 the four sizes are 2,2,2,3. A high vertex adjacent or at positive even distance to z requires ≥t high complementary copies, since z uses either a slot among its t R-neighbors or one of its t inside-R positive-even slots. Hence the set K of such high vertices is empty at t=4, and at t=3 is contained in the single size-two class complementary to the size-three class. In both cases |K|<t. No neighbor of z can lie in a block: only tips can neighbor z and every tip has high neighbors from both complementary pairs, including some high vertex outside K. Thus z must have a neighbor r in K. Then all t R-neighbors of r are in {z}∪K, since they are at distance at most two from z; excluding r leaves at most |K|<t available vertices. Contradiction. This proves k<2t for t≥3. The smaller equality k=4,t=2 is separately excluded for an actual counterexample by the independently checked k=4 results.
