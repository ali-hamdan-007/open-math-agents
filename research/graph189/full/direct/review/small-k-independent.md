# Independent layer allocation and completion checks

The all-orders k=3 proof in factor/k3-order.md is valid. In the only maximal single-edge allocation pattern all three anchors require their own private columns, and the edge endpoints then each see the other endpoint's column at distance two. The three-vertex path case with two missed columns is similarly reduced by the middle anchor's lone column. For a triangle of anchors, an active column with j assigned third-layer vertices costs j points plus one per other active column at every incident anchor. These bounds give at most four third-layer vertices and n≤11 when the minimum is also three, with the previously proved exceptional smaller-degree case completing k=3.

For k=4 at minimum degree four, k4-independent.py separately canonicalizes the anchor graph then the B-mask and allocation profile under its stabilizer. It finds 0 feasible seven-vertex allocations in the third layer; any larger allocation can be restricted to seven. There are 76 distinct six-vertex profiles at the only possible large order n=15. A generic bitmask BFS/edge-decision search checks diam≤4, forced exact-two/four vertices, and lower degree bounds, returning no completion in 1,535 search calls. It does not require Hamilton-path testing.

The generalized Python program layer-independent.py independently checked the exceptional-root cases (k,a)=(4,3),(5,3),(5,4), with a the root's degree. Respective counts are 0,3,178 cases after refining the size of the fourth layer, and no qualifying completions. It found 10,878 unrefined profiles, 11,206 refined size cases, and zero qualifying completions in 53,878 decision/propagation calls. The smaller-root runs require the previous diameter-five and lower-degree work. No graph-theoretic conclusion beyond these prerequisites is assumed by the enumerations.

An additional general order bound uses a maximal matching from anchors to those second-layer columns assigned at least one third-layer vertex. Each unmatched active column has a matched incident anchor, so distribute its whole allocation to a matched row and keep each matched column's own allocation at its own matched anchor. For each other matched row j, if the current anchor sees its matched column use one allocated third-layer vertex from j, if it misses that column but sees its matched anchor use that column, and otherwise use its matched anchor at distance two through the root. These extra witnesses are disjoint by layer, the matching and the disjoint assigned third-layer groups. With t matching edges each row therefore carries at most k+1−t third-layer vertices. If the root has a nonempty fifth layer and is minimum, R1 implies it is the unique low vertex and its anchors and the fifth layer are all high. Each anchor sees every fifth-layer vertex at distance four, further lowering each row capacity. Thus |L₃|+|L₅|≤⌊(k+1)²/4⌋, also when there is no active column. The even layers satisfy |L₂|+|L₄|≤k, which gives n≤1+δ+k+⌊(k+1)²/4⌋.
