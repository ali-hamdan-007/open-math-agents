# Independent check of the odd boundary for a longest-path omitted component

Write k=2h+1, h≥1. The candidate omitted component C has order h+2. Every high member x is adjacent to every other member by the prior internal-degree inequality. It has exactly h path contacts: having at least h+1 yields either more than k brackets when all runs are singletons, or internal degree ≥a_x+1 in the nonsingleton case. The latter exceeds |C|−1. At most one member of C is low, so C is complete. All other vertices of C can witness an unshared attachment at distance two from x. Hence |A|+r_x≤k with A the union of all path contact positions and r_x the run count. If each high member had a nonsingleton run, its two positions two apart would both be exclusive: a second contact at either position permits a lengthening splice through two adjacent vertices of C. This would give at least 2h+2 distinct positions. Thus some high member has singleton runs, giving |A|≤h+1. A nonsingleton run for any other high member would leave fewer than h positions for that member with singleton runs. Consequently |A| is h or h+1, each high member has h contacts, and all high runs are singletons. No two different marks can have the same sole C contact, because another high vertex cannot miss both. Thus every pair admits a spanning-C splice and every gap and tail has at least m=h+2 nonmarks. This also applies after any path rotation fixing the vertex set.

For h≥2 there are distinct second-step representatives W₀ for the path brackets B because each intervening gap contains ≥4 vertices. W denotes all vertices outside C∪A∪B. The endpoint pivot estimate from the even audit gives at most m−1+s_P nonmark neighbors, where s_P is the total gap excess over m.

When |A|=h, B already uses k−1 of the even budget of a high clique member x. Its eccentricity is at most four because a nonempty fifth layer in a 2-connected graph would require at least two fourth-layer vertices. Apart from a possible fourth-layer singleton or exceptional extra distance-two point Z, every W vertex is at distance two from some mark. Indeed a distance-three point is reached from x via a bracket or Z. If Z has distance two and lies outside B, it has a mark neighbor since x has no C-to-W neighbor via the clique. A distance-three point cannot be adjacent to any mark. On summing the bracket and mark witnesses, the count of W outside W₀ that can be at distance two from some mark is at most h²−D−E+η. Here D sums marked degrees, E sums |S_a minus U_a|, and η counts representative losses when a mark itself sees a representative. If a loss occurs, all losses come from the one exceptional point Z in W₀. Let u be the mark that owns its bracket. Each affected mark a other than u contributes one either to d_A(a) (when au is an edge) or to its |S_a minus U_a| (when au is absent), so η≤1+D+E. In this case all W outside W₀ are covered. With no losses at most one W outside W₀ is uncovered. Both versions give |W|≤h²+2h+1, whence s_P≤h−1 using |W|≥h²+h+2+s_P. There is a longest path on the same vertex set having both endpoints high: rotate away from the possible low endpoint through a second neighbor, which exists by 2-connectivity. At most one endpoint neighbors A, because any such endpoint is the sole even point beyond B. Thus one high endpoint has degree ≤h+1+s_P≤2h, a contradiction.

For the other case set t=h+1=|A|, so m=t+1, k=2t−1. Each high clique member x misses exactly one mark; that mark and the 2(t−1) brackets of its own marks exhaust its even budget. If all marks are covered by some high member, W is anticomplete to A, all W vertices are at distance two from a mark, and no representatives are lost. At most one unordered pair of marks lack a shared clique neighbor: each high must miss one of those two, so every other mark is adjacent to every high member while each of the original two is high-covered. Summing the remaining mark, bracket and W₀ witnesses gives |W|≤t²+2 and hence s_P≤1. The endpoints therefore have degree ≤t+1<2t−1 for t≥3.

If some mark l is low-only, it is the unique such mark. Its sole clique contact z is the unique low vertex. Let r≥1 be the number of marks seen by z, and p the number of W vertices seen by l. All other marks miss W. The 2r own brackets, t−r unseen marks through the clique, and all p vertices through l are disjoint members of the distance-two set at z. Thus p≤t−1−r. Missed-mark pairs without a common clique member cost at most 2(t−r) ordered incidences. The only possible lost representatives at l and the possibly uncovered W outside W₀ are disjoint subsets of its p neighbors in W. The additional clique-distance-two witnesses sum to 2t−r: l misses all t high clique members and every mark missed by z has z at distance two. They are disjoint from every earlier category. Thus s_P≤−1+2(t−r)+p−(2t−r)≤t−2−2r≤t−4. Both endpoints can see at most one mark, so have degree ≤t+s_P+1<2t−1. This case is impossible.

For h=1 use a direct count without choosing W₀. A high vertex of the omitted triangle has two clique neighbors and one mark; a low vertex has no mark. A single mark would disconnect the triangle upon deletion, leaving two marks, high-covered with disjoint clique contact sets. Each omitted vertex misses at least one mark, consuming in aggregate at least three of the six distance-two slots at the marks. All W vertices occupy further such slots by the fully covered saturation argument, giving |W|≤3. The three gaps each have ≥3 nonmarks and only four bracket vertices in total, giving |W|≥5. This excludes k=3.
