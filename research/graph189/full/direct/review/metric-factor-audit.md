# Independent mathematical audit

## Local degree sums and unique common neighbors

In wedge-cover.md, a vertex omitted from the three closed even-distance sets has odd distance from all three wedge vertices. These distances are equal because successive differences are ≤1. Every member of Q belongs to both endpoint sets, including both endpoints themselves. Thus summing the three caps overcounts the covered vertices by at least |Q|. Separating the common neighbors at distance one yields the claimed lower bound on T. At distance at least three, the second step of any geodesic from the center must miss both endpoints, since either endpoint edge would shorten its path to the same target. The radius-two ball really is the three-neighborhood union plus its missed vertices. Consequently the saturation contradiction giving the local L₁ condition is sound.

In irregular-center-cycle.md, uniqueness of the common neighbor yields |Q|=2 by L₁ and forces the union to miss exactly one vertex b from the ball. The ball count is exactly 1+d(w)+k=d(u)+d(v)+2. Thus the second layer is saturated, and the center w has degree d(u)+d(v)+1−k≥δ+1. In particular, the only possible vertex below k can never center such a wedge. Every member of T is at distance three from w since a fourth layer is excluded by saturation. Its neighbors in the radius-two ball all belong to the missed set, hence only b. As |T|≥2, choose a high member t. The common neighbors of w and b contain a high vertex a: otherwise their unique common neighbor would be the exceptional vertex, already excluded as a center. Then a,t have b as their only common neighbor, since all neighbors of a are in the radius-two ball around w. Degree equality at b puts it above k, with saturated second layer. Its new missed vertex differs from w because w is adjacent to a. Repeating gives an arbitrarily long walk on eligible vertices in the exact distance-two graph with no immediate reversal, impossible in a finite forest.

In unique-center-cycle.md, the specialization to degree set {k,k+1} gives the original low endpoints, high center, and the clique assertions for J and T. The latter follow since the Q-set at b for each pair a,t contains just a,t, while J and T are anticomplete by their distances from w. I checked the definition of L₁ and Theorems 5 and 7(a) directly in Asratian, Broersma, van den Heuvel and Veldman (1996). The exceptional family has k=p and order 2p+1, so it cannot enter the high-order result. Corollary 8 independently supplies traceability when every distance-two pair has two common neighbors.

## Minimum cycle-and-edge covers

In minimum-cover.md the disjointness of U and the shifts H follows by combining two spanning paths into a cycle using the two crossing edges. This remains a cycle on four distinct vertices when both pieces have order two. The exact count on the union after deleting A follows by inclusion-exclusion, and |H|≥|A| because a cyclic shift is bijective. It is legitimate to add the internal distance-two and all larger even-distance counts since those sets are disjoint from the external witnesses. Removing the number of external contacts of a high neighbor gives the stated local consequence exactly.

For different external pieces an edge between the two successors joins three spanning paths into one cycle. On the same external piece, successive original attachment vertices are forbidden by the preceding crossing-edge exchange; different nonconsecutive attachments therefore give two disjoint path segments on deleting their outgoing edges. The proposed chord joins those segments into the requisite spanning path. Loops are excluded in the coincident-successor case. Applying disjointness in both orientations shows that neither x nor q touches the common shifted set. Adding x gives the claimed independent set for a triangle.

For a two-vertex piece there is at most one unit available for the remote even vertices and surplus terms. A nonempty fifth layer in a 2-connected graph requires at least two vertices in its fourth layer. A fourth-layer vertex therefore forces saturation, and equality of the two shifts forces invariance under the two-step rotation exactly as asserted. Every nonempty proper parity class disjoint from its shifts is covered by U=A(q). The triangle difference bound, the union bound, and the degree bound for a piece of arbitrary order all follow directly. No external Hamiltonicity claim is made solely from the existence of the cover.

## Actual second-layer sizes and four-cycle pieces

In exact-ball-cycle.md even the relaxed measured-ball alternative forces the unique-common wedge center to have degree at least δ+1 because d(w)+s(w)=d(u)+d(v)+1 and s(w)≤k. It therefore cannot be the unique low vertex. I checked the exact upstream R1 lemmas five_endpoints and diameter_le_five. The high chosen t in T has odd distance ≥3 from the high center, and that distance is ≤4 by R1. Thus it is exactly three and still has only b as a neighbor in the radius-two ball. The new center satisfies the required numerical threshold by the wedge equality. The forest argument remains valid.

In four-piece-obstruction.md the distinct endpoint palettes and the parity coloring for the four-cycle permit every properly colored cycle to splice the corresponding pieces, even through parallel edges of the original quotient. Subdivision gives a simple graph. Yeo's theorem indeed yields a vertex with at most one incident connection color per deleted component when the graph has no properly colored cycle. Subdivision vertices cannot be witnesses because interpiece edges are nonbridges, and pieces of size two or three cannot be witnesses by 2-connectivity and their distinct contact colors. Four-cycle witnesses split the other components into the two claimed sectors, with exact cross distances unaffected by possible internal chords. The diameter-five saturation arguments, the universal-within-component argument for a depth-two vertex, and the single-sector order bound at k=3 all check. In the latter case both vertices of the empty sector's separator pair actually have degree three, so the second-smallest and minimum degrees are both three.

In balanced-bipartite-pieces.md, if one side P of a spanning balanced biclique has an internal edge, the edge supplies a double-vertex gap in a spanning path between prescribed vertices of Q. Thus using different colors exactly as specified gives a spanning path across any differently colored contacts. If all exterior components attach solely to P, the independent Q vertices each have degree t and see t−1 same-side vertices plus every external boundary vertex at distance two. There is at most one external boundary vertex, while t≤k+1 ensures there are at least two external vertices, contradicting 2-connectivity. If both sectors occur the piece itself is an induced balanced biclique. The same distance-five lemma and the stated first-, second- and third-layer counts then exclude every depth case. In a Hamiltonian complete multipartite piece the largest part has order at most half the vertex count. Equality gives a spanning balanced biclique and strict inequality gives the Ore bound for Hamilton-connectedness. This verifies the stronger obstruction at arbitrary piece sizes.

In chordless-pieces.md the local equality gives equality of external neighborhoods of high vertices two steps apart on a chordless factor cycle, and the inclusion for a sole low vertex. The resulting common external-neighborhood classes give the stated exact internal distance-two contradictions above cycle lengths five and six. A connecting edge incident to two flexible pieces can be changed at both ends: its new first end is still adjacent to its old opposite end and therefore, by that opposite piece's common-neighborhood or low-subset property, to every permitted high replacement of the old opposite end. Consecutive high choices within each flexible piece are thus simultaneously realizable. Exterior sectors for a six-cycle have at least two eligible high ports per boundary vertex; a cycle vertex misses only one vertex of the opposite parity, giving every cross distance used in the biclique-sector bound.
