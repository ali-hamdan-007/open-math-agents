# Independent order and connectivity audit

Checked the weighted third-layer argument in ../metric/subquadratic-order.md. Let h be an anchor's largest exact second-layer intersection with the third layer C of a minimum-degree root. Outside the columns adjacent to a maximizing anchor there are at least |C|−h assigned vertices. Each of the p other anchors assigned positive mass takes at most h and supplies a distinct additional distance-two witness outside C. Thus |C|≤h(k−h+1). In the other direction weight each column degree by its assigned load. The A contribution is at most |A|h. Undirected symmetry rewrites the B contribution by rows of neighboring loads. Each row's own and neighboring loads are disjoint and count only adjacent or distance-two vertices in C, giving the bound |B|(h+k)−|C|. This proves |C|(k−h+1)≤|A|h+|B|(h+k). Multiplication gives |C|²≤3k³. The combined second and fourth layers have size at most k. The fifth layer is at distance four from any first-layer vertex by the high-pair distance-five exclusion, hence has size at most k. Therefore n≤1+3k+floor(sqrt(3k³)).

Checked ../coord/connectivity-and-exterior-cycles.md. The set version of Menger applies since any deletion of fewer than l vertices leaves surviving vertices on both sides connected. Trimming preserves disjointness and distinct ends. Either tail can use all d cycle vertices. Each intervening gap can use the longer cycle arc with at least ceil(d/2)+1 vertices. This gives n≥3d+l+(l−1)(ceil(d/2)+1), which implies the stated quadratic contradictions in both parities at d=2ceil(k/2). A wholly high omitted component of order below 2ceil(k/2) is Hamilton-connected by Ore. Its matching of t distinct contacts would violate the upper bound using full traversals on every gap and both tails. A 2-connected wholly high component beyond this threshold violates the disjoint-cycle inequality by Dirac.

If any high vertex is omitted, deleting the possible low vertex from its component leaves minimum degree ≥ceil(k/2)−1, hence a cycle of size d≥ceil(k/2) for k≥6. With l=min(κ,d), if l=d then the gap inequality gives at least d²/2. Otherwise κ≤1+4n/k. In particular the connectivity is O(sqrt(k)) for sufficiently large k. For k≥256 and κ>k/2 we have d,l≥k/2, giving n≥k²/8>1+3k+sqrt(3)k^(3/2). For a sole omitted low vertex, contact distances one and two on P are impossible by insertion or exchanging the midpoint to omit a high singleton. Hence its 2δ distinct path shifts force 2δ≤k. This excludes the low-only case for κ>k/2.

Also checked the two-endblock strengthening under κ>k/2. Let h=ceil(k/2), t=floor(k/2). Every endblock with all noncut vertices high has its noncut degrees at least h, so Fan's distance-two degree condition gives a cycle of length at least min(|B|,2h). The cycle bound forces it to be Hamiltonian, with order at least h+1. Each of two such endblocks admits t distinct direct-edge contacts with distinct ends in P, by deleting its cut vertex together with any smaller vertex cover. The colors on distinct marks have total weight 2t. With q=ceil((h+1)/2) a same-color interior gap can traverse q+1 vertices, while distinct colors give at least 2q+1 even for a shared cut. A tail can traverse at least q+h+1. Summing weighted gaps, tails, both blocks and marks gives n≥2u+2+4h+2tq for u distinct marks. This exceeds the existing quadratic bound in each parity. The argument applies whenever two endblocks have all noncut vertices high, including components that have a low vertex elsewhere. These conclusions leave open the unrestricted mixed exterior case.

Final check for a mixed 2-connected exterior C with κ>k/2: Fan gives m=|C|<2h and a Hamilton cycle. Adding a new marker of degree two at any prescribed endpoint pair gives a graph of order m+1. All missing edges between high vertices have degree sum at least 2h≥m+1, so their addition preserves Hamiltonicity by Bondy–Chvátal. In the filled graph all prescribed pairs support a spanning path through the sole low z, with at most one exceptional pair, its two neighbors when its internal degree is two. Indeed, when z is an endpoint its second available neighbor avoids the other endpoint. Otherwise choose two neighbors as a three-vertex segment through z which does not exhaust both endpoints. Among t+1 distinctly matched C-to-P contacts, at most one interval can have that exceptional pair of ends. Both tails traverse the whole component via the Hamilton cycle. Even pricing this interval at zero gives n≥(t+1)+(t+2)m, a contradiction at m≥h+2 in both parities. Hence any mixed component surviving the preceding exclusions has a cut vertex, its block tree has exactly two leaves, and z is a noncut vertex of one of them.
