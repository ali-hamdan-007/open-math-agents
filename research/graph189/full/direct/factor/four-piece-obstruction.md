# A minimum cycle-and-edge cover needs a piece of order at least five

Let G be a two-connected qualifying graph for Graph 189. Write k for its second-smallest degree, n for its order, and suppose n>3k+2 and δ(G)≥3. The previously proved strict Hall expansion gives a spanning partition into cycles and single edges. No such partition minimizing the number of pieces can have every piece of order at most four. The minimum-degree assumption includes all unresolved cases because the theorem for minimum degree at most two is already established on paper.

We use the established metric lemma for Graph 189 that all distances are at most five and every distance-five pair contains the unique vertex of degree less than k. In particular when all vertices are high the diameter is at most four.

## A four-cycle with separated contact pairs

Assume that a minimum-piece partition has all pieces of order at most four. Let M be the multigraph contracting each piece and retaining its distinct interpiece edges. There are at least two pieces since n>3k+2. Subdivide each M-edge once to get a simple edge-colored graph H. The half-edge at a piece receives the color of the attachment vertex on that piece, except that for a four-cycle the two vertices of each parity class share a color. Use different palettes for different pieces. A properly colored cycle in H would visit distinct pieces, and in each the two contacts would be distinct for order two or three, and of opposite parity on a four-cycle. In each case deleting their joining edge from the cyclic ordering gives a spanning path between the contacts (use the given edge for order two). Joining the paths would yield a single cycle spanning all these pieces, a contradiction.

Anders Yeo's theorem (J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 69 (1997), 222–225, DOI 10.1006/jctb.1997.1728; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0095895697917287) now gives a vertex v of H such that in every component of H−v the edges to v have at most one color. A subdivision vertex cannot serve: the original interpiece edge is no bridge by two-connectivity of G, hence its two ends in H−v are connected and the two half-edges have distinct palettes. At a piece C the components of H−C correspond to the components of G−V(C), where the residual half-edges simply become leaves. Each component of G−V(C) attaches to at least two distinct vertices of C, since otherwise deleting its unique contact would disconnect G. Thus orders two and three cannot serve. We obtain a four-cycle C with opposite pairs P and Q such that every component of G−V(C) attaches only to P or only to Q. Let X and Y be the unions of the corresponding component vertex sets. P is complete to Q. Chords within P or within Q are allowed.

## Order bound for the four-cycle structure

For x∈X let i be its distance to P and define X_i accordingly. Define Y_j using distance to Q. Distances across the sectors equal d(x,y)=i+1+j, since the two separator pairs are disjoint and completely joined. A member of X_i is at distance i+1 from both vertices of Q; the symmetric fact holds for Y_j.

First suppose both sectors are nonempty. Each has at least one high vertex, because a sector consisting only of the possible exceptional vertex would have degree at most two. If both have vertices at depth at least two, the diameter bound forces both maximum depths to be exactly two. Every pair in X₂×Y₂ is then at distance five. One of the two layers must consist solely of the unique low vertex; by symmetry let X₂={z}. A vertex of Y₂ sees every X₁ vertex at distance four and some Q vertex at distance two, giving |X₁|≤k−1. Similarly z gives |Y₁|≤k−1. A vertex of X₁ sees both Q vertices at distance two and all of Y₂ at distance four, giving |Y₂|≤k−2. Consequently n≤4+(k−1)+1+(k−1)+(k−2)=3k+1.

Otherwise assume X=X₁. Since X has a high vertex with all its neighbors in X∪P, |X|≥k−1. If Y₂ were nonempty, any y∈Y₂ sees X at distance four and at least one Q vertex at distance two, exhausting its cap. In particular it has no vertex at intrinsic distance two within its own component of Y: such a vertex would also be at distance two in G since a nonedge stays a nonedge. Thus y is universal within its component. This component attaches to both vertices of Q by two-connectivity; y misses both, so each is at distance two from y through that component. This gives |X|+2>k. Thus Y=Y₁, and |X| and |Y| are at most k, each witnessed by either opposite separator vertex. Hence n≤2k+4≤3k+2.

Finally suppose one sector is empty, say Y. Then the two vertices of Q have degrees at most three. As δ≥3 we have k=3, every vertex has degree at least k, and the diameter is at most four. All X depths are therefore at most three. For q∈Q the layers X₁ and X₃ sit at distances two and four, respectively, so |X₁|+|X₃|≤3. Write P={p,p′}. If p and p′ are nonadjacent, each has the other at distance two, and at most two vertices of X₂ can be seen at distance two by either one. Since X₂ is covered by their two distance-two sets, |X₂|≤4. If pp′ is an edge and one member of P is adjacent to all X₁, then that member sees all X₂ at distance two and |X₂|≤3. Otherwise each misses an X₁-vertex which it sees at distance two via the other, leaving room for at most two X₂-vertices apiece. Again |X₂|≤4. Hence n≤4+3+4=11=3k+2. All cases contradict the assumed order.
