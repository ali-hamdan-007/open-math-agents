# A minimum cover has a non-Hamilton-connected piece without a spanning balanced biclique

Let G be a 2-connected qualifying graph with n>3k+2 and k≥3 the second-smallest degree. If the minimum number of pieces in a spanning cycle-and-edge cover is at least two, every minimum cover has a piece C of order at least five for which G[C] is not Hamilton-connected and has no spanning complete balanced bipartite graph. In particular G[C] is not a complete multipartite graph. This strengthens the separate four-piece obstruction and permits minimum degree two. We use the established consequence of the Graph 189 hypothesis that the diameter is at most five, with every pair at distance five containing the unique vertex of degree less than k. Strict expansion gives existence of a cover.

Suppose all pieces are Hamilton-connected or contain a spanning K_{t,t}. For a Hamilton-connected piece color the half-edges of the subdivided quotient by their original attachment vertices, with separate colors for distinct vertices. For a spanning K_{t,t} with parts P,Q not already treated, t≥2. Opposite-part endpoints always admit a spanning path. If P has an internal edge then any two distinct Q vertices are endpoints of a spanning path: order Q starting and ending at these prescribed endpoints and fill the t−1 intervening gaps by the two adjacent P vertices together in one gap and a singleton P vertex in every other gap. Thus if both sides have an edge the piece is Hamilton-connected. If only P has an edge, use one color for P and distinct singleton colors for Q. If neither side has an edge, use one color per side. In every case a pair of differently colored ports supports a spanning path. Use disjoint palettes across pieces.

The argument with Yeo's theorem in four-piece-obstruction.md gives a witness piece at which each component of the exterior attaches in just one color. Each exterior component attaches to at least two distinct piece vertices. This rules out Hamilton-connected pieces as witnesses. If only P has a chord then every component attaches only to P. More generally consider the case in which one side, say Q, is independent and all exterior components attach only to P. Every q∈Q has degree exactly t, so k≤t since t≥2. Every q sees Q∖{q} and every exterior vertex touching P at distance two, hence there is at most k−t+1≤1 exterior boundary vertex and t≤k+1. As n>3k+2, the exterior contains at least two vertices, contradicting two-connectivity. This also handles the empty-sector case when both sides are independent.

It remains to consider an induced K_{t,t} with nonempty exterior sectors X and Y attaching only to P and Q respectively. Let X_i,Y_j denote the positive distance layers to P,Q within their sectors. For x∈X_i, y∈Y_j, d(x,y)=i+1+j and x is at distance i+1 from every Q. Each q∈Q sees Q∖{q}, X₁ and X₃ at positive even distances. Hence |X₁|+|X₃|≤k−t+1 and likewise |Y₁|+|Y₃|≤k−t+1. Each x∈X₁ sees Q at distance two and Y₂ at distance four, so |Y₂|≤k−t. Both sectors have depth at most three, since neither is empty and the diameter is at most five. If X is shallow, combining the inequalities gives n≤2t+2(k−t+1)+(k−t)=3k−t+2. The same holds when Y is shallow. If neither sector is shallow then both have depth exactly two; X₂×Y₂ consists of distance-five pairs. Thus one second layer is a singleton, say X₂, and the corresponding bound is n≤3k−t+3. All cases contradict n>3k+2.

A single-piece cover is already a spanning cycle. Every piece of order at most four is a single edge, a triangle, or contains the spanning K_{2,2} of its cycle. For the complete multipartite corollary, any Hamiltonian complete multipartite graph of order m has largest part size at most m/2 by separation along its Hamilton cycle. At equality it has a spanning balanced biclique, and for a strictly smaller largest part its minimum degree is at least (m+1)/2, giving Hamilton-connectedness by the classical Ore degree-sum criterion for Hamilton-connected graphs. The two-vertex piece is Hamilton-connected as well.
