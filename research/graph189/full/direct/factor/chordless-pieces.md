# The required minimum-cover obstruction has a chord

Let G be a 2-connected qualifying graph with n>3k+2 and k≥3, where k is the second-smallest degree. Suppose the minimum number of pieces in a spanning cycle-and-edge cover is at least two. Every such minimum cover has a piece C of order at least five for which G[C] has a chord of its factor cycle, is not Hamilton-connected, and has no spanning balanced complete bipartite graph. Strict expansion gives cover existence. As in balanced-bipartite-pieces.md, use the established diameter-five endpoint property for Graph 189.

## Equality at an induced cycle

Suppose C induces precisely its factor cycle, of order m≥5. Write A(v) for the exterior neighbors of v. For x∈C, at least one of its two cyclic neighbors is high. Its two vertices at cyclic distance two are not adjacent to x, hence are ambient distance-two vertices inside C. Apply the local inequality of minimum-cover.md to x and any high cyclic neighbor q. The two internal distance-two vertices exhaust deg_C(q)=2. Hence x has no further distance-two vertex in C, q has ambient degree k, and the other cyclic neighbor r has A(r)⊆A(q). All high vertices have exactly k−2 exterior neighbors, so two high vertices two cyclic steps apart have equal exterior sets. A low vertex's set is contained in the set of either high vertex two steps from it.

When m is odd, adding two cyclic steps gives a single cyclic ordering; deleting the possible low vertex leaves its high vertices connected in that order. Thus every high member of C has the same nonempty A, with the possible low member having a subset. If m≥7, a high member has at least m−4≥3 other nonadjacent high members, all at distance two through A. This exceeds its exact allotment inside C, proving m=5. Whenever an exterior vertex touches C it is adjacent to all four or five high members. Every pair of interpiece edges assigned to traverse C can therefore be rerouted to use a consecutive pair of high vertices on C, with their exterior endpoints fixed. Opening C at that consecutive pair gives its spanning path.

When m is even, adding two cyclic steps gives the two parity classes. In each parity class its high vertices remain connected in this ordering after deleting a possible single low vertex. Thus the high vertices of each class have common nonempty external contact set, and a low member's contacts are a subset of its class's set. A member of the fully high parity sees all m/2−1 other members of that class at distance two, so m≤6. Hence m=6. The two common exterior sets are disjoint: choose an antipodal pair of high vertices, possible even with one exception. If their sets met, they would be at distance two in addition to the two cyclic-distance-two vertices already forced at either endpoint. Every exterior contact with either parity is adjacent to all high vertices of that class. For interpiece edges touching opposite parities one can choose high consecutive ports on the six-cycle, preserving their exterior endpoints. Such ports exist because each parity has at least two high members and a vertex of either parity misses just one of the opposite parity on the six-cycle.

## Inserting the flexible pieces in the colored quotient

Suppose each piece is Hamilton-connected, contains a spanning balanced biclique, or induces its factor cycle. Use the colors of balanced-bipartite-pieces.md for the first two kinds. The remaining induced pieces have order five or six by the preceding argument. On a five-cycle give each vertex its own color. On a six-cycle color by parity. In a properly colored cycle of the subdivided quotient, reroute the two contacts of each flexible five- or six-cycle as just described, and use the previously proved spanning paths elsewhere. Rerouting at the two ends of a common interpiece edge is compatible: first change one endpoint, retaining adjacency to its old opposite end. The new endpoint is then an exterior contact of that opposite end and so is adjacent to any permitted high replacement there. These changes preserve distinct pieces and realize one cycle covering all selected pieces, contradicting minimality.

Yeo's theorem must supply a monochromatic-component witness piece, and the previous biclique argument rules out witnesses of the first two kinds. For the five-cycle a witness is impossible since its colors are individual vertices and each exterior component has at least two distinct contacts. At a six-cycle witness write P,Q for its independent parity classes of size three. Every exterior component attaches exclusively to one class. Neither sector is empty, since otherwise all three vertices in the opposite parity would have degree two, contradicting k≥3. With X_i,Y_j the depth layers in their sectors, every boundary vertex touches at least two high vertices of its own parity. Each opposite-parity vertex in the cycle has only one nonneighbor across the bipartition. It follows that d(x,q)=i+1 for x∈X_i and q∈Q, by taking a shortest route from x to its boundary and then selecting a high P port adjacent to q. Moreover d(x,y)=i+1+j for x∈X_i and y∈Y_j: after reaching the boundaries of each, choose a high Q port at the Y boundary and an adjacent high P port at the X boundary. The matching lower bounds follow because the sectors can communicate only by crossing between the two parity classes. The parity-mates of any cycle vertex are also at distance two in the six-cycle. These are exactly the distance facts used in the nonempty-sector bound of balanced-bipartite-pieces.md. Substitution of t=3 gives n≤3k−1 if a sector is shallow and n≤3k if both are deep, impossible. This proves the assertion.
