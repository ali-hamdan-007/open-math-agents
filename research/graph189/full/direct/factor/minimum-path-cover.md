# Small pieces in a minimum undirected spanning path cover

Let every vertex of G have at most k vertices at distance exactly two, let all but at most one vertex have degree at least k, and suppose δ(G)≥3. Let F be any partition of the vertices into paths with the minimum possible number of paths. Orient the paths arbitrarily to index their positions. A point below degree k is called low. No order assumption, two-connectivity, or distance beyond two is used.

**Singletons.** A singleton x is low. An adjacent endpoint of another path would allow a merge, as would two consecutive neighbors on one path. Its neighbor sets A therefore avoid endpoints and consecutive positions. Let H(A) be both shifts by one position over all paths; its vertices are at distance two from x. If r counts the maximal progressions of A in steps of two, over each separate path and parity, |H(A)|=|A|+r. Indeed each run of size s yields s+1 shifted positions and different runs have disjoint shifts. Since δ>0 there is at least one run. Hence deg(x)+1≤k. This applies in any minimum cover, including one obtained by a size-preserving exchange.

**Two-vertex pieces.** No member of F has two vertices. Suppose xq were such a piece with q high. Write A and B for the respective sets of neighbors on the other paths. They avoid all endpoints, and H(A) is disjoint from B: otherwise the entire piece xq can be inserted at an edge of another path. Each point of (H(A)∪B)∖A has distance two from x. Consequently

    k ≥ |B| + r + |A∖(H(A)∪B)|.

Since |B|≥k−1 and |A|≥2, A consists of one step-two run, disjoint from H(A), and A⊆B. Replacing the midpoint between two consecutive members of this run by x,q gives another cover of the same size, leaving that midpoint as a singleton. Hence this midpoint is low. If x is low, this gives two low vertices, impossible. Otherwise both are high. At k≥4 the run has at least k−1≥3 members, yielding two distinct possible low midpoints; at k=3 all vertices are high by δ≥3. This proves the exclusion.

**Triangles.** A member of F with three vertices whose induced graph is a triangle is impossible at k≥6. At k=5 such a triangle must be entirely high. Its three vertices must all have the same three exterior neighbors, and their positions on the other paths break into one singleton run and one two-position run with the unique low vertex between its positions.

To see this, write A_i for the exterior contact set of triangle vertex i and r_i for its number of step-two runs. Every A_i avoids all other-path endpoints, since the triangle can open at any vertex. If distinct triangle vertices attach to consecutive positions, insert the whole triangle to reduce the cover. If they attach two steps apart, insert the entire triangle in place of the intervening vertex, giving a minimum cover with that vertex as a singleton. Thus any such midpoint is the unique low vertex. For {i,j,l} the triangle, U=A_j∪A_l is disjoint from H(A_i). Every point in (H(A_i)∪U)∖A_i is at distance two from i. Therefore |U|+r_i≤k, and 1≤r_i≤2, since δ≥3 and at least one of j,l is high with at least k−2 exterior neighbors. For two high j,l it follows that |A_j∩A_l|≥k−3.

Consider a contact in A_j∩A_l on a nonsingleton step-two run of A_j. Every incident pair in that run at that contact must have the unique low vertex as its midpoint. Thus throughout all nonsingleton runs such common contacts can only be at ends of the one allowed gap, each with no other incident pair. A common set of size at least three across at most two runs consequently requires exactly a two-member run (both common) and a singleton run. Indeed with two common contacts in nonsingleton runs they must be both ends of that one gap, which is then the whole run; to obtain a third common contact another singleton run is needed. With at most one common contact in nonsingleton runs, at most one other singleton run is available unless both runs are singletons. Thus |A_j|≥4 forces |A_j∩A_l|≤2. This rules out k≥6.

For k=5, if the low vertex is in the triangle, no exterior midpoint can be low, so common contacts only occur in singleton runs. But |A_j|≥3 and r_j≤2 imply at most one singleton run, contrary to |A_j∩A_l|≥2. Thus the triangle is all high. If every r_i=1, each A_j is a single progression of length at least three. At most one common point can occur in such a progression since two common endpoints of the lone possible low gap would require the entire run to have size two. This again contradicts |A_j∩A_l|≥2. Hence some r_i=2. The union |A_j∪A_l|≤3 forces A_j=A_l of size three. As all these contacts are common, at most one step-two gap occurs among them. Since r_j≤2, exactly one such gap occurs and r_j=2. Applying the union inequality there forces A_i=A_l too. The gap must have the unique low vertex as its midpoint. This proves the stated configuration.
