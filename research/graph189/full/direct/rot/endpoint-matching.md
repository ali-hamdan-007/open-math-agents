# A third endpoint beyond the degree bound

Assume the full Graph 189 bounds and 2-connectivity, and retain the nonspanning longest path P and its attainable endpoint set R as in `remote-endpoint.md`. For every k≥4, one has |R|≥k+3 and at least k+2 endpoints are high. Thus every omitted vertex has at least two distinct high attainable endpoints at odd distance at least three; the distance is exactly three when the omitted vertex is high, by the registered R1 diameter and distance-five results. Everything below also applies to the family generated from one longest path by two-ended rotations.

For a high e∈R let F_e be the set of its positive even-distance vertices outside R. This set is nonempty. Take a shortest path from e to any omitted x. The distance is at least two. If it is even, x belongs to F_e. If it is odd, the predecessor of x has positive even distance from e and cannot be attainable because it is adjacent to an omitted vertex.

The preceding theorem leaves only |R|=k+2 to exclude. Each endpoint has at least k compatible mates, so at most one other R vertex is incompatible with it. Adjacent vertices are incompatible, hence G[R] is a matching with possible isolated vertices. For each high e, an initial set of pivots yields at least d(e)−1−d_R(e)≥k−2 distance-two vertices in R. Indeed the distinct pivot predecessors are either e, neighbors of e, or at exact distance two via the pivot. At k≥4 there are at least two such distance-two candidates, at least one of them compatible. Choose a path from e to such a mate f. Its d(e) distinct pivot predecessors now give the same count *excluding* f. Adding f gives at least d(e)−d_R(e) distance-two vertices of R, and therefore

    1 ≤ |F_e| ≤ k−d(e)+d_R(e) ≤ 1.

It follows that d(e)=k and d_R(e)=1 for every high endpoint e. Every path starting at a high r begins with its matched neighbor r′. Indeed r′ lies on the path, is adjacent to r, and cannot be its far endpoint. Rotating through r′ makes its predecessor an attainable endpoint. That predecessor is an R-neighbor of r′, and r is its sole R-neighbor.

Now fix a path from a high left endpoint e to the unique low member f of R if one exists; otherwise take any compatible f. Such an e exists for any fixed f by the high-mate rotation. Each of the k−1 nontrivial pivots from e produces a distinct high endpoint r (the only possible low one is fixed at f). The second vertex of the rotated path is the original predecessor of r because r≠e and the initial prefix was reversed. By the preceding paragraph this predecessor is the matched partner of r. Two of these new endpoints cannot belong to the same matched pair, since both would have to precede each other on the original path. The k−1 pairs are disjoint; none uses the final vertex f, which cannot precede r or itself be a new left endpoint. Hence 2(k−1)≤|R|−1=k+1, a contradiction at k≥4.

At k=3 the compatible distance-two mate still exists: if d_R(e)=1 its sole incompatible vertex is at distance one, and if d_R(e)=0 the first pivot count gives at least two candidates. Thus |R|=5 would force a matching on R covering all high endpoints, impossible unless R includes the unique low vertex. That vertex has degree two by 2-connectivity. The earlier independent all-diameter minimum-degree-two result already gives a spanning path in this separate case.
