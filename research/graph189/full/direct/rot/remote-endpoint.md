# At least k+2 endpoints on every longest nonspanning vertex set

Let G be finite, simple and 2-connected, and let k be its second-smallest degree with multiplicity. Suppose each vertex has at most k vertices at distance exactly two. Fix any longest nonspanning path P and let R consist of the endpoints of all paths with exactly vertex set V(P). Then |R|≥k+2, and at least k+1 members of R have degree at least k. Call such vertices high. The proof also applies if the family is restricted to the paths obtainable from P by finitely many Pósa rotations at either end, since that family is closed under single rotations and path reversal as an undirected path.

Under Graph 189's bound on all positive even distances, each omitted vertex x has a high attainable endpoint at odd distance at least three: among the k+1 high endpoints, at most k are even-distance, and none can be adjacent to x since that would extend a longest path. If x itself is high, some high attainable endpoint is at distance **exactly three**: the registered R1 lemmas `diameter_le_five` and `five_endpoints` give diameter at most five and at least one low vertex in any distance-five pair. The cardinal proof does not use these registered lemmas or the cap on distances above two.

Call two endpoints compatible if they occur as the ends of a path in the family. Every u∈R has a high compatible mate. If its given partner is low, rotate that partner through a neighbor other than its immediate successor, available by 2-connectivity. This neighbor cannot be the other endpoint: adjacent endpoints would close a cycle on V(P) and connectedness would let us add an omitted vertex. All neighbors of any attainable endpoint belong to V(P). Starting with a high mate, its incident pivots produce at least k distinct compatible partners for u (one predecessor per pivot, including the trivial rotation), hence |R|≥k+1.

Suppose |R|=k+1. All pairs of R are compatible, and no two are adjacent by the cycle argument. Each high e∈R has degree precisely k: its incident pivot indices on a path from e to f map to distinct new endpoints in R−{f}. As there are k of the latter, this predecessor map is onto. Given r∈R−{e}, choose f∈R distinct from e,r, possible since k≥2. Realize a path oriented from e to f. The successor of r is adjacent to e by surjectivity of the predecessor map. Thus r is at distance two from e. All k other members of R exhaust the exact-distance-two cap at every high e.

For adjacent b,b′ outside R and any high e∈R, b is adjacent to e exactly when b′ is: if one is adjacent and the other is not, the latter is an additional vertex at distance two from e. Hence every component of G−R has a constant neighborhood among the high members of R. The component containing an omitted vertex has empty neighborhood there, because a neighbor of an attainable endpoint must lie in V(P). Such a component can have neighbors in R only at the unique possible low vertex. If there is no such attachment G is disconnected. Otherwise deleting the low vertex separates that component from the high members of R. This contradicts 2-connectivity and proves |R|≥k+2.

More generally write d_R(e)=|N(e)∩R|. For a high e and any realizing path from e, its pivot predecessors supply d(e) distinct attainable endpoints, including e itself; after also discarding any neighbors of e, the remaining at least d(e)−1−d_R(e) are distance-two vertices in R. Thus the number of positive even-distance vertices outside R from e is at most k−d(e)+1+d_R(e). Also d_R(e)≤|R|−1−k since every e has k distinct compatible nonneighbors. Consequently if |R|=k+2, the induced graph G[R] has maximum degree at most one and each high attainable endpoint sees at most two positive even-distance vertices outside R.
