# Odd second-smallest degree

This companion to `even-exterior.md` excludes the former equality component for all odd k as well. The resulting theorem, with the same hypotheses, is that every component outside any longest path is the singleton low vertex or has at least ceil(k/2)+2 vertices, for every k≥2, with unrestricted graph order and diameter. In particular a path missing at most ceil(k/2)+1 vertices guarantees a longest path omitting at most the low singleton. The earlier low-singleton argument then gives traceability when 2δ>k (k≥3). The theorem is on paper.

Let k=2h+1≥3 and suppose the exterior component C of a longest path P has m=h+2 vertices. The metric degree bound in the companion gives q_x=d_C(x)≥ceil(k/2)=h+1 for every high x in C. Thus all high vertices are universal in C, and C is a clique. Let a_x be the number of its contacts on P. Every high x has exactly h contacts: it needs at least h by degree, and h+1 contacts would either give at least 2h+2 shift witnesses when all runs are singletons, or imply q_x≥a_x+1 by the nonsingleton bound using a high neighbor y (d(y)≥k). Write A for the union of all contacts of all C vertices, and t=|A|.

The shift witnesses of x are disjoint from A: a consecutive contact of another clique member permits a two-vertex insertion, and a consecutive contact of x permits a one-vertex insertion. All A positions missed by x are at distance two via C. Hence t+r_x≤k, where r_x is the number of its mark runs. In particular t≤2h. Any nonsingleton run of x has at least two contacts exclusive to x among C, because an additional contact y at either position permits splicing x,y over the intervening path vertex. If all at least h+1 high clique vertices had a nonsingleton run, their exclusive contacts would give t≥2h+2. Some high x thus has h singleton runs, giving t≤h+1. No high vertex can now have a nonsingleton run, since two exclusive contacts would leave every other high vertex at most h−1 of the t positions. Thus t∈{h,h+1}. Two distinct positions of A admit distinct contacts in C. Otherwise both are attached solely to the same vertex, leaving another high clique member at most t−2<h contacts. Splicing through C shows that the h−1 or h intervening gaps and both outer tails all have at least m nonmark vertices.

Let B denote the 2t bracketing positions immediately preceding and following A and put W=V(G)−(C∪A∪B). Then |W|≥(t+1)m−2t, and the excess above this lower bound dominates the path excess s_P, defined as the sum over all gaps and tails of their lengths less m. By the rotation bound from the companion, an endpoint with at most j neighbors among A has degree at most m−1+s_P+j. All of these assertions persist under rotations on V(P). We may arrange that both endpoints are high: if one is low, rotate it using a neighbor other than its immediate successor. Such a neighbor exists by 2-connectivity, and the opposite endpoint cannot be adjacent since closing the path into a cycle would permit extending into C. The changed endpoint is high while the other endpoint stays fixed.

## All high clique members share the same h contacts (t=h)

Here h≥2, since a sole attachment would separate C. Fix a high x in C. Its shifts B have size 2h, so there is at most one additional even-distance vertex Z. The eccentricity of x is at most four: a nonempty fifth layer would be separated by the unique fourth-layer vertex. Every member of W except possibly Z is at distance three from x. Its geodesic passes through a bracket or an extra second-layer vertex, the latter necessarily Z. Every such second-layer vertex has an A-neighbor, since no vertex of C has path contacts beyond A. Thus every w∈W except possibly Z is at exact distance two from some a∈A; a W vertex having a direct neighbor in A is itself Z. At most one endpoint has a neighbor in A, because all vertices outside C∪A neighboring A lie in B∪{Z} and no member of B can be an endpoint on P.

Take the representatives W₀, S_a, d_a and U_a of the companion proof; m≥4 ensures distinct representatives. Set D=Σd_a and E=Σ|S_a−U_a|. All A pairs share a high contact. In the three-set count of the companion, the only possibly invalid representative is Z when it lies in W₀ and directly neighbors a. Let η be the number of such lost incidences. If Z is a representative of the bracket b belonging to the marked position u, every loss at a≠u has b∈S_a and either a∼u or b∉U_a. Hence η≤1+D+E. If Z∈W₀, all W−W₀ are covered at distance two from A; if Z∉W₀ there is no loss of representatives and at most one uncovered member of W−W₀. In either case, summing the three-set counts with cap 2h+1 gives

    |W−W₀| ≤ h²−D−E+(1+D+E) = h²+1.

On the other hand |W−W₀|≥(h+1)(h+2)−4h+s_P=h²−h+2+s_P. Thus s_P≤h−1. With two high endpoints, at least one has no neighbors among A. Its degree is at most m−1+s_P≤2h, a contradiction.

## One further attachment (t=h+1)

Here m=t+1, k=2t−1, and |W|≥t²+1+s_P. Each high x misses one mark u and has as its exact second layer the 2(t−1) brackets of its own contacts together with u. They exhaust the full even-distance cap. If a mark a touches a high clique vertex x, it has no neighbor in W, because any such neighbor would be an additional second-layer vertex from x.

First suppose every mark touches a high vertex. Every w∈W is at distance three from each high x. It sees a bracket, since the alternative second-layer predecessor would be the missed mark u, which has no W neighbors. Thus W is covered at distance two from A. Among the unordered pairs of marks at most one pair can lack a common high contact: a disjoint pair a,c forces every high vertex to miss one of the two, so all other marks touch all high vertices and both a,c are touched by some high. Relative to a complete common-contact count, the missed marks in the three-set argument therefore lose at most J=2 ordered incidences. If t≥3 (so the representatives exist), summing the same three-set counts with cap 2t−1 yields

    (t−1)²+s_P ≤ |W−W₀| ≤ t(t−2)−D−E+J,

so s_P≤1. Each endpoint has no neighbor in A and degree at most t+1<2t−1, impossible.

If one mark l touches only the unique low z∈C, then every other mark touches every high in C. Write r for the number of contacts of z, so l is among them and r≥1. The distance-two layer of z contains the 2r brackets of its own contacts, the t−r missed marks, and all p=|N(l)∩W| vertices of W adjacent to l. These are disjoint, hence p≤t−1−r. Now only l has any W neighbors. Every W vertex missing from the distance-two coverage by A belongs to N(l): its geodesic of length three from high x passes through a bracket of its high contacts or through l. A direct W neighbor of such a bracket is always seen in two steps from its high-covered mark. Also a counted representative in W₀ can fail to be at distance two from the relevant a only when a=l and the representative lies in N(l). The uncovered members of W−W₀ and the lost representatives total at most p, being distinct members of N(l)∩W.

An unordered A-pair with no common clique contact must be l together with one of the t−r marks missed by z. The loss in counting missed marks is at most 2(t−r) over all a. Each a also sees every missed *clique member* at distance two via its contact. These additional witnesses are disjoint from all other counted sets. Across A there are exactly 2t−r such incidences: l misses the t high vertices of C, and t−r marks miss z. Consequently, when t≥3,

    (t−1)²+s_P ≤ |W−W₀| ≤ t(t−2)−D−E+2(t−r)−(2t−r)+p.

Thus s_P≤−1−r+p≤t−2−2r≤t−4. Each endpoint has at most the single marked neighbor l and has degree at most t+s_P+1<2t−1. This yields the same contradiction.

Finally, if t=2 and h=1 (k=3), a possible low clique member already has its two neighbors in C, so it has no contact on P. Both marks therefore touch high vertices and W is covered at distance two by the two marks, as above. The marks miss at least three of the six possible incidences to C because each high has exactly one contact. Every such missed member is at distance two from the mark through C. The two marks together have room for at most 2·3−3=3 members of W. Yet their two tails and one gap require |W|≥3·3−4=5. This disposes of the final case.
