# Connectivity bounds from a cycle outside a longest path

Let G be a qualifying finite 2-connected graph, k its second-smallest degree, δ its minimum degree, and κ its vertex-connectivity. Fix an arbitrary longest nonspanning path P. The following arguments combine actual path extensions with upper bounds on the order n.

Suppose a cycle D of length d is disjoint from P. For any 2 ≤ l ≤ min(κ,d,|P|), where |P| counts vertices, Menger's vertex theorem gives l disjoint D-to-P paths, including distinct endpoints in each set. Indeed every separator between the sets has at least l vertices: a smaller deletion leaves a vertex of each set and keeps G connected. Trim the paths so that their interiors miss both sets, and mark their ends on P. Each outer tail of P contains at least d unmarked vertices, since it can be replaced by one connector and a path visiting every vertex of D. Between consecutive marks the two connectors and the longer of the two arcs of D can replace the gap. That arc has at least ceil(d/2)+1 vertices. Maximality therefore implies

$$ n ≥ 3d+l+(l-1)(\lceil d/2\rceil+1). $$

The first d counts the disjoint cycle itself. Attached paths need not be edges. The count ignores their extra internal vertices.

**Half-degree connectivity.** Suppose k ≥ 4 and κ ≥ t = floor(k/2). There is no disjoint cycle of length at least 2ceil(k/2). Use l=t in the preceding inequality (|P|≥k+1 because at least one endpoint is high and all its neighbors lie on P). For k=2t the lower bound is at least t²+7t−1, greater than the upper bound t²+5t+1. For k=2t+1 it is at least t²+8t+4, greater than t²+6t+4. The upper bounds use n ≤ 1+δ+k+floor((k+1)²/4), from the third-layer argument.

There is also no nonsingleton Hamilton-connected component C outside P. Its order is at least ceil(k/2)+2 by the longest-path component theorem. In the bipartite graph of edges between C and P there is a matching of size t. Otherwise a vertex cover of size less than t would separate surviving vertices of C and P in G. The t matched contacts have distinct ends on both sides. Replacing a gap or either tail using a spanning path of C costs |C| unmarked vertices in every one of the t+1 intervals. Consequently n ≥ t+(t+2)|C|. This is at least t²+5t+4 for even k and t²+6t+6 for odd k, each above the respective upper bound.

Every wholly high omitted C has internal minimum degree at least ceil(k/2). This internal-degree assertion is the path-attachment count in ../rot/even-exterior.md; it uses the exterior high-neighbor argument. By the Hamilton-connected form of Ore's theorem, if |C| ≤ 2ceil(k/2)−1, the degree sum of each nonadjacent pair in C is at least |C|+1, so C would be Hamilton-connected. Hence |C| ≥ 2ceil(k/2). If C were 2-connected, Dirac's circumference theorem would give a cycle of length at least min(|C|,2δ(C)) ≥ 2ceil(k/2), already excluded. Thus such a component has a cut vertex. These conclusions do not assert that all exterior components are wholly high.

**Asymptotic connectivity.** For k ≥ 6, if any high vertex is omitted, take its component C, and delete the unique low vertex from C if it belongs there. The remaining graph is nonempty and has minimum degree at least ceil(k/2)−1 ≥ 2. A longest path in it gives a cycle of length at least ceil(k/2), by closing back from an endpoint to its furthest neighbor. Set l=min(κ,d). Since κ≤δ≤k<|P|, this value is allowed in the displayed inequality.

The third-layer incidence bound in ../metric/subquadratic-order.md gives n ≤ 1+3k+sqrt(3) k^(3/2). If l=d, the displayed lower bound grows at least quadratically in k and contradicts this upper bound for sufficiently large k. Otherwise l=κ. Since d≥k/2, we have κ ≤ 1+4n/k ≤ 13+4/k+4sqrt(3k). In particular, along any sequence of counterexamples with unbounded k that omit a high vertex from a longest path, κ is O(sqrt(k)). A longest nonspanning path omitting no high vertex misses just the unique low vertex. The case of that singleton and the bounded-connectivity region remain open.

An explicit consequence is traceability whenever k ≥ 256 and κ > k/2. If a high vertex were omitted, the cycle just constructed has d ≥ k/2 and l ≥ k/2. The displayed lower bound is at least k²/8. For k ≥ 256 the subquadratic upper bound is strictly smaller, since sqrt(k) ≥ 16 and sqrt(3) < 7/4. If instead the only omitted vertex is the low z, its neighbors on P cannot be consecutive (insert z) or two positions apart (replace the midpoint with z, producing a longest path that omits a high singleton, contrary to the exterior component bound). All predecessor and successor positions of its δ neighbors are thus 2δ distinct distance-two witnesses. This forces 2δ ≤ k, contradicting κ > k/2. No assumption of regularity or an upper bound on n is required for this implication.

**Two endblocks.** Suppose κ > k/2, k ≥ 4, and a wholly high component C is omitted. Write h=ceil(k/2), t=floor(k/2). We have just shown C has a cut vertex. In each endblock only the cut vertex can have internal degree below h. A bridge endblock is impossible since its noncut vertex would have only one neighbor in C. Fan's circumference theorem for a 2-connected graph in which every distance-two pair has an endpoint of degree at least h gives a cycle of length at least min(|B|,2h) in any endblock B. The excluded disjoint-cycle bound shows |B|<2h, so B has a Hamilton cycle of length at least h+1.

Choose two endblocks. From each, match t distinct noncut vertices to t distinct marks on P. These matchings need be disjoint only within a color. If one matching failed, a cover of the contact edges by fewer than t vertices, together with the block's cut vertex, would separate its surviving noncut vertices and part of P, contrary to κ≥t+1. Each endblock has at least h noncut vertices.

Let u be the total number of distinct marks; their weights are 1 or 2 according as they represent one or both blocks, and sum to 2t. Put q=ceil((h+1)/2). Between consecutive single marks of the same color, the longer arc in that block uses at least q+1 vertices. Whenever opposite colors can be chosen, a path through both blocks uses at least 2q+1 vertices. Indeed join the entry to its cut vertex and the other cut to its exit along longer arcs and connect the cut vertices by a simple path outside the two block interiors. The cuts may coincide. Every gap therefore contains at least 1+q(w_i+w_j)/2 vertices. Each outer tail can be replaced by a path visiting the whole opposite endblock together with at least q vertices from the nearer block on the way to its matched port. The two tails each have at least q+h+1 vertices.

Summation, using |C|≥2h+1 and endpoint weights at most two, yields n≥2u+2+2tq+4h. Since u≥t, for even k=2t this exceeds t²+5t+1, and for odd k=2t+1 it exceeds t²+6t+4. Thus at κ>k/2 a wholly high exterior component is impossible at all k≥4. In particular, if two vertices have minimum degree, every vertex is high and G is traceable. If a counterexample has κ>k/2, k≥4 and a unique low vertex, that vertex must belong to the sole omitted component of every longest path; that component cannot be a singleton by the preceding 2δ bound.

The same two-block calculation only needs the noncut vertices of the two chosen endblocks to be high. Thus if the surviving mixed component has a cut vertex, its block tree has exactly two leaf blocks, and the low vertex is a noncut vertex of one of them. A 2-connected mixed component is also a possible residual case.

**Excluding the 2-connected mixed component.** Suppose the remaining C containing low z is 2-connected. Every pair of its vertices at distance two contains a high vertex of internal degree at least h. Fan's theorem and the disjoint-cycle exclusion imply |C|<2h and give a spanning cycle of C. For any distinct prescribed endpoints, adjoin a degree-two marker adjacent exactly to them. In the resulting graph of order |C|+1, every missing edge between high vertices has degree sum at least 2h≥|C|+1 and can be added by Bondy–Chvátal cycle closure. A Hamilton cycle in the augmented graph is equivalent to a spanning path with the prescribed endpoints. Thus the endpoint question can be tested after making all vertices other than z a clique. In that graph z has at least two neighbors. If z is a prescribed endpoint, start through a neighbor distinct from the other endpoint. Otherwise use two distinct neighbors to bracket z. The ordered clique path can contain those two consecutively unless they are precisely its prescribed endpoints; if z has at least three neighbors the bracketing pair can always be chosen to avoid this. There is consequently at most one exceptional unordered endpoint pair, the two neighbors of z when its degree is two.

Match t+1 distinct marks on P to distinct vertices of C by the same vertex-cover argument. At most one consecutive pair of marks corresponds to the exceptional unordered pair. Both tails can use the spanning cycle, and every other gap can use a spanning path of C. Even allowing zero vertices in that one gap gives n ≥ (t+1)+(t+2)|C|, already larger than the quadratic upper bound since |C|≥h+2. This excludes the mixed 2-connected component and leaves the two-endblock case described above.
