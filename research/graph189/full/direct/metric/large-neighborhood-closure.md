# Completing a large closed neighborhood in traceability closure

Let G be a two-connected graph under the Graph 189 hypotheses. For a vertex w put A=N(w), B=V minus N[w], a=|A| and b=|B|. If b≤k and a≥2k+b−2, G is traceable. In particular any qualifying graph of radius at most two is traceable when n≥4k−1, by applying the statement to a center. This proof only needs the exact-distance-two bound and at most one vertex of degree below k.

Assume a≥2k+b−2. The main argument treats b≥2; the smaller cases are given below. For x∈A define V(x)={y∈B : distance from x to y is at most two}; let f(x)=|V(x)|. All other A vertices missed by x are at distance two through w. Each member of V(x) is either a neighbor or uses a further slot of the exact-second-layer bound. Consequently d(x)≥a−k+f(x). Whenever the sum of the degrees of nonadjacent vertices is at least n−1=a+b, their edge can be added without changing traceability, by Bondy–Chvátal closure in the cone. Thus for x,y∈A it suffices that f(x)+f(y) plus all subsequent degree gains at the two endpoints is at least two. We show this closes A completely.

Every A vertex with f≥2 can already be joined to all A, and all pairs of A vertices with f≥1 can be joined to each other. Write P=N(B)∩A. Since G is two-connected and B is nonempty, |P|≥2. Each p∈P has f(p)≥1. If some p∈P has f(p)≥2, it joins every A vertex x of visibility zero, to which it was initially nonadjacent (it has a B neighbor). Each such x then gains one degree and is closeable to every remaining member of P, gaining a second degree if needed because |P|≥2 and x was initially nonadjacent to all P. Thus all zero-visibility vertices are now complete to A. All other pairs were already added.

It remains that every p∈P has f(p)=1. Let the label of p be its sole B neighbor. Every B neighbor of a label would also be visible from a p carrying that label, so each label is isolated within B. Connectedness implies that all B vertices are labels. Distinct labels have disjoint contact sets, and contacts for distinct labels are nonadjacent in G, again by f(p)=1. Since b≥2, the addition of all pairs of positive-visibility vertices gains at least one degree at every p. Now every p is closeable to all A. A vertex of zero visibility gains at least two edges to P; all pairs in A can again be filled.

Now N[w] is a clique of size a+1. Every vertex there has degree ≥a, while every outside vertex except possibly one has degree at least k≥b. All these high outside vertices can be joined to every member of the clique. They can then be joined to each other since 2(a+1)≥a+b, using a≥2k+b−2. If a sole exceptional vertex remains it has a neighbor by connectedness, and the remaining clique can be traversed starting at that neighbor. The resulting graph has a spanning path and all edge additions preserved traceability.

When n≤2k+1, all vertices of degree at least k close to a clique, and the one possible remaining vertex has a neighbor. When n≥2k+2 and b=0, a≥2k+1 suffices to fill A directly. When n≥2k+2 and b=1, a≥2k and the required sum of effective visibilities is at most one: every positive-visibility vertex closes to all A. Each zero-visibility vertex was nonadjacent to every member of P, hence gains a neighbor and can join all A as well. This finishes all cases using only the second-layer bound. For the radius corollary, b=|N₂(w)|≤k and n≥4k−1 gives a≥2k+b−2.
