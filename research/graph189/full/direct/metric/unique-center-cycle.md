# A necessary cycle of maximum-degree vertices in the distance-two graph

Suppose G is 2-connected, all degrees belong to {k,k+1}, the second-smallest degree is k, every even-distance set including its center has size at most k+1, and n>3k+2. Form D on the vertices of degree k+1, joining precisely those pairs whose distance in G is two.

**Theorem.** If D is a forest then G is Hamiltonian. Without the order assumption, G is traceable under the same hypotheses, using the previous bounded-order theorem. In particular this holds if at most two vertices have degree k+1, or if all vertices of degree k+1 form a clique in G.

**Proof.** The general wedge-cover lemma makes G an L₁-graph. Suppose u,v are distance-two vertices whose unique common neighbor is w. For this wedge C={w} and Q contains u and v. The L₁ condition |Q|≤|C|+1 therefore gives Q={u,v}. Consequently its three-neighborhood union U has size d(u)+d(v)+1≥2k+1. It cannot fill B₂(w), since then the wedge-cover lemma gives |Q|≤|C|−1. But |B₂(w)|≤2k+2. Thus |B₂(w)|=2k+2, d(w)=k+1, |N₂(w)|=k, d(u)=d(v)=k, and M=B₂(w) minus U is a singleton {b}. Since N₂(w) fills the positive even-distance budget, w has eccentricity at most three. The number of vertices at common odd distance at least three from u,w,v is at least n−3(k+1)+2≥2, by the lemma with C₁ empty. Call this set T. Its vertices are at distance three from w and have b as their only neighbor in B₂(w), since any such neighbor is a step-two vertex on a shortest path.

Pick any a in N(w)∩N(b) and t in T. Then a is nonadjacent to t, as their layers from w differ by two. Their unique common neighbor is b, since N(a) is contained in B₂(w) and t has only b there. The preceding conclusion applied to a–b–t shows that b has degree k+1. Thus w,b are joined in D. Let c be the unique missed vertex of the new wedge. It is different from w since w is adjacent to a and hence belongs to the new union. Applying the same construction repeatedly gives an arbitrarily long walk in D which never returns to the vertex from two steps earlier. No finite forest admits such walks. Hence when D is a forest every distance-two pair has at least two common neighbors. Asratian–Broersma–van den Heuvel–Veldman (1996), Theorem 7(a), now gives a Hamilton cycle since the exceptional spanning K_{p,p+1} family has n=2k+1. ∎

**Further restrictions on the transition w to b.** All vertices of J=N(w)∩N(b) and T have degree k, by applying the unique-common-neighbor argument to every pair a∈J,t∈T. Also |J|≥2, since otherwise w,b themselves would be endpoints of a unique-common-neighbor pair despite their degree k+1. Each J member is adjacent to every other J member and each T member to every other T member. Indeed for a∈J and t∈T, Q={a,t} at center b. Every other J member is adjacent to a or t and cannot see t by its layer from w. Every other T member is adjacent to a or t and cannot see a for the same reason. This proves both clique assertions.
