## The anchor

Fajtlowicz's original paper defines radius-critical using every proper connected induced subgraph. The original publisher abstract was accessible, but its full PDF was not. The ciliate result is stated without additional hypotheses as Theorem 4 of DeLaViña, Larson, Pepper, and Waller, who credit the 1988 paper explicitly. Their setting is finite, simple, connected graphs. The anchor is induced, with no assertion or use of isometry. The radius-four omission in the later authors' headline classification concerns their separate equality case for independence number and radius, not the ciliate theorem. At radius four the possibilities are P8, C4 with attached length-two paths, C6 with one leaf per vertex, and C8. The first two are excluded here.

- [Fajtlowicz, A characterization of radius-critical graphs](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.3190120409)
- [DeLaViña et al., Theorem 4 on page 317](https://www.uhd.edu/documents/academics/sciences/0911-0119.pdf)

## Completeness of the cycle search

Every first-layer vertex on C8 has one of the 32 tested nonempty anchor masks, and every mask has at least two contacts. A mask cannot repeat because two such vertices and two of their shared contacts would give a four-cycle. The program sorts vertices by these distinct masks, and when adding one it considers all subsets of possible edges to prior first-layer vertices. Rejecting a candidate pair edge is legitimate only if the induced anchor plus the two vertices already exhibits a forbidden subgraph. This covers the whole first-layer boundary, independently of whether a weaker prototype permits fewer prior edges.

Each first-layer vertex starts a four-vertex induced path in the anchor plus that vertex. If y has distance at least five to the cycle, take a shortest path from y to the cycle and concatenate the portion through its first-layer contact with the checked four-vertex path. All earlier vertices on the shortest path have no edge to the cycle, so the concatenation is induced and has at least eight vertices. Therefore every vertex has distance at most four to the cycle and distance at most three to the full first-layer boundary. A boundary with no exterior first-layer vertex is the whole graph by connectedness and gives the required inequality directly.

For a hypothetical radius-four extension, retain the entire current induced subgraph and its vertex labels. A tested 3-center has a vertex at ambient distance at least four. This witness is outside the current induced subgraph. Take a shortest path from the witness to the current vertex set. Its length is from one to three because the first-layer boundary stays present. Path vertices after the first new vertex have no neighbors in the current vertex set, and the new path is induced. The exact contacts of the first new vertex have no shared old neighbor with one another, by the square prohibition, and each has current distance from the center at least four minus the path length. The recursive search considers this exact contact set and keeps all older reference pairs. A path of length at most three between a retained pair in any later partial graph would also be an ambient path, so pruning it is safe. All graph caps throw errors rather than silently close a branch. The replay obtains 790 boundaries and 819 witness states, reaching order 18. The state count certifies this recurrence, without asserting that a proof must use that number of states.

## C6-ciliate density

Let B be the first layer off the C6-with-leaves anchor, X the second, and Y the third. The C++ verifier checks all contact masks and the relevant pairs. A second-layer vertex touches only bridges in B, and distinct bridges have distinct and disjoint opposite-pair contacts, including any optional leaves. Hence there are at most three bridges and they are nonadjacent, since an edge in B requires a common anchor contact. A shortest path to the anchor from a vertex of distance at least four would extend the verified five-vertex path starting at a bridge to an induced P8.

Each edge inside X must have a shared bridge. Without one, the edge and one bridge at each endpoint form a chordless three-edge link between bridges, which the finite check rejects. A vertex of Y has exactly one X-neighbor. If it had two, square-freeness forbids their sharing a bridge, so the two are nonadjacent. The three-vertex path through Y between the two X-neighbors, followed by a bridge's verified five-vertex induced path, would give P8. An edge inside Y must have the same unique X-neighbor at both endpoints, since otherwise that edge followed by a route through X to a bridge's five-vertex path gives P8.

Every neighborhood induces a matching and isolated vertices, since two adjacent edges within one neighborhood would give a four-cycle. The total neighborhood independence is therefore 2m−3t for m edges and t triangles. The twelve-vertex anchor contributes 24, leaving slack 12 relative to three per vertex. For contacts from B into the anchor, a mask with d contacts and e edges among those contacts contributes 2d−3e. Each first-layer vertex costs at most three for its contacts except bare bridges, which can each cost four. Thus B costs at most 3|B|+3 for its anchor contacts. For X the excess of the weighted bridge contacts with degree d is at most choose(d,2) above three. No two X vertices can share two bridges, so the total excess is at most three. Each Y vertex costs two for its unique X-contact. Each edge internal to B, X, or Y gives a triangle with a shared neighbor in the preceding layer. These triangles are distinct from each other and from the two-anchor-vertex triangles already counted. After the triangle offsets, internal-layer edges add no positive cost. Thus total neighborhood independence is at most 3n−6, which suffices since Fajtlowicz's anchor itself contains an induced P7.

The component argument in the main note gives the same bound. For a fixed bridge x touching a component T off the boundary, no induced P4 starts at x inside T+x. Thus each second-layer vertex has a unique parent, siblings induce a matching, and edges between distinct parent groups can only come from the matching on first-layer vertices. Connectedness leaves at most two such first-layer vertices, and each extra sibling edge gives a distinct triangle above the spanning tree. Applying the same argument from another bridge shows that if those bridges have disjoint contacts in T, an arbitrary contact of the second is at distance exactly two from the first, giving the prohibited chordless three-edge link. Therefore a bridge pair is charged in at most one component.
